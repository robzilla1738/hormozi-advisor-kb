# How I Lost $217,000 on A Day Trade & How I Trade Now

## Core thesis
Holding beats trading. Short-term trading turns gains into ordinary-income tax, so a trader must massively outperform just to match a buy-and-forget investor. Alex shares a recent ~$200K mistake: he sold a position that came back to his sale price, so he paid ordinary-income tax for nothing. The title says $217,000; he says "about 200 grand." His prescription: keep ~97% in stable assets, cap speculation at ~3%, "pretend you're a dead person," and get a hobby.

## Frameworks & models
- **Study anecdote** (he's unsure whether it was Fidelity or Schwab): the best-performing account profiles were people who bought and then died, followed by people who forgot their password. Inactivity wins.
- **Tax math on $100**:
  - Trade: $100 → $200, and the $100 gain is taxed as ordinary income (~45%). You keep $155.
  - Hold: same $100 gain at capital gains rates. You keep $180.
  - Break-even: a trader must turn $100 into ~$245 to net $180. That's ~45 extra points of growth just to match. Verified: 100 + 145.45×0.55 = 180 [inference: check of his figure].
- **Speculation bucket**: 3–5% of the portfolio to "scratch the itch," which he calls managing his own idiocy.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- ~95–97% of net worth in very stable assets. If you need the itch, set aside ~3% for pure speculation.
- Avoid selling inside a year (short-term gains = ordinary income).
- Don't try to beat quant firms with hundreds of analysts. He suggests ~75% of people trying lose money.

## Playbook steps
1. Put the core in passive, stable holdings. 2. Ring-fence ≤3% for speculation. 3. Make it hard to trade: "throw away your password," or give it to someone you'd be ashamed to ask. 4. Use the freed time to make money elsewhere or get a hobby.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Day and swing trading to beat the market.
- Selling winners early because something "felt overvalued."
- "Buying the dip" and watching it fall another 50%.

## Examples / case details
- At the time: owner of Prestige Labs and Gym Launch, ~$120M in sales over 44–45 months.
- His passive holdings outperformed his active ones.
- A friend of 20–25 years grew from managing ~$5M to ~$3.5B and told him "you need a hobby."
