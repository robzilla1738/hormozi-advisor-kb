## Core thesis
Q&A with six business owners (Zev, Selena, Joe, Nicole, Andy, and an unnamed enterprise B2B leader). Recurring themes: give value before you ask, because trust multiplies conversion; exceptional work takes far more hours and focus than people expect; change your environment to change your behavior; keep models simple; scale by attracting talent as well as customers. Several answers are personal or relationship advice rather than business advice.

## Frameworks & models
- **Three ways to get lead lists**: assemble manually, buy from brokers, scrape with software. He starts with scraping (cheapest): run the avatar search in the top 4–5 tools, work a 100–500-lead sample from each, double down on the source with the best response rate.
- **Lead magnet rule**: give something free that people would normally pay for, even a micro piece.
- **Give until they ask**: keep giving free value until people ask what else you have; find the common thread; poll for permission; then build the paid offer.
- **Trust before the ask**: 100 cold "buy my thing" asks ≈ 1 sale; offer to solve a problem free → ~20 yes → about half buy the next solution ≈ 10 sales (10× conversion). "A purchase decision is a prediction of future value."
- **Environment drives behavior**: move even 30 minutes away; let friendships that live only in the past fade ("if I'm going to cut this person eventually I might as well cut them now"); decide what you'll give up (he gave up football, fantasy and Netflix when he was opening gyms 7–10).
- **Fair or abundant exchange** with family: reset the "rules of engagement" with candor, then enforce them by reducing cadence and proximity. Family "morally gets leeway but not infinite".
- **Tall-ladder fruit**: valuable outcomes are in plain sight; you just climb longer (200 hours on one 90-minute presentation; 6 hours a day for 2 years on a book; committed to 3 videos a week for 10 years).
- **Queen of a puddle → pond → lake → ocean**: master one avatar first.
- **Semi-private gym model** (credited to Rick Mayo of Alloy): $300–400/mo, 2–3×/week, wealthier avatar, 1,200–1,400 sq ft, one manager (~$40–50K base + 10–20% profit share) + 2 helpers, ~120–150 members (Dunbar's number cap), ~50% margin, ~$500–600K/yr per location.
- **Promo "wrappers"**: rotate challenge names to fight ad fatigue but push them all into the same sales process so ops don't change.
- **Yes-man / no-man partnership**: ask the cautious implementer "what would you need to see to feel comfortable?" and turn it into a checklist that unlocks each next location.
- **Skill-stack order = order you make money**: a base skill or product → promotion/advertising (leads) → conversion/sales → leadership/ops.
- **$1K→$1M is skills; $1M→$100M is character; $100M→$1B is (his guess) time.** Scaling speed tracks talent-recruitment speed: a "two-sided funnel" of customers and talent.
- **Enterprise B2B**: hire people out of target Fortune 100 departments, then cross-sell divisions/geographies; pitch so the stakeholders look good and carry no job risk; frame that it takes "hours of work, not time" (48 one-hour meetings over 2 years or 3 months); BAMFAM; gated content → script "what are you getting this for?" → navigate the org.
- **Newsletters/media**: niche, high-value audiences have the best LTV; content is the top of funnel → subscribers → sell ads or your own products. Follower counts matter less because algorithms favor content quality.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Gym launch: 3-month pre-launch, spend disproportionately (~$30K) on pre-sales, open at full capacity, be in the black month one.
- Semi-private churn target ~3% (5% okay). At $400/mo and 5% churn, LTV ≈ $8K, so ~$500 CAC is easy. 120 members × 5% = 6 to replace a month ≈ $3K/mo of ads.
- Focus on the offer that makes the most money now; cut side projects.
- Pick work where you forget about time; the volume required is fixed.
- A partner's non-negotiable goals must match (goals → values → lifestyle). He ended an earlier engagement over different goals.

## Playbook steps
1. Build lead lists via scraping tools, test samples, double down. 2. Lead with a free version of something normally paid. 3. Give until they ask, poll, then build the paid offer. 4. Block your first ~4 hours daily for the one exceptional product and document the effort. 5. For a new gym location: 3-month pre-sale → open full → keep churn ~3–5% → replace ~6 members a month.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Generic free value that everyone else gives too.
- Underestimating the work required ("a whole day used to feel long").
- Being half-in across multiple avatars or projects.
- Opening a location fast to make money instead of pre-selling.
- Choosing a partner you must avoid talking to; settling for a "$45,000 employee" relationship.
- Sales reps "waiting for a response" with no next meeting booked.
