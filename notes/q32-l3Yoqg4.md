## Frameworks & models
- **CLOSER**: Clarify why they're there → Label them with a problem ("so what I'm hearing is… does that sound right?") → Overview past pain (what tried, how long, how did it work — the "pain cycle", repeat until options exhausted) → Sell the vacation → Explain away concerns → Reinforce the decision. Final R added later because the sale continues through onboarding and check-ins.
- **Only three things come out of your mouth on a sales call**: questions, restatements, anecdotal stories.
- **Three pillars**: every solution can be pitched as three elements (fitness: workouts/nutrition/accountability; leads: timely/qualified/exclusive). Prospect is missing at least one or they wouldn't be on the call → "unlosable situation unless you choose to lose the frame."
- **Sell the vacation, not the plane flight**: Maui is the destination; tiers differ only in how they get there (swim / boat / commercial / private jet).
- **Belief-breaking stories**: short analogies, no jargon (brushing teeth = external accountability becoming internal habit; you don't need motivation to watch Game of Thrones → make food something they look forward to).
- **Three decision questions** for stalls: does this meet your needs? do you like us/our values? do you have access to funds (or know someone who does)? If yes ×3, proceed.
- **Words ≈10%, tonality ≈90%** (credits Jordan Belfort's book). Trick vs train: "conviction will correct your tone."
- **Belief isn't binary**: would you bet $1K vs $10K on it; would you sell it to your mother.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If the prospect hasn't clarified why they need a solution, do not ask for the sale — sit in the pain.
- Pitch portion ≤3 minutes; they buy based on how understood they feel.
- Memorize two things: the stories and the obstacle overcomes (the "red zone"); know the question milestones on the front end.
- Train salespeople on the prospect, not the product.
- If your company has no testimonials / you don't believe in it, find another product.
- First 48 hours post-sale decide whether the customer believes in you long term.
- He doesn't hire seasoned closers; believers newer to sales can beat them.

## Playbook steps
1. Clarify ("what made you reach out… why is that important?"; push past "just wanted information").
2. Label the problem and get them to own it.
3. Loop the pain cycle; frame it as "not your fault — you were missing one piece."
4. Pitch three pillars with stories, under 3 minutes.
5. Ask; handle objections — price, decision-maker (rely on past agreements, ask forgiveness vs permission, delayed close: deposit now, rest Friday, tear up contract if partner says no), stalls ("what's your main concern? let's think about it together"; "what are you most afraid of happening?").
6. Reinforce: personalized video naming the onboarding person, gift in the mail, handwritten card.
7. Training: 60 min/day, 5 days/wk — ~25 min read script aloud, ~5 min drill obstacles, then review recordings (what went right/wrong, next time), drill as a team.
8. Conviction tactics: reread testimonials aloud daily with the team; owners fix every product hole and never blame the customer.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Talking about the product/company (ego: "we're a $100M company") eight minutes in without knowing the prospect's revenue, profit, pain.
- Jargon/technobabble about modules, macros, funnels — "sounds like work."
- Misarticulating the prospect's problem.
- Ignoring customers right after the sale.
- Salespeople burn out "not because they can't handle no but because they can't handle how they feel about themselves" when the product doesn't merit the price.
- Fancy sales systems: "simplicity scales."
