## Core thesis
Live hot-seat Q&A with six trades/home-service owners (intro frames it as brick-and-mortar/HVAC; guests are roofing, electrical, HVAC-adjacent, junk removal, commercial construction/elevators). Recurring theme: problems are usually multi-step (fix price/offer → cash flow → hire redundancy → more lead sources); trade-offs are preferences, not right/wrong; pay for talent; separate inbound/outbound; double down on the channel you're already good at; think in opportunity cost.

## Frameworks & models
- **Regret = imagined upside without the downside you didn't suffer.**
- **Want less or trade more**: dissatisfaction comes from wanting both (cookies vs six-pack). Short-term profit can be made up; family time can't.
- **Levels of "who"**: first $50K hire → six figures → $250K/$500K/$1M+ employees. "The best talent's always in the future" (credited to president Sheran).
- **Keep passive passive, active active**: investments are fine if they change nothing about your days.
- **Cost of the big thing** = all the new things you give up; "four or five big runs" left in a 30–40-year productive life.
- **Multi-step solutions**: "if it were one thing, you probably would have already seen it" — second/third-order fixes.
- **Offer premium + guarantee**: faster / more reliable / easier, guaranteed (e.g. on time and on budget or "I'll give you all my profit back") → raise prices 20–40%; worst case you're back to today's price.
- **More wells**: dependence on a few referral partners gives them all the leverage.
- **Management diamond** (blockers): don't know they need to, don't know how, don't know when, something blocking, motivation.
- **Key man = #1 risk**: no one will buy it, and you'll eventually want to kill it yourself.
- **Inbound is what you graduate to**: separate outbound and inbound teams; inbound = "Christmas tree of the career path," fewer reps, different (lower per-sale) commission, more volume and reliability.
- **Reject the premise that only one channel works**: if a competitor acquires customers in that channel in your market, it's repeatable.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you have enough demand but can't handle volume → raise price to fund redundancy (2–3 backups per role).
- Customer price complaints: watch what they do (6 years of repeat buying), not what they say.
- Lower tolerance for mediocrity; tech making $300K/yr profit justifies ~$50K to recruit one.
- Recruiting unlock: national ads + generous relocation package (e.g. $25K now, $25K at month 6).
- Techs who must show up at set times and attend meetings are employees: keep W-2, "go more legit, not less."
- "If you got half your time back, could you double the business?"
- CAC $7,500 vs ~$6,500 revenue per customer = doesn't work.
- Funnel inbound leads to one proven "savage" closer while iterating, so offer/lead source/process are the only variables; avoid too many "news."
- Sales and marketing are core → keep in house; hire a real marketer and pay more than you expect.
- Channel choice: pick the one with highest overlap with your/team's existing skill; first do more of what already works (risk-adjusted) before learning a new channel.
- Burn-down rule (his "unreasonable" advice): when a new opportunity is orders of magnitude better, sell/exit the old one even cheaply; decide on opportunity cost.

## Playbook steps
- Founder stuck on comfort (Thomas, roofing ~$6M → $100M goal): accept a short-term profit hit to hire high-level leaders; keep real estate passive.
- Electrical contractor (Corey, $1.6M, ~$650K kept): guarantee-backed price increase → hire backup tech → outreach to other HVAC companies ("let me quote some of these jobs") → keep advertising/hiring.
- HVAC owner (Tanner, $1.5M, $700K net, Bozeman): time study (15-min alarms for a week), rank by revenue value/uniqueness, delegate bottom half, spend to recruit techs.
- Roofer (Trenton, $3M → $10M): send door-knockers back to the street, one top closer on inbound, hire a marketing leader in house.
- Junk removal (Art, $1M → $10M): go from a few networking events/week to several/day; reinvest in a second channel later.
- Construction ($11M, 18% margin) + elevator co ($3M year one, 30% margin; Adrian): reasonable path = spend ~$800K profit on 2–3 leaders for construction; Alex path = exit construction within ~90 days and go all-in on recurring elevator business.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Combining inbound and outbound teams (door-knockers get lazy once fed leads).
- Making passive investments active for ~5% better returns.
- Name-dropping/ego keeping the founder involved in everything.
- Switching to 1099 to save cost on people who are employees.
- Spreading across several channels before one works.

## Examples / case details
- Alex fire-sold six gyms in 90 days, getting "on six what I should have made on one," then hit ~$1M/month within six months on the next thing.
- Opportunity math: if a perfect construction exit ≈$5M, he'd expect to build $1M of recurring elevator revenue in a quarter worth that enterprise value [figures his hypothetical].
- Elevator business: grew to $3M while attention split — "you might be at eight" with full attention.
- Intro: $106K book launch in a weekend; acquisition.com portfolio >$250M aggregate; pitch for acquisition.com/roadmap (10 stages by headcount).
