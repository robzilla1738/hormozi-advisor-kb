# How to Change Your Life

## Core thesis
You aren't getting what you want because you want too many things at once and won't make tradeoffs. Everything has a price and a value. People stall in "decision purgatory" because the cost is certain while the upside isn't. Downside is smaller than feared (worst case is roughly crashing on a couch) and upside isn't as life-changing for happiness as imagined, so consciously choose the trade and bet on uncertainty and delay. Pure mindset; no business tactics.

## Frameworks & models
- **Structurally impossible wish list**: house in a ski town *and* near the beach, secluded *and* walkable, cozy *and* lots of land. You can't have it all, so pick.
- **Price vs value**: critics only see the cost, not the payout. Whether a trade is good depends on the person and context ($500 shoes: billionaire vs minimum wage).
- **Why trades feel hard**: the loss is guaranteed and fixed; the gain is uncertain.
- **Worst-case reality check**: for a socially functioning person in the developed world, the worst case is a couch, still with food and medical access.
- **Hedonic set point** [he attributes much of subjective well-being to inheritance/disposition]: the brain evolved to find threats, so solving problems won't make you lastingly happier. Bets have less downside *and* less emotional upside than imagined.
- **Arbitrage lies in the uncertain and the delayed**: most people won't pay those two prices. Humans overestimate downside and underestimate upside (evolutionary pessimism bias).
- **Life happens for you**: no regret if you believe you had to go through it to get here.
- **Criticism filter**: he has never been criticized by someone ahead of him in that domain. "Judge your character by the quality of your enemies."
- **Exposure test (Expedition 8)**: an 8th-grade germaphobe went 5 days in the woods without washing hands and nothing happened. Other people's opinions are like those germs.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- "You can't expect a 1% outcome without having a 1% tradeoff."
- Be conscious of (1) what you're trading and (2) what you're trading it for. The worst trades are the unconscious ones (lost your 20s' fun *and* built no skills or nest egg).
- If something is solvable, someone knows how. Do something for that person (money, a favor, relational capital) so they help you.
- If it doesn't work, nothing changes; if it works, everything does. So take the first step.

## Playbook steps
1. List what you want and find the mutually exclusive items. 2. Choose the trade explicitly, cost and payout. 3. Play the worst case out in specifics. 4. Ignore critics who aren't ahead of you. 5. Act despite uncertainty; retry until it works ("all failure besides death is psychological," a quote he attributes to Jocko).

## Mistakes he warns against
- Staying in decision limbo, which costs years with nothing gained.
- Expecting to be fully understood on the internet.
- Judging other people's trades without their context.

## Examples / case details
- Claims: Acquisition.com portfolio at $250M aggregate revenue. Broke the Guinness record for fastest-selling nonfiction book: "$106 million in sales," 3.6M books in under three days (as stated).
- Ray Dalio (~$30B, ~79) roasted as a "grifter" in TikTok comments: "you will never be legitimate to the ignorant."
- Contrasted with people who backpacked Europe in their 20s; he chose to build skills instead.
