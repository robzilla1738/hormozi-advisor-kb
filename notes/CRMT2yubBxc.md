## Core thesis
Your attention is the scarcest asset. Spend nearly all of it on the vehicle that grows net worth fastest, which for most people is income: business or skills. Regularly take dividends out of the business as downside protection and park them in truly passive, liquid index funds. Switch focus to managing wealth only when ~10% annual growth on investable assets exceeds what you earn per year.

## Frameworks & models
- **Dividends = downside protection**: ~90–95% of businesses fail within five years ("whatever it is") and only ~1% ever sell. "I would rather not stack the chips on a 99-to-1 bet," so take chips off the table regularly. Captions read "91 to one"; [inference] likely 99:1 given the 1% claim.
- **Opportunity cost of active investing**: $1M passive at ~10% = $100K/yr. Active investing might add 5–10% ($50–100K) but takes half your mind, while the business's $500K net free cash flow could grow to $750K–$1M with full focus.
- **Owner earnings / net free cash flow**: what you can take out after reinvesting in growth and competitive advantage.
- **Crossover rule**: when 10% of investable net worth > annual income, shift attention from growing income to growing wealth.
- **Liquidity via borrowing**: you can take loans against index holdings at ~50–70% LTV at low rates ("1–2%") to fund new ventures or real estate.
- **Invest in yourself first**: skills are the highest return. Selling skills built over ~2 years can take you from $60K to $250–500K.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Cash reserve: about 6 months to 2 years of normal living expenses ("so you can sleep well at night").
- Everything else goes into truly passive vehicles (S&P/index), not real-estate flipping, crypto or speculation that eats attention.
- Early savings ($500–2,000/mo) are better spent on skills, coaching, courses and books than invested.
- Employees: tie yourself to revenue. Show with metrics how you make the company money and that losing you would cost more than keeping you. At an income cap, keep living costs low and plow surplus into liquid assets.

## Playbook steps
1. Invest in skills (highest ROI). 2. Grow income or the business with ~100% of your attention. 3. Pull dividends regularly. 4. Keep 6–24 months of expenses in cash. 5. Put the rest in indexes and forget it. 6. Borrow against it if needed. 7. When 10% of assets > income, shift to wealth management.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Banking everything on a future sale for "some magical number."
- Spending mind-share on active investing too early. He admits he made this mistake.
- "Stepping over dollars to pick up pennies": saving small sums instead of investing in earning power.
- Calling side hustles "passive."

## Examples / case details
