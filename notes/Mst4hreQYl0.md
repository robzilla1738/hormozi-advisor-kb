## Core thesis
Most businesses send traffic straight to "book a call / get a quote." That only works for a warm audience. Instead, use a lead magnet: a mini offer that completely solves a narrow problem, free or low cost, which reveals the next problem your core offer solves. You convert more of the same traffic (he says 3–5× more hand-raisers) without spending more on ads. Lead magnets: 3 types × 4 delivery methods; name them by testing with your audience; then repeat the CTA. (Leads book p. 31.)

## Frameworks & models
- **Lead magnet definition**: "a complete solution to a narrow problem" that reveals another problem solved by the core offer; "when someone pays with time now, they're more likely to pay with money later."
- **3 types**: (1) **Reveal a problem**: assessment/audit, e.g. a website speed test; best for problems that get worse while waiting (posture, termites, tax/cash flow). Bonus: show the delta to "solved." (2) **Free trial / sample** (Costco sampler): limit uses, quantity, time, or a combination; deprivation comes when it's removed. (3) **One step of many** (first coat of paint, first laser session of 6–8, first 2 course videos, colors for a stylist).
- **4 delivery methods**: software/tools (calculators, assessments, templates; e.g. Neil Patel URL analyzer); information (mini courses, guides, expert interviews; infinitely scalable, no ops drag; e.g. his Scaling Roadmap = tool + info); services (free audits, done-for-you; only for qualified leads); physical (his books; "CEO" shirts for proven CEOs). Methods can be combined.
- **Sell at the point of greatest deprivation**: entree → dessert; don't solve the core problem for free. Solve a problem that triggers deprivation for what you sell.
- **Be specific on the negatives, vaguer on the positives**: nailing their problems builds belief and keeps you compliant with realistic expectations.
- **Naming conventions**: number + outcome + time frame ("3 emails that turn cold leads into clients in 24 hours"); "how to [yay] without [boo] even if [insecurity]"; adjective + good thing ("the lazy funnel template that converts like crazy"); "X mistakes that [prevent good thing]."
- **CTA formula**: (1) clear, not clever; (2) exact next action; (3) a reason to act now (scarcity/urgency if you have it; any reason beats none, per the line-cutting study). Maximize ability to ask × number of asks by giving value between asks.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Test the packaging/headline more than the contents; the name can change uptake 2–10×.
- One lead magnet failing ≠ lead magnets don't work ("bad lead magnets don't work").
- Freebie-seeker worry: qualify with a dropdown and route unqualified leads elsewhere; give free services only to leads with budget, authority, need, and urgency.
- Free-service math: $25 hard cost (worth $250+), 1 in 4 buys → $100 CAC; fine if a customer is worth much more. CEO shirt at $3–4, 1 in 20 converts → ~$100 per CEO.
- Webinars are read as a sales pitch by more sophisticated audiences; a "free case study" video converted better for him.
- Service businesses (he says 78% of businesses) have real capacity limits, so state them ("4 spots"). Big businesses: use rolling cohorts ("only X start per week").
- If you spend 2 years writing a book, spend 2 days testing the title; long-term sales come from word of mouth.

## Playbook steps
1. Choose a type (reveal problem / trial / one step). 2. Choose delivery (software / info / service / physical). 3. Name it, testing with small ad tests or polls, texts, or A/B comments to your avatar ("if you're not a business owner, don't answer"). 4. Qualify. 5. Embed CTAs in, before and after the magnet, and follow up ("did you like the free thing? you'll love this paid thing").

## Mistakes he warns against
- Only offering "book a call / submit for quote" to cold traffic.
- Giving away the core solution for free.
- Blaming the channel after one bad execution.
- Advertising the vehicle instead of the result.
- Forgetting the CTA, or asking only once.

## Examples / case details
- April 2016: paid $25K for a mastermind; the recommended webinar flopped. He swapped it for "Free case study: how we added 213 members and $112,000 to a small gym in San Diego," and the next morning Leila's calendar was full.
- Ashley (personal stylist) used a book-a-call CTA; colors are the step-one magnet.
- Allen (software co.) top ad: "4 reasons why you'll never have a million-dollar agency."
- Gym Launch gave 4 months of agency/lead services free, then charged after the deprivation built.
- First business: "free training project," a year of free training for testimonials; after it, almost all paid.
- "Big Booty Boot Camp" beat "6-week deadlift and squat seminar."
- $100M Leads: split-tested 6 headlines, plus the image and subheadlines.
- Speed pitch: each second of load time costs ~3% of conversion; 9s → 3s = 18%.
