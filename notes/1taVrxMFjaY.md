# NO NEW FRIENDS (My "Extreme Views" on Friendship)

## Core thesis
Friendship is a continuum, not a binary. He openly calls his view transactional: the pros of a relationship should outweigh the cons, and the one-strike disqualifier is not rooting for you (resenting your wins). People want the version of you that best serves them.

## Frameworks & models
- **Relationships as investments:** time spent either way, so invest where returns are highest. **Abundant exchange:** if both sides feel they get the better end, the relationship continues indefinitely.
- **Hard thing vs easy thing:** if the easy thing were right, you'd already have done it, so do the hard one.
- **Pruning, not burning bridges:** the tree has limited sunlight and water.
- **Seasons of friendship:** tracks align, then diverge. Honor the season.
- **Two exits:** let contact frequency fade (most friendships), or for family and very close friends, have a hard conversation using Leila's rule: keep the other person as a human at the forefront rather than being right, and own "I've changed".
- **Spouse = the ultimate ally**, who should root for you hardest and never resent success.

## Decision rules
- If they're more likely to help your long-term goal, they've earned their spot ("paying rent for my time"). If not, decide whether the relationship matters more than the goal.
- These rules matter more when many people compete for your time. If you're not there yet, they may not apply.
- Reply to "you've changed": "You're right. And you haven't."

## Examples
- A close FBI-agent friend: an escape from business. Both value excellence at their craft.
