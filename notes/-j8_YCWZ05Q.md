# How to Get Your Customers to Stay FOREVER

## Core thesis
These are 13 retention lessons from Skool (~22.3M users), credited to Sam and Skool News. Churn is a leaky bucket. It falls sharply with tenure, so design to get people past month 1, day 90 and month 6. Fix through subtraction (deliver the original promise with less friction and less stuff), not addition. "Growth really just looks like a flat retention curve."

## Frameworks & models
- **Leaky bucket**: MRR grows when joins > cancels, is flat when they're equal, and declines when they're fewer. Track joins vs cancels monthly and label the business growing, flat or declining.
- **Benchmarks (Skool; for consumer/online services, not insurance or alarms)**: platform average 80% monthly retention (20% churn). The best groups have <10% churn. Poor groups have <70% retention (30%+ churn). 20% → 10% churn doubles LTV.
- **Churn drops with time (cohorts)**: months 1–3 run 20%+. After day 90 it's ~10% or less (halved). By month 6 it's ~2% (5×). Reframe: "How do I get someone past day 90 / month 6?" Month 1 = activation, onboarding, expectation setting, honest promises, showing up. Day 90 = deliver an outcome or behavior change. Month 6 = connection with other members.
- **Fix by subtraction**: make a promise, then remove all friction from getting it. "If you were to simply fulfill the promise you originally made, they'd probably stay."
- **Overwhelm (#1 churn reason after price)**: Planet Fitness found members used only cardio plus some weights (~10–15% of a club), so it offered just that for ~$10. Using 1 of 5 paid-for things feels like 20% of the value, while offering just that one thing feels like 100%. A Skool community deleted content and churn went 30% → 5% (6× LTV). "Value per second, not seconds of value."
- **Fight-for / let-go survey**: "If I deleted everything, what one thing would you fight for?" and "If I removed one thing, which wouldn't you miss?" Chart it, keep the top 1–2, and forget the rest.
- **Design for extremes**: a busy path (explicit "start here" plus a weekly cadence, value from a distance) and power-user depth (optional side quests).
- **Snakes and ladders**: remove the snakes (friction, confusion, overwhelm) and move the stickiest ladder (best call replay, key implementation) into onboarding. Get a fast win within 24 hours.
- **Belonging**: an employee with a close friend at work stays ~5× longer (via Leila), and the same logic applies to customers. Introduce regulars to new members like a party host. Anchor the community with 10 true regulars: DM them, learn their goals, spotlight them, invite them to contribute, matchmake new people to them.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If fewer than 7 in 10 stay monthly, retention is your highest-ROI lever.
- A community or offer under 90 days (or 6 months) old will show inflated churn. Don't overhaul because month-1 churn is high unless nearly everyone leaves.
- Ask cancellers why using a DM script, tag reasons (price, overwhelm, not using, missing feature, not what I expected), and fix the top 1–2 every ~20 cancels. Don't knee-jerk on one reply.
- Ask the longest-tenured members why they stay. The recurring answer is your retention engine, and the rest is optional ("if one thing gets people to stay, why do five?").
- Price objections: tier on evidence. Ideal customers saying too expensive → add a lower or standard tier. Non-ideal customers saying it → ignore it; the price is filtering. Skool added a $9 tier alongside $99 (~11× cheaper) and kept premium features on the higher tier.
- Default cadence: one big thing per week (call plus recording) and a weekly best-of post.
- Add an annual plan at ~16–17% off ("buy 10 get 2"). Annual churns less (one decision instead of 12, more committed and qualified). Do it today.
- The owner shows up daily (set an alarm: clear notifications/DMs, moderate, read complaints every morning). Praise good behavior and delete bad content and bad people ("deleting cancer").
- Add tactics one at a time and keep them consistent: "100 small things consistently over a very long period."

## Playbook steps
1. Track joins vs cancels monthly and compare to benchmarks.
2. Build a month-1 activation plan with a fast win in 24 hours and the sticky thing up front.
3. Run cancel-reason tagging and best-member interviews.
4. Run the fight-for/let-go survey and cut everything else.
5. Create a busy path plus power-user depth, with a weekly call/recording and a best-of post.
6. Add an annual plan (~16–17% off) and an evidence-based lower tier if ideal customers cite price.
7. Show up daily, and recruit and spotlight 10 regulars who welcome new members.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Dismissing "it costs too much" as people not understanding the value (his earlier habit).
- Fixing churn by adding features or content, which gives members more reasons to feel they're underusing it.
- Overreacting to normal early-cohort churn.
- An absent owner. Members who feel the owner doesn't care leave.
