## Core thesis
"28 ways to stay poor," built by inversion thinking (from Charlie Munger's "how to live a miserable life" talk). Our brains find problems more easily than solutions, so he lists the ways to fail and then flips them. "If you don't make many mistakes, you succeed without trying." This is an older video: Acquisition.com is described as a portfolio "over $100M a year." It's mostly mindset rather than tactics.

## Frameworks & models
- **Inversion thinking**: ask "how would I destroy X?" (a marriage, a business, a body), list every way, then do the opposite.
- **The 28 ways to stay poor** (numbered as in the video; he counts 7–8 together): start tomorrow; read lots of books and do nothing; take advice from poor people on how to be rich; pick a spouse who makes you feel guilty for working; fail once, quit forever; think the world is fair; blame your circumstances; complain; expect the government (or others) to save you; value others' opinions over your own; avoid discomfort; tolerate mediocrity from yourself and others; make and break promises; wait for perfect conditions; prioritize looking rich over being rich; avoid working on what matters most; say you'll do something and don't; do what everyone else does; do your best instead of what it takes; talk more, do less; start something new every day; believe what others think of you over what you think; repeat the same mistake; be replaceable; find something that works and then stop; hire dumb people; assume you're always right; spend more than you make.
- **Change cycle**: uninformed optimism → informed pessimism → valley of despair, where serial starters jump to the next thing ("half-built bridges").
- **When-then fallacy / moving goalposts**: you're successful the moment you decide you are. Success is a process (living your values), not a number.
- **Replaceability** (credited to Naval): if a degree can qualify you for the job, you're replaceable. Learn things others can't do. Competing on doing it "for less" only works with an operational strategy like Walmart's.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Assume the world is unfair and act accordingly.
- Hold a standard above the one you demand of others: "most people don't need to be led, most people need a model."
- Do what is required, not "your best." If you can't, get better and do it "so long that it would be unreasonable for you to be bad."
- Start in the worst conditions. If you can succeed when things are bad, you'll stay successful when they get bad again.
- When something works, keep doing it "until you're bored out of your mind."
- Spend less than you make at any income. You can always downgrade your lifestyle.

## Playbook steps
1. Pick a goal area (business, marriage, body, money).
2. List every way to destroy it.
3. Flip each one into a rule for living (his flipped list is the closing "hard-hitting blip").
4. Prioritize the few needle-moving tasks, usually the ones you're procrastinating on.
5. To make money, do the simple known thing: reach out, promote, and when someone says no, "Cool. Talk to somebody else."

## Mistakes he warns against
- Reading 52 books a year without acting on them.
- Asking a spouse for permission instead of support. You'll resent them if you give up your dreams because of them.
- Lots of activity without progress, and "mental masturbation" busywork.
- Routines, affirmations, and posting goals in place of actually doing the work.
- Being able to make emergency money (payroll, taxes, a surprise bill) but not doing it for your own goals.
- Valuing being right over being rich, and refusing advice from more successful people.
