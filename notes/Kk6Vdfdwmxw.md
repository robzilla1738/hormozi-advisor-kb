## Core thesis
There are five levers that raise lead quality, and each one usually cuts volume. Alex's default is the opposite: cast the widest net to get flow, then "put the onus on the model rather than on the marketing." That means building offers, upsells, indoctrination and sales choreography so you can afford any traffic. Use the quality levers only when volume is overwhelming or operations can't keep up.

## Frameworks & models
- **5 lead-quality levers** (each one raises quality and lowers volume):
  1. **Targeting**: zip code, income, profession. Rich zip codes bring better leads, but they are more contested, more expensive and lower volume.
  2. **Number of steps**: more actions before the call means more qualified leads, because they're in more pain or more compelled.
  3. **Nature of the offer**: a free giveaway brings far more volume and weaker prospects. "Click to schedule a sales call" brings fewer, more qualified ones. Giveaways work if you have a good "mousetrap" (monetization) behind them.
  4. **Application**: paragraph answers are harder and filter more than checkboxes. Have them state their goal and problem and list their biggest unknowns, and set the precedent that the solution costs money. Use a minimum-budget question (e.g. if the product is $1,000: "willing to spend at least $500?").
  5. **Value-first content**: a 5-minute tactical video that gives real value produces leads who already trust your authority, compared with a compelling ad that teaches nothing.
- **Know your cash curve by cohort**: for every $1 in, how much is back by day 15, day 30 and day 60. His example: day 30 ≈ $3.50, day 60 ≈ $7. Without these numbers, marketing decisions are hard.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Start with the widest net and work backwards. "The worst thing in the world is not having any flow to begin with."
- Add qualifiers only when volume is so high and quality so low that you'll give up half or a third of throughput, either to get a higher ticket or because the team can't handle the flow.
- Example: in a national straight-to-call campaign the team can't keep up with, it's worth doubling lead cost to put up a fence.
- When leads seem weak, usually look first at the monetization system behind the lead, not the front end.

## Playbook steps
1. Run broad traffic to get flow. 2. Build a series of offers, upsells, indoctrination sequences and preframes. 3. Track cohort cash at day 15, 30 and 60 per $1 spent. 4. Only if overloaded, add levers 1 to 5 one at a time.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Applying every qualifier by default, which leaves money on the table because each one is a bottleneck on volume.
- Tweaking the front end when the real issue is the model.
- Running marketing without knowing your per-dollar return over time.

## Examples / case details
- He ran the same wide-net, back-end-monetization model for the brick-and-mortar gym, the supplement company and Gym Launch.
- Online fitness model: seven-figure gym owners are moving to it because they get the same average price per client with unlimited lead flow at about 1/5 the cost per lead. It took 3 to 4 iterations to tune.
