# The Business Model That's So Simple, Anyone Can Try It

## Core thesis
Alex demonstrates the "Core 4" ways of getting customers using a mock trash-pickup business, with skits followed by breakdowns: warm outreach, posting free content, cold outreach, and paid ads. He says there are 8 ways in total, and this video covers 4. Start by giving the service away free to people you know, then use those free customers for reviews, referrals and name-drops. Grow from there through content, then volume cold outreach, then ads.

## Frameworks & models
- **Core 4.** One-to-one vs. one-to-many, crossed with people who know you vs. strangers: warm outreach, free content, cold outreach, paid ads.
- **Warm outreach script.** Personalized acknowledgment or compliment (ideally tied to a character trait) → a question that steers the conversation → "how do you have time to do X?" (selling time back) → ask whether they know anyone, rather than asking for their business → reason why: "taking on 5 case studies / trials free for reviews" → both outcomes framed ("if it's good, leave a review; if it sucks, tell me") → three-way text intro on the spot.
- **Four ways a free customer makes you money:** they become a customer, leave a review, send a friend, or give feedback.
- **Frame for free work.** "You're helping me out." Don't act like they owe you. You have no track record, which is why it's free.
- **Limits of warm outreach.** Your time, and the number of people you know (about 1,000; at 100 a day that's 10 days).
- **Hook → Retain → Reward for content.** The first 3 seconds matter most. Hold attention with lists, steps or stories using open loops: name the step, explain why, repeat. The reward is a subversion or laugh worth sharing, or a CTA.
- **No-based questions.** "Would you be opposed to…" and "would it be a huge inconvenience…" get slightly more yeses than yes-based asks. He uses these to get spouses to take the trash out and to close at the door.
- **Content direction for a local business.** Either consumer tips or recruiting content aimed at future staff, or both.
- **"People need to be reminded more than taught."** Repeat themes across formats.
- **Engaged lead.** Anyone who likes or comments is a lead you can message ("I saw you engaged").
- **Views as room size.** 17 views is a full classroom.
- **Cold outreach door script.** Look the part (work clothes, trash over the shoulder) → name-drop the free customer next door ("I help the Johnsons") → ask "do you have someone who does X?" (both answers work; already paying means they're more likely to buy) → no-based close.
- **If they already have a provider,** compare offers: "do they do it for free this month?" Free month, then $29/month. Use humor and rapport.
- **Collecting payment at the door.** Take their ID, swap it for the card, and write the card number yourself.
- **Three problems in cold outreach, with fixes.** No contact info → build a list via software, brokers, or self-scraping Yelp/Google Maps/groups (best, because the list is "virgin"). What to say → script plus BFV (big fast value), meaning an offer superior to what others charge. Too few replies → volume through automation and collaboration.
- **Paid ads.** Call-out (visual, or auditory like "Las Vegas homeowners") → hook → agitate the pain with specific details → "3 things we do better" (opens a loop and organizes the benefits) → guarantee → CTA. Ads are like content except they always end with a CTA.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you're not making money, your time is worth about zero, so giving the service away free is fine.
- Leverage one free customer per neighborhood into many sales through name-dropping.
- Once you get a yes, stop talking and go.
- About 80% of people decide in the first 3 seconds, so test hooks the most. Write many hooks for one ad body by cutting the first ~5 seconds and swapping in new ones.
- Who appears in the ad, and the setting, attract the avatar more than anything else. Use 2 to 4 different people in ads to broaden reach.
- Mention several pain points, because the first one may not be the one they feel.
- In the example offer, missing one pickup costs the company 3 months free.

## Playbook steps
1. Text 5 to 10 contacts and offer the service free in exchange for reviews or feedback. Ask for three-way intros.
2. Post hook-retain-reward content. Message everyone who engages.
3. Build a list and knock on doors or reach out cold, name-dropping free customers, with a BFV offer and high volume.
4. Run paid ads: call-out, hook, pain, 3 benefits, guarantee, CTA. Test hooks heavily.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Resenting free work.
- Asking for business instead of asking whether they know anyone.
- Talking after the yes.
- Avoiding repetition in content.
- Being discouraged by low view counts.
- Fearing rejection: "no is the price of yes."
- Using only tired purchased lists.
