# If you're ambitious but inconsistent, please watch this

## Core thesis
Addressed to a young man who quit sales after making $80K because it "wasn't his passion." Passion is a bad filter for what to do. "Pursue your passion" is true only in the vague, never in the specific. Any business funnels you the hardest, worst problems related to the thing you love, so most of the time is spent on non-passion work. What carries you is proficiency, duty, and frustration tolerance: staying consistent after it gets *boring*. "Interests get you started. Proficiency keeps you going, but pain tolerance helps you finish."

## Frameworks & models
- **Passion only works unscaled**: the only way to do *only* your passion is to stay small, work when you feel like it, and be so good that you keep raising prices. Even then you still do sales, billing, taxes and sourcing.
- **Business = a funneling mechanism of the hardest problems** (his paraphrase of Elon Musk): the problems too hard or too painful for anyone else.
- **Addiction narrows the things that bring joy; fulfillment broadens them.**
- **"Passionate about being excellent"** (a friend who built a high-end cookie bakery): attach passion to your values and standards, not the product. He grew to love business by getting good at it.
- **Reinforcement ≠ reward**: we do what we're reinforced for. Example: taking shocks so your family doesn't. Pain can be reinforcing when tied to duty. Money-for-shocks needs more variety over time.
- **Duty and provider pride**: like ancient farmers, who weren't passionate about rice; they took pride in providing. Once others' families rely on you, "you can't take days off."
- **Frustration tolerance**: how many times you can be rejected or go unrewarded and try again. It is learnable, a skill. You "suck for a long time" until you suck so little you're good.
- **Name the voice**: replace "what would people say" with the actual 1–4 names, and ask if that person's opinion should rule your next 80 years.
- **Future-self shame test**: would the man you want to become be proud of quitting now? The shame of falling short gets him "back in the ring."
- **All failure besides death is psychological** (Jocko Willink [caption: "Jaco Willick"]). Phil Knight: you're out of business when there are padlocks on the door, not when the bank account runs out.
- **Nostalgia = memory minus pain.**

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Don't use "not my passion" as a reason to quit; check whether it's really an inability to tolerate hardness or boredom.
- Make money doing one thing and keep your passion separate if needed. He keeps fitness as a non-profit gym with no members.
- Put yourself in no-fail situations (his gym lease held all his net worth, so he couldn't quit).
- Make decisions that are harder short term and easier long term; sit in the discomfort of unsolved problems.
- Pick a path and commit; every path (sales, wholesaling, real estate, agency) can work and will suck longer than expected.

## Playbook steps
1. Pick a viable path, not a passion. 2. Commit; remove exits where sensible. 3. Do the boring reps repeatedly (scripts, ads, doors, emails). 4. Improve the reps so you hate them less; that is getting better. 5. Tie the pain to duty and your future self. 6. Ignore unspecified "other people"; be cringe early.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Quitting when it gets boring, not even hard; that is where most people fall off.
- Expecting a passion business to feel like "never working a day in your life."
- Letting the opinions of people who won't be at your funeral rule your life.
- Romanticizing the past ("good old days").
- Offering "follow your passion" advice because it's politically convenient.

Today: a gym with no members that he loves, not his income.
