## Core thesis
A retrospective on four businesses that each passed $10M: Gym Launch, Prestige Labs, ALAN, and Acquisition.com. The repeatable patterns are an exceptional product that drives word of mouth, client-financed acquisition, leverage through distribution (affiliates, agencies), aligned incentives, and hiring top-down once capitalized. The honest misses: over-hiring support, Prestige Labs splitting his focus, and using a dev shop instead of an in-house CTO.

## Frameworks & models
- **Client-financed acquisition**: if the first sale covers the cost to acquire and fulfill that customer plus the next one, capital is no longer a constraint ("put a dollar in and get two customers back"). He contrasts this with the loss-leader model and has used it in every business.
- **Community best-practice mining**: pick a member problem (churn, margin, leads, close rate), survey for the top performers, put ~20 of them on a call, and keep only the few things they all do. Roll it out only if it's "worth the cost of change."
- **The inch from good to great takes years**, and the word-of-mouth return compounds. Gym Launch's deliverable took 6 years to assemble.
- **Distribution leverage ladder** (ALAN): small business → agencies (each with ~50 SMBs) → webinars with big agency audiences ("one pitch... a thousand customers").
- **Aligned incentives**: ALAN was paid per show-up, and SMBs and agencies wanted show-ups too.
- **Debt types**: every business incurs financial, management, cultural or technical debt. Acquisition.com chose financial debt (hiring top-down early) so it wouldn't have to pay down the others.
- **Recruiting as core competency**: inbound volume, private-equity prestige over "Joe's dry cleaning," and HoldCo training/directors.
- **Purpose = learning**: co-owning many industries lets you cross-pollinate ideas.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Software requires an in-house CTO. Dev shops are "about as close to a complete scam as you can come."
- Run pricing surveys early. Repackaging how ALAN charged (usage, recurring, API) unlocked 4× pricing power.
- Appointment throughput: more open days, hours and slot increments drives large gains. Owners who offered one hour a week to new customers then blamed marketing. Fixing scheduling usually adds 20–40% to ad throughput.
- Don't promote an inexperienced frontline rep straight to director.
- Acquisition.com looks at businesses doing $3M+, mostly $5M+; the average portfolio company was ~$17M.

## Playbook steps
1. Start with manual hustle for the first customers. 2. Build an exceptional product. 3. Structure front-end cash to fund the next acquisition. 4. Launch adjacent products to your existing distribution base in stages (10 → 20–30 → 20–30 → everyone). 5. Sell through aggregators like agencies and affiliates. 6. Align pricing to the outcome customers want. 7. Hire senior people top-down once capitalized.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Over-hiring: an inexperienced support director hired 35 reps when 5 were needed. Laying off 30 hurt their Glassdoor for 2–3 years.
- Starting a second company that stalls the first. Gym Launch stopped growing the year Prestige launched: "I probably shouldn't have done it."
- Outsourced software development.
- Limited appointment availability.
- "Small business owners stay small. They make small business owner decisions."

## Examples / case details
- **Gym Launch**: a licensing model with ~100% margins. The average gym added ~$100K/yr profit. Peak value was ~$150M; after COVID (a third of clients closed) it sold for $46.2M to American Pacific Group. First customers: a VA scraped CrossFit emails for a Facebook lookalike audience; the webinar got 80 opt-ins and zero buyers. He then friended and messaged them manually and charged $500 for in-person turnarounds. Licensing ramp: ~$120K → ~$300K → high $400Ks → $780K → $1M/month. The average gym took home ~$30K cash in its first 30 days vs ~$36K/yr typical profit, giving ~100:1 return on marketing for 18 months. Facebook ad arbitrage started around 2013. Copycats without depth went out of business. Caption says the best and average gym both did ~$600K/yr (likely garbled).
- **Prestige Labs**: supplements sold only through gym owners. It did $1.7M in its first full month. A client spent $700 on a list he gave her vs his $400 price, which convinced him. Pricing was 30% higher on the main site, there was a second subscription per customer, and orders shipped direct with no added fulfillment. Startup cost ~$4M (~$1M on a custom gym POS/kiosk, ~$3M product). Problems: supply chain, predatory law firms, non-sticky consumers.
- **ALAN**: AI/ML appointment setting, paid on show-ups. It got just under 20% of leads in the door vs 9% for manual work, cheaper than a $2K+/month front desk. It reached $1.7M/month within 6 months of two agency webinars. He sold 75% to a strategic buyer in an all-stock deal after $12M TTM.
- **Acquisition.com**: portfolio does $200M+/yr. It's the first business built on personal brand and inbound content, slower with roughly one deal a month. Top-down hires: directors of sales, marketing, people, IT and portfolio ops.
