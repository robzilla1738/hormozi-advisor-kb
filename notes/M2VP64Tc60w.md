## Core thesis
There are only five ways to grow how many people you can sell to: go up market, down market, adjacent, broader, or narrower. A sixth option is to keep getting better at what you already do. Start niche, then expand in sequence. At a meetup of $30M–$250M businesses, he saw that nobody had magic he lacked. They had stayed in one business longer and served a bigger TAM.

## Frameworks & models
- **Triangle of the market: up / down / adjacent / broader / narrower.**
- **Up market** (salons → multi-location chains or franchises; Salesforce SMB → Enterprise). Pros: bigger deals, less churn, more sophisticated buyers, no non-payment. Cons: harder sales, sometimes 6–18 month enterprise cycles. "If you increase the quality of your customer you increase the quality of your company."
- **Down market** (salons → stylists; gyms → trainers; small business → aspiring entrepreneurs). Pros: an endless supply of emotional buyers and many chances to iterate. Cons: recurring revenue is hard, payment problems, unrealistic expectations, and churn from buyers going out of business. It wins on marketing and sales more than on product.
- **Adjacent market**: the same promise delivered a different way (hair salons → nail salons; chiropractors → physical therapists; gyms → dance studios). Same value with only "lipstick" changes to the product, rolled out vertical by vertical. Bring in an insider from that market who has seen your success and pay them. One adjacent market can double or triple your market size. It is slower and needs specialized knowledge.
- **Broader**: generalize the promise across all verticals (salons → beauty; gyms → fitness). It can 10× TAM overnight, but the offer becomes templated with less nuance, and you compete against every entrenched vertical specialist.
- **Narrower (more specific avatar)**: add qualifications without changing the avatar, e.g. "small businesses with $30K/mo revenue and 2+ employees." He learned this from Vista Private Equity's former head of packaging and pricing: analyze the top 20% of customers, then get more like them. He says that roughly 5×'d companies without much extra infrastructure.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you're new, niche down. Narrow is easier to attract and to deliver to.
- If you are better at marketing and sales than at delivery, down market may fit at first.
- Go broad only once you have champions and case studies in several verticals and know you can still deliver significant value to each. "Oftentimes people go too broad too soon." "If you want to go fast go broad but if you want to be good you're staying narrow for a little."
- Use 2–5 common traits for the narrow avatar, never more than 5.
- Below about $10M/yr, don't worry about having fewer people to sell to. Focus ruthlessly on the avatar you serve best and say no to everyone else.
- Each business watching should pick one move. An enormous business eventually does all five.

## Playbook steps
Going narrower: 1. Survey every customer about their stats and what they like most. 2. Find the best customers: those who spent the most and stayed longest. 3. Run a common-factor analysis to find 2–5 shared traits. 4. Rewrite all marketing language for that avatar. 5. Map the content and channel your best customers came through, then reverse-engineer that buying process and push every prospect through it on purpose.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Going broad too soon, before you're good enough.
- Changing direction instead of making steady, consistent improvements.
- Expecting product quality to fix churn in down-market segments. About 30% go out of business each year because of who they are.

## Examples / case details
- A friend's CRM for tiny businesses has 4% monthly churn, and 100% of it comes from customers going out of business or starting something new.
- Neil Patel and GaryVee run normal SEO and agency businesses but sell to the Fortune 500.
- Tesla started expensive for the rich and stepped down market twice. Facebook started in colleges, then went broader. Salesforce started with SMBs, then went up market.
- Portfolio companies: "we can three or four x the profit of the business just by going narrow."
