## Core thesis
If you aren't making the money you want, the product isn't good enough. Anyone who has customers is already being discovered, and those customers aren't referring others. The ultra-wealthy focus on product and compounding (word of mouth), while small operators focus on promotion. "Make sales to get customers," not "get customers to make sales" (Dan Kennedy). Promotion should only get the product right, then amplify it.

## Frameworks & models
- **Three product questions**:
  1. **10× price (value)**: if it cost 10× (or $100K/$1M), what would the product and experience need to be?
  2. **1/10 price but better (profit)**: what one-time, scalable assets would let you deliver more for far less, profitably?
  3. **One last customer (experience)** — his favorite: if you could sell only one more customer and all future growth had to come from them unprompted, what experience would make them a raving fan?
- **Six ways to get customers**: paid media, earned media (following), contact list, manual outbound, affiliates, word of mouth. The first five scale linearly; **word of mouth is the only one that compounds**.
- **Compounding test** (from a billionaire friend): "If it's not going to grow on its own over time it's not worth doing." One-time effort that compounds beats effort you must repeat daily.
- **Time as asset vs liability.**

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If customers aren't referring, fix the product before scaling promotion.
- Once the product is right, "juice it" with promotion and then let it compound.
- A product good enough for referrals reduces churn, negative reviews and bad-mouthing.

## Playbook steps
1. Ask why current customers aren't referring. 2. Run the three questions (value, profit, experience). 3. Build one-time assets that raise value at scale. 4. Design a remarkable experience. 5. Then layer promotion.

## Mistakes he warns against
- "If more people just found out about my product, all my problems would be solved."
- Short-sighted selling to get to the money fast.
- Skipping the up-front investment and patience needed to make the product great.
