# The New Way of Making Content In The Age of AI

## Core thesis
AI won't disrupt every creator equally. Creators sit on a risk continuum based on what the consumer risks by acting on the content: entertainers, then B2C educators, then B2-prosumer (money), then B2B. AI disrupts the low-risk end first. To compete, build your brand on proof, both third-party accomplishments and real-time demonstration, and engineer demonstration into your existing business motion so you "capture, not create" content.

## Frameworks & models
- **Creator risk continuum**: *Entertainers* (memes, stand-up; the goal is to be consumed and value is self-contained) → *B2C educators* (hair, makeup, diet; education aims to change behavior, but stakes are low, e.g. Huda Beauty demoing live) → *B2-prosumer* (saving, investing, disputes; needs more credibility: an attorney (Erika Kullberg, caption "Erica taught me"), Vivian Tu from finance, Dave Ramsey) → *B2B* (business advice; needs heavy proof). Higher stakes mean people want more demonstrable proof. AI disrupts low-risk first.
- **Proof = a signal that lowers personal risk**: with identical content, the person who has built 10 sales teams beats the one who prompted GPT, "and not just by a little bit." Elon is the top business influencer because he's the richest.
- **Two kinds of proof**: third-party (exits, revenue, headcount, number of companies) and demonstration (watching someone use their expertise live), which is hard to fake.
- **Self-licking ice cream cone**: bake proof into delivery so that sales generate marketing, which generates customers. Example: sweepstakes tickets inside hair extensions, with winners installed in-salon on camera.
- **Capture, don't create**: record what you already do (Q&As at events, calls with community members). Transcribe your virtual meetings and have AI pull the interesting moments, so you tell real stories instead of made-up ones.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- The higher-risk the advice you give, the more proof you need, and the safer you are from AI in the short to medium term.
- If you have few customers, do free audits or work for people publicly and document it ("Who says no to that?").
- Consumer products: literally use the product on camera. B2B agencies: bring clients in and walk through campaigns.
- Do content formats you enjoy (for him, Q&As) so volume is sustainable at 20–50 pieces a day.

## Playbook steps
1. Place your content on the risk continuum. 2. Lead with credible third-party proof. 3. Add real-time demonstration: live calls, audits, product use. 4. Engineer proof loops into delivery (sweepstakes, in-person installs, client walkthroughs). 5. Record and transcribe meetings and mine them with AI. 6. Document while delivering, at no extra time.

## Mistakes he warns against
- GPT-generated advice with no real-world proof.
- Saying "this doesn't work for my business": "You're not going to make it, dude."
- Sitting in front of the camera to manufacture every piece of content.
