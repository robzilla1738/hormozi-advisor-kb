## Core thesis
A remote Q&A with a UT Austin entrepreneurship class, whose teams turn $100 into a validated business ("validation = a stranger handing you money"). Pick a boring existing business you have some exposure to and do it faster and easier. Don't take on financial debt while you're still paying "ignorance debt." Partner only for money, skill or time you lack. Promote through the core four / lead-getters. Give the first 5–20 away free for reviews and referrals, ask non-buyers why, fix it, and repeat for years. Success is consistency, not cleverness. Speakers: Alex, plus the instructors and students asking questions.

## Frameworks & models
- **Learning = same circumstance, different behavior; intelligence = rate of learning.** Knowledge without behavior change isn't learning.
- **Leverage** = output relative to input. Big decisions come down to **risk-adjusted return**, adjusted for how many "coins" you have. A 1-in-10 shot at 100× is a good bet only if you get many tries. "The more I need to win, the higher the probability bet I'm going to make." Example: he'd take 100% of $1M over a 1/100 shot at $500M, even though the second has 5× the expected value.
- **Stair-stepping**: build skills in 3–5-year seasons that each stack on the last (fitness → gym → multi-location → turnarounds → thousands of locations).
- **Fallacy of the perfect pick**: your first business won't be your last. Be "directionally correct," then iterate.
- **Types of debt**: ignorance, financial, management, technical. "All businesses start in debt. It's just not always financial." Don't carry financial and ignorance debt at once, because they compound.
- **8 ways to promote**. Yourself: warm one-to-one, content (one-to-many), cold one-to-one, paid ads. Lead-getters: customers (referrals), employees (if you make 10:1 on your time and pay someone 3 of the 10, you get 3:1 on their time), agencies (trade money for ignorance debt and learn the platform), affiliates.
- **Partner test**: the partner must bring money, skill or time you don't have. Splitting 50/50 halves everything, so "be darn sure this person is going to double the value of the business." Otherwise, hire.
- **Hidden costs** non-buyers reveal: time, effort (things they must start doing) and sacrifice (things they must give up), on top of price.
- **Supply/demand leverage**: iterate until demand exceeds capacity, then name your price. This is why the influencer model works (Kim K needs only 0.1% of 300M followers).
- **Rule of 100**: 100 primary actions every day, first thing (reach-outs, minutes of content, or $100 on ads, scaled to budget), for 100 days, and "you will have a business."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Don't use debt on a first business. Quoting Buffett: "if you're smart you don't need it, if you're dumb you shouldn't use it."
- Start with a service business: no capital, just time and an internet connection.
- Start with warm outreach, not paid ads, so you learn customers' language for later ads.
- About 1% is a benchmark for any cold activity (100 reach-outs → conversations → follow-ups → a sale). A beginner may take 3× as many (300).
- Don't chase passion. You're passionate about what you're good at; "use what you got."
- Respect competitors: the guy with 20 locations isn't an idiot. Model first, then iterate.
- "Don't model where I'm at now, model how I got here."
- On affiliate deals: price is what it's worth to the buyer. Keep ownership of the audience asset; don't hand over the list.
- When choosing alternative education: lots of positive reviews, a long track record, and an expectation of directional progress, not instant income.

## Playbook steps
1. LLC, bank account, payment processing (about a week). 2. Pick the easiest of the 8 channels; ask warm contacts "do you know anybody who…" rather than pitching them. 3. Do the first 5–20 for free, do great work, ask for a testimonial and referral. 4. Ask strangers to pay, using those reviews. 5. When people say no, ask why, fix it, and ask "if I fixed that, would you buy?" 6. Repeat until demand exceeds capacity, then raise price. 7. Rule of 100 for 100 days. "Then you add 30 years."

## Mistakes he warns against
- Partnering with buddies who have the same skills. He calls it the biggest early mistake; he had 8 failed partnerships.
- Expecting money in 6 months. It took him 5 years at 80 hrs/week.
- Grand-slam swings followed by starting over after each strikeout.
- Blaming trauma or parents, which "gives them the power."
- Deciding a market is capped too early. He has 38 photography studio locations: "just open another one."
- Avoiding feared work. He paid a web developer for 3–4 years, then rebuilt the site himself in 4 hours: "it took me four years to decide to take the first four hours."

## Examples / case details
- **Gym Launch bet**: he spent ~$100K on credit with ~$1K in the bank. Context: he was a six-time gym owner with 30+ turnarounds, so the real risk was getting a processor in time. It took a month to set up two high-risk processors at an 8% fee plus a 10% reserve, so 18% of top line was withheld, against Stripe's ~2.99%.
- **Massage affiliate idea**: get local businesses to put a free massage in their new-hire packets, then upsell.
- **Student party-promotion business** (a 1,500-person list) was offered ~$200–300K by marketers for exclusive promotion rights. His view: legit, it's an affiliate deal; protect the list.
- **Why he sells no courses**: he monetizes influence through deal flow, investing in companies with $1–10M profit.
- Early on he slept at his business for 9 months.
- The fear exercise: name the 1–3 people whose opinion you actually fear ("f— Sarah").
