## Core thesis
A live teardown of a small business: Ashley Capps runs AC Styles, a luxury personal styling service doing $309K revenue and $130K profit (42% net margin) after 4 years and 1,000+ clients. Her core economics are already healthy: LTV $10K, blended CAC $600 (~16:1), a 100% show rate, a 50% close rate, and only 1 of 40 recurring clients churned in a year. Volume is the only problem ("the N is low"). Alex does not overhaul the business. He picks the cheapest, highest-probability levers in order: fix the leaky ad funnel with a lead magnet and qualification, add attribution tracking, spend more on a channel that already returns many times its cost, and 10× the affiliate outreach that already produces a third of revenue. He leaves continuity alone. The goal is reframed from $5M to "triple to $1M this year."

## Frameworks & models
- **Recurring vs reoccurring revenue**: recurring = membership or subscription; reoccurring = repeat buying without a subscription (Coca-Cola). The quality of a product is shown by the % of customers who buy again. "Acquire a customer once and have them pay you for the rest of their lives."
- **Channel % vs time %**: find out what share of customers each source produces, compare it with where your time goes, double down on the big source, then ask if it's tapped out ("most times you can do more").
- **Fix core economics first, then shift to the front end**: once price, gross margin, stick rate and referrals are good, the job becomes getting far more leads (advertising).
- **Chinese rule of 3 and 10**: systems break when they triple, so he sets goals in triples.
- **Lead magnet design**: risk-free, easy and fast. Do the work up front so the prospect doesn't have to ("pre-digest the food," "baby bird"). Between a more valuable but slower lead magnet and a less valuable but immediate one, prospects prefer the immediate one. Peel off the first step of your paid offer, template it to cut your time, and give it only to qualified people.
- **Honey trap**: a very valuable, costly lead magnet, offered only to leads who meet stated qualifications.
- **BANT qualification**: Budget, Authority, Need, Timing. Ask the fewest questions needed to decide whether to take the call. Use the answers to score and prioritize leads.
- **Good friction vs bad friction**: good friction filters out bad leads only; bad friction (slow page loads, a photo-upload step) drives out everyone. Move high-friction asks (photos) into follow-up.
- **Opt-in → qualified/unqualified split**: qualified leads go to the scheduler on the thank-you page. Unqualified leads get an automated free item (e.g. a generic palette for their hair and skin type) so they don't fill the calendar.
- **Pre-call videos**: a VSL before booking plus a second video between booking and the call. Move the repeated pitch out of the call so the call focuses on the person and their decision. This also keeps sales staff from wearing out.
- **Expand the gap on the call**: use a pre-built template (e.g. 1 of 10 color palettes chosen from the intake answers) to show "you're here, you want to be here, you're missing this," then pitch.
- **Affiliates ≠ referrals**: affiliates are independent businesses with their own audiences and advertising that send leads in exchange for money, free stuff or both. Each one becomes a recurring "node" of business. Keep them active like customers (e.g. events).
- **LTV:CAC as a money machine**: if $1 in returns many dollars within ~30 days, "why would you have any kind of limit on that budget?"
- **Price discovery**: "keep edging up the price until people stop saying yes," then back down a little.
- **Price anchor**: a higher-priced option raises uptake of the other option even if only ~10% pick it.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- A Google high-intent search landing page with a good lead magnet should opt in ~30% of visitors. The general range for qualified traffic is 20–50%, sometimes 60%. Her 1,800 clicks gave 8 sales, so conversion is the leak; he estimates ~500 leads from the same spend if it's fixed.
- Cold outbound normally gets 0.5–1%. She got 6 affiliates from ~40 emails (15%), 10–15× normal efficiency.
- Don't show pricing on the page; qualification questions plus a service description "give us 80%." Don't squeeze too hard on income: take a call with high need, immediate timing and a medium budget; skip a zero budget.
- More qualified calls means more nos but more money: closing 30% of 10× the calls still gives ~3× the deals.
- Don't add a new channel (Facebook) while a working, very profitable channel can still take more spend.
- Attribution tracking comes before more ad spend: "otherwise you're just spending money in the dark." "You're getting a return by accident rather than on purpose."
- Raise ad spend only within sales capacity. She can take 2–3× her ~12 calls, so go from ~$300 to $600–900/mo and accept slightly higher lead costs.
- When resources are small, sequence by likelihood of working and lowest effort. Quick wins (an afternoon PDF, a headline change, a budget click) come before slower efforts (manual affiliate outreach). Defer other optimizations until volume brings data and cash.
- Two avatars: if you love one and hate another (e.g. 30/70), cut the hated one. If you like both, keep both until more data exists (revisit at $3–5M: retention, value, referrals).
- Hold off on new business lines (her education/corporate idea) because the core can likely reach $2–3M first.
- Affiliates: 1–3 events a year is "more than enough" to keep them active. Pitch collaboration, not competition, by putting them in one room. Re-send to non-responders.

## Playbook steps
1. Fix the funnel: VSL, a lead magnet (a personalized color/tone analysis or "how to best use your existing closet," offered as a complimentary style assessment for qualified people), BANT qualification on the booker, and skin/hair/eye personalization questions.
1.5. Install attribution tracking before spending more.
2. Raise Google ad spend from ~$300 to $600–900/mo.
3. Affiliates: send 20 personalized emails/day (~400 total, about a month) to four segments (high-end personal trainers, executive coaches, matchmakers/dating coaches, divorce attorneys). Source them from Google, Instagram and social first, then list brokers (national list filtered to the city). Hold one event per quarter to keep them active.
4. Leave continuity as is: $8,500 onboarding over 60 days, then $2K/mo or $4,500/quarter.
- Projection: funnel 2–5× (he uses 2×) × ad spend 2× × affiliates 3× (10× of one third) ≈ 12×, or $300K → $3.6M.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Pricing from your own wallet instead of the client's or the value delivered, which is common with luxury services. Selling your own time leads to over-restricting calls.
- Having no lead magnet, or a bad one that makes the prospect do work (measurements, building a mood board).
- Not knowing the % of customers from each source, and having no data at all ("the data can often tell you where your opportunities are").
- Being afraid to spend on marketing that has a proven return.
- Being afraid to go after high-income customers or watering the offer down for the low end.
- Letting affiliates go inactive.
- Making too many changes at once for a resource-limited small business.

## Examples / case details
- Offer: style assessment (colors for complexion, silhouettes for figure, lifestyle fit, mood board), closet teardown at the home, closet inventory for remote "pull this" packing lists, 5–10 new outfits, then a monthly or quarterly plan with lookbooks and on-demand texting. ~10% of revenue comes from ~10% vendor commission on clothes. A former one-time $3,500 tier was dropped because the luxury tier makes more.
- Clients: 50/50 men and women in their 40s to mid-60s. Favorite avatars are socialite moms with kids' events and male CEOs. Husband-to-wife cross-sells happen, usually started by the wife. Minimum ~$1M/yr income; ~$25K all in plus clothing budget. Triggers: having a baby or a body change, divorce, starting to date, an image that doesn't match a C-suite/VP status.
- Funnel numbers: ~12 leads, 8 appointments, 4 sales per month (as stated). Lead sources split ~⅓ affiliates, ~⅓ paid (Google + Thumbtack), ~⅓ referrals.
- Google: $3,400 for 2024, CPC $1.89, 1,800 clicks, 8 sales. It sent traffic to an Airtable application, then a brochure, then a call, with no incentive.
- Thumbtack: $6K spend, 58 leads, 8 sales, CAC $750. Caption says "$38 a lead," but $6,000 / 58 ≈ $103 [inference: figure mismatch in source]. 90–95% of leads lack purchasing power; kept partly for SEO ranking.
- Affiliates: 4 matchmakers + 2 private social clubs found by Googling, ~40 manual emails offering a complimentary style assessment to their new clients. ~⅓ of revenue (~$100K/yr), so ~$2,500 per email sent.
- Recurring base: ~38 grandfathered clients mostly at $1,500/quarter (~$500/mo); new pricing began in September with ~2 clients on it. Churn 1 of 40 in a year ≈ 2.5%.
