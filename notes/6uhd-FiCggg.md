# Why "Following Your Dreams" is Keeping You Poor
id: 6uhd-FiCggg / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6uhd-FiCggg / duration: 1488s (~25 min) / tags: 01-what-business-to-start, 12-mindset-leverage, 13-quit-or-pivot
Signal: medium

## Core thesis
"Follow your passion" gets cause and effect backwards: passion comes *after* competence, which comes from deliberate practice that isn't enjoyable. Pick what the world will pay for that you're good at (or can get good at), get a feedback loop going, take money seriously, and connect the work to serving others. "Passion happens as a consequence, not a requirement." This is a career and choice-of-path video more than tactical business advice.

## Frameworks & models
- **Chance encounter → deliberate practice → competence and recognition → identity → passion.** He says people who "always had a passion" just found their thing earlier.
- **Skills are easier to compare than preferences**: "better at English or math?" is decidable; "pizza or tacos?" isn't. Decide on skill.
- **Circle of competence** (Buffett): focus on what you understand best, not what excites you.
- **Liking the craft ≈ 5% of the business**: passionate gym owners and cooks who open restaurants go broke because the business skills are the other 95%.
- **Rule of 100** (from *$100M Leads*, outbound step 4: 100 reach-outs a day), applied to skill-building through deliberate reps.
- **Supply and demand of passions**: popular passions (acting, singing, painting) mean oversupply and heavy competition.
- **Everything is overhead** (Bezos): if you love 30% of your day, you're winning. The other 70% pays for it.
- **Job / career / calling**: the three stonecutters do the same work with different meaning. The Japanese floor sweeper ("as if paid $1M a day"). The waiter who got a "do better" tip.
- **Martyrdom effect**: people tolerate far more pain on behalf of others (his electric-shock example, cited without a source; he says "you can choose to believe it or not"). Follow purpose, not passion.
- **Spending money is a skill** (his own theory): happiness can keep rising with money if you learn to buy intangible returns.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Choosing between paths: pick the one with higher earning potential. "If you're about even on both, follow the money."
- "Commitment is the elimination of alternatives." Burn the boats on a skill path.
- Keep passions separate from income if you need to: "what stops you from just painting when you want to paint and then making money when you want to make money."
- Good work also needs decent pay, good coworkers, recognition, security and purpose. Weigh the environment, not just the activity.

## Playbook steps (his "instead of follow your passion")
1. Start with curiosity, even mild interest (cites Duckworth).
2. Embrace learning: set skill-specific goals and accept that mastery comes before passion.
3. Value existing abilities (circle of competence) and build from them.
4. Commit to deliberate practice and burn the boats.
5. Set realistic expectations: all work is partly tedious, so aim for overall satisfaction.
6. Take money seriously; tiebreaker: follow the money.
7. Change your environment: go where people are passionate about it to get feedback loops.
8. Let passions evolve; don't make it a forever decision.
9. Connect the work to serving others.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Starting a business around a hobby while ignoring business skills (the broke gym owner).
- Waiting for motivation or passion to strike.
- Expecting work to always be fun.
- Over-identifying with one passion that the world may move past (MySpace pages).
- Self-focused motivation.

## Examples / case details
- Mission of Gym Launch at the start: "Alex not being broke," after losing everything for the second time. It later became "take an industry from its knees to its feet."
- Rehearsed his book-launch presentation 90 times: 90 minutes × 3 times a day ≈ 4.5 hours a day, reviewing the recording and tweaking each time.
- Ping pong: likes it, but won't do 500 forehands and 500 backhands, so passion ≠ willingness to practice.
- His dad: "if I did what I was passionate about, I'd be a bartender at a ski slope." A mentor: "Never try to make money out of something you love."
- Gates explored law, math and programming. Phelps swam about 8 hours a day.
- His mission: make real business education accessible to everyone.
