## Core thesis
"You can beat 99% of people simply by continuing to work without needing your immediate reward." Work ethic × time is "the most dangerous money-making combo." Entrepreneurship feels like uncertainty, and that feeling doesn't mean you're doing it wrong. The rush to get somewhere fast makes people hop between things, which guarantees failure. Build skills ("income insurance"), long foundations and a 7-year view of work/life balance. Treat hate as data. Weigh advice by whether the person has taken others where you want to go. Start out by giving value away for free, then raise the price step by step.

## Frameworks & models
- **Count in hundreds**: hundreds of minutes, hours and days toward one goal. It forces far more volume.
- **Pottery parable**: the group told to make the most pots also made better pots. He personally prefers investing more in the first draft.
- **Runway metaphor**: "the longer the runway, the bigger the plane." Delaying reward raises the outcome.
- **Value equation applied to entrepreneurship**: people want it guaranteed, easy and fast, and entrepreneurship is the opposite of all three. That's why "fast, easy, guaranteed" pitches get so many ad dollars.
- **Foundations**: brand (reputation built over years, the "bamboo roots" effect, e.g. 5 years of content then a product that explodes) or product (code, regulation, manufacturing and prototypes). Services need neither, which is why he likes them for starting out.
- **Sharpen the axe**: one person spends a year turning a dull axe into a "lightsaber," catches up in year 2, and pulls far ahead from years 2 to 10.
- **Three slot machines**: same cost, same effort and stress (a local restaurant vs a billion-dollar app), with payouts of $100K, $10M or $1B. Everyone lines up for the $100K. Payout timing is another dimension.
- **The fastest way to $1M/yr isn't the fastest way to $10M**, and the fastest way to $10M isn't the fastest way to $100M.
- **Skills are income insurance.** After losing everything, he made $100K the next month on a maxed-out credit card. "The gyms weren't what I built; my skills were."
- **Work/life balance on a 7-year horizon**: seasons of work. "Work that you don't need a weekend from." Never have something you want to retire from.
- **Weekend math**: 104 days/yr. If weekends set you back and then take 2 weekdays to recover, you only net about 3 days/week. Someone who keeps going on weekends moves "like seven times as fast." [inference: rhetorical, not a computed figure]
- **Winners focus on winning, losers focus on winners.** Winners focus on customers, while competitors copy the winner and lose sight of their own customers. "If anyone ever copies you, you've already won."
- **Pie is bigger than you think**: Gym Launch had 45 gyms in Atlanta, out of about 1,500 gyms there.
- **Envy vs jealousy**: envy is wanting what someone else has (from people behind you). Jealousy is fearing you'll lose what you have (from people ahead of you). Treat hate as feedback and don't add a second layer of meaning to it.
- **Sadness vs anxiety**: sadness is a lack of options, so solve it with knowledge. Anxiety is many options with no priorities, so solve it with a decision. Disappointment is an unmet expectation: change the expectation or change reality. Stoic tool: imagine it happening 1,000 times.
- **Being-goals**: "what would the person I want to be do here?" This is 100% in your control. He also talks to his future self for grace and advice.
- **Friendship tests**: do they genuinely root for you when you win, and do they make you better?
- **7 levels of advice credibility** (low → high): 7 social-media strangers; 6 people who know you but don't have what you want; 5 someone who knows someone who got there; 4 someone who's been there; 3 someone who took someone else there; 2 someone who took someone like you exactly where you want to go; 1 someone who has been there and has done that for people like you multiple times. Example: Michael Jordan's great playing record vs poor team-owner record.
- **Give-give-give pricing ladder**: start free. When demand overwhelms your time, go to 20% of your target price, then 40%, 60%, 100%, 500% while demand holds.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Work only counts as a work day if it produced a clear output.
- If they don't have what you want, don't listen to what they say.
- Hate from people behind you is envy, so disregard it. Hate from people ahead of you is probably decent data. If many people sue at once, maybe there's something real.
- Never let a competitor dictate your behavior. His biggest mistake, about $25M actually lost, came from reacting to a competitor.
- Spec work to someone ahead of you: do it for about 100 people, not 1. Give it without asking for anything in return. Usually nothing happens, sometimes it leads to another opportunity, and rarely to the exact gig. "Most of the people on my team came from doing that."

## Playbook steps
1. Pick a long-horizon game and count effort in hundreds.
2. Build skills and foundations (brand/product) rather than chasing quick payouts.
3. Don't go backwards on weekends; work your leads on weekends.
4. Starting out, give valuable work to about 100 people for free, then step prices up as demand exceeds your time.
5. Filter advice through the 7 levels.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Rushing and hopping between opportunities.
- Judging yourself on other people's time horizons, like an employee's paycheck timeline ("have you made money yet?" two weeks in).
- Lining up for the $100K slot machine.
- Quitting a job so you won't have to work weekends.
- Offering "value in exchange for a testimonial" from a 100-follower account. It undervalues the other person's time and is "delusion."
- Fearing a hypothetical punishment for an action you haven't taken. He nearly didn't sell Gym Launch for $46M because of one person's imagined opinion.
