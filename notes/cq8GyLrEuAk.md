## Core thesis
A personal-lessons list (he says 13 in the intro, the title says 14, and 14 are delivered). The business-relevant threads: learn by listening and let others edify you; give customers simple words to describe you; go deep on a few great books; win through focus and elimination; goodwill and audience compound faster than money; do ordinary things for extraordinary lengths of time; if it's worth doing, it's worth doing well; negotiate everything except your values (positive-sum, long-term); gain status by giving to the group; failure → learning → success → complacency is a cycle; pay any amount to remove your "ignorance tax."

## Frameworks & models
- **Assume you're the dumbest in the room**: talking means you can't learn. The best positioning is someone else saying "do you know who that guy is?" Let others edify you.
- **Control what they say**: give people simple language to describe your business. Example: acquisition.com's mission went from "document and share the best practices of building world-class companies" to "make real business education available to everyone."
- **One great book five times > five mediocre books**: skim, discard, and reread the gems until you can teach them or build a presentation and framework from them. If your behavior doesn't change, you learned nothing. He prefers older books (written to transmit knowledge, not to sell).
- **Champions have less, not more**: they eliminate things, are more ruthless with their time, and say no.
- **Goodwill compounds faster than money**: an audience can 10× in 12 weeks; money rarely can. It's "tax-free" until you choose a conversion event.
- **Extraordinary = commitment to an ordinary action over time**: stay the course as long as your original premises remain true.
- **Worth doing → worth doing well**: first filter whether something is worth doing at all, then accept that great takes 5–10× the work of good. He spends about a year on a book because it sells for decades.
- **Four-point negotiation**: your need today, your need in 10 years, their need today, their need in 10 years. Find the closest point and explain your reasoning to them. Negotiation is not zero-sum.
- **Humility** (Clayton Christensen): "not decreasing your regard for self but increasing your regard for others." Status comes from giving more to the group than you take.
- **Cycle**: failure → learning → success → complacency → failure. Either the business is growing, or it's teaching you a lesson; "beat the boss." Regression to the mean.
- **Ignorance tax / ignorance debt**: making $50K means "not knowing how to make $1M" costs you $950K a year.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Only weigh opinions from people with a vested interest in your success *and* the context to advise you.
- Vendors: don't squeeze every last penny. Giving the extra 10 and keeping goodwill beats disloyalty over the long run.
- Learn from anyone making more money than you; take your ego hat off.
- Do fewer things and do them well.

## Playbook steps
[inference] Listen first in new rooms → sharpen a one-line description of the business → pick a few deep books and teach them → prune commitments → build audience/goodwill → negotiate with the four-point map.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Bragging to people who turn out to be ahead of you.
- Trying to "PR" your way out of a bad reputation instead of changing behavior ("why don't you stop being a piece of [ __ ]," from his dad).
- Saying yes to everything and spreading thin.
- Ruthless one-sided deals that compromise your values.
- Chasing quick bucks and shortcuts.

## Examples / case details
- Self-respect came first, then others' respect. Status through service made him fraternity president.

- Mantra "f*** happiness": accepting where he is removed the "not enough" deficit and freed his energy for action.
