## Core thesis
Scale or Fail episode with Tina Sue (Hi Mama, holistic program for parents of kids with autism/ADHD/speech delay: $770K revenue, $555K profit, 72% margin). The founder is the bottleneck because she equates selling with being bad. Hormozi fixes it "back to front": offer → conversion → traffic. Structure the pitch, make renewal the default, price one-on-one time so people self-serve, turn delivery Q&A into content, and route everything to one conversion point. "People only feel sold to if it's bad."

## Frameworks & models
- **Back-to-front fix order**: offer → conversion process → traffic.
- **Structured webinar (~45 min instead of a 3–4 hr marathon)**: big promise → who am I (origin story) → old way/new way (I did X, it didn't work, discovered the secret, got results internally and externally, it worked for avatars X/Y/Z) → top 3 concerns, each as *what they think → why it's wrong → what's right → proof* (stats, stories, as many forms as possible) → offer → FAQ. Use slides and visuals for people half-listening.
- **FAQ by frequency**: bucket every DM question and answer them in order of share (e.g. #1 = 40%, #2 = 25%, then the long tail). Keep answering while people are still on. "There's no new questions."
- **Sell cold so warm also converts**: "If you sell like it's warm to everyone, you'll only convert warm."
- **Upsell timing "in flight"**: sell at the halfway point, the two-thirds point, then a last chance, while pain is elevated and they're mid-decision rather than starting a new one (marathon shoes vs. after the race).
- **Separate delivery from pitch**: "Your delivery needs to become your content and your pitches need to be your pitches."
- **Price ladder for 1:1**: one-to-many vs. one-on-one should be ~5× the price, not 2×.
- **Business responsibility circle**: your job is that the business survives and grows; to do that it must take care of customers.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Current: 9–10% of live attendees buy. With a structured pitch to a warm audience he thinks she could roughly double it.
- Back-end upsell take rate 10%. Selling only at the end typically gets ~1/3; selling mid-process gets ~1/2 to 2/3+.
- Restructure from a 12-month ($2,500, 3-month intensive + 9 aftercare) to recurring $2,500 every 6 months so renewal is opt-out ("legal right" via billing, "psychological right" via a reminder push).
- Checkout $2,500; one-on-one call purchase $3,000 (+$500 = his "hourly" for her time). If everyone pays the extra, raise prices. Buying on the call/now = cheaper + bonus.
- $150/mo 1:1 coaching add-on is mispriced: price it ~5× (think $8–10K) or kill it.
- 3-day "no sweat" refund window to handle the absent-spouse objection. Onboard the next day to cut cold feet and refunds.
- Weekly organic webinar to mop up DMs; keep the waitlist for paid launches.
- 30–50 Q&A answers per week = 30–50 posts. Plus green-screen answers, that's 3–5 posts/day per platform. Run the winners as ads.
- Route all platforms' CTAs ("DM me on Instagram") to one conversion point.
- 3-second delay on the CTA banner in ads gave higher-quality traffic.
- Scaling roadmap: 5–9 / 10–19 employees → productize, cut unqualified leads/payment plans, sales aids (webinar), weekly game tape across CS/sales/marketing, CS playbook.

## Playbook steps
1. Convert the offer to 6-month recurring. 2. Build the structured webinar + FAQ section. 3. Tiered pricing: checkout vs. +$500 for 1:1, bonus for buying now. 4. Add a 3-day guarantee + immediate onboarding. 5. Vibe-code a dashboard agent that searches past Telegram answers and suggests replies for the team to approve or edit. 6. Weekly webinar for DMs. 7. Clip delivery Q&A + green-screen answers; post the 200 unposted testimonials; get editors. 8. Push every platform to IG DMs; winners → ads to launch/waitlist.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Believing charging more, upsells or structure = "bro marketing"/bad. "The business is in it to make money."
- Answering every DM personally. You have to "take a local cost to have a global benefit."
- Hooks like "a lot of people have been asking me this question" ("terrible hook").
- Long programs when a faster result is possible ("tooth pulled in 5 minutes or 5 hours?").
- Mixing delivery and pitching; small 8-parent Zoom calls instead of 50–100 on a webinar.
