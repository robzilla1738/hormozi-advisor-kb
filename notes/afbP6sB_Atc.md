# Watch this to keep more customers

## Core thesis
Case study of a SaaS portfolio company (55+ avatar) that saw churn spike and satisfaction drop 90 days after launching a new product. Fixing three areas — onboarding, expectations set in sales, and misaligned CS incentives — saved ~$2M in bottom-line profit in 6 months, cut cancellations 61%, and took ascensions from 20% to 48% (2.4×). Retention, not just acquisition, is what lets a business compound.

## Frameworks & models
- **First 24–48 hours post-purchase frame the whole relationship**; adding onboarding can add 2–3 months of LTV on recurring offers.
- **CLOSER in onboarding**: the last R ("reinforce the decision") = re-run Clarify / Label / Overview and re-sell the vacation; tie next steps to the customer's stated goal and *why* (at Allen this doubled or tripled follow-through; he doesn't recall the exact number).
- **Quick win**: get them set up/logged in on the call; patch support in live if anything breaks.
- **Delivery quality raises perceived likelihood of achievement**: "The best way to get someone to expect value in the future is to provide value today", so less hard selling is needed.
- **Two universal upsells**: more of the thing they bought, or more help with it.
- **Buying windows / identity shift**: purchases cluster right after a decision ("I'm a runner now"); sell the identity's adjacent products then (marathon shoes → shorts, tank, headphones) or "above-average Andrew" down the street will.
- **Complaints = inverted value equation**: "too techy" = effort and sacrifice; "not enough support" = lower perceived likelihood; "too slow" = time delay.
- **Baton pass + BAMFAM** ("book a meeting from a meeting", credited to Chiron Svat [spelling per caption]): sales → onboarding → account rep, introduced live, so the customer always has one known contact and a next meeting.
- **Bow-tie business**: marketing → sales → onboarding → expand out. Lead nurture decreases outreach over time; customer escalation *increases* it (1 touch day 1, 2 day 2, 3 day 3, across email/SMS/call).
- **Script every recurring conversation**, including CS — ~90% of customers bring the same few problems.
- **Two-business comparison**: one adds 100/200/300 customers a year but loses everyone; the other adds 100 a year and keeps them. Both have 300 customers in year 3; only the second compounds.
- **Metrics as intervention**: tracking alone improves things (daily weigh-ins).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you have no onboarding, add it first.
- Pilot expensive changes on a small slice (here 15% of new customers); define success stats in advance; a failed pilot means "not this fix", not "don't fix".
- Pay a 10–15% back-end commission for saving customers ("$200 to save a $2,000 sale" beats $0).
- CAC litmus: CAC should rise only with CPMs (e.g. ~15%/yr); if it doubles or triples fast, suspect anti-referrals from unhappy customers.
- Allocate internal resources like an investor: a $70–100K rep who saves $500K–$2M/yr is a 5×+ return, so take it every time.
- Lowering expectations in sales didn't reduce close rate; over-promising lowers believability.
- Lower the reading grade level of pages, scripts, ads and emails.
- Outreach frequency depends on the avatar: low-ticket buyers like frequent contact; affluent buyers "just want it to work".
- Low refund rate ≠ happy customers; track CSAT and NPS.
- Proactively downgrade underusing customers. At another portfolio company these became the second-highest-LTV avatar.
- His #1 investor metric: how many of last year's customers still pay you.

## Playbook steps
1. Pilot one-on-one onboarding (vs 25-per-call) with CLOSER reinforcement, the goal and why, and a quick win. 2. Tell them "we take notes for you"; send a summary, tailored tutorials and a point of contact; baton-pass live. 3. Make the next offer (more of it / more help). 4. Listen to sales calls; rescript anything creating false expectations. 5. Put in-app chat support in; use where chats come from to steer the product roadmap (82% of complaints from one page). 6. Create a dedicated escalations (triage) team so sales stops fielding support. 7. Pay them a save commission. 8. Use escalating multi-channel outreach. 9. Script and role-play CS calls. 10. Track CSAT/NPS; call unhappy customers.

## Mistakes he warns against
- One-to-many onboarding with company mission/vision content and a hard pitch with no next step.
- Sales reps handling unhappy customers: kills conviction, the strongest predictor of close rate.
- CS with no incentive and no script ("what can I help you with?").
- Being afraid to make more offers. He calls it an ethical duty to present solutions.
- Ignoring problems by not tracking them; fearing unhappy customers ("that's where the gold is"; cites Paul Graham).
- Thinking you're too big for good CS (Chick-fil-A, Amazon).

## Examples / case details
- Old process: 1 person running 6–8 calls a day with 25 customers each; a third of each call spent resetting passwords.
- Results: −61% cancellations; 25% of escalation calls ascended; overall ascensions 20% → 48% (2.4×); ~$2M saved, with upsell revenue on top and near-pure margin because CAC is already paid.
- Escalation math: a $1,000 product with a $4,000 upsell: if 1 in 4 escalated customers ascends, that roughly covers the refunds from the other three.
- Unhappy customers pick up at very high rates.
- Notes "cost of change is fixed": new launches spike problems (Apple lines).
