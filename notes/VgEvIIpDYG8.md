## Core thesis
Pick a niche where you have real experience, or go get it by doing the thing (starting at the bottom) before you try to teach or serve the niche. Most people skip to higher-leverage plays, like an agency or coaching for the niche, without having done it, so their clients get no results. You don't need anything new; do an old thing better. Expect about 5 years before anything really happens. "Your work works on you more than you work on it."

## Frameworks & models
- **YC's past-experience criterion**: industry years give you the lingo, the avatar, the problems, and visible opportunities.
- **Business = find a problem people think is big, solve it, charge, make the math work.** How well you solve it sets your virality through word of mouth (his book sold 100K copies in 6 months with no promotion).
- **Career pyramid**: the peak depends on the depth of the foundation. There's a sequence; you can't skip.
- **Do → get good → choose**: once you're good, decide whether to teach it or scale doing it (e.g. a realtor builds a 100-agent team instead of an agency).
- **Volume creates depth**: "If you don't stop doing something for 10 years, you'll be really good at it… and you'll like doing it." Passion follows competence.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Don't sell how to do something you've never done. "Stop listening to people about how to play the game who've never been anywhere but the sidelines" (Leila tweet).
- Poor client results, churn, or no testimonials → you're not good at the core thing yet. Fix competence; don't chase a $100K/mo goal.
- Pick something you're "at least not repelled from" where you see people making money, then stick with it for an inordinate time.
- Do first, read second: sell before reading sales books.
- Start = bank account + LLC + decide what to sell. Promotion and selling come first.
- About a third of the questions he gets come from too short a time horizon (90 days vs 5 years).

## Playbook steps
1. Choose a non-repellent niche where others make money. 2. Get industry experience, from the bottom if needed. 3. Sell and fulfill until you're good. 4. Then decide: teach (agency/coaching) or scale the operation.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Picking a niche you know nothing about.
- Trying to skip to leverage.
- An artificial self-imposed timeline.
- Thinking you're better than you are, which is "the slowest path."
- Reading 100 books before starting.

## Examples / case details
- An agency owner on his software (a majority stake was recently sold) spent 9 months with no client results. His one successful client succeeded because of the client, not the agency. Alex told him to close the agency, get a realtor's license, generate his own leads, and build a real estate business, then teach it.
- A gym owner was rejected by about 10 banks for lack of experience. He worked 3–4 years at Bally's, rose to personal training manager, saved, then opened his own gym without a loan.
- Alex started at 23.
