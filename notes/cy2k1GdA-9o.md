## Core thesis
A salesperson has three jobs: maximize opportunities, convert the highest % of them, and do it consistently for a long time. The best reps are available more, pull calls forward, BAMFAM, ask for referrals, prep, listen 2× more than they talk, "breathe the script," handle obstacles before price, ask again, track data, and never blame circumstances. Sales = increasing the likelihood the prospect buys.

## Frameworks & models
- **Three buckets**: opportunities → conversion → consistency (meta-skills).
- **Availability**: in his scheduling software (~4,000+ appointments/day across industries, analyzed by two data analysts), total available time slots was the single biggest predictor of sales. More slots also raise show rate (people book at the time that truly suits them and are in "buying mode").
- **Pull-ups**: drag later-week appointments into today's open slots (same-day shows higher); bump unconfirmed same-day calls and fill with responsive leads. Solar rep who immediately called every new booking (close now if they have time, else fill a slot later today) sold ~4× the #2 rep; rolled out portfolio-wide.
- **Two SOPs**: on-the-call SOP and off-the-call SOP (follow-ups, pull-ups, cancellations) — most companies lack the second.
- **BAMFAM** (book a meeting from a meeting; from a friend, Chiron): never end without the next meeting set; address the obstacle right then.
- **Kill list**: visible list of whales / high-value prospects to work daily.
- **Referral ask**: "Who do you know who is as awesome as you who would also benefit?" ("who," not yes/no; compliment sandwich). Playful fallback: "why do you hate me so much?"
- **Pre-call show-up tactics**: voice memo/video, preference question (shirt size, gloves, which gift card).
- **Prep rule**: ~10% of call time for repeated calls (60-min → 6 min; 2-hr → 12 min); flip it for rare high-stakes meetings (quarterly: ~6 hrs prep per hour). Notes prep you for the next call and make recommendations feel contextual (salesman → consultant). "Big guys win on reputation, small guys win on preparation."
- **Be like smoke**: answer questions with questions; "they believe nothing you say, everything they say." Ask what they did before, liked, disliked; then highlight what they liked. Commands vs questions ("Tell me about your day") to break pattern. Don't ask for the sale unless you know they'll buy (lawyer rule).
- **Breathe the script + concision**: memorize so you're present; trainer drilled the opening 30 seconds — correct, then 3 correct reps in a row. Scripts bloat via superstition (the "cough" story) — keep them tight to take more calls.
- **Manager drills**: rehearse script every morning, role-play, one skill at a time.
- **#10 Kill zombies up front (obstacles vs objections)**: obstacles are before price (easy), objections after (bomb ticking). Three sources: circumstances (time/money/fit), other people (spouse, partner), self (doubt). Moving from outside → authority → "I need to think about it" means you're getting deeper.
- **Past/future stories**: Susan story → "you want permission, not support"; play forward 5 years (5 lbs/yr → 25 lbs). Time: "resourcefulness not resources"; start while busy. Ask time commitment up front, then "what changed?"
- **Tonality/humor** (learned from Sam Bakhtiar's "ghetto tone") to defuse hard moments, then tone switch: "what's really going on?"
- **"What are you afraid of?" → best case/worst case** — his most-used close.
- **Ask again (looping)**: resolve the concern, then ask again — unlimited times, as long as each concern is addressed.
- **Dogs / horses / tigers** (from Mike Ary): hire tigers who "kill for sport."
- **Metrics as skill proxy**: book %, show %, offer %, close %, calls-to-close, cash collected.
- **Lead routing**: score leads; best leads → best closers, worst → newest reps (cheap practice).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- The rep with the most hours worked always sells the most (in every company he's seen).
- Referrals: only ~1 in 3–4 customers refers, but referral close rates ~80–90%; 1 close out of 4 calls → 2 of 5; effectively halves CAC. A new rep who asked "who else would you like to bring?" outsold #2 by ~50%.
- Consumer transactional sale under ~$1–2K: handle the spouse objection on the spot; multi-call B2B may need stakeholders.
- About 1 in 3 who face "what if he says no?" decide to buy anyway.
- A 20% lift in schedule rate is as good as a 20% lift in close rate.
- Never ask again without resolving the concern — that annoys them.
- Give feedback (e.g., lead quality) 1:1 to the manager, never on group calls.

## Playbook steps
1. Maximize availability (evenings, weekends). 2. Run off-call SOP: call new bookings now, pull up calls, refill slots. 3. Pre-call touches + preference question. 4. 10% prep + review notes. 5. On call: ask, listen 2:1, surface obstacles before price. 6. Present only what they said they liked. 7. Close; on no → find why, resolve, ask again; use best/worst case. 8. BAMFAM + "who do you know." 9. Track the full funnel metrics. 10. Managers: daily single-skill role-play; route best leads to best closers.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Refusing same-day appointments / limited availability out of ego.
- Ending calls "jolly" with no next meeting.
- Hiring talkers; answering prospect questions with statements.
- Telling a rep nine things to fix at once.
- Adding superstitious lines to the script.
- Avoiding the landmine heard early in the call — it will explode at the close.
- Treating weak leads as not worth taking seriously (makes your skill duller).
- Blaming leads, comp plans, circumstances; complaining publicly.

## Examples / case details
- Built teams 0→40 and 0→60; he personally closed 4,000+ sales.
- Leila's nail salon: first two refused same-day bookings and lost the sale and the future customer.
- Gym-owner outreach: researched owners' social profiles, offered layout suggestions to double usable floor space.
- Dave Ramsey envelope lady: "worst case you can't afford food — you lose weight."
- Jacob (started at ~15) volunteered as "garbage man" for weak leads → more reps → got better.
- Timeshare top rep (~$3M/yr commission among ~3,000 reps) used his CEO hour to ask for the best leads → his income 5× ; rolled out nationally → business 5×. Top earner going $200K → $800K made recruiting 10× easier.
- Portfolio company scaled sales team 0→40 in just over a quarter, adding $21M profit.
