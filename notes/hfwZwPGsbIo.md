# Why Doing More Is Actually Making You Less Money
id: hfwZwPGsbIo / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hfwZwPGsbIo / duration: 1287s (~21 min) / tags: 13-quit-or-pivot, 12-mindset-leverage, 08-ops-scaling, 01-what-business-to-start
Signal: high

## Core thesis
Live hot-seat with Kira, a single mom of five running a publishing house/5-day book-writing adventures, a TV show, and a women's business school (~$1M last year). Focus beats spread: pick the one business the market keeps paying you for and remove the other "doors." Channel the founder's novelty itch into marketing/content instead of constant business changes, because every change costs ~20% performance up front. (Guest speaker is Kira; advice is Alex's.)

## Frameworks & models
- **Magnifying glass**: talent/work ethic = size of the glass; it does nothing unless focused on one point.
- **Trade novelty for loyalty**: staying spread thin buys novelty at the price of impact/size; going all-in buys a big team-and-systems business at the price of novelty. Business is like fine wine, understood deeper over years/seasons.
- **Go where the money's at / bloodhound for value**: repeat buying and "solve this for me" requests are market feedback. If customers keep knocking on door 1 of 3, crush door 1 and remove the others.
- **The ~20% change tax** (his anecdotal observation): any change to sales, leadership, onboarding, or training causes ~20% immediate performance drop, even if the change is superior; recovery takes ~4–5 months. Left alone, people tend to improve anyway (~the 5% you hoped to gain).
- **Risk-adjusted 20% rule**: only make a change if it's a ~40% improvement with a ~50/50 shot (≈20% risk-adjusted, matching the cost).
- **Punch card of changes** (after Buffett's 20-investment punch card): ~52 tests/yr max on automated things like landing pages (one variable at a time, enough traffic); people/process changes ~1 per quarter per department, fewer in bigger companies.
- **Alex's big list of ideas**: write every idea on phone list with estimated upside and probability; rank-order; don't tell the team; pull one only when team has bandwidth.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Don't trade a guaranteed ~20% loss for a hoped 5% gain.
- Require ~40% upside at ~50% odds before changing anything.
- Let an idea "season" 2–4 weeks (a month); if you still think about it, it may have legs.
- New idea must beat the other pending ~50% ideas already on the list.
- Accept that "some [stuff] stays [stuff]"; fixing one of 100 annoyances often drops four others.
- Use real-time metrics (NPS, ascension, churn, satisfaction) so you can see the cost of changes; monthly/vague data hides it.

## Playbook steps
1. Identify the offering the market repeatedly pays for (the "10-year-old" of your businesses); go all-in, remove the others. 2. Point your media asset (e.g. the TV show) at that core offer. 3. Channel creative/ADD energy into marketing, content, ads, books. 4. Log every new idea privately with estimated value × likelihood. 5. Season ideas 2–4 weeks. 6. Only deploy one when it clears the 40%/50-50 bar and beats others, and the team has bandwidth.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Chasing every opportunity / FOMO (he had 9 businesses—six gyms, chiro & dental agencies, turnaround launches—doing a few million and stuck in multi-year plateaus).
- Bored leaders "burning down the city" instead of "painting it."
- Over-optimism on change upside (expect 40%, get 5%).
- "Test test test" without counting the cost of each test; changing multiple variables at once.
- Changing so much you never know what broke.

## Examples / case details
- Kira closed $1.25M in five minutes to a crowd of 30 at a January event; books are what bring people to her; her financial adviser Julia confirmed book financials are best.
