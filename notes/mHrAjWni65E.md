## Core thesis
Most businesses make more in the last month of the year than in any other, because buyers spend more and competitors advertise more. Run a promotion and break through with one of four "gifts": a loss leader, discounted referral gift cards, a scarce high-ticket package, or free-with-prepay. Wrap it with the MAGIC naming formula. All four work year-round.

## Frameworks & models
- **Loss leader / splintering the offer**: sell a well-understood, price-known item at a loss to bring people in, then profit on ancillaries whose prices they don't know (diapers vs. high-markup bottles; he believes Diapers.com used this). For services, itemize your components (facility, accountability, workouts, nutrition, holiday alcohol guide), peel one off, and put 90% off it.
- **Gift card strategy**: (1) one full unit of service in dollars (e.g. $200); (2) market to existing customers, limit 2 per person; (3) 80–90% off (a $200 card for $20); (4) only as gifts for friends and family; (5) capture recipient contact info at purchase and do a 3-way intro; (6) apply the $200 as credit toward a $1,000 offer, making it an $800 sale. You get paid to acquire new customers, and they're referrals who buy and stay at higher rates. Gift cards are generally lucrative due to low redemption (Best Buy) and float.
- **High-ticket, high-scarcity**: a 10–100× "ultimate version" limited to 5–10% of the customer base (start at 5% so it sells out, then go to 6–7% next year). Customers are fractal: 80/20 applied twice ≈ the top 5%. Build hype, announce the drop time ("have your credit cards ready"), and post countdowns (4 left... sold out).
- **Free with prepay / buy X get Y**: make the free part bigger than the paid part (buy 1 get 2–3 free) while raising the price of the one. Require a prepay, with no payment plans on discounted deals. For services, create a new tier (+1–2 low-cost extras, e.g. $100 → $200/mo) so later full-price customers aren't upset, then offer buy 6 get 6 or buy 4 get 8. Limit quantity. Also usable as a grand-opening strategy (first 50 patients) to cash-flow the build-out.
- **MAGIC wrapper**: Magnetic reason why, Avatar callout, Goal, Interval (time frame), Container word (blueprint, accelerator, intensive, launchpad, getaway). Example: "Busy Mom's 30-Day Relaxation Getaway" for a spa.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Holidays: always have a promotion running.
- Scarcity: limit gift cards to 2 per person, high-ticket to ~5% of customers, and prepay deals to a fixed number.
- Discounted deals must be paid in full up front.
- Holiday-only new tiers are ideal because "no one has any comparison for the pricing."

## Playbook steps
1. Itemize your offer components. 2. Pick a promotion: loss leader, gift cards, high-ticket scarcity, or buy X get Y. 3. Set limits and prepay terms. 4. Name it with MAGIC. 5. Build hype before launch and post live scarcity updates. 6. Collect referral contact info and upsell recipients in a sales consult.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Not promoting in the highest-spend month.
- Becoming dependent on prepay promotions. He declined to invest in some businesses because they relied on them too heavily.
- Allowing payment plans on deep-discount prepays.
- Offering promos to "everyone" without an avatar callout.

## Examples / case details
- Nashville boot store: "buy one get two free" with boots priced ~$600 vs a typical ~$200. It targeted bachelorette parties and was still there and bigger 10 years later.
- Shirts: normally $5 each; $10 for one plus two free = 3 shirts for $10, about a 33% discount. He says "like a 30% discount," but it's perceived as far more.
- Membership: $100/mo = $1,200/yr vs. "the year for $600 one time" up front.
- High ticket: 100 customers, 5 buy a $5,000 package = $25,000, which can double a $10–20K/month business that month. Captions read "$55,000" and "$22,000 and $20,000"; for a $200 base, 10–100× = $2,000–$20,000 [inference: caption errors].
