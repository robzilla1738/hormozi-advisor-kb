## Core thesis
Behaviorally, trust = making yourself punishable by someone and betting they won't punish you. There are four types (who is at risk × who punishes), a two-question test before extending trust, and a rule for being trustworthy: zero punishment, because one betrayal undoes years of reinforcement. Self-described departure from business content; ends with a plug for acquisition.com/roadmap (10-stage $0→$100M+ roadmap).

## Frameworks & models
- **Four types of trust** (who's at risk × who punishes):
  1. You risk, they punish (you share a secret/info).
  2. They risk, you punish (they hand you the "knife"; you're being trustworthy).
  3. You risk, environment punishes (you rely on their word/action, e.g. "pick my kid up"; or follow their advice and reality punishes).
  4. They risk, environment punishes (they rely on your word or advice).
- **Two-question trust test**: (1) Track record of protecting what they've been given? (2) Does burning you cost them more than protecting you (is betrayal a bad deal for them)? Both yes → extend trust.
- **Why trust at all**: a relationship's ceiling = shared context; more context lets people serve you better but also hurt you more.
- **Zero-punishment rule**: trust built through small reinforcing cycles is erased by one punishing event ("I trusted him for 20 years… one thing"). Be reinforcing at disclosure ("your secret's safe, it's your news") and never use it later, including via third parties ("loose lips sink ships").
- **Dead tree on the vine**: breaking your word for short-term gain doesn't harm the relationship; it likely ends it, just slowly.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- For material disclosures, calculate independently: what do they gain by betraying vs. what do I risk? Beware when the incentive to wrong you grows too large.
- Short-term win (e.g. winning an argument) is never worth losing the relationship.
- Explain distrust concretely: list the times you made yourself punishable and they used it.

## Playbook steps
1. Before material trust: run the two-question test. 2. Extend in small increments. 3. When others confide, respond reinforcingly and keep it contained. 4. Keep your word and give advice that works, to compound trust/influence.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Dismissing this as "transactional" (everyone weighs pluses and minuses; he's just defining it).
- Using disclosed information in a fight or sharing it onward.
- Breaking your word for short-term benefit.
