## Core thesis
The "heart" side of business (why we exist, how we behave, who decides what) is ignored because it's hard to quantify, yet it can get 2×, 4×, 8× more output from the same people. Core values are non-negotiables, not aspirations: you hire and fire on them. They must repel people, use your own words, and be few (three). Acquisition.com's three: unimpeachable character, sincere candor, competitive greatness. He applies them to employees, the portfolio founders he invests in, clients and himself. (Mentions Acquisition.com ~$85M/yr and a recent 66% stake sale of two companies at a $46.2M valuation.)

## Frameworks & models
- **Brain vs heart of business**: same strategy and rules, but a new coach (Remember the Titans) gets more from the same team because they believe.
- **Core heart questions**: Why do we exist? How do we behave (values)? Who's in charge of which decisions? Who does what?
- **Values rules**: (1) non-negotiable and innate, not aspirational (Southwest "have fun" = fireable if you won't); (2) draw a line in the sand, e.g. justice vs mercy. "If your values do not repel people then they are not values they are platitudes"; (3) phrase them in your own sayings (Gym Launch: "speed is king," "do the boring work," "don't sugarcoat it," "have humility"); (4) keep it to three; chunk up overlapping ones to a through-line.
- **Values scale decision-making**: they let others decide without the founder ("should I hire this person even though Alex is not involved?").
- **Acquisition.com values**:
  1. **Unimpeachable character**: the through-line of loyalty, integrity and long-term thinking. Seal Team Six tests: Can I trust them with my life? With my wife (leave for a month, no flirting or stray looks)? With my money (hold more than they've ever had for a month/year/decade, untouched)? Ego in a high performer = cancer. Watch *how* they answer, not just what. "What hot water could I put this tea bag in?"
  2. **Sincere candor**: no progress without feedback. Self-awareness to give accurate, constructive feedback plus humility to receive it without taking it personally. Unspoken resentments fill the space between people and degrade communication; running a company is clarity of communication.
  3. **Competitive greatness** (from John Wooden): being at your best when your best is needed; enjoying a hard challenge.
- **Vibe test**: reading the three values should tell you what the place is like and who won't fit.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Three values is the sweet spot; with 10 it's impossible to decide.
- Accept candidates only at 3 out of 3; two out of three is a no.
- "If you would not do business with someone for a decade, do not do business with them for a day" (clients, employees, partners) [Naval-ism, per Alex; caption shows "nalisms"].
- Ambitious and candid but weak character → no, because things will get hard in business and they'll revert.
- If most people don't fit your non-negotiables, good; most people shouldn't be in your company.

## Playbook steps
1. List candidate values; find the through-line and chunk up. 2. Write them in your own phrases. 3. Cut to three that repel. 4. Use them as hire/fire and client/partner filters at 3/3. 5. Design interview questions that reveal character in how they answer. 6. Prune existing team and clients who don't fit ("short-term pain for long-term gain").

## Mistakes he warns against
- Aspirational or platitude values that don't draw a line.
- Too many values.
- Rationalizing a "wisp or edge that feels off" because of other strengths (he did this earlier in his career).
- Keeping high-performing egotists, who are cancers to culture.
- Bending a non-negotiable for 2 out of 3.
