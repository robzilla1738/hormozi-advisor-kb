**Speaker note:** This is NOT Alex. It is Leila Hormozi interviewed by Brooke Castillo, on the Acquisition.com channel. Attribute every point to Leila (or Brooke where noted).

## Core thesis
Leila's edge is making companies well run and profitable: build → optimize → scale. That makes them sellable (PE buyers called their team the best leadership they'd seen) and lets founders take cash out. Money didn't change their lifestyle. It shifted identity. "The ability to make money changes people," not money itself. Hire with character, clarity and a vision big enough to hold employees' careers.

## Frameworks & models
- **Build → Optimize → Scale**: companies at $3–10M lack things (sales manager, processes, avatar, differentiation). At ~$20M those things exist but aren't optimized. Most people go build → scale, and things break. Once optimized, a $20M company can open channels, change sales strategy or add a product to reach ~$60M quickly.
- **"Whatever you grow is going to grow"** (Brooke): problems scale too. Optimize before scaling, or you scale bad word of mouth. She cites the stat that a bad experience is told to ~8 people and a good one to ~2. Negative reviews also block top talent.
- **Win-win-win** (citing John Mackey): every decision must be a win for business, customer and employees. Ask "how do I make this a win?"
- **Hiring secret**: (1) "You can't hire anyone who is a better person than you." Elite talent won't come to someone without elite character (John Wooden). (2) Provide clarity (documented role, one-year plan) and opportunity: "a vision so big for your company that their vision for their career can fit inside of it." Recruiting deck: vision, mission, values, a page on the company in 5 years, 5-year projections including employees' salary growth.
- **Life in seasons**: one area goes into focus mode (6–12 months or 1–4 years) while the rest are on maintenance.
- **Two values will conflict**: drop the judgment and aim to be "imperfect at all of it."
- **Fear is a mile wide and an inch deep**: get over it by doing.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Run companies very profitably so the operator isn't in scarcity mode and can extract cash.
- When early employees outgrow their fit (the $1M team that can't take you to $5M), don't "junk drawer" them. Find 3–5 companies in your network that fit them better and offer intros.
- Hire people who like change. Tell hires their role may change ~50% within 3 months. Fastest-growing companies have people who change fastest.
- Can't attract top talent? Ask your current team and the candidates who turned you down why. In one case it was old negative reviews.
- The acquisition firm turns down companies whose founders lack the character to attract top talent.
- Don't carry "everyone depends on me" pressure. Assume people are resourceful, so you decide with a sound mind.
- Each level takes about the same effort as the last. Don't get complacent.

## Playbook steps
1. Audit what's missing versus what's unoptimized. 2. Fix and optimize before adding channels or products. 3. Define the values the business needs and hire for them. 4. Show candidates a 5-year vision deck with projections. 5. Set role-change expectations. 6. Exit outgrown employees with warm intros.

## Mistakes she warns against
- Build → scale without optimizing.
- Thinking a good business has no problems. "If you don't have problems your business is dying."
- Buying things to look rich rather than out of authentic desire.
- Taking money advice from people with less money. Complex wealth moves require attention.
- Expecting a partner's support to cure your own self-doubt. Define "support" together.

## Examples / case details
- Gym Launch was "a huge failure" for ~1.5 years, then took off. They later sold Gym Launch and Allan (software) and have Prestige Labs. They grow companies from $3–10M to $100M.
- Leila had never managed anyone and Googled everything (state taxes, churn).
- PE buyers praised the leadership team and successors.
- Started Acquisition.com the day after the sale.
- Her grandmother was among Iran's first female teachers.
