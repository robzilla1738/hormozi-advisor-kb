## Core thesis
A keynote ("level seven entrepreneurship"): speed is not activity, it is making the right few strategic decisions and fewer mistakes ("micro slow, macro fast"). Money is a denomination of time and leverage multiplies time. He uses four frameworks to judge any opportunity, including portfolio companies: (1) scale the entrepreneur, (2) scale the market, (3) scale the deliverable, (4) scale the business. Fortune ≈ entrepreneur × market × deliverable scalability × business acumen. "It's not how hard you row but what boat you are in" (Buffett story).

## Frameworks & models
- **Four scaling frameworks**: entrepreneur, market, deliverable, business. Opportunity = market × scalability of deliverable. A level-10 entrepreneur on a level-1 opportunity does "pretty well but not that great"; a weak operator on a level-10 opportunity still makes money.
- **Entrepreneur bottleneck ladder**: every business is bottlenecked by the founder lacking a skill, a character trait, or a belief. Like theory of constraints, you are limited to the lowest rung. Examples: running ~10 businesses = trait (focus); running gyms instead of licensing ("level 10 skill set in a level two opportunity", Russell Brunson) = belief; "no good salespeople exist" = skill (recruiting, hiring, training, managing a sales team).
- **Binaries vs continuums**: it's not whether you can manage a sales team but how well. Rephrase a complaint as a solvable question ("How do I create consistency in sales?").
- **Skill stacking**: skills compound into asymmetric returns (math → bookkeeping → accounting → tax → insurance → deal structure → capital markets "rainmaker"). His stack: fitness → nutrition → sales → local marketing → operations → scaling sales teams → B2B national marketing, which took him $0 → $28M/yr in 20 months once he had the belief.
- **Starving crowd / market attributes**: pain (they desperately need it), purchasing power, easy to target, growing.
- **Avatar choice**: pick the one you can help most (quick); one you can productize for with low operational drag, which creates margin (medium).
- **Five ways to scale a market** (salon example): up market (multi-location, chains), down market (stylists), adjacent (lashes, nails), broader (med spas, massage), deeper (his favorite: buy competitors, new platforms, add outbound, raise ad spend).
- **Four C's / leverage pyramid** (borrowed partly from Naval): labor, capital (both need permission), code, content/media (permissionless, zero replication cost). His income jumps, each ~10×: employee (4 figures/mo) → self-employed trainer (5) → gym owner using labor (6) → licensing = labor + media (~$1M/mo income) → adding capital (target 8 figures/mo) → full stack.
- **Delivery cube**: 1-on-1 / small group / one-to-many; DIY / done-with-you / done-for-you; support channel (text, chat, email, phone, Zoom); consumption format (written, live, audio, video); speed & convenience (24/7 vs 9–5, response time); plus thought experiments.
- **Thought experiments**: what would you deliver at 10× the price? How could you deliver more value at 1/10 the price with things that cost nothing to replicate? If customers could only come from referrals, how would the client experience change?
- **Only six things you can sell**: products, services, access, media, risk, money, each physical or digital. Experiences = product + service + access. Add a component to your offer stack without adding operational drag.
- **Stage focus by revenue**: $0–1M: one product, one avatar, one channel (goal: consistently sell something people want). $1–10M: raise lifetime gross profit per customer with higher-leverage deliverables. $10–50M: professionalize (delivery consistency, tracking, CRM, finances) by hiring experienced people who've done it. "Framework X": attract talent, with multiple CEOs running their own P&Ls.
- **Six external functions mirrored internally**: lead gen, nurture, sales, customer success, ascension, resell ↔ recruiting applications, booking interviews, hiring, onboarding, training/career path, retention. Track hiring metrics like ad metrics.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Under seven figures with multiple businesses: stop and pick one ("niche-hopping fallacy": all could work, none will if you pursue them all). Test: if one vanished, how easy would it be to make the other win?
- If a competitor makes more than you, they're better at the game. Learn from them instead of blaming them.
- Prefer growing markets (tailwind); avoid shrinking ones (the newspaper ad platform shrinking ~25%/yr).
- "Great entrepreneur, poor market, market wins. Poor entrepreneur, great market, market wins."
- When "saturated" at a small share of a big market (a $2M agency in a ~$10B chiropractor market), go deeper before switching markets. If something's working, do more of it.
- If people aren't referring you, fix the product before promoting it.
- Small and big goals take similar effort, so make them big; level-10 talent only joins big opportunities.

## Playbook steps
1. Audit your skill stack and skill/trait/belief deficiencies; work on the one keeping you from your goal. 2. Pick a market with pain, money, targetability, growth. 3. Scale the market: deeper first, then up/down/adjacent/broader. 4. Move the deliverable up the leverage pyramid (code/media), using the delivery cube and the six sellables. 5. Run the stage-appropriate focus for your revenue band. 6. Diagnose growth limits through the internal talent funnel.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Mistaking activity for speed; changing direction every 90 days ("I'd rather get rich for sure than get rich quick").
- Unrealistic expectations (millionaire in 90 days); not living as if you'd accept 5 years of nothing for a sure result.
- Multiple channels or businesses before $1M.
- Thinking passive income is the goal: he found exiting the org chart "wildly boring," and the real wants are options and engaging work ("make active income cool again").
- Blaming the sales team when the gap is your own skill.

## Examples / case details
- Gym Launch's first ad: a walkthrough of a gym; 191 signups in 19 days at $500 each, sold 1-on-1.
- Business path: 6 gyms off cash flow in 3 years → Gym Launch $2.4M/mo → supplements $1.7M/mo → software $1.6M/mo (sold) → photography chain $1.1M/mo in 14 months. Acquisition.com portfolio ~$85M/yr.
- Closed ~4,000 one-on-one sales over ~3.5 years (from his CRM).
- Stalled around $30–40M with gym/business-owner market caps; peers doing more had picked mass markets with expensive products.
- Ad agency licensed its winning ads monthly to DIY clients: +~$1M/month profit.
- Book selling ~$250K/month through word of mouth with no funnels (his math).
- Cold-call team (~26): the goal of +10 BDRs was missed for 3 quarters because HR did only 5 interviews/week, which just replaced frontline churn. Fixing recruiting ads and process gave 10 hires in 2 weeks and doubled applications.
- Neighbor took home ~$1B/yr (100% owner, $3.8B sales, 26% net margins): "you enter a new club as the smallest member."
