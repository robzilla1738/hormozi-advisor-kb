## Core thesis
Acquisition.com's model: free media turns raw attention into contact info, low-ticket conversions (email list, Skool, books) ascend business owners into high-ticket services, and cash is redeployed into real estate, ventures and private equity. Everything depends on one promise: make real business education accessible and make "our free stuff better than everyone else's paid stuff," with absolute brand credibility. Originally an internal recruiting video.

## Frameworks & models
- **Attention → capture → conversion → ascension → redeploy flywheel**: Media (content, courses, podcasts, YouTube) → contact capture (Mosey Minute email list, Skool ~$9/mo, books ~$30) → Advisory Practice (L1 ~$5K, L2 ~$35K, L3 ~$135K, sold mostly by phone) → revenue redeployed into ACQ RE (multifamily real estate, can raise from audience), ACQ Ventures (1–5% bets or seeding companies to sell into the distribution base), and private equity (30–100% stakes).
- **Onion / ascension**: wantrepreneurs helped mostly for free; owners who grow ascend and get monetized.
- **Disney analogy**: media = brand; ACQ Network = Disney+ (scalable membership); Advisory Practice = theme parks (meet the people from the case studies); Skool = a franchise like Star Wars; future spokes = insurance, lending, sales AI.
- **Planned ACQ Network** (~2026): ~$5–10K/yr membership for business owners, AI consultant trained on advisory work, industry calls, community, plus group-negotiated vendor deals (agencies, credit cards) so savings exceed the fee and "no one would ever want to leave."
- **Distribution-led deals**: future PE/venture bets chosen because they can directly benefit the base of businesses, not just because ACQ can grow them.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Monetization sweet spot: businesses ~$500K–$50M revenue; under $500K is monetized lightly (ads, low ticket, books) with the goal of helping them grow into the core.
- Membership price should be a barrier high enough to filter to business owners, not "super expensive."
- Credibility is the precondition for the whole flywheel.
- Team members who run the process across many businesses become the founders ACQ backs.

## Playbook steps
1. Invest money, effort, team in free media.
2. Capture contact info via low-friction conversions.
3. Ascend through tiered offers (L1→L2→L3), mostly via phone sales.
4. Redeploy cash into adjacent assets that serve the same audience.
5. Add a scalable network/membership as the base, with ancillary services as ascension paths.
6. Make a VSL that sells your company to recruits.

## Mistakes he warns against
- (Implied) Monetizing small audiences directly instead of helping them grow into buyers. [inference]
- Letting free content be worse than competitors' paid content.
