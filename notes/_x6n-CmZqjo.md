## Core thesis
Alex relays notes from a call with an unnamed mid-70s operator whose privately held companies (~50 of them) do over $1B/yr in revenue (Alex once addresses him as "Ben"). These are Alex's secondhand notes, not the billionaire's own words. Growth beyond ~$30M comes from people: three superstars per business (acquisition, delivery, ops/shared services), CEO profit shares (~5%) plus the right to buy in, and giving up control. The jump from $100M to $1B came after the founders broke the company into ~100 pieces with incentivized leaders and stepped back. Invest for return *of* capital, not return *on* it.

## Frameworks & models
- **Access ≈ your level × 5–10**: you can usually reach people about one zero above you on achievement. Below that you pay (masterminds, coaching). "Buy a millionaire lunch" doesn't work if you offer nothing.
- **Founder influence dilutes** with each management layer. That is why Alex was stuck at $30–40M for ~3 years before the people breakthrough (now pacing ~$85M).
- **Three superstars per self-sustaining business**: acquisition (gets clients), delivery (keeps promises), and operations/shared services (keeps the first two "out of jail": bills, payroll, legal, IT).
- **Superstar test**: "I've never not thought someone was a superstar and then later realized they were." Mis-judging in the other direction happens; this direction never does. If you don't immediately think a senior hire is a superstar, they probably aren't. "The biggest thing that gets in the way of excellent is good enough."
- **Company size ∝ number of superstars** (Alex's conclusion). At larger scale you need fewer hunters and more "superstar tenders" (farmers). Growth is stepwise (growth, plateau, growth), and plateaus are de-bottlenecked by an experienced person.
- **Profits are unnatural**: left alone, a business breaks even. Creating profit takes will and is the CEO's sole responsibility.
- **Incentives**: ~5% of profits to each company CEO, plus the option to truly buy in to growing chunks (one CEO went from 5–10% to 30%+). The business becomes a conglomerate of profit centers, each with a specialized driver. "Show me the incentives and I'll show you the outcome" (Munger).
- **Partnering**: partner only with people of complementary skills (acquisition + delivery + ops), never clones of yourself. Pay vendors (e.g., an accountant) in cash, not equity. Early equity is the most expensive.
- **Return of capital > return on capital**: a couple hundred million in multifamily real estate. Rule of thumb: never buy below an 8 cap rate. Prefer lower-risk buildings and moderate leverage. "Poor people buy the lottery ticket; rich people own the lottery game and make 10% a year." Doubling net worth changes nothing; halving it does, so focus on downside mitigation.
- **"Advanced people are advanced because they never don't do the basics."** Simple, not easy.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- "Every single person… is three amazing hires away from all the growth you've ever wanted."
- From $30M to $100M is about giving up high-level decision control; superstars want authority and the credit.
- Stepping away without enough talent leads to a crash (Alex has done it multiple times).

## Playbook steps
1. Raise your own achievements to earn access to higher-level mentors.
2. Install superstar heads of acquisition, delivery and ops.
3. Give each business-unit CEO a profit share plus a buy-in path.
4. Split into profit centers with specialized drivers.
5. Step back. Accept that leaders may overrule you and be right.
6. Put wealth in downside-protected assets (cap rate ≥ 8, sensible leverage).

## Mistakes he warns against
- Partnering with similar-skill friends because "they seemed cool."
- Giving away equity for work you could buy.
- Hiring "good enough" leaders.
- Greed with the pie; refusing to make others wealthy.

## Examples / case details
- The billionaire sat at ~$100M for 4–5 years and burned out. He and his partner broke the company into ~100 pieces with profit sharing and phantom equity and "checked out." It reached $500M five years later and $1B five years after that. He credits luck and leaders' decisions he had opposed.
- Alex at the time: owner of ALAN, Gym Launch, Prestige Labs and five other companies, just under $100M/yr; Leila co-CEO.
