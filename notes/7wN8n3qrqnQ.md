## Core thesis
The perceived value of a favor decays over time for the receiver and grows over time for the giver. Information behaves the same way. That's why coaches get resented ("I would've figured it out anyway") even though, per a meta-analysis he cites, mentored people do about 4× better. Business implications: capture testimonials and asks right at the moment of the result, and keep delivering new wins ("telephone poles") because value has an expiration date.

## Frameworks & models
- **Coaching data (as relayed by friend "Dr. Cashi")**: a mega meta-analysis of 40,000+ studies, controlling for income and IQ, found people with mentors or coaches had 4× greater success. (Secondhand claim, unverified.)
- **Favor perception asymmetry** (from a Cialdini co-authored book, roughly "50 Scientifically Proven Ways to Be Persuasive"): the receiver values the favor most immediately after relief from pain, and it declines. The giver thinks it was "nothing" at first and inflates it over time.
- **Information decays fastest**: incredibly valuable on consumption, then its perceived value drops rapidly.
- **Telephone-pole model of goodwill**: each new favor or win is a pole. Without new poles the wire sags to the ground.
- **New gaps after every bridge**: solving one problem moves the student to the next plateau with a new gap, so stay far enough ahead to keep building bridges.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Ask for the return favor, referral or testimonial immediately after delivering the result. E.g. a client loses 25 lb: film the testimonial right as they step off the scale, not 6 months later.
- Teach new things on a regular cadence to keep perceived value up (retention lever). [inference]
- As the student, credit your helpers, including negative mentors (his book acknowledgements list good and bad). Tony Robbins: also blame them for your resilience.

## Playbook steps
1. Design fulfillment around fast wins. 2. Capture testimonials and asks at the peak moment. 3. Keep delivering fresh breakthroughs on a schedule. 4. Level yourself up to stay ahead of clients' next gap. 5. Don't take post-hoc ingratitude personally.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Waiting months to ask for testimonials.
- Resenting clients for "what have you done for me lately?" Accept it as human bias.
- Burning bridges with past mentors.
