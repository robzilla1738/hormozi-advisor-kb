## Frameworks & models
- **Lollapalooza / golden BBs**: big outcomes come from 2–6 incremental advantages multiplied together, not one silver bullet.
- **Market crisscross**: US education ~$1.3T. Formal ~$850B is shrinking ~6%/yr (~$50B/yr); e-learning ~$200–250B is growing ~20% (~$40B/yr). Money is moving from the "mediocre middle" universities to niche creators. "I'd rather be in this boat." Directional correctness matters more than exact numbers.
- **Golden ratio (passive growth)**: % of customers who refer per period ÷ % who churn. 10/10 = flat; 20/10 = doubles every interval. Most businesses are ~2% vs 10% and have to fill the bucket with ads. Pour gas only once referrals > churn.
- **Enterprise-value leverage**: same $10M revenue / $3M profit. Service at ~5× profit = $15M (doubling adds ~$15M). E-com at ~2× revenue = $20M (+$20M). Tech at 10–20× revenue = $100–200M (doubling adds ~$200M). Same effort, far more value.
- **Borrowed platform trust**: his book converts ~2–3% on Shopify vs ~30% on Amazon (10×). Marketplace pages beat self-built pages. Copy the layouts of high-traffic sites (video, image carousel, reviews, buy button, bullets).
- **Community business model**: eyeballs (post / ads / outreach / partner with an audience) → community page → recurring $1–$999/mo. Low-risk front end, with optional ascension.
- **Care × willingness-to-pay matrix** (ProfitWell 14,000-person survey, as he recalls it): high care + high WTP = (1) exclusive access (Q&A, proximity), (2) exclusive content (courses, articles), (3) events (in person). Newsletter is expected (high care, low WTP); merch and discounts are lower.
- **"I am a ___" identity**: build community around the lifestyle and identity your product leads people toward.
- **Centralization → decentralization cycle**: like Rome eroded by barbarians, a million niche creators each beat the giant at one sliver. Creators win on specificity and proof, ~zero cost of delivery, and ~zero cost of distribution.
- **Obsessive, not compulsive**: ideal founders hold attention on one goal for years without compulsively changing direction.
- **Partnership coverage**: Product / Promotion / People. A partner should be obsessed with a different function than you, or "one of you isn't necessary." Equity needn't be split equally.
- **Underdog vs incumbent pitch**: every position has an advantage. Underdog: "you get all my attention." Incumbent: "we've systematized the result."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Only promote or endorse something that "grows on its own" and is really good, because the referrer gets blamed (the Sally hairstylist analogy).
- Put effort where the valuation multiple is highest. Same work, more value.
- Beginners: choose the simplest model with the lowest risk and fewest moving parts.
- To make it sellable: buyers want long-term recurring revenue that works without the founder. A founder-face community carries key-man risk, so build it differently if you plan to exit.
- Don't worry about exit/asset quality at the start. Make the first dollar, and the next steps become clear.
- Influencer partnership: target 50K–150K-follower accounts with real engagement, ideally ones you already follow. Don't shoot above your league. Take a small cut (e.g. ~20%) at first; "don't be greedy up front."
- Content businesses suit international audiences; physical products would cut out maybe ~70% of a global audience (shipping, tariffs).

## Playbook steps
**Zero-to-partner (his start-over-at-zero plan):**
1. Pick 10–100 influencers you already follow (50–150K followers).
2. DM their commenters and interview about 5 people on what community they'd want; record and take notes.
3. Build a deck: strategy, rollout phases, customer insights, and an estimate such as "1% of your audience = $X/mo."
4. Pitch as the dedicated underdog: "you'll have my cell; it's just me."
5. If they say no, show that deck to the next influencer as a portfolio piece. "The only way you lose is if you don't work."

**Free-offer playbook:** look at what everyone else charges for (calls, courses, community, tools, conviction), make it better, and make it free (Skool Games: 14 days free, 3 calls a week, a free course).

**Other ways people won:** cold email plus phone at $500–$1K/mo; monetizing an existing audience with a strong CTA and bonuses; ads; operator-partner with an influencer.

## Mistakes he warns against
- The silver-bullet fallacy.
- Scaling with ads when churn exceeds referrals.
- Self-built storefronts with no inherent trust when a platform would lend credibility.
- Pitching a partner empty-handed ("you have value and I have nothing").
- Learning from people who haven't done it and get key points wrong.
- Getting stuck in personal development before making a dollar: "all you got to do is make a dollar and then you're in business."
- Facebook groups for paid communities: paid reach is ~300 of 6,500 members, there's no payment integration, and someone has to remove non-payers by hand (a member's testimonial).
