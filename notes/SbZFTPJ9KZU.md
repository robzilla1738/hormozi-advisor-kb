# The 4 RAREST skills on earth that make BILLIONAIRES...

## Core thesis
Fortunes come from four scarce, high-value skills, one for each form of leverage, done at maximal scale and at no incremental cost: (1) raise and multiply money (capital); (2) capture and multiply attention (media); (3) return time to people (technology); (4) influence behavior at mass scale (labor / other people acting for you). The skills are degrees, not binary, and they compound. If you're broke, you're usually not spending your time on these. (Mentions acquisition.com is ~$85M/yr at recording.)

## Frameworks & models
- **Four scarce skills ↔ four leverages**:
  - Money: raise it from others AND allocate it to multiply faster than alternatives.
  - Attention: capture it AND multiply it organically at no incremental cost (Joe Rogan; Zuckerberg — people tell friends). Media = "the substrate through which impressions are conveyed."
  - Time: automate something lots of people spend time on, build once, save everyone time forever ("save everyone on earth 10 minutes a day and you'll be a billionaire"; Google). A friend's line: "all technology automates luxury," with luxury = convenience, less effort and sacrifice.
  - Influence: change beliefs → change the decision-making calculus → change all downstream behavior (religious conversion, Thomas Jefferson). Past a point, top persuaders aggregate power rather than money.
- **Two qualifiers**: assume maximal scale; measure mastery by degree.
- **Two components each**: acquire AND multiply. The key is multiplying at no incremental cost to you.
- **Compounding**: each skill compounds (money → more money; time saved → more users; attention → referrals → more attention; influence → people tell people).
- Naval Ravikant quote he cites: these skills "can't be taught but can be learned."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Allocate time toward whichever of the four you are working on; audit which ones you lack and how far you've mastered each.
- Prefer zero-incremental-cost delivery (often software) when trying to return time.

## Playbook steps
1. Audit your skills against the four buckets, by degree of mastery.
2. Pick the bucket(s) you're moving toward and spend time there.
3. For the time route: list what many people spend time on, then automate it once.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Spending time on low-value, non-scarce skills.
- Misusing "exponential" (it means compounding on itself).

## Examples / case details
- Joe Rogan and Zuckerberg (attention), Google (time), Jesus/prophets and Thomas Jefferson (influence).
