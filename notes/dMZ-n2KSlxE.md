## Core thesis
Build influence, not views. He defines influence as "high likelihood of compliance with requests." It comes from stacking four elements, SPCL (Status, Power, Credibility, Likeness), in your content. Then maximize time spent with prospects (long-form, and next, live interactive streams) and volume. "The content is the targeting," so make it for your avatar and set view expectations by the real size of your market. Recorded live (he says so).

## Frameworks & models
- **SPCL**:
  - **Status**: "someone who controls reinforcers in a given environment," meaning control of scarce resources people want. The bartender has status in the bar, not on the street. Status is a continuum, not a binary.
  - **Power** (his pick if only one): say-do correspondence. I say X, you do it, a good thing happens, and you're more likely to comply next time (10 stock picks that pop).
  - **Credibility**: third-party, observable proof (Guinness judges paid to verify the launch; a hard-to-fake $10M building behind him in ads).
  - **Likeness**: shared values or appearance. Leila's audience is ~54% female vs his ~89% male on the same topics. Just be you; don't play an archetype "NPC."
  - Influence is domain-specific (he would have little for fashion tips). One event can check several boxes (a $100M launch = money/status + credibility). Parents max all four, which is why they influence us so much.
- **Content application**: open by demonstrating status (stats), give actionable steps viewers can follow (power), use third-party proof (credibility), be yourself (likeness).
- **Reinforcement cycles per format**: two 1-hour videos = 120 minutes ≈ 480 fifteen-second shorts of exposure. Shorts serve to get someone to risk time on a long video.
- **Applause meter** (a UK vs US influencer soccer game, per a conversation with MrBeast): A-listers got near-zero applause; shorts creators a bit; long-form creators loud; live streamers made the arena erupt. "The internet will always move towards truth": rawer and less curated wins.
- **Media 3.0 = "live interactive."**
- **Volume**: Rogan, PBD and Ramsey put out hours a day. His 35,000 pieces/yr ≈ 100× a $1–2M business posting 365/yr, which he says brings ~100× the prospects. "Far more linear than you would expect." Roughly 1 in 10 shorts is a 2–3× outlier.
- **Social → interest media**: the algorithm reads your topic, background and clothes and shows content to people with matching interests. A viral grandma-slap video reaches humor fans, not buyers.
- **Market-size math** (US Census per him): ~9% own a business (~32–33M). 95% are under $1M; 5% are over $1M (~1.5M people); over $10M ≈ 1 in 250 (caption says "4%," likely 0.4% [inference]); over $100M ≈ 1 in 3,000. So 100K views on a $1M+ owner video is "crushing it."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Judge content by IRL responses from target customers (texts from respected owners, in-person asks), not views.
- Ask in-person buyers what content they like best and make more of it (Cash Cows-style breakdowns).
- If you sell services, you're an educator, not an entertainer: content should change behavior toward buying.
- Set view expectations against your addressable market, not MrBeast.
- A small following can be enough: two businesses he reviewed did $1M+/yr with under 5,000 followers.

## Playbook steps
1. Bake SPCL into every piece (status hook, actionable steps, third-party proof, authentic self). 2. Prioritize long-form and live to maximize minutes with prospects. 3. Use shorts as a bridge to longs. 4. Raise volume. 5. Make topics for the avatar's interests. 6. Measure with IRL feedback and market-adjusted expectations.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Chasing views with meme or entertainment content that shows no SPCL.
- Posting too little and trying to "outsmart" the volume requirement.
- Adopting a stock persona instead of your real quirks, and doing things without knowing why.
- Comparing view counts across different-sized markets.
