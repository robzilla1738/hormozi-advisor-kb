# It took me 36 years to realize what I'll tell you in 26 minutes…

## Core thesis
"Follow your passion" is a myth. Passio means suffering, so the job is to find a goal you love enough to suffer for. Suffering is a fixed cost on every path, and only the reward varies. Be passionate about the why and how, not the what, and don't quit just because the path hurts. "Success and failure are on the same path, failure is just an earlier exit." Mostly a mindset video with little tactical business content.

## Frameworks & models
- **Passion exists in the vague, not the specific**: if you succeed, ~95% of your day is supporting work, not the loved activity. The activity itself changes, and you adapt to what's left. The "passion window" only persists for employees or solopreneurs who don't scale.
- **Rarity makes love**: his monthly group of 10 entrepreneurs (avg ~$10M businesses) is his most expensive offer, and he loves it monthly but would hate it daily. Same with a favorite pizza place.
- **$10 rides**: hated, moderate, and swing-for-the-fences goals all cost the same suffering (regret on one end, difficulty on the other), so aim big.
- **Reframe, don't escape**: change your frame and perceptions, not your conditions. "Everything must be hard before it can be easy."
- **Why/how over what**: the why and how are internal and persistent. The what (hobbies) is external: "treats… moments… can't be requirements."
- **Why bigger than you**: self-serving goals get satisfied quickly, and "you'll only be able to overcome obstacles that are smaller than you."
- **Love = what you're willing to give up to maintain it**. Cites a study he describes: people's shock tolerance tripled when each shock spared a loved one.
- **Hedonic adaptation**: after a major life change, well-being returns to baseline, so the fixed cost stays the same. Change the reward or who you do it for.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Suffering in pursuit of something meaningful is not a signal you're on the wrong path. Don't quit because you aren't "loving every second."
- Don't delay starting while "waiting to find your passion." Find something people value and do it even though it sucks.
- No path has greener grass: "it's fertilized with shit" (former CEO Suzanne).
- Commit to not stopping. If you don't stop, you can't be called a failure.
- Spell out the worst case specifically (food is cheap, and hardship becomes steady state).

## Playbook steps
1. Pick a goal and reason bigger than yourself (family, provision, a cause).
2. Accept that most daily work won't be enjoyable, and treat enjoyable moments as treats.
3. When suffering prompts quitting, check: is it suffering on a meaningful path (keep going) or not?
4. Make the worst case concrete.
5. Keep going; don't explain stopping.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Quitting a job to go "all in," then quitting entrepreneurship because it isn't fun (the young man who prompted the video).
- Building a business around a hobby: he started a gym because he liked lifting and eating with friends, and its first year was the most miserable of his last 15.
- Believing a change of path will end the suffering.
- Self-serving goals once your needs are met. After his exit he had "no quest" and was miserable during a year off (Mexico).
