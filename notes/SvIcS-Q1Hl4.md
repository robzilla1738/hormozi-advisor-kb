## Core thesis
"Cash flow is oxygen." When a business is bleeding cash or stuck, prioritize opportunities and threats before strengths and weaknesses (an "OTSW" order instead of SWOT). Slow fixes like culture or retention systems won't save you if payroll is due in 30 days. The fastest cash always comes from selling more to existing customers. There's no time to build a new acquisition campaign. Pick the play with the highest impact for the fewest resources that fits how fast you need the money. Gym-owner framing; Alex says he's partly addressing his coaches.

## Frameworks & models
- **Opportunities/Threats first, then Strengths/Weaknesses**: on a call, first listen for cash lying on the ground and for massive threats. Things like more ads, working leads, or better sales sit in strengths/weaknesses.
- **Weakness vs threat**: bad retention is a weakness, but churning ~30%/month makes it a threat.
- **Opportunity cost**: choosing one play means saying no to others. A good coach's value is a bigger arsenal of proven plays.
- **Three fast-cash plays, all to existing customers:**
  1. **Paid-in-full / "reason why" campaign**: e.g. "I just turned 30, taking 3 people on a one-year transformation, $3,000, text me." ~3 of 100 take it → ~$9K. Limit the spots.
  2. **Internal play** (abs challenge, boot camp, etc.): (a) sell it at $100–200 for a ~20–25% take rate of the customer base, or (b) make it free (~85% take) and upsell food/supplements/services (he says 80–90% close on the upsell). Gives ~$10–20K in a month.
  3. **Hybrid: add a recurring tier** of 1:1 nutrition/accountability. Give all members 28 (or 21/14) days free, then offer to keep it for $49/week (~$200/mo), half the price new people pay.
- **Speed-matching rule**: need money tomorrow → paid-in-full text blast; can wait 2 weeks → internal play; can wait a month → hybrid recurring tier.
- **High impact, low resources** is the best kind of play.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If payroll is due within ~30 days, don't lead with retention systems (~12 weeks to pay off) or culture fixes. Do those after the cash plays.
- Don't sell every paid-in-full slot. Limit it.
- Hybrid math (his numbers): 150 members → ~85% take the free trial (~120) → assume half stay (60) × $200/mo = $12K/mo; 20% to a coach ($2,400) → ~$9,600/mo profit. One person can handle 60 clients.
- Annualized he says $144K top line / "120 bottom line." [inference] $9,600 × 12 ≈ $115K, so "120" is rounded.
- Once the cash is in, use it to fix the processes that caused the problem (e.g. pay someone to work on retention).

## Playbook steps
1. Check for threats (e.g. heavy churn) and the biggest opportunities sitting unused.
2. Decide how fast you need cash.
3. Run the matching play to existing customers: text-blast paid-in-full, internal challenge (paid or free + upsell), or free trial → recurring 1:1 tier.
4. Use the breathing room to fix the root causes (retention, culture, processes).

## Mistakes he warns against
- Prioritizing important-but-slow fixes (culture, retention systems) while bleeding cash.
- Getting overwhelmed by too many plays instead of picking the one that makes the most money for the least resources.
- New coaches defaulting to "more ads / work leads / sell better" when a faster opportunity exists.
- Starting a new marketing campaign when you need cash now. It takes too long.

## Examples / case details
- If a gym doesn't have a 1:1 nutrition accountability add-on, he calls it the biggest opportunity: ~$15K/month in 28 days with no ad spend.
- Prestige Labs: gym owners can sell supplements without touching inventory, e.g. buy at $5, sell at $20.
- Paid internal challenge: 150 members × ~25% ≈ 35 people × $150 ≈ $5K, but it adds fulfillment work.
- A daily check-in text (weight, showing up) also pushes churn down.
- He considered doing a monthly 30-minute call with every follower and worked out it would still take 3 months, so he teaches the lessons from calls instead.
