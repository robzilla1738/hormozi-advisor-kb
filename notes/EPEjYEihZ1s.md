# Warren Buffett's #1 Rule for Business Owners

## Core thesis
The #1 metric is **net free cash flow**, Buffett/Munger "owner earnings": what an owner can take out after the business reinvests enough to stay ahead and keep its competitive advantage. Most owners either make no profit while calling it "reinvesting," or reinvest everything chasing a mythical exit (99% never sell). Get paid to grow. Take profit out monthly above a set cash floor, and reinvest only where return on capital beats the S&P.

## Frameworks & models
- **Net free cash flow / owner earnings**: profit in excess of what's required to run and grow. He reports it for all entities.
- **Three owner categories**: (1) no profit, "reinvesting" is self-deception; (2) profitable but reinvests it all, ending up asset-rich and cash-poor; (3) takes cash out while growing (his preference).
- **Capex-heavy businesses are worse**: Buffett and Munger dislike businesses that must pour profit back in, like manufacturing.
- **Magic Formula** (*The Little Book That Beats the Market*): low P/E (cheap) plus high return on capital (quality). He says it beat the S&P ~25 years. About half the picks tank and the rest are mispriced bets that outperform.
- **Return on capital**: $1M for one location earning $100K/yr is 10% ROC. $1M for 10 locations earning $100K each is 100% ROC.
- **Operational risk**: your business may be outlawed, blown up or forced to move. "Tomorrow may never come," so bank cash monthly.
- **Cash sweep line**: set a non-negotiable cash floor based on projected fixed costs (e.g. $100K or $500K). Sweep everything above it out every month.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If reinvesting returns about 10%, just put it in the S&P. Putting $500K–$1M a year into the S&P makes you "really really wealthy."
- Feel the monthly deposit in your personal account ("better or worse than last month"), not just numbers sitting in the business account.
- Don't grow faster than needed for arbitrary or projected expectations; irresponsible growth adds waste, risk and mistakes.
- Reward yourself for the risk you take.

## Playbook steps
1. Calculate net free cash flow for each entity and put it in finance reporting. 2. Set the cash floor. 3. Sweep the excess to your personal account monthly. 4. Rank reinvestment options by return on capital and compare against index returns. 5. Build wealth outside the business too.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Opening another location every time you make money. He did this with his gyms: equipment, leases, signage, glass walls, and no cash.
- Banking on an exit: the chips are stacked against you.
- Calling losses "reinvestment."

## Examples / case details
- Sold six gym locations as assets ("garage sales") for about $200K all-in after partnerships. Over 4–5 years that's about $40K/yr. He then lost it all in a failed venture with a partner before Gym Launch and promised himself never again.
- Told gym employees who wanted a raise: imagine working all month and, instead of being paid, having your savings cleaned out. "That happens to me all the time."
- Applied the cash-sweep line with an entrepreneur in a company he has equity in.
