# This Business Was Stuck. Here's How I Fixed It.
id: 4GQLJjH9-oA / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4GQLJjH9-oA / duration: 1947s (~32 min) / tags: 05-sales-process, 09-marketing-content-ads, 04-lead-gen-channel, 07-hiring-management
Signal: high

## Core thesis
A portfolio company running a webinar funnel had plateaued at ~$570K a month for about 6 months. It passed a $10M run rate (~$860K a month) within 4 months. The offer, channel and team stayed the same; they fixed each leak in the funnel in order: show rate → booking rate → close rate. The lessons: look at the data to find the drop-offs, fix the front (the frame), execution beats the plan, measurement is itself an intervention, making something better is lower-risk than doing something new, and you get zero benefit from what you don't communicate.

## Frameworks & models
- **Show-rate stack** (18% → 24%, a 33% lift; he wants 25%+): (1) a 9-email sequence written to be more benefit-driven and simpler, hitting different angles (pain, speed, ease, being stuck/plateaued); (2) SMS (95–99% open rate), which he thinks was the biggest lift; (3) behavior-triggered emails (open or click → exclusive behind-the-scenes content); (4) voicemail drops, plus video for people who engaged. Alternate pain and proof. The **key reminders**: 24 hours before, the morning of, and 1 hour before, all of them by SMS. One reminder a day is fine. CTAs in the emails keep engagement up and protect deliverability.
- **Show rate ≈ close rate**: a 33% gain in show rate has the same effect as a 33% gain in close rate, but it's easier, can be automated, and is one-time work.
- **Webinar booking stack** (+33% booking rate): study the drop-offs and engagement spikes; obsess over the first 5 minutes and first 60 seconds. (1) A faster intro using **proof → promise → plan**. (2) Say it's live (date, time, weather, current events). (3) A takeaway through explicit qualifications for who it's not for. (4) Honest scarcity: name your real limit ("we only have N slots; we typically book out"), first come, first served. Add scarcity "on steroids": ask people to say in chat when they book, call out names as they book ("Rosie booked"), and announce "sold out" by day or half-day.
- **Close rate** (14% → 35%), four fixes:
  1. **Frame**: cut the small talk. Use: "I'm X, background in ABC, I work closely with [the edified person from the webinar]. What resonated most?" → "The purpose of this call is to see if you qualify for our guarantee (or bonus, or start date); not everyone does," based on 3 factors (BANT-style). That puts the prospect in the position of earning your favor.
  2. **Discovery**: stay chunked up at a high level and avoid the detail traps. You ask, they answer. "Stop getting cute": reps who start playing consultant lose control of the call.
  3. **Pitch**: a three-pillar pitch instead of a feature stack. The **DIY/DWY vs DFY rule**: for do-it-yourself or done-with-you offers, make the process look simple (1-2-3). For done-for-you, show many steps (11, 27) so they believe it works but don't want to do the work themselves. He sees done-with-you as a DIY sale plus accountability. If you have six steps, group them into three. Before pitching, get them to invite you: "...I think we can help; where do you want to go from here?"
  4. **Execution**: role-play; granular KPIs per closer (show %, offer-made %, losses by objection); personalized coaching; give the best leads to the best closers; cut a below-KPI closer's calendar in half, then give them 1–2 weeks to prove themselves or they're out; pay commission on cash collected so paid-in-full is rewarded (on a $10K sale, 10% of a first $2K installment = $200 vs $1,000 for paid in full, 5× more).
- **Congruence / baton pass**: the ad, webinar headline, webinar opening lines, sales call and onboarding all match each other.
- **Delegation vs abdication**: when you delegate, stats stay the same or go up; if stats drop after a handoff, you abdicated. Stay side by side with a checklist until they get it. Know "where the bodies are buried" in every department.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Webinar show rate: below 25% is poor (18% was "really not good"). A 14% close rate after a webinar is "bad."
- Fix the biggest leak first. Don't work on stages that aren't constraints (call show rate was already high, so they left it).
- If an automated webinar or VSL won't convert, go live more often for a while to get the reps in.
- "Typically books out" is fair to say if it happens more than half the time.
- Not everyone is worth training to sell; prioritize the best closers.
- Better before new: improving what already works is the better risk-adjusted move. "Use what you've got."
- If you hate looking at a stat, look at it twice.

## Playbook steps
1. Map the funnel (CTR → opt-in → schedule → show → close) and measure each step. 2. Fix the show rate with multi-channel reminders. 3. Fix the webinar opening plus live cues, qualifications and scarcity. 4. Fix the call frame → chunked-up discovery → invited three-pillar pitch. 5. Role-play, add granular KPIs, give the best leads to the best closers, pay commission on cash collected. 6. Communicate every real constraint. 7. Then add an upsell (covered in another video).

## Mistakes he warns against
- One webinar a week, leaving days between booking and attending.
- Meandering intros (people can lose ~40% of the audience in the first 2 minutes).
- A feature-stack pitch that overwhelms the prospect.
- Founders who drift away from revenue-generating work too early.
- Paying closers the same whether they close or not.
- Having real scarcity, a deadline or a live event and not telling people.

## Examples / case details
- Booked calls went from 58 to 102 per webinar on the same ad spend (~76–77%). Close rate went from 14% to 35% (he says about 2.4×; [inference] 35/14 = 2.5×). CAC went from $5,763 to $2,868. ROAS went from 1.62 to 3.96. Monthly revenue went from ~$570K to $860K (the caption reads "5 169,000," most likely ~$570K per the opening) with a 30.7% profit margin, which he puts at ~$250–300K a month in profit.
- Another portfolio company rescripted the first 5 minutes of a 3-hour presentation and got +20% in close rate.
- He re-shot this video's intro 5 times.
- He claims that at a 10% margin, a 10–20% revenue increase can grow profit up to 5×. [inference: that is only true if most of the new revenue drops to profit; treat it as directional.]
