## Core thesis
Confidence should come from proof you can't refute, not from "fake it till you make it." Build up a record of facts about what you've done. Carry confidence from one domain into a new one by finding similar past wins. Treat character traits as skills you train through repetition. Give your own evidence-based opinion more weight than the imagined voices of critics. Conversational format: Alex answers an interviewer.

## Frameworks & models
- **Proof-stacking identity**: "stack these bricks that no one could refute." State facts (e.g., "I've done this many sales") instead of claiming to be good, which makes a "house of an identity" grounded in reality.
- **Chunk up / chunk down transfer**: for a new skill, find a past situation that's similar in nature, generalize it, then apply it to the new one. Example: asking girls on dates → "approach strangers when there's high stakes" → sales. Repeated transfers lead to "I am good at acquiring skills quickly."
- **Domain-specific vs general confidence**: someone confident at Call of Duty can reason, "5 hrs/day for 3 years made me good; the same input will make me good at this."
- **Traits are skills**: "everything is human behavior in response to conditions." Repeat the response to the same stimulus until "in these situations I tend to act this way" is true.
- **Critic-voice audit**: (a) does that person really think that, and do I care? (b) do I want to give them the same power over my life that I give myself? It's usually 1–3 voices replayed in your head.
- **85-year-old self frame**: ask what that version of you would think. Also: "the person with the longest time horizon wins."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- When criticized: if there's truth in it, fix it; if there isn't, learn nothing from it (avoid "the wrong learning").
- Your opinion of you should carry the highest weight because you have the most evidence.
- Scaling goals: extrapolate from proof (did X → doing it ~5× longer makes the bigger goal a "reasonable bet").

## Playbook steps
1. List irrefutable facts or counts of the work the person you want to become does. 2. For a new domain, chunk up from a similar past win and apply it down to the new domain. 3. Put yourself in the situation repeatedly to train the response. 4. Remind yourself of the evidence, not affirmations. 5. Audit the critic voices in your head and defuse them. 6. Consult your 85-year-old self.

## Mistakes he warns against
- "Fake it till you make it": confidence without proof.
- Believing "I haven't done anything yet." Everyone has transferable experience.
- Seeking outside approval and letting imagined third parties direct your life.
- Optimizing for others' opinions on trivial things like clothing.
