# Behind the Scenes of The $100M Leads Launch [Ep 006]

## Core thesis
A behind-the-scenes documentary of the $100M Leads launch event. The launch was designed so the book's promotion would itself demonstrate the lead-gen methods (warm outreach, affiliates, paid ads), the same way $100M Offers was "an offer so good people would feel stupid saying no." He built a ~17-minute value stack, then gave it away free. He gave up a short-term payday (he cites $50M) to build brand association and trust on a long time horizon. Mostly vlog and narration, so it is light on teachable frameworks. Other voices (team, Leila, Trevor references) appear briefly.

## Frameworks & models
- **Meta example**: the product demonstrates its own concept. Offers was an irresistible offer; Leads was promoted using every lead method in the book, even though one post to his audience would have sold plenty.
- **Meeting filter**: "What problem are we solving?" and "Who's required to solve it?" If the decision is already known, he isn't needed. "The most expensive cost in almost all companies is meetings" (an hour-long meeting ended in 28 minutes).
- **Presentation craft**: one idea per slide paired with a visual (from the "Death by PowerPoint" TED talk plus sales/marketing practice). Compress many hours into high value-per-second, like a stand-up routine built from 30-second bits.
- **The stack**: a direct sales tactic proven across industries. Stack value, anchor down through prices he won't charge ($12,076 → $4,997 → $2,997), pause, then reveal the price (free). Suspense "touches the bottom of the pool," which makes it memorable ("villain to hero in 10 seconds").
- **One-of-one → one-of-zero content**: say and show things only you can; then do what no one else will do.
- **Brand = associations**: he wanted to be associated with a historic event and a free product more valuable than others' paid ones. "If you measure on a one-year time horizon then it will never make sense."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Prepare relentlessly ("men of talent and men of preparation; I prefer to be the latter"); he prepped for two weeks, all day.
- Own your stuff: self-publish, front the printing, house and ship it yourself; a traditional publisher owns your rights and wouldn't allow a $1.99 book or free chapters.
- He sold on Shopify because Amazon typically caps a new book at 5K–10K hardbacks.

## Playbook steps
[inference] Launch pattern: 1. Build the product to embody its own lesson. 2. Promote using multiple channels (warm outreach, affiliates, paid ads, community watch parties). 3. Rehearse a one-idea-per-slide presentation. 4. Build a long value stack with price anchors. 5. Over-deliver (free) to earn trust and brand association. 6. Have backup streams for infrastructure overload.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Underestimating load: 562K link clicks in ~60 seconds broke systems despite simulators. ~140K couldn't get in; the team pivoted to a YouTube stream (~40–50K recovered, with a 3-minute delay). Zoom rooms were misconfigured so attendees could unmute.
- Judging long-term brand moves on short-term ROI.

## Examples / case details
- Over 500K registered; 100K+ at check-in.
- Bonuses: Lead Magnet Mastery, First Five Clients framework, Referral playbook, Affiliate playbook, $0–$100M scaling roadmap. Stated total value $1,276 (later anchored as $12,076; captions inconsistent).
- About 100K books sold on day one, vs Offers' ~500K copies over two years. He thinks Leads sells more because more people perceive they lack leads than lack an offer.
- Afterward his name trended on Twitter, the audiobook hit #7 on all of Amazon, and the podcast went from #50 to #26 overall.
- Trevor co-wrote the books. His frame during tough edits: "there's a 17 year old who's going to sleep with this book under his pillow… you owe it to him."
