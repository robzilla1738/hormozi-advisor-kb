# Building a $3,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 57 Mins

Signal: high

## Core thesis
Live teardown of Ben's Unfiltered Hospitality, a cocktail/bar-program consultancy: 2024 $1.1M revenue, $123K net (~11.3%), CAC ~$11.8K, LTV:CAC 1.7:1, ~$10K/mo ad spend, goal $3M. It's trying to sell a $30K offer to cold strangers off a straight call funnel. "The right offer at the wrong time is still the wrong offer." The fix is a better avatar callout, a lead magnet, and a cleaner funnel with a VSL before the call. Add a fast, easy ~$5,800 front-end (pricing audit) that ROIs immediately, then ascend customers into the bigger program/retainer. Fix the churn on the retainer before pouring in more leads.

## Frameworks & models
- **$100M scaling roadmap (10 stages)**: Ben is at stage 4, "prioritize": saying yes to every avatar and getting unqualified leads. The fix is to specialize the product, make better free stuff and creative, and add qualifications/friction. (Promotes acquisition.com/roadmap.)
- **Ad copy (Leads book p.136)**: the callout is by far most important ("$4M+/yr restaurant owners"); once it's right, "80% of the work of advertising is actually done." Then a pain-based hook, a yes question, an if-then, or a ridiculous result. **What/who/when** framework explodes the value equation (dream outcome, fast, likely, easy, plus the nightmare/pain) into many copy variations.
- **Pain articulation**: describe their pain more precisely than they can and they'll believe you can solve it, "almost better than any promise." Pain is also compliant. Vary success descriptions for different drivers (profit vs recognition/being cool).
- **Benefits/moments over features**: don't say "data-driven menus" (sounds like work). Say the moment ("no more disaster back bar"; guests opening the menu wanting to try many drinks). Put the benefit as the headline and the number as the subtext ("get rid of waiting lines" / "50% boost in speed of service"). "Predigest the food."
- **Three lead magnet types**: reveal a problem, a sample/trial of the solution, or one step of a multi-step process. Moves people from low trust to high trust before the sale (Leads p.30: an offer/lead-magnet mismatch is going straight from low trust to sale).
- **Friction sweet spot**: good friction = qualifications; bad friction = re-entering info, slow loads. "The bigger the plane, the longer the runway."
- **Funnel**: page 1 = headline, sub-headline (= the biggest concern about the headline, often "without X, Y, Z"), image, opt-in. Page 2 = VSL (Vimeo, not YouTube) above an integrated scheduler. Post-booking = call-prep video covering top concerns plus lead magnet delivery. Remove the logo above the fold on cold traffic. Add disclosures for results claims.
- **Front-end/back-end logic**: depends on how much information the buyer needs and on cash flow. A front end pulls cash forward / shortens payback. Brand-based inbound can go straight to the expensive offer; cold traffic needs a small first jump.
- **Rollover upsell**: credit the $5,800 toward the retainer if they buy within the window. He prefers spreading the discount across quarters (e.g. $10K/qtr → $8.5K for four quarters) over a one-time credit (9K → 4K first quarter), to avoid "an easy yes then cancel."
- **Waived-fee offer**: "I charge $30K for this; commit for the year and I'll waive it; cancel at two quarters and you pay it." This becomes an A/B close against quarter-to-quarter.
- **Ultra-high ticket**: offer the founder doing it personally at ~5× price, presented before the team option.
- **Upsell timing**: three shots within the ~30-day window. Milestone moment (first weekend after the price change), the next week, then last chance. "I never want to ask you for money I haven't already made you."
- **Founder sells first**: highest conviction, fastest feedback on avatar/price/copy, no commission. Keep taking calls until you can't.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- CAC for this small-business restaurant buyer should be ~$1,500–2,500 (fine up to ~$3,500), so cut current CAC by at least 2/3.
- Under ~20% upgrading from core to upsell = "ding ding," an offer issue on the back end and/or front end.
- Target ≥50% of $5,800 buyers upgrading to the back end.
- Lead magnet landing page benchmark: ~30% opt-in (Ben ~7%).
- Ad testing budget: about 2× allowable CAC per campaign. Specifically, let an ad spend 2× the 30-day cash/profit from a customer before shutting it off, as long as it's getting leads. Once messaging works, make ~10 similar variations and scale while you can still take the calls.
- Test lead magnets head-to-head (don't stack them) and track all the way to LTV, not opt-ins. Example: a $97 vs $47 product converted about the same, but $97 buyers were far more likely to spend more later.
- Churn after ~2 quarters on the retainer is a warning. "If you can't fix churn, there's no point in doing any of this… why would we pour more into a leaky bucket?" Fix by showing value: log into their systems and present their numbers monthly (~2 hrs/account is worth it). Look for consumables (constant staff turnover → ongoing training). Otherwise accept the one-time nature and price for it. Graduation-style businesses are fine, with less enterprise value.
- Prospects are ~10× less likely to buy than customers, so make them customers fast.
- Walk through your own funnel on a regular cadence.

## Playbook steps
1 & 3 together: redo the funnel (3-step, Vimeo VSL above an integrated scheduler) plus a new lead magnet (e.g. "top 10 worst drinks to serve and what to replace them with" vs a profit-calculator tool, split test). 2: ad callouts ($4M+ restaurants, pain hooks). 4, 6, 7 together: sales process. Setter tells them to watch the video ("first question he'll ask is did you watch it"). Agenda-set the call. One-call close on the $5,800 pricing audit. Ascension via rollover plus the waived $30K offer. Then tell the ads team to spend more.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Selling a $30K offer cold off a call funnel, arm-wrestling people across 3+ calls.
- Feature-heavy copy; your logo/brand name in prime ad or landing real estate on cold traffic.
- Making leads enter info twice; never testing your own funnel.
- Spending too little to get enough volume at a high ticket.
- Positioning a cheaper offer as "DIY" (low uptake). Position it as a narrower done-for-you thing instead.
- Passion-project owners resist obvious business decisions, which is one reason to move up-market.

## Examples / case details
- Ben's ex-investment banker → bartender → bar owner path; consultancy founded in 2020. Phase 1 "better cocktail consulting," 90 days, $30K. Phase 2 retainer ~$9K/quarter with weekly KPI calls, seasonal menus, and quarterly 2-day on-site training. Fewer than 20% upgrade; retainer clients usually stop after two quarters; the team wasn't filling out the scorecards.
- Front end: a "profit/pricing booster" audit. Recalculate last year's profit at the new prices ("you would have made $100K"); the only cost to them is reprinting menus.
- Projections (no guarantee): the funnel, callout and lead magnet could triple lead volume. Closing at 30–50% (owner-operators sometimes ~80%) vs today's rate (caption shows "close rate 66" early and "6% close rate" later; [inference] 6% is likely the real figure) could on its own beat current revenue.
