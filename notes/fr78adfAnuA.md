## Core thesis
AI is today's internet. You don't need to become an AI business, but you must use AI in your business to make it better, cheaper, faster and less risky. Don't advertise that you use it, because customers only care about outcomes. The owner should do the automating personally: "cloud to dirt" knowledge (strategy down to API plumbing) lets you see possibilities a tech hire won't. Human psychology and persuasion don't change, so proof still matters, especially in B2B. He sees about an 18-month window of outsized opportunity. Caption note: the opening says "over $250,000 in aggregate revenue," almost certainly a caption error, since other videos cite a much larger portfolio [inference]. The video includes an ad for the acquisition.com/roadmap lead magnet.

## Frameworks & models
- **AI is like the internet**: every business is on the internet without being an internet business. Being early ("tech forward," like having a webpage once was) earned disproportionate returns.
- **Cloud-to-dirt knowledge**: vertically integrated knowledge, from high-level strategy and people down to connecting APIs. The owner knows the business best, so the owner sees which combinations are valuable. Tech people default to "commoditized automations."
- **Apples-to-apples test**: compare AI against your human process only after giving the AI the same training and SOP effort. Don't compare a half-built AI to a process optimized over 6–20 years ("John Henry wins again").
- **AI makes what you already do easier and faster. It doesn't remove the need to do it**: you still need a working sales process. Would you have a human call every single opt-in?
- **Intuition is pattern recognition**: strip the "magic" out of your workflow and describe the observable behaviors so they can be automated.
- **Better / cheaper / faster / risk-free**: the four outcomes customers care about.
- **Proof problem**: AI content and avatars lack proof. That matters less for consumers (a beautiful avatar is its own proof for skincare) and still matters for B2B.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Don't advertise AI use. "No one cares."
- You should automate your own workflow rather than someone else, because it frees you for more valuable work.
- Dedicate a fixed time block and automate one portion of your day end to end ("click to close"). That is his "only ask."
- An AI setter is not drag-and-drop between businesses. Treat it like moving a sales rep to a new company: it will be bad at first.
- "You are not too busy to do this."

## Playbook steps
1. Find a YouTube tutorial for the specific automation you want. 2. Paste the link or transcript into an AI: "I want help building this." 3. Follow the steps, and when stuck, screenshot and ask "what do I do now?" Repeat. 4. Describe your workflow as observable behaviors. 5. Train the AI as thoroughly as you trained humans before judging it. 6. Expand across departments.
- Content idea pipeline example: a bot reads your calendar and transcribed calls, then picks the week's 5 most interesting situations, which become true stories ("do epic shit, talk about the epic shit you did").

## Mistakes he warns against
- Thinking you must become an AI or tech company.
- Assuming "the tech nerds" will do it for you.
- Putting an AI setter on top of a broken sales process, or having it call every opt-in.
- Romanticizing your specialized knowledge as unautomatable.

## Examples / case details
- PayPal cut fraud losses by ~$700M in a year with AI pattern detection. JP Morgan's COiN saved ~350K lawyer hours (12K credit agreements in seconds). Klarna (caption: "Clara") replaced 700 support agents and saved ~$40M in a year. Figures as stated in the video.
- His book launch: 5 AI agents handled ~120K support tickets and resolved 90% with no human.
- An AI SDR matching their human team.
- Marketing: AI for ideas, scripting/packaging, auto thumbnail tests that learn winners, and trend research crossed with brand. "Self-licking ice cream cone": yesterday's content gets a CTA overlay and launches daily as an ad. A Skool wins tab feeds 6 proven static templates as ads for Vantage (his $1M+ community).
- Sales: lead enrichment, personalized voice notes and images by DM, AI-to-AI scheduling handoff, much faster speed to contact.
- Legal: one general counsel running agents for first and second responses, cease and desists, etc., instead of ~100 paralegals.
