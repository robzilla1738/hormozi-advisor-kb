## Core thesis
He and Leila screen 10–20 service, info, e-learning and coaching businesses a week with a three-part "opportunity vehicle" test: (1) how many units could potentially be sold, (2) profit per unit (value delivered vs cost to deliver), (3) competitive and supply-demand dynamics. Use the same test to reconfigure your own business toward a bigger opportunity. TAM matters for the next 3–5 years but understates long-run potential because founders' visions expand.

## Frameworks & models
- **Opportunity vehicle checklist**: units (durable demand, e.g. weight loss, since food won't stop tasting good), gross profit per unit, and competitive dynamics (how new the space is, whether incumbents would price you out).
- **Two of three isn't enough**: cell phone service has huge demand and pennies of marginal cost, but you'd have to beat Verizon and AT&T. A hypothetical crypto-mining rig earning $50K/yr for ~$500 in a brand-new space checks all three.
- **Biggest companies sell something very valuable that costs almost nothing at the margin**: Google, Facebook ads, telecom, software. Info/education businesses have software-like margins without the development cost, but higher churn.
- **One-time vs ongoing consumables**: information is consumed once (the skill gets learned). Community, accountability, and the new problems each solution creates are consumed repeatedly. Pull those out to raise LTV and stickiness.
- **"Problems are blank checks"** waiting to be cashed.
- **TAM critique**: Facebook started with a college-yearbook TAM. Vision, adjacent silos and M&A expand the market. A business dies or sells when the founder runs out of vision.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Service businesses: **80%+ gross margin minimum**. At $1,000/mo, delivery must cost under $200. If you target 50% net margin, the remaining 30% ($300) covers marketing, admin, payroll, HR, finance and legal.
- "If you want to help more people, you need gross profit in order to scale." Charging more isn't bad.
- Don't change avatars too early. You can probably scale one avatar to ~$100M/yr. Evaluate TAM over a 3–5-year window. Expanding from $100K to $200K by adding a silo "was dumb."

## Playbook steps
1. Can this serve more people? 2. Can the outcome be made more valuable or cheaper to deliver (raise gross profit per unit)? 3. Is there a newer or less contested space, or a way to redefine yours as unique? 4. Add ongoing consumables (community, accountability, next-problem solutions) to fight churn.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Valuable but costly-to-deliver models with thin margin.
- Low pricing out of guilt.
- Entering markets dominated by giants.
- Adding avatars prematurely.
