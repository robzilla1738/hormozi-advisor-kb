## Core thesis
Most businesses aren't sellable for real money; they're asset sales at 1–3×. To get an institutional exit ($5M+ deals; he cites a median ~6.7× trailing-12-month multiple for institutional buyers, admitting he can't recall the source), first remove the "deal killers", then run a banker-led process (prep → market → close) that takes ~12–18 months from start to cash. Price is not proceeds: terms (earn-outs, seller financing, rollover, escrow, working capital, indemnities, non-competes) decide what you actually keep, and leverage comes from being willing to walk away. Caption has transcription errors (e.g. "alex ramsey", "dildos process" = diligence process).

## Frameworks & models
- **Process phases**: Phase 0 prerequisites → find banker/broker → Phase 1 prep → Phase 2 marketing → Phase 3 close.
- **Deal killers (prerequisites)**: too small; customer concentration (esp. relationships tied to you); no defined, repeatable sales/acquisition process; too few buyers contacted; wrong timing (selling too late, during decline); overvaluing it; not growing / small TAM / no growth story; key-man risk; single-channel dependence. Have a good answer to "why are you selling?"
- **Enterprise value = future sales discounted by risk** (how likely they are to happen). Show lots of future sales with high likelihood.
- **Buyers buy what it will become**: you need a believable 10× growth story.
- **Age adds value**: even flat, more operating years = less risk ("always goes up in value except when it doesn't" — quoting Frasier Preston of APG); but decline makes it a "falling knife".
- **Banker vs broker**: bankers for institutional (~$5M+); brokers under ~$5M.
- **Funnel numbers**: ~500 buyers contacted → ~98 NDAs → ~14–15 IOIs → ~half do management presentations → 2–4 LOIs → bid against each other → 1 signed exclusive LOI → diligence → close.
- **Timeline**: ~12 weeks to find bankers + ~20 weeks prep/marketing + 60–90 days diligence; plan for ~12–18 months to cash.
- **Buyer types**: financial (PE/family office — price-focused, more numerous) vs strategic (pays the best multiple because they extract more value; fewer). He's sold to PE/family offices, strategics, friends, competitors, customers, partners.
- **Headline vs take-home example**: $100M "sale" − $25M earn-out − $25M seller financing − $20M rolled = $30M cash → team/equity splits → ~$24M → tax ~30% → fees/escrow → ~$17–18M.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Contact hundreds of buyers ("talked to 10 people… got to talk to 500"); push lazy bankers to send more and follow up.
- Choose bankers who recently sold businesses like yours in market AND size; meet ~4–6 bankers.
- Banker fees: negotiate out/down upfronts and breakup fees; set minimums (no fee below X); pay them on cash you receive, not total enterprise value (selling 10% at $1B EV with 4% of EV = $40M of your $100M).
- Write down the number you'd accept, and why, in a letter to yourself before talking to bankers.
- Say "if I don't get this, the deal is off" on key points; institutional buyers say no to everything unless you'll walk.
- Ignore speed as a bargaining chip ("30-day close" took 112 days); slow close favors the buyer; only lever is keep growing hard during the process.
- Escrow under ~10% is good; keep working capital left in the business as low as possible (dividend out excess cash beforehand if you're small and not reinvesting).
- Negotiate hard on indemnities beyond fundamental reps (ownership, trademarks); narrow non-compete definitions; know your next move and say it plainly.
- Anything you relied on in selling must be in the schedules, or "it's as though it didn't happen."
- Consider taking ~10% less from people you and your team like.
- He stated upfront: full leadership exit, no transition, no earn-out, no seller financing ("if I keep working two years I might as well own it").

## Playbook steps
1. Fix deal killers over years (de-key-man, multiple channels, diversified customers, sustained growth for 12–18 months before sale). 2. Network for intros to relevant banks. 3. Send one-page teaser → call → sell them on selling your business → in-person pitches; negotiate fees. 4. Prep: audited financials/quality of earnings, CIM (100+ page memo), buyer list, NDA. 5. Market: blind teaser → NDA → data room → pitch deck/management presentations → IOIs. 6. Final offers/LOIs, bid against each other, weigh price AND terms, fit, people. 7. Exclusive LOI → diligence (QoE, legal/compliance, customer + employee interviews, background checks, employment contracts). 8. Purchase agreement: indemnities, non-compete, schedules, definitions, working capital, escrow. 9. Close via escrow; payouts come off the top in predefined order.

## Mistakes he warns against
- "I'll be ready in 18 months"; selling too late.
- Believing newsworthy 20× multiples are normal.
- Trusting bankers to get the best deal — like real estate agents, they want *a* deal; also they over-hype valuations.
- Getting greedy after LOIs; letting emotion drive.
- Non-compliance (an acquaintance had two nine-figure deals blow up).
- Rolled equity can extend non-competes via the new entity's articles.
- Bragging about headline price that's mostly paper.

## Examples / case details
- Sold Allen software ($12M TTM revenue, price under NDA); two companies in 2021 for $46.2M (sold 66% to APG); five brick-and-mortar locations; one shut down.
- APG spent ~$1M on diligence and (his rough guess) ~$250K researching customers.
- Final acquirer came from a buyer his CFO pushed bankers to re-contact.
- Redefining one term changed the non-compete's meaning in his favor.
- Attorneys spent ~6 hours signing documents on his behalf; payday is anticlimactic. Video ends with the actual closing call (Leila present).
