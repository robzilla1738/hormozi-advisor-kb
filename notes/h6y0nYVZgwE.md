## Core thesis
Most small businesses aren't growing because the owner isn't putting enough into the one input that makes money: letting people know the business exists. Find your single constraint with a simple chain (advertising → schedule/show/close → price → churn). Then spend the first 4 hours of every day on that constraint, and buy the time back by paying vendors for "human stuff" (food, cleaning, laundry). Also fire "Susan," the mediocre employee who lowers your standards. "What you're not doing is obvious"; the real problem is why you're not doing it.

## Frameworks & models
- **Inputs → efficiencies → output**: the first input is that people know you exist, through the core four: outreach, content, paid ads, and referral partners/affiliates.
- **Two ways to make more money**: get more customers (traffic × conversion) or make them worth more (raise price, cut churn).
- **Diagnostic chain**: not growing → not advertising enough. Advertising but not converting → find which of schedule, show or close is below benchmark. Full and closing but no profit → mispriced. High price but everyone leaves → make the product better.
- **Content for local businesses**: platform algorithms are global, so content's job is to raise conversion, not acquire customers. Prospects check whether you look legit, friendly and close by. With zero content, one post a week is a high-ROI "heartbeat." If content is your main channel, start at 2/day (Alex's team posts ~400/week).
- **Brand is built 3 ways**: what you say, what others say (both "approximations," i.e. advertising), and what people experience (the product). Deliver on the promise and customers become the ones who talk.
- **Ad creative system**: save every ad you like into an album. Before making ads, review your all-time winners plus the week's new finds and merge elements. Remake winners verbatim, Google any effect you want to learn, run them, and repeat daily.
- **Questions beat statements in sales**: "No one can oppose you if you make no statements"; "whoever answers questions in the sale is the one who's losing." Turn objection questions around: "What certifications do you have?" → "Which one are you looking for?"
- **Reverse-engineer the goal**: find activities per customer, then scale volume. Content: 30 posts → 5 customers = 6 posts per customer, so 25 customers needs 150 posts/month = 5/day. Outreach: 100 reach-outs → 1 customer. At 10 customers, that's 1,000/month, which he calls ~30/day (really ~33). For 50 customers, 5×, he says ~150/day (5,000/month ≈ 167/day) [arithmetic note]. More volume also makes you better: hot sales weeks vs rusty dead weeks.
- **One constraint**: every business has one constraint at a time. Clear it and the business grows until it hits the next one. So the conference "sticky note" should hold only the right three things.
- **Buy back 96 hours**: food, cleaning and laundry take ~96 hrs/month (his remembered stats; he's unsure whether some are weekly). At ~$1,500/month to outsource them, that's ~$15.6/hr. It's worth it if your active work earns more than ~$15/hr, but only if the hours go to the constraint. You'd happily pay $1,500 to clone your best employee, so do it for yourself. "You can only outsource your food once in your career."
- **Double time**: spend half the day on tomorrow's job and half on today's.
- **Reward planes landing, not just crashes**: praise the behavior you want, or people stop doing it (the dishes analogy).
- **Business = reallocating time, money and energy**: you can train anyone to do anything (even neurosurgery); the question is whether it's worth the cost. Turning a 7 into a 10 is faster than rehabbing Susan.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Spend 4 hrs/day, 6 days a week, on the primary acquisition input, or on whatever is the biggest problem until it's solved, then switch. Do it before the team arrives or after they leave. Fires get put out anyway, and many go away if ignored.
- Under ~$100K/month, you mostly just need more people to know you exist.
- Ad spend is a fixed daily commitment: "you spend this money before you eat."
- To get more referral partners (chiropractors, doctors), ask, give gifts, and "send them business first."
- If you're full and not profitable, raise the price: email or send a letter announcing the new price going forward.
- Churn: run exit interviews, which can save ~half by moving people to a higher-support tier. Touch base every ~2 weeks. Track attendance; dropping from 3×/wk to 2 to 1 means intervene ("I missed you"). Over-communicate in the first 24–72 hrs of onboarding (where, when, who welcomes them, reconfirm), which he says adds 2–5 more months of purchases.
- Fire Susan within 30 days. What you see is her best behavior, she sets your standard, and she repels good people. Taking over her work (~20 real hours that you pay 40 for) will likely take you ~5 and expose systems to fix.
- Brick-and-mortar chains: he has never seen one scale without an operator on a profit share, usually 10–20%, earning ~$75–100K/yr. Get the unit dialed enough to pay that and still profit.
- Don't open a second location until you can leave for 6 months and it still grows.

## Playbook steps (the sticky note)
1. Spend 4 hrs/day on the constraint (for most of the room: advertising). 2. Replace "human stuff" hours with vendors (~$1,500/mo for ~96 hrs). 3. Get rid of Susan, and raise standards so better people want to work with you. Supporting steps: reverse-engineer activity targets, role-play sales daily, run the churn routines, and onboard heavily.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Not advertising and blaming the market, the leads or the price ("All leads suck until they become customers").
- "My gym's amazing if people just came in." If your current customers aren't sending you more, you're not that good.
- Treating non-emergencies as emergencies (a 4:30 a.m. water-main call: "call a plumber").
- Being "Susan" as a client: buying education to prove it won't work. Instead, aim to become the teacher's number-one result.
- Fitness owners who are disciplined about their bodies but skip the hard business work: working leads, follow-ups, handwritten cards, member events, reviews, referrals, ad skill.
- Soft "it's my fault, I didn't communicate" talks instead of firing; can't have hard conversations with people you like.
- Management by silence plus punishment.
- Signing a second lease while the first gym isn't profitable.
