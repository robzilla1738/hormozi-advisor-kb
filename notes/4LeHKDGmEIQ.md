## Core thesis
Expectations make or break you. Happiness is "haves over wants": lower the wants and you get happier. In learning and business, the damage comes from a binary expectation ("Did I make it or not?"), when skills sit on a continuum. Each course, coach or mentor adds bricks to a bridge from where you are to where you want to be. Ask "Am I closer or further from my goal?", not "Am I there?" Aimed at younger entrepreneurs; it's an early podcast from his Gym Launch era.

## Frameworks & models
- **Bridge & bricks**: the goal sits across a valley. Each skill is a brick, and any level of goal has its own bridge (0→$100K/yr, $1M→$2M/mo, $100M→$1B).
- **Psychological binary vs biological continuum**: not "diabetic or not" but "how diabetic"; not "smart or dumb" but "how smart."
- **Credit attribution error**: a business might need 38 core skills. Someone gets 24 from earlier coaches who "failed" them and the last 14 from Gym Launch, then credits Gym Launch with everything. Franchisors got gyms "70% of the way across the bridge" and Gym Launch did the last 30%.
- **Teaching/learning fit**: you might absorb only 25 of a teacher's 38 bricks. The lesson can land later from someone else, but the first teacher still helped.
- **College comparison**: people spend $200K and four years without being upset at not earning, because they expected nothing.
- **Formula**: raise expectations on activity and effort ("absolute intolerance to anything but excellence"). Lower expectations on outcomes.
- **5-year investor lens**: if I keep adding bricks for 5 years, am I better or worse off?

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Judge any learning purchase by whether it moved you closer, not whether it got you to the destination.
- Learning that something is not a priority is valuable in itself.
- Don't keep starting new bridges. Jumping between opportunities after "two steps of progress" means you never cross. Success often comes on attempt #7, built on skills from six failures.
- Ask: (a) Is my expectation reasonable? (b) Can I lower it? (c) Can I raise my activity expectations until beating the low outcome expectation is believable?
- Use mentors to spot the missing bricks.

## Playbook steps
1. Define the goal (the other side of the bridge). 2. List the skills (bricks) needed and count the ones you have, e.g. "22 of my 38." 3. After each course or coach, ask "Closer or further?" 4. Find the next missing brick, with a mentor's help. 5. Keep effort standards high and outcome expectations low. 6. Stay on one bridge over a multi-year horizon.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Binary outcome thinking about courses and coaching ("Bought a copywriting course and still not a millionaire").
- Trashing former coaches or franchisors ("Screw that third-grade math teacher"). Admitting you had many teachers doesn't lower your status.
- Opportunity-hopping.
- Treating a perceived setback as a setback.

## Examples / case details
- He paid $120K for 4 hours with Grant Cardone and got enough value in the first hour. He paid $50K for ~8 hours with someone else on a higher-level project, realized halfway through it wasn't a priority, and still counted it as progress.
- His young neighbor's unrealistic expectations mirrored his own, "just add zeros."
