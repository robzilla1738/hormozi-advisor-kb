## Core thesis
Fast wealth comes from three levers: (1) price on the value you create, not your cost, using the value equation; (2) a fast cash conversion cycle, where first-30-day cash pays for the next customer ("client-financed acquisition"), so capital stops limiting growth; (3) "wealth alchemy", meaning permanent customers × an enterprise-value multiple build tax-free asset value far beyond taxed cash flow.

## Frameworks & models
- **Value equation**: dream outcome, perceived likelihood (risk), time delay, effort & sacrifice. Value = the ROI (e.g. $100K) minus a discount for risk, delay and effort. B2B: the outcome is money. B2C: translate status/pain into worth. Most valuable version: immediate, no effort, guaranteed, exactly what they want.
- **Table example**: plastic table ($50–100) vs $20K hand-carved table. Sell status, social proof (lower risk), same-day delivery (time), white-glove assembly (effort).
- **Price is the biggest profit lever**: $100 table, $50 COGS, $30 other costs = $20 net. Repositioned at $1,000: $50 table + $200 delivery + $130 sales/other = $620 net. A 10× price gives 31× profit (checked: 620/20 = 31).
- **Client-financed acquisition**: CAC $100 + COGS $50. He wants first-transaction cash to cover this customer's CAC, the next customer's CAC and COGS. He first says $250, then corrects to the rule **2 × (CAC + COGS) ≤ 30-day cash collected** = $300. (Caption wording: "two times CAC plus cogs"; his $300 matches 2×(100+50).)
- **Why 30 days**: credit-card float is interest-free for ~30 days. Recover cash in 7 days and you can cycle 4×/month; in 1 day, 30×.
- **Get paid sooner / capital stack**: from employee (paid after) to contractor, surgeon, insurer, church/royalty (paid off the top). Your position depends on leverage, so choose to get paid earlier.
- **Two ways to get more cash up front**: raise the price, or sell more things right away (upsells, downsells, alternate offers). Run each upsell through the value equation too.
- **Hyper-buying cycle**: a new identity decision (e.g. running a marathon) opens a chain of problems: shoes → socks → shorts → tank → carrier → coach/app/hydration. Make the next offer available.
- **Permanent CAC**: free-trial CAC $100, 1/3 convert → $300; 1/3 of customers stay forever → ~$900 permanent CAC, for $1,200/yr ARR at $100/mo. With a 10× revenue multiple (B2B SaaS with good retention), each permanent customer = $12K enterprise value. 10,000 customers = $9M spend → $12M ARR → $120M EV. (Captions read "$122,000"/"$112,000" per customer; the math gives $12K [inference: caption error].)
- **Vehicle matters**: the same unit economics in a business valued at 7× EBITDA ($3M profit → $21M) is ~2.3× on $9M, not ~13×.
- **Business A vs B**: A acquires 100/200/300 and loses them all each year (a churn factory). B acquires 100/yr and keeps them. Both have 300 now, but B is the better business: time works for you. Investors value on the same factors as the value equation.
- **Permanent customer** (agency example): someone who has stayed 2+ years. If 1 in 10 becomes permanent, the real CAC is 10× CAC. Target lookalikes: even if CAC doubles, going to 1 in 3 makes it $600 vs $1,000.
- **Tax framing**: $3M profit, 50% tax → $1.5M. If you also added $12M ARR worth $120M, that's $1.5M tax on ~$123M of gains. He says ~1.5%; the math is ~1.2% [inference]. Unrealized growth compounds untaxed.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Sell off the value you create (the customer's "future wallet"), not your wallet or theirs today.
- Don't compete on price. He cites a McKinsey survey: 85% compete on price, 1% win. Dan Kennedy: no reward for second cheapest.
- Don't take a deal spending $100 to make $50 up front unless there are follow-on purchases. Target 30-day gross cash ≥ 2×(CAC+COGS).
- Ignore "industry standard" payment terms (e.g. net-60). Offer quarterly prepay with a ~5% discount baked into the price, or add onboarding, to collect cash up front.
- CAC is roughly similar between competent competitors in an industry. The winner is whoever monetizes each customer better (8× revenue per customer → ~50× profit vs a consulting client).
- Spend more effort keeping customers than doubling acquisition. Define and measure the % that become permanent.

## Playbook steps
1. Rebuild the offer on the value equation: raise the price and add risk reversal, speed and done-for-you.
2. Design upsells, downsells and alternate offers for the first 30 days.
3. Collect up front (prepay, bundles, onboarding fees).
4. Check 30-day cash ≥ 2×(CAC+COGS), then reinvest each cycle (use credit-card float).
5. Measure permanent-customer %, compute permanent CAC, and retarget ads at the permanent-customer avatar.
6. Pick a vehicle with a high multiple; build asset value rather than optimizing tax on cash.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Pricing a "$20K table" like a plastic table, then not having the margin to deliver extras.
- Fear of making offers ("people hate being sold but love buying").
- Accepting industry-standard delayed payment.
- Being a churn factory that only grows through more marketing.
- Obsessing over tax loopholes instead of building enterprise value.
