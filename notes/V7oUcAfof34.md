## Core thesis
Structure your business as if you'll sell it, whether or not you will — that's what gives freedom. The goal is ownership, not CEO-ship: the company must maintain AND grow without you.

## Frameworks & models
- **CEO vs. owner:** Buffett owns See's/GEICO, doesn't run them. If it can't run without you, you're owner + CEO (less sellable / lower price).
- **Nose in, hands out (NIHO)** (from a mentor): strategic advisor in meetings; walk away with no to-dos.
- **Cadence:** quarterly meetings with each portfolio leadership team, plus as needed.
- **6-month ownership test:** install a new leader, step back for 6 months — does the company grow in top line and profit (barring deliberate capex)?
- **Remove yourself from central comms:** get off company Slack/Asana; create a separate channel with only executives.
- Faceless brands (GEICO gecko) are more transferable than personality brands.
- Founder progression: doing → managing → leading → vision.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Being CEO is full-time; don't be CEO of two companies (his "terrible mistake").
- If you claim to be passive but have no CEO, you probably aren't passive.

## Examples / case details
- Portfolio just under $100M/yr; 3 owned outright, 5 minority stakes. One Slack with all portfolio executives. Took over a decade to get here.
