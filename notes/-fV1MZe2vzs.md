## Core thesis
Two thinking errors cause wasted effort: (1) treating a **dichotomy to be managed** as a **problem to be solved**, and (2) using a **psychological binary** when reality is a **continuum**. Start any discussion with "What problem are we solving?" (Title promises four frameworks; the transcript covers two.)

## Frameworks & models
- **Dichotomy vs problem:** delegation vs micromanagement. Both extremes fail: abdication on one end, a stifled team and you as bottleneck on the other. You manage the middle forever. Other dichotomies: fairness vs equality (tax codes), justice vs mercy, variety vs consistency in marriage.
- **"What problem are we solving?"** Pause people who dump context. Then ask whether it's even a problem: can we reasonably eliminate it, is it catastrophic, or is it a cost of doing business to improve over time (e.g. customer complaints in an early product)?
- **Continuum vs binary** (from Dr. Trevor Cashey): not "off my diet" but "200 or 500 calories over". Not "Facebook doesn't work" but "to what extent, and where's the drop-off?"

## Decision rules
- Don't mis-prioritize normal operating problems as existential threats.
- 10 over yesterday → 10 under today, rather than "I'm off, might as well eat 19 pizzas".
