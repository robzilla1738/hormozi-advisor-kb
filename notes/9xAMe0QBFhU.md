## Core thesis
Gross margin creates net margin and is the #1 health metric he checks. Service businesses should be above 80% gross margin; each halving of delivery cost (80→90→95%) doubles profitability per unit and lets you acquire twice as many customers for the same cost.

## Frameworks & models
- **Gross margin = (Revenue − COGS) / Revenue.** For services, COGS = incremental cost of one more unit (delivery payroll hours) — excludes front desk, HR, rent.
- **Net margin** = what's left after everything.
- **Two levers:** cut delivery cost or raise price.
- **Margin increments:** 80→90 = half the cost = 2x as profitable; 90→95 = 2x again. Biggest companies run ~99% gross.
- From gross profit you pay CAC (e.g., willing to spend 20% of revenue), all overhead, and profit.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Rule of thumb: any service business must be >80% gross margin.
- $60 service with $20 cost = 66% GM. Fix: cost to $12 (80%) or price to $100 (80%).
- Brick and mortar often nets ~12%. Moving 66% → 80% GM takes net from ~12% → ~26% (≈2.2x).
- Net 15% → 20% is a big jump in take-home.

## Playbook steps
1. Compute incremental cost per additional client.
2. Compute GM.
3. If <80%, raise price and/or reduce delivery cost until >80%; then push toward 90/95.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Feeling bad about charging much more than cost — without margin you can't profit, scale, or help more people.
- Not knowing your gross margin.
