# Brutally Honest Advice to Build Your Confidence

## Core thesis
Don't build confidence, build evidence. Confidence is a prediction that what you expect will happen, earned through reps and proof. Judge yourself on controllable inputs (effort versus maximum possible effort), not lagging outcomes. Play infinite games where not quitting means winning. Ignore other people's opinions and "because" stories about your past, and choose the path that makes the more epic story.

## Frameworks & models
- **Evidence → confidence**, not the reverse. "Fake it until you make it" becomes "walk it before you talk it." Confidence is domain-specific, so practice the actual thing, not something adjacent.
- **Confidence = probability**, as in statistics: the likelihood that what you predict happens. Raise it by having done it (review the script rather than psyching yourself up in the mirror).
- **Message 10%, context 90%**: proof (e.g., his $46M exit) gives content its reach. "Solve for the proof and then the content will take care of itself."
- **Too good to fail**: make the work so good that each viewer sends it to one other person. Then it's a time game.
- **Compare inputs, not outputs**: compare a top rep's calls, script practice, hours, and weekends, not his commission check. If you're new, do 2–3× the inputs of those ahead of you (Kobe's two practices a day).
- **Leading vs lagging metrics**: track calls, not sales; calories, not weight. Shift your definition of winning to what you control.
- **Effort delta**: winning = zero gap between how hard you tried and how hard you could possibly try. "Nothing left in the tank."
- **Finite vs infinite games**: marriage, health, and business are infinite. The point is to keep playing, so "if you don't give up you win."
- **Chunk up commitment**: commit to "doing hard work" broadly (calls and CRM entry alike) as character building.
- **I-am / because statements**: drop the "because" (an unchangeable past cause). Say "I do X, period," then change the present behavior. His three stages: blame trauma → rewrite the story → behavior without a because.
- **Heroes vs villains** (Donald Miller): same painful backstory. Villains hurt the world back; heroes stop others from being hurt. "Heroes use pain, villains are used by it."
- **"And that's okay"**: decide something isn't a problem.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Podcasting stat he cites: 90% don't get past episode 1, 9% don't get past 20, and 21+ episodes puts you in the top 1%.
- When choosing between options, pick the more epic story. "No 85-year-olds are regretting their epic stories."
- Remove quitting from your menu of options; "try your next idea."
- Others' opinions about your life are their preferences. Don't build your life in opposition to critics, which gives them control.
- Document the hard times (the floor-sleeping, the broke moments), not the success.

## Playbook steps
1. Pick the skill and do the actual thing repeatedly.
2. Practice the script and rehearse in high-stakes-like conditions.
3. Track inputs daily and match or exceed the leaders' inputs 2–3×.
4. Judge each day by effort delta and character built.
5. Keep shipping (20+ episodes) and judge your 1st vs 20th vs 100th.
6. Photograph and record the struggle period.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Seeking confidence before action. Confidence without evidence is "a delusion."
- Fear of humiliation leading to "the long-term humiliation of never amounting to anything."
- Measuring yourself on views or outcomes you can't control, which makes you quit too soon.
- Leaning on "potential, comma, and trauma."
- Letting close people's opinions steer you. "They want the version of you who best serves them."

- First sales consult: the prospect said she'd go home for her card. He told his boss he'd closed her, and six guys laughed for a minute straight.
- Podcast: 4.5 years of two episodes/week, ending at ~2–3K downloads/month. He treated 10–15 listeners like a lunch-and-learn and 200 like an auditorium. It took off after renaming from "Gym Secrets" to "The Game" and selling a company for $46M.
