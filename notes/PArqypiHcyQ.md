## Core thesis
"Own it." Treat every customer, employee and spouse relationship as week-to-week, even with a 12-month contract. The real contract is for today. Churn is the market telling you you're no longer in the customer's best interest. Make the value so far beyond the price that leaving would be crazy. This is a reposted Gym Launch-era walk-and-talk from his private community; he says any numbers are dated.

## Frameworks & models
- **Core tenets**: Gym Launch had five; "own it" is one. Tenets are a reference for how you and employees make decisions.
- **Week-to-week mindset**: "We just got the contract; now we have to fulfill." He treats his Gym Lords group as week-to-week and his marriage as day-to-day.
- **People act in their own self-interest** (from a mentor): predict that, and you'll always be right. So align relationships so that staying and paying is in their best interest.
- **Value discrepancy**: Chipotle charges $7 for what feels like a $20 meal. Aim to be 10× more valuable than your price, while that price is 3× the competition's.
- **Everyone forgets what you did**: we remember our own contributions and forget others'. Just do more again today.
- **Customers, not competitors**: competitors copy last month's stuff, so keep innovating for customers.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Use contracts for protection, but never rely on them to keep customers. "If you rely on contracts your life will be living hell."
- Read churn as a signal you're doing something wrong, not that customers are flaky.
- Small things drive cancellations: rude staff, lateness, a dirty bathroom, the same playlist. The stated reason (busy, moving, doctor's note) is a cover.
- Appreciate customers and employees continuously, not once a month.
- If employees underperform or trainers leave with clients, look at your leadership and how much you invest in them first. In service businesses, staff whose needs aren't met won't meet customers' needs.

## Playbook steps
1. Adopt "own it" as a tenet. 2. Treat every renewal as being resold daily. 3. Audit everyday touchpoints (staff behavior, cleanliness, music). 4. Build a value gap of ~10× the price. 5. Invest in employee growth. 6. Treat churn or discontent as a prompt to improve, not to blame.

## Mistakes he warns against
- A victim mindset about customers who quit or "don't honor their word."
- Complaining about long contracts you signed (franchise agreements) while expecting customers to honor theirs.
- Resting on your laurels: "you're only as good as your last paycheck."
- Praising once and going silent for two months.
