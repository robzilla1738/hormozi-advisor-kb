# The #1 Reason Young People Stay Poor

## Core thesis
Young people stay poor because they optimize for earning instead of learning. Take roles where you're the junior around experts, double the required volume, and stack skills — skills compound non-linearly. Earning too early ends the learning.

## Frameworks & models
- **Learning season vs. earning season:** know which season you're in; stop comparing to people earning more now.
- **Double the quota:** with no skill you must do 2x the work for the same output (100 calls required → 200). 2–4x volume pays down "ignorance debt" faster (Kobe's two practices a day).
- **Shadow the #1 performer.**
- **Skill stacking compounds (1+1+1 > 3):** math → bookkeeping → accounting → taxes/insurance (CFO) → M&A/entity structure (rainmaker); Jay-Z: rap → promotion → label/distribution → recruiting artists.
- **Employer view:** earning-season hires get "worked" (must extract more than paid); learning-season hires get mentorship and investment.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Choose the job with the best mentors, even at lower pay.
- Respect is earned; the younger you are, the more you must earn it.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Negotiating for $3k more instead of taking a $60k learning role; becoming "head honcho" at a small company with no mentors ~12 weeks in.
- Dunning-Kruger overconfidence.

## Examples / case details
- 52% of high school seniors (survey he cites) think they'll be millionaires by 25.
- Sales kid: 200 calls/day, shadowed top guy, became a top closer, then took a pay cut to join a more prestigious company.
- Buffett offered to work free for Ben Graham ("you're overpriced").
- Alex: magna cum laude Vanderbilt in 3 years; took lower-paying consulting role to work with CEO; asked 40 gym owners to work for free; got minimum wage.

## Notable lines
- "If you go for the earning early, you stop the learning."
