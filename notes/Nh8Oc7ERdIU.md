# How to Get Ahead of 99% of People

## Core thesis
Only ~0.4% of businesses (1 in 250) hit $10M+/yr; ~90% never reach $1M. Reaching the top takes two things: a high-leverage entrepreneur (skills + non-limiting beliefs) and a high-leverage vehicle. Leverage = what you get out vs what you put in. Climb the leverage ladder (collaboration, capital, code, content), pay down "ignorance debt" by buying skills (the best-returning investment), live below your means, transition through a side hustle, and treat business as an infinite game you never quit — even if the vehicle changes.

## Frameworks & models
- **Entrepreneur = skills + beliefs**: he folds "character traits" into skills; soft skills are just skills that are hard to measure and can be trained via micro-behaviors (smile, greet by name, don't interrupt).
- **Known limits vs unknown unknowns**: the reasons you give for why you can't do something are known; the costliest limits are possibilities you don't know exist.
- **"Level 10 skill set, level 2 opportunity"**: same skills in a better vehicle unlock value (gyms → done-for-you turnarounds → licensing).
- **Leverage = output/input**: skilled cold caller gets 10 appointments from 100 dials vs 1 = 10× leverage. Buffett: "It's not about how hard you row, it's about what boat you're in."
- **4 Cs of leverage** (adapted from Naval): collaboration (people), capital (other people's money), code, content. You can max out just one.
- **His income ladder**: employee (4 figures/mo) → self-employed (5) → employing people (6, gyms and turnarounds) → licensing/content (7) → adding capital at Acquisition.com (8) → 9 figures = "add time."
- **Time multiplies leverage**: every vehicle switch restarts at zero; year 4 of a business usually beats year 1 of a new one. Panda Express: 45 years in one vehicle.
- **Ignorance debt**: someone earning $50K "pays" $950K/yr for not knowing how to make $1M.
- **Strategy = allocating limited resources against unlimited options** (prioritization).
- **"SME 500 over the S&P 500"**: S&P ~9–10%/yr; $1K there → ~$1,100; $1K on sales training with implementation that lifts income $40K → $220K = +$180K every year. Skills compound (sales + lead gen multiplies demand on sales skill) and can't be taxed, seized, or divorced away (his father's lesson after fleeing Iran).
- **Capital + know-how**: Acquisition.com buys at decent valuations with a margin of safety because it can grow businesses — his edge over Buffett (who doesn't work in businesses) plus 60 more years.
- **Document → Demonstrate → Duplicate**: skill transfer (also used to backfill roles).
- **Earning → learning → earning**: get paid to learn; take a pay cut if needed; ask employers for the skill you want; leave when learning stalls.
- **Transition steps**: 1) live below your means; 2) stack cash and start a side hustle (as a path to a main hustle); 3) match current income, sustain 6 months, then cut the cord.
- **Infinite vs finite games** (Simon Sinek): finite = known players, rules, end; infinite = keep playing. Business, health, and marriage are infinite. "The day you stop playing is the day you lose."
- **Give, give, give, then ask**: in groups, give your skills away free until they ask what they can do for you.
- **Rising baseline**: skills set your floor (sales → $400K–$1M as a top car salesman; marketing + sales → a $1M/yr minimum).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If someone ahead of you in business gives advice you paid for, take it — otherwise it's like burning the money.
- If behind in skill, do ~2× the top performer's inputs (Kobe "two-a-days").
- Side hustle: don't quit until it matches your income and holds for 6 months (rule out seasonality/volatility). If 40 hrs/wk there doesn't out-earn your job, you're not doing it right.
- Don't jump industries; half-pivot within your industry, or switch roles/functions instead of industries. YC: no solo founders; prefers industry/personal experience with the problem.
- Content: educate only on what you've done; until then, document. Credibility protects you from hate.
- Paying ~25% more for an A player vs a B player can yield ~5× output — "why would you never make that trade?"
- Live below your means so the business can take calculated offensive risks.
- Separate your identity and self-worth from the business so you can make hard decisions.
- First skill to learn: sales ("if no one buys it, it's zero").

## Playbook steps
1. Audit your beliefs and stated reasons you "can't". 2. Seek rooms of people ahead of you; give value first. 3. Pick the highest-leverage vehicle your skills allow; consider a half-pivot within your industry. 4. Invest in skills with implementation (sales first, then lead gen). 5. Get paid to learn; document/demonstrate/duplicate. 6. Keep expenses low; stack cash. 7. Side hustle → match income → sustain 6 months → quit. 8. Add leverage types over time (people → content → capital). 9. Stay in the game long-term.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Staying in a low-leverage vehicle out of attachment (his "America's next gym" dream).
- Hopping vehicles/industries and resetting reps to zero.
- Teaching what you haven't done.
- Buying education and not doing the work (purchase feels like solving the problem).
- Asking for a job before giving value ("I'll make 100 free videos if you hire me").
- Expecting every business to be Facebook; judging your side hustle by others' opinions.
- High fixed lifestyle costs that leave no buffer.
- Quitting an industry when you really just disliked one function.
