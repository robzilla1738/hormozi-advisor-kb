# 14 Money Mistakes to AVOID in Your 20's

## Core thesis
Your 20s are "a season of learning, not a season of earning." Your savings are too small to compound into much, so put the money into the highest-return asset, yourself and your earning power, instead of status purchases or index-fund-only saving. Build systems that make self-investment automatic and wasteful spending hard.

## Frameworks & models
- **Season of learning vs earning**: take jobs to learn. Employers who pay a premium are "paying the premium for everything you know and have nothing to add to you."
- **Invest in yourself over compounding small savings**: $500–1,000/month in an index fund looks big in a 60–70 year calculator but ignores inflation ("$4 million in 60 years is… like a million dollars today"). Spending $10K to double or triple your earning capacity returns more.
- **Pay your education first**: take money off the top into a self-investment account. Let it stockpile and deploy it when a learning opportunity shows up, rather than asking "what's left at month end?"
- **Automate investing, add friction to spending**: separate accounts (rent, investing, food, car). Only the allowed spending money hits your main account. He cites Dave Ramsey on cash hurting more than credit.
- **Measure money in hours worked**: at $20/hr with about 20% payroll tax, a $100 shirt costs about 6 hours of work.
- **Model the right season**: reputation comes from what you've done plus what you say you'll do, and both coming true. Copy what successful people did before, not what they do now. "Flying private doesn't make you rich."
- **Personal records on savings, not income**: compete on what you put into the self-investment account each month.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Once you earn more than about $15/hr, buy takeout and spend the time working. His math: about $10/meal × 2/day ≈ $600/month, versus about $50/week net savings from grocery cooking. [inference: his comparison is rough; the point is time value]
- Never "go shopping" with no agenda. Buy only what's on a pre-approved list.
- Live with more people in a better, convenient area rather than fewer in a bad one. Pay a premium to be near highways, the gym, and cheap food.
- Never listen to people who have smaller goals for your life than you do.
- Check your bank account every morning.
- Spend on rare one-time experiences you can only do in your 20s (e.g., backpacking Europe in hostels, the Grand Canyon), in limited amounts, without draining the investment account.

## Playbook steps
1. Set up separate accounts and automate a self-investment transfer off the top. 2. Make spending manual and painful. 3. Shop only from lists. 4. Choose jobs and roommates for learning and shared goals. 5. Price purchases in hours of work. 6. Track savings as your PR. 7. Check your balance daily.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Underspending on education and overspending on status (the Lambo and paid-checkmark flexes). "The people who matter are not impressed."
- Taking jobs to earn, not to learn.
- Plain saving or index-only investing as the path to getting rich.
- Bad roommates: irresponsible ones, or responsible ones with smaller goals who undercut you. "In the beginning what becomes success looks like failure."
- Avoiding looking at your bank account.

## Examples / case details
- His team had about 1,000 applicants for a YouTube editor role. The chosen candidate asked for an extra $3,000/year, which Alex says missed the plot.
- He lived in a 7-person house in California for about $400/month in a nice area.
- At 15 he worked at Smoothie King for $7.25/hr: a 6-hour shift was about $43, roughly $36 after taxes. Coworkers spent $15 on lunch next door, "working 6 hours for 20 bucks."
- He knows people who earned hundreds of thousands, even millions, a year and have nothing.
- He stayed poor until he started checking his account daily.
