# How To Make Ad Headlines That Get Clicks [ALEX HORMOZI]

## Core thesis
Basic, repeatable ad structure for local (fitness) Facebook ads. Banner headline = Duration + Benefit/Result + Power Word. Then attention-grabbing creative, copy that calls out who it's for and not for, a subhead/lead-in tied to the headline benefit, and a landing page congruent with the ad. Fundamentals beat cleverness; "good marketers beat mediocre marketers just by not being lazy."

## Frameworks & models
- **Headline formula (3 parts)**: (1) Duration: 21-day, 28-day, 6-week, 42-day; it implies commitment and when the result arrives without being a guarantee. (2) Benefit/result, made concrete or status-based: six-pack, bikini, little black dress, drop two belt notches, "skinny friend" (seeing the prospect through their friends' eyes). (3) Power word: challenge, blueprint, accelerator, intensive, program, transformation.
- **Ad anatomy (Facebook)**: banner headline → creative (Lead Gen Scrambler: group workouts, sweaty selfies, eye-contact images, selfie pitch videos, food pics, testimonials) → first lines call out who it's for and who it's NOT for (within ad compliance: no "you"/"you ladies") → subhead/lead-in tied to the benefit → implied authority ("1,000+ people in this local area") → CTA → congruent landing page.
- **Headline and creative don't need to match**; the lead-in and landing page do need to tie back to the headline benefit.
- **Color/font matching**: avoid black/yellow, black/red or stencil "G.I. Joe" lettering for women; black/green or black/blue work for men. Permanent Marker (Google Fonts) works for both. "Colors and fonts matter. Sometimes even more than the words."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If the banner lacks duration + benefit + power word, conversion likelihood is "much lower. Period."
- "Who it's not for" is as powerful as, or more than, "who it's for": selectivity is magnetic and raises click quality.
- Base the call-out on data: find which clients make the most and stay longest, and write the copy for them.
- Split funnels by segment (men/women; finer: 55-year-old vs 25-year-old women) with different messaging and pages.

## Playbook steps
1. Write the banner headline (duration, benefit, power word). 2. Match colors and font to the prospect. 3. Pick proven creative. 4. Open the copy by attracting and repelling. 5. Lead-in subhead tied to the benefit ("Wouldn't it be nice to be the skinny friend for once?… it's habits, and habits are teachable"). 6. Implied authority, then CTA. 7. Landing page congruent with the ad's offer and result. 8. Segment funnels.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Gym owners (even in his community) running ads that skip the headline basics.
- Mismatched colors and fonts for the audience.
- An incongruent landing page.
- Laziness and skipping fundamentals ("the advanced people just never don't do them").

## Examples / case details
- Gym Launch ads: "no personal trainers; only gym owners with 25+ clients, full-time, signed lease," based on data analysis showing gym owners made the most money and stayed longest.
- Example banner: "28-Day Drop Two Belt Notches Transformation" with a jeans/belt image; lead-in: "noticed you keep going up a notch every six months? It's not permanent and we know how to fix it."
