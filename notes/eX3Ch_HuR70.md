## Core thesis
A business teardown of Savage X Fenty. Most of Rihanna's ~$1.7B net worth comes from companies, not music. He ranks five moves by importance: (1) partner to cover your deficiencies (LVMH: manufacturing, retail, online expertise), (2) pick a good market, (3) brand affinity, (4) affiliates, (5) a Grand Slam membership offer that turns an intermittent purchase (lingerie) into a recurring one. It is especially useful as a guide to applying $100M Offers to e-commerce and subscriptions. Revenue figures are his estimates ("not super public").

## Frameworks & models
- **Partner for your deficiencies**: LVMH brought 118 factories, 6,000+ stores and teams for DR ads, branding and PR. "Businesses are limited by the collective brain power" inside them. A one-brain company is capped at one lifetime.
- **Distribution is a top partnership asset**: Rihanna brought media distribution and LVMH brought physical distribution. Alex brought media distribution to Skool, and Gym Launch's gym base let Prestige Labs do $1.7M in month one.
- **Four market criteria**: pain, purchasing power, easy to target, growing. Applied: 40 nude shades (a pain she lived with), affordable pricing ($3–$30 items) for a mass audience, an audience she could already reach, and growing markets (lingerie ~5% CAGR, ~$80B; activewear ~$600B at ~23.5% CAGR). Holding share in a market growing ~23.5% a year doubles you in about 3–4 years [inference: rule of 72 ≈ 3.1 yrs; he says "4ish"].
- **Brand affinity as switching force**: at equal price and quality, buyers switch to the brand they feel affinity for. Faceless corporate brands leave room for personalities (MrBeast vs Hershey). Nike stays iconic by renting legends' personal brands.
- **Value equation applied to product**: dream outcome (feel like a "baddie"), likelihood (fits my skin tone and size), effort (easier clasp, nursing bra, comfort), time (shipping).
- **Whisper → Tease → Shout launch**: a mysterious account she alone followed got 177K followers in 24 hours with zero posts (curiosity), then a teaser, then "live, buy now" across all platforms.
- **Platform-native content**: high-fi aesthetic images on IG, trends, collabs and TikTok Shop on TikTok, long-form try-ons and VIP promotion on YouTube. Little crossover.
- **Affiliates compound into permanent distribution nodes**: Allan was built on affiliates, Prestige on gym-owner affiliates, and Skool gets ~65% of traffic from referrals. Treat affiliates as another customer tier that needs its own Grand Slam offer (cash plus rev share, turnkey assets, brand-additive perks like the Emmy-winning fashion show). Affiliates and brand shape each other in both directions. Pick affiliates spanning varied beauty ideals to widen reach.
- **3% vs 97%**: direct response fights over the 3% ready to buy now. Brand moves the 97% along the continuum (maybe to a 15% pool, 5× larger). DR wins at 30 days and brand wins over a year, which is where 20–100× ad returns come from.
- **Membership offer stack** ($59.95/month, credit-based): member discount (up to 25%), VIP media/app content (community), personalized monthly picks, **skippable** in the first ~4 days of each month (no charge), credits roll over (acts like layaway/savings), 90-day fit guarantee, early access, birthday-month gift, free shipping over $59, 15% off sister brand Fenty Skin, a free item after every 5th billing (bonus delayed past the likely average churn point), easy cancel.
- **Lead magnet = "reveal a problem" via a personalization quiz**: an 8-step fit/style quiz → account (sunk cost, consistency) → discount code. Acquisition.com equivalent: the scaling roadmap (10 stages × 8 functions, 0–500 employees).
- **Credits work like gift cards**: an interest-free customer loan, the fastest cash cycle, and unredeemed balances (not cashable) become profit.
- **Member-first checkout**: the button reads "member add to bag." Good/better/best price display: guest $12.95, member $9.95, new member $5.18 (60% off). The membership is the primary offer and premium/guest is the downsell.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Ideally build products around a pain you've lived with for years (Rihanna; Sara Blakely's sneaker heels).
- Match price positioning to the brand. Going luxury ($5–10K) would have flopped for Rihanna's mass, inclusive brand.
- Ask for more info in a lead magnet if it makes the result more valuable. Distinguish good friction from bad friction.
- Add third-party financing for physical products, or lose ~30% of sales (his estimate).
- Put recurring bonuses just past average churn.
- An affiliate program doesn't promote itself. Make affiliates an offer so good they feel stupid saying no.

## Playbook steps
1. List your core deficiencies and partner with whoever has them at scale. Bring your own distribution as currency.
2. Score the market (pain, power, targetable, growing). Prefer tailwinds.
3. Build the product around a lived pain and the value equation.
4. Launch whisper → tease → shout.
5. Make platform-native organic content that demonstrates use or expertise.
6. Recruit affiliates aligned with the brand, give them an offer plus turnkey assets.
7. Build a membership with a discount, community, personalization, skip, rollover credits, guarantee and delayed bonuses. Make it the default checkout.
8. Capture data with a personalization lead magnet.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Trying to learn and do everything yourself.
- Plain box subscriptions ($50/month for a box), which he predicts would churn heavily.
- Thinking affiliates will just promote you.
- Only chasing the 3% who are ready now.
- Fear of lowering conversion by asking more in a quiz.
- Note: he mentions the membership drew some regulator "dings" but doesn't elaborate.
