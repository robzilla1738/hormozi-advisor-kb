# A YouTuber Asked Me "How To Business". Here's My Answer

Signal: medium

## Core thesis
Advising Unspeakable, a YouTuber with ~200M monthly viewers who's been posting 10 years (audience mostly 12–16), on whether to launch an energy drink or toy brand, a car shop (0 to 60, with a planned parts marketplace), or a game. Alex's answer: double down on the media business, which is the cash cow and what he's best at. Build a 1–3 person co-host bench so he can phase out of being on camera (like Red Bull, the brand stops being one person). Build out sponsorships (he says income could "5x"), drop the marketplace, and take product deals for equity rather than launching cash-eating CPG. Most of the dialogue is Unspeakable's (guest, not Alex).

## Frameworks & models
- **Market × brand × monetization vehicle**: find what overlaps all three. Influencers often copy another influencer's play and end up "massively under-monetized."
- **Opportunity formula** (as he states it): units sold × gross profit per unit ÷ competitive moat [inference: the moat term likely means competition, i.e. dividing by competitive intensity]. Equivalently, number of people × money made per person. Alex's own model is a tiny number of people × large money per deal.
- **Original distribution is only the launch pad**: sustained growth needs new distribution plus a product good enough that the first ring tells friends. Slapping your name on a white-label product fails.
- **Media businesses are uniquely profitable**: marketing is fulfillment, and sponsorship money goes straight to the bottom line (Buffett owned newspapers). Naval-style line: you only sell because you don't know how to market.
- **Choose by likelihood × upside × unknowns**: the marketplace has the same effort as scaling Unspeakable, lower likelihood, and about the same upside, so cut it.
- **Phase-out plan**: over 6–12 months, add 1–3 regular co-hosts, test audience reaction, rotate them in, and let them lead until the founder makes cameos.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- "How can you do more of what we're currently doing?" A high likelihood of success inside your wheelhouse beats new hard ventures. "It's all hard."
- Games score highest on reach, gross profit and ease of fulfillment, but "if the game sucks, the game sucks." Energy drinks are cash eaters.
- Litmus test for any equity brand deal: you love the product.
- Set a goal (e.g. 4× income in four years from the core) and reverse-engineer it.

## Playbook steps
1. Rank vehicles by likelihood, upside and unknowns. 2. Double down on the core media business. 3. Build a co-host bench to remove founder dependency (52 → 100 videos/yr at the same quality). 4. Build out a proactive sponsorship sales function (currently all inbound: Nerf, Lego, Elmer's, WWE, 5-Hour Energy, Hot Pockets, Honey, mobile games). 5. Do equity brand deals with funded brands. 6. For the car shop, keep the ops partner, add a junior content runner, and sell sponsorships through it.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Copying other influencers' product launches.
- A personal brand you can't step out of (Unspeakable: "if The Rock was like... I'm gonna hire this guy").
- Launching cash-hungry CPG without an exceptional product.

## Examples / case details
- Unspeakable is almost 25 and doubts he'll want to fill his house with sand at 35.
- The channel began as car vlogs about six years earlier, then shifted to challenges as YouTube moved from Casey Neistat to MrBeast-style content.
- The 0 to 60 shop is at SEMA in Vegas, with integrated drops like "first 20 custom mods available."
- The miniseries idea came from Airrack.
