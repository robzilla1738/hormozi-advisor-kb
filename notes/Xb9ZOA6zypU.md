## Core thesis
This is the origin story of Gym Launch, from the 2016–17 rock bottom to a rocket-ship ramp. After a DUI head-on collision he cut to one business. Cash-flow disasters followed: his payment processor held funds, then a 35% refund rate under a done-for-you model he didn't control end to end. Out of desperation he packaged the same skills into a licensing/consulting product, which grew from about $100K/month to $2M+/month and $17M profit the next year. Lessons: your skills, traits and beliefs are the real assets; businesses are just "vehicles." Move the same skills into higher-margin vehicles (service → done-for-you → licensing/media). And control fulfillment end to end.

## Frameworks & models
- **Theory of constraints applied to attention**: a system grows until it hits its bottleneck. Running 9–10 "businesses" was his constraint.
- **Vehicle progression**: same skills, better vehicle: gyms → done-for-you launches → licensing code and media, which has "no cost of reproduction" and the highest gross margins.
- **Skills are the assets; businesses are manifestations of them.** "Your work works on you more than you work on it."
- **Value-to-price logic**: if the system makes an average gym $30K collected in 30 days, charging $16K (vs about $500K franchise fees for similar systems) means customers "double their money in the first 30 days."
- **Foundation → peak**: the foundation of skills, traits and beliefs sets how high the peak can go. "The amount of money that you'll make will be predicted by the value that you provide to the marketplace."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Track profit, not revenue. He made "a couple million a year" in revenue and took home "basically nothing."
- Control the business end to end. Selling contracts that someone else fulfills hands the customer relationship to them; result: 35% refunds.
- When exiting a business, "just write the checks, do right by everyone" (his coach's advice), even when not strictly owed. He paid a $22K commission on money the processor held, leaving himself $1K.
- A brick-and-mortar merchant account can freeze a virtual or multi-location business. Get the right (high-risk) processing in place before scaling. Expect reserves and monthly caps (his were $50K per processor) after heavy refunds.
- If you're 2 years in and not making money, "multiply that by three." That's about where he was when he started making real money.
- Make the offer risk-free when you're unproven: "everyone always got way more value than they paid me."

## Playbook steps
How the original done-for-you Gym Launch offer worked: "I'll fill your gym in 30 days for free." He paid ads, travel and hotels, kept the upfront cash, and the gym kept the contract value; a $500 refundable deposit held the date. Average about 200 sales in 21 days (about 10/day).
Then the pivot: 1. Sell the method to gyms already queued for launches ($6K, then $8K; $60K in a day). 2. Call all 32 past launch gyms with "want me to show you how I filled your gym?" ($300K the next month). 3. Let product results drive word of mouth. 4. License all materials: ads, copy, scripts, lobby setup.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Running too many ventures at once: 6 gyms, 2 agencies and launches, with half a bottle of Johnny Walker a night to sleep.
- Scaling from one launch to six at once ("why go from one to two when you could go from one to six").
- No PCI compliance: contracts were scanned into Dropbox. He also underestimated admin load, with one part-timer handling about 50 sales a day.
- A sell-and-leave model with no ownership of fulfillment. Gym owners told clients to refund and re-sign directly with them, and at about 20% margins a $100K refund hit wiped out his savings.
- Chasing refunds by selling more launches, which felt like "a death trap."
- Skipping the "Rocky cut scene": taking one course and expecting to be a millionaire in 6 months.

## Examples / case details
- He sold 5 of 6 gyms and shut 2 agencies. A partner drained the last gym's account. He was left with about $23K.
- Christmas Eve: the processor held about $100K for 6 months. His resting pulse was about 120 in a movie theater at 27. Leila: "I would sleep with you under a bridge."
- Launched 6 gyms at once with $1K cash and a $100K-limit credit card, burning about $3,300/day.
- Leila's online coaching was about $3K/month. A sales letter written in 2–3 days brought in about $1K/day online.
- Revenue ramp in 2017: about $100K → $300K → $480K → $780K → $1M → $1.2M → $1.5M → $1.8M → $2.2M per month. About $3M profit in the last 4–5 months of the year, $17M profit the next year, and "zero to 30 million" revenue in a year.
- "The product brought us so much forgiveness from our customers" while infrastructure was built mid-flight.
- His $100M Offers book at 99¢ sells about 1,000 copies a day with no funnels or ads, which he credits to word of mouth on a good product. He says Acquisition.com does about $85M a year in revenue.
