## Core thesis
He learned sales and marketing by doing, not reading. Read only what you'll execute; learn by applying (buy a course on the exact thing and follow it); budget money for failure. Selling well comes from conviction plus genuine care.

## Frameworks & models
- **Execute-before-next-book rule:** don't read another book until you've executed the current one → read a few books many times (first: How to Win Friends and Influence People).
- **Learn by course-follow-along:** want a webinar? Buy a webinar course and build it while following.
- **Learning slush fund (from Ezra Firestone):** decide what you'll "blow" monthly on failure to learn (e.g., 5–25% of monthly income, e.g. $500/mo) to remove emotional attachment.
- Pay people above you to break beliefs about what's possible, not to hand-hold skills.
- Read for specific solutions to current bottlenecks, not amorphous topics.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Sell first, then read sales books — they'll make sense.
- Marketing starter loop: run an ad → landing page → collect contact info → call them.

## Mistakes he warns against
- "Productive procrastination": reading about things you haven't done.
- Selling something you don't believe in → cognitive dissonance, rep burnout and churn.

## Examples / case details
- 6–12 months of self-help reading during consulting job; life unchanged; books contradicted each other.
- Never did sales training before selling; first sales product bought when managing multiple teams doing a few hundred thousand/month.
- Paid someone 1:1 to show him how to run gym ads. Read ~30 books before becoming a deca-millionaire.

## Notable lines
- "My favorite book on sales is to get out there and start selling."
- Reading about what you haven't done "is a productive way of feeling accomplished without actually doing anything."
