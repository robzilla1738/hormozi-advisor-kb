## Core thesis
Your choice of advisors (lawyers, accountants, investment pros, deal partners, mentors) can "make or break" your net worth. Judge each one on two traits: **integrity**, meaning they have your best interest at heart and their incentives are truly aligned with yours, and **efficacy**, meaning they have the skill to deliver. Test both by interviewing many candidates, checking incentives, and learning enough to judge them yourself. (Context: his portfolio was ~$85M/yr at the time.)

## Frameworks & models
- **Integrity × Efficacy grid**: mom = trust but no skill; expert = skill but "knows exactly how to screw you."
- **Interview breadth = perspective**: the "Julie and 10 trainers" example (keto vs. low-fat vs. balanced vs. macros). Without many opinions you have no basis to judge. He calls this qualitative research, as in consulting.
- **Apparent vs. true incentive alignment**: a realtor is paid per unit of time, so they're incentivized to sell fast, not high. At 5% on $500K they earn $25K; dropping to $450K costs them only $2,500 ($22,500), but costs you $50K.
- **Restructured tiered commission**: 0% below $400K, 3% from $400–450K, 15–20% above $450K, so each increment matters to them about as much as to you.
- **Skill-testing steps**: read 2–3 books on the topic first so you can follow the conversation and don't look exploitable; bring a trusted person along; interview many and play their answers off each other ("what about this?").
- **Warren Buffett / "would they hide me?" test** for true friends and partners.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Interview as many advisors as possible, sourcing referrals from your network and posts (he suggests 10–20).
- Prefer trusted intent with 8/10 skill over unsure intent with 10/10 skill.
- Assume some industries are structurally incentivized against you (he names insurance and mortgage brokers).

## Playbook steps
1. Gather referrals from reputable sources. 2. Interview many candidates. 3. Map each one's real incentive (per unit of time) and restructure the deal to align it. 4. Read 2–3 books before the conversations. 5. Bring a trusted second opinion. 6. Cross-examine candidates using the others' answers. 7. Choose on integrity first, then skill.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Taking the first expert's view as the truth.
- Laziness: people spend more time choosing a vacation than choosing where their money goes.
- Assuming commission means aligned incentives.
- Entering expert conversations ignorant and exposed.
