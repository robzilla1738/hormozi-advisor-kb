# How I Gained 7.8 Million Followers In 40 Months (6 Key Lessons)
id: HVOubeXUcx0 / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HVOubeXUcx0 / duration: 2732s (~46 min) / tags: 09-marketing-content-ads, 04-lead-gen-channel, 12-mindset-leverage
Signal: high

## Core thesis
After 40 months, about $4M spent on team, equipment and software, about 1,000 hours on camera and 35,000 pieces of content (resulting in 7.8M new followers, 2B impressions and 1M+ books sold), he is doubling down on six changes: edutainment → education; content "for us" → "for you" (business owners); wide → narrow topics; views → ad revenue as the metric; shorts → longs; assume they know you → assume nothing. The core finding is that audiences don't funnel. Entertainment viewers want more entertainment, and education viewers (the ones who buy books, opt in and apply) want education.

## Frameworks & models
- **Three content buckets**, defined by their most common outcome: entertainment (the goal is getting people to watch), education (the goal is changing behavior), edutainment (both). "You don't control the audience."
- **The "no funnel" finding**: entertainment people → more entertainment; shorts viewers → more shorts; long-form viewers → more longs. These groups do not convert into each other "at any appreciable rate." Cross-platform is a partial exception (find you on TikTok, watch longs on YouTube).
- **For-you tactics (5)**: 1. Packaging that is clear, not vague (thumbnail and title say exactly what the video is; he rejects the curiosity-gap advice). 2. Intros that offer proof instead of just confirming the thumbnail. 3. The "meat" is lists, steps and stories rather than BeReal/vlog style, and language matters more than "razzle." 4. Visuals: effective production rather than overproduction (show data on charts, not flames or color-changing backgrounds). 5. Pre-search instead of fixing in post.
- **Proof → Promise → Plan intro**, drawn from re-watching his top 35 educational videos. Proof: why listen to you. Promise: what they'll get. Plan: what happens next. Any order works, so lead with whichever matters most. Business educators should lead with proof.
- **Ad revenue = views × RPM** as one *paired metric* (quantity × audience quality). The general rule: pair metrics, e.g. tickets resolved + resolution rating.
- **Low-latency metrics**: pick the metric with the shortest delay to the behavior you want to change. He went from a 6–12 month deal cycle to near-real-time ad revenue, which allows "a 100 feedback loops… than one every year."
- **Assume nothing**: introduce yourself every time, say why they should listen, fully explain references (inside jokes put newcomers "on the outside"), and treat every viewer as a stranger. Use a consistent intro with small variation (the Simpsons couch gag) to build positive association and reward fans.
- **Anything works better than nothing; some things work better than others; nothing works forever.** Do more of what works until it slows, then find the next thing.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Optimize content for the metric that tracks the *right* people (ad revenue/RPM), not views. The highest-RPM month also produced the most book sales, opt-ins and applications.
- Put about a quarter of the old post-production time into pre-work. That eliminated about 90–95% of post work, which he says means roughly 4× output at higher quality. "An ounce of pre-work is worth a pound of post."
- Keep shorts volume the same but shift topics to business; put more emphasis on longs, which drive more conversions.
- Read *between* the lines of comments. Praise may come from people you aren't trying to serve.
- Repost your best piece from last year. He bets it does as well or better, because new audience members haven't seen it. "We need to be reminded more than we need to be taught."

## Playbook steps
1. Define your ideal viewer (for him, business owners). 2. Choose narrow topics they care about (business models, leverage, selling). 3. Use a clear thumbnail and title. 4. Open with proof/promise/plan. 5. Teach with lists, steps and stories, emphasizing language. 6. Use only visuals that aid understanding. 7. Research up front, edit minimally. 8. Track ad revenue per piece in real time and build baselines. 9. Write titles for strangers, introduce yourself, explain references. 10. Re-post your greatest hits.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Believing the entertainment → education (or shorts → longs → customers) funnel.
- Copying entertainers' tactics because they have the biggest followings. The principle carries over, but the tactics don't.
- Vague, curiosity-bait packaging.
- Overproduction that distracts from learning; "fix it in post."
- Using views as the north star.
- Titles that assume people know you ("The Alex Hormozi guide to haters").
- Making no content at all for 90 days.

## Examples / case details
- 40-month numbers: YouTube 0 → 2.23M; Instagram 7K → 2.5M; LinkedIn 0 → 324K; TikTok 0 → 856K (caption says "$856,000"); X 0 → 617K; Facebook 0 → 25K; podcast 3K → 25M downloads; email 0 → 700K.
- A friend running a $10M+/yr business said the content "doesn't resonate as much" anymore. That was the trigger for the for-you change.
- Of the last 30 days of reels, 8 of the bottom 12 emphasized razzle, while 9 of the top 12 (75%) emphasized language. One of his best long videos is 70–80% words on screen.
- 90-day experiment with mostly entertainment/edutainment: views rose 2–3× while ad revenue fell by half.
- A 100K-view video earned more ad revenue than a 10M-view video.
- Retitle examples: "Business influencer crushes haters and shows how you can too"; "Day in the life of $200M/yr CEO."
- He reiterates the 2021 exit ($46.2M, American Pacific Group; Gym Launch and Prestige Labs). Portfolio does $200M+/yr. Ad revenue isn't how he makes money.
- Pitch at the end: Skool Games ("3.44%" or "33.44%" of people who finish month one make their first dollar; the caption is ambiguous) and free courses at acquisition.com/training.
