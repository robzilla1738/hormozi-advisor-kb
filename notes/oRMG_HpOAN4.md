## Core thesis
Alex runs through 20 numbered lessons from 13 years of business (9 companies sold, the last for $46.2M; portfolio ~$17M/month). The themes: price higher and anchor; advertise relentlessly; build proof; talk to customers; sell with a simple repeatable framework; 10x what already works before trying anything new; bill for longer periods; reuse "sawdust"; unify sales and marketing; hire people who raise the bar; and accept that stress and not knowing how are the job.

## Frameworks & models
1. **Anchor**: list an option priced 10–100× higher that you never plan to sell. It lifts conversion on the core offer and lets you nudge the core price up. A friend's weight-loss business added a version at 6× the price; it outsold the core and tripled profit overnight. About 10% of customers want the most expensive option. Showing a $100 offer to someone with a $1,000 budget costs you $900; showing a $1,000 offer to someone with $100 costs you $100.
2. **Nobody knows you exist**: his book launch had 500K+ registrants, yet only 1 of the people who stopped him over 4 weeks knew about it. People "need to be reminded more than taught." Keep flares up until people are in market (3/6/9 months). **Core four 4×4**: 4 hours a day. **Rule of 100**: 100 min of content, 100 outreaches, or $100/day in ads.
3. **Stair-step**: until ~$100K/month, put all focus on advertising. Then fix the leaky bucket (referrals, retention, repeat purchases) before you 10× the front end again. Otherwise you plateau.
4. **One channel / one avatar / one product** under $1M/yr. Add a second channel at ~$1M/month once the first runs without you. He waited until $4M/month (paid ads, then an outbound team). Outbound took 12 months to reach half of revenue, so expect 6–12 months.
5. **Start free**: lowers the stakes. Hidden costs (what customers give up or must start doing) are often the most expensive part, so cutting them lets you charge more. Free customers pay you in testimonials, referrals, and staying. Then charge a small price and keep raising it.
6. **Proof > promise**: a plain offer with 1,000 testimonials beats an amazing offer. Proof should be recent, visual (bank screenshots; weigh-in video > photo > words), high volume (screenshot 100 reviews; a floor-to-ceiling lobby wall), and lead with pain (pain-first testimonial ads "convert significantly higher"). Ask for testimonials at peak satisfaction; sell at peak deprivation.
7. **Raise prices, measured**: you will hear no more often. Price doubled → conversion fell 35%. With a $500 cost: at $1,000 you keep $500 per sale; at $2,000 you keep $1,500 (3×). Even with 35% fewer sales, profit is ~1.95× (0.65 × 1,500 = 975 vs 500). Test 4–5× price differences. Inflation: $79 in 2017 ≈ $100 in 2024. Raise at least 3–6% a year. See's Candies raised prices 50 years running. For existing customers, send a price-increase letter (what they'll get, inflation, "keep my promise," effective date, grandfather until a date).
8. **Talk to customers** (Paul Graham): it solves nearly every problem. Learn the words that make their "eyes light up," then reorder scripts, headlines, and roadmap. Call buyers and non-buyers. Skool runs a monthly top-10 contest with a Vegas day, partly to get super-user feedback. That feedback settled months of debate about a controversial product change. **Cancel/refund calls**: get angrier about the problem than they are, then ask "what would it take for me to make this right?" Use super users for the roadmap and non-buyers/churners for friction.
9. **CLOSER**: Clarify why they're here; Label the problem; Overview past attempts (pain cycle, to raise deprivation); Sell the vacation, not the plane flight (a 3-point pitch); Explain away concerns (time, money, decision maker, avoidance, etc.); Reinforce the decision (customers judge you on the first 24 hours after purchase, so make promises and keep them faster than promised). He cried the first time someone else closed a sale with it, because it meant the business could be duplicated.
10. **10× what works first**: "why can't we 10x this?" About 2 times in 3 the answer is "we could." The third time, the blocker is the constraint (e.g. lead lists, ad creative). For content, 2× volume × 3× quality = 6×; template your best piece. **5 stages**: uninformed optimism → informed pessimism → valley of despair → (most restart here) informed optimism → achievement. Treat "the woman in the red dress" (shiny new thing) as a warning flag. Know where the bodies are buried in each department.
11. **Growth, stagnation, and decline are all stressful**. Calling something a problem is the bigger stressor; your future self will see today's problems as small.
12. **Look-back window**: customers judge on their most recent period. Agency example: month 1 paid $5K, got $40K; month 2 paid $5K, got $5K; month 3 they cancel. Bill less often to extend the window. He'd cut price 30% to get 3–6 months upfront if it cut churn 3×. Offering annual prepay (not as default) gets 10–15% uptake. ~10% paying 12× roughly doubles upfront cash (0.9 + 0.1×12 = 2.1× [inference check]). Add a guarantee only for prepay. **Cash conversion cycle**: his growth spurts came when 30-day cash was 2–3× the cost to acquire and deliver for two customers. He went from $1,000 (Dec 2016) to $4.4M/month in 20 months.
13. **Sell sawdust**: use assets you already have, with no added operational drag (acquisition.com workshops reuse the portfolio team; rent gym space during idle midday hours; excess capacity like Uber and Airbnb; double- or triple-dip, e.g. interventions become content).
14. **Arm salespeople**: a spreadsheet mapping each customer concern to the content that overcomes it. Send it before or after calls. Sales went up and he no longer had to train reps on it.
15. **Unify sales and advertising** under a CRO. They sit on one continuum from low- to high-information buyers: sales fills the holes advertising didn't answer. Kale at Gym Launch became CRO, then ran the business.
16. **Three legs of the stool**: acquisition, delivery, operations (ops serves the other two as a vendor). These map to the 3 ways to raise enterprise value: more customers, worth more, less risk. Every big business ends up with the same structure, so don't chase passion; "love the game."
17. **The person with the highest standard should be in charge**, not the one with tenure or likability.
18. **Raise the average bar** (from Bezos): learn from candidates in interviews; interview 10–20; judge by the quantity and quality of metrics, and how their actions tie to outcomes (for leaders: how those outcomes drive the business). Example: he rejected a qualified media buyer who wasn't better than anyone on the team. "Your best talent is in your future."
19. **5 reasons someone doesn't do it**: didn't know you wanted it → didn't know how → didn't know when → not motivated (autonomy, investing in them, discretionary effort ~10×; "what if you don't and they stay?") → something is blocking them (best chef, no eggs). Example: the media team edited from home because office upload was slow; $120K for Google Fiber (1.2 Gbps) brought them back. Attack the reason, not the person.
20. **The hardest work is work you don't know how to do**: find the one priority that makes the other goals irrelevant. Don't replay level 2 when the level-3 boss is waiting. A complex problem is a moat ("bigger rock, bigger bag of money"; "we get paid $150M when we solve it"). Price the problem (hire that sales director = $5M). Learning = same condition, new behavior; intelligence = rate of learning. "You'll learn 100× more from your first 100 phone sales than 10,000 hours of reading."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Before judging a price change, know your baseline close rate and get a statistically meaningful sample. At a 40% close rate, 2 early no's are expected. Doubling price and dropping to 30% is "still a great deal."
- Advertising is the only focus below ~$100K/month; one channel/avatar/product below $1M/yr; second channel at ~$1M/month.
- Raise prices at least 3–6% a year.
- Interview 10–20 candidates; hire only people who raise the average of the team they join.
- Promote the person with the highest standard.
- Be cash-unconstrained when 30-day gross profit ≥ 2–3× the cost to acquire and deliver.

## Playbook steps
- Price: add an anchor tier → test 4–5× price gaps with real sample sizes → existing customers get a letter with a grandfather date.
- Growth: Rule of 100 → one channel to ~$100K/mo → fix retention, referrals, repeat purchases → 10× the working channel by removing its constraint → add a second channel.
- Retention: cancel calls (get angrier than they are, ask what would make it right) → offer prepay with a guarantee → extend the billing window.
- Team: CRO over sales and marketing → three functional heads → diagnose performance with the 5 reasons.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Overreacting to the first no's after a price increase.
- Avoiding calls with customers, churners, and non-buyers.
- Chasing new channels, products, or businesses (repeating stages 1–3) instead of 10×-ing what works.
- Quitting businesses because they're stressful.
- Monthly billing when value is lumpy.
- Sales vs marketing turf wars; resenting the sales team.
- Promoting by tenure or likability; hiring people who lower the bar.
- Re-solving problems you already know how to solve.
- Leaving prices flat through inflation (20% margin + ~26% cost growth → zero profit).
