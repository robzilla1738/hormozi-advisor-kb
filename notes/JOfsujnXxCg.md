# I Posted ONLY Business Content for 30 Days [STRATEGY REVEALED]
id: JOfsujnXxCg / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JOfsujnXxCg / duration: 1524s (~25 min) / tags: 09-marketing-content-ads, 04-lead-gen-channel, 08-ops-scaling
Signal: high

## Core thesis
After 35,000 posts in 40 months (7.8M audience), he made six content shifts and judged them on business outcomes rather than views. Narrow, deep, education-first content for his real avatar (business owners, ~9% of people) produced fewer views per video but more valuable viewers. RPM, subscribers, opt-ins, book sales and applications all rose within 3–4 weeks. "Likes ain't cash, views ain't cash, cash is cash."

## Frameworks & models
- **Six shifts**: (1) edutainment → education, where education is defined as changing behavior; (2) "for us" (content your team suggests) → "for you" (the avatar); (3) wide (fitness, relationships) → narrow (business only); (4) views → ad revenue (views × RPM) as the north-star metric; (5) emphasize shorts → emphasize long-form; (6) assume more → assume nothing: introduce yourself every time, no inside jokes.
- **Paired KPIs**: pair a volume metric with a quality metric in every department (support: speed of resolution plus quality; cleaning: cleans per day plus customer reviews).
- **Consumption preferences don't transfer**: he found no evidence that shorts viewers become long-form viewers on YouTube. People watch the format they already prefer. Long-form drove the most applications, book sales and opt-ins.
- **Deep vs wide**: some videos are both, e.g. leverage and branding (his highest revenue-per-view video is also his most viewed). Deep-and-narrow videos (scaling a 40-person sales team, mega-influencer partnerships, the 46-minute sell-my-company decision video) reach a slice of a slice and are still worth making.
- **Book sales as a leading indicator** of traffic quality.
- **Room-size reframe**: 100 views = a room you'd cross town to speak to. 50K views is 50× a 1,000-person conference. Free distribution is "infinite leverage."
- **Operating beliefs**: volume negates luck; don't get the same scar twice; state the facts and tell the truth (so you must collect data); say what only you can say and show what only you can show. Early on you can't out-outcome people, but you can out-work them ("I made 35,000 posts").
- **Wide shorts on purpose**: shorts = facial recognition / top of funnel, monetized by later retargeting ads (e.g. Skool ads to would-be starters), not by direct conversion.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Optimize content on a revenue-weighted metric (RPM × views, opt-ins, sales), not raw views.
- Fine with 10K views instead of 400K if those viewers are $10M+ owners.
- Don't judge a video by views; views show how many people it's relevant to, not quality.
- Don't let your team stand in for your avatar when picking topics.
- Listen to comments from your target buyers. He admits ignoring them.
- Production polish didn't matter: a staged keynote with slides performed the same as talking to camera or a whiteboard. Lower post-production allowed more output.

## Playbook steps
1. Define the avatar and check that content targets them (ask one directly). 2. Narrow topics to their money problems. 3. Switch the KPI to revenue-weighted metrics plus paired quality metrics. 4. Prioritize long-form for conversion. 5. Introduce yourself in every piece. 6. Cut whizbang editing and raise volume. 7. Keep some wide shorts for recognition and retarget them with ads. 8. Iterate: keep what works and don't repeat mistakes.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Chasing views by going broader and more entertaining outside your expertise (common among educators).
- Making content for your team rather than your customer.
- Assuming the audience already knows you.
- Comparing your numbers to others and feeling bad about small audiences.

## Examples / case details
- Results after the shift, 3–4 weeks in: RPM +68%; comments per view +25% (shares negligible, likes slightly down); total long-form views up ~29.56% vs the prior 6-week average despite fewer views per video; subscriber conversion +24.6% with absolute subscriber growth up; opt-ins +26%/week; book sales 2×.
- A friend doing ~$10M/yr, squarely his avatar, had stopped watching because the content felt irrelevant.
- About 50 people, including portfolio founders, messaged praising the return to whiteboard business content.
- He started at zero on every platform; his first YouTube videos got ~100 views.
