## Core thesis
Five lessons from crossing $250M (2024): (1) every change carries a ~20% performance cost, so only make changes with big upside; (2) revenue retention beats virality as the gold standard; (3) required LTGP:CAC depends on how manual your attraction/conversion/delivery are (3:1 only for fully leveraged models; 20–30:1 for manual ones); (4) $1–3M is "the swamp" because you need help you can't afford; (5) "rush is imaginary" — pick one business and say no to everything else.

## Frameworks & models
- **Cost of change**: his estimate (explicitly "no science") — ~20% drop in effectiveness in any changed function, especially people-involved ones (onboarding, sales, outbound; not a headline split test). Constant changing = permanently ~20% below potential. Changing nothing yields ~5% improvement as people get more efficient.
- **ICE** (borrowed from investing): Impact, Confidence, Ease (resources + timeline). Ideas logged on a "GROWTH" sheet; ranked when the team has bandwidth.
- **Stick vs spread**: retention (does it stick) ≠ virality (do people share). Virality requires (a) no disincentive to share (B2B clients may not want competitors to know) and (b) high communication frequency/density among customers and prospects — more common in consumer.
- **Revenue retention vs logo retention**: 100 customers Jan 1 → their revenue on Dec 31. Logo retention counts customers; revenue retention can stay high if fewer customers spend more. He tracks revenue retention.
- **LTGP, not LTV on revenue**: $100/mo × 10 months = $1,000 revenue, but with $20 COGS per shipment LTGP = $80 × 10 = $800.
- **Leverage-adjusted LTGP:CAC**: three functions — attraction, conversion, delivery. Paid ads + checkout page + software (no supply limits) → 3:1 fine, even 1.5:1 workable. Manual outbound + 1:1 sales + concierge delivery → want ≥20:1, aims 30+. Mixed → in between.
- **Lumpiness**: scaling manual models means paying ramping SDRs, ramping closers (lose twice), and hires who never become proficient (lose three ways), plus delivery hiring; ratio "undulates like a wave"; faster scaling = more inefficiency. Recruiting a senior person costs 25–30% of first-year pay (~$80K on $300K).
- **The swamp ($1–3M)**: e.g. $2M revenue × 20% margin = $400K profit; a star hire ≈$250K all-in = risking more than half your income on one person. Choices: work overdrive, hire, or both.
- **Scaling roadmap is by headcount, not revenue** (acquisition.com/roadmap; pitch for their Vegas program).
- **Counter rush with the end state**: look at people 20 years ahead — nearly all have one massive business (Bezos/Amazon before Blue Origin; Panda Express founder). Decide = Latin "to cut off."
- **Hard morphs**: no one knows you → no one pays → people complain. Difficulty is constant; its nature changes.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Only take bets with ≥20% upside to justify the guaranteed cost of change; skip the many 5% tweaks. Accept the business won't be perfect.
- Prefer medium-impact/high-confidence over big-impact/low-confidence.
- Sub-$1M: just promote/advertise and learn to sell.
- Gold standard metric for any business: revenue retention.
- If high disincentive to share AND low customer interaction frequency, don't count on virality.
- LTGP:CAC: highly leveraged → 3:1 ok; manual across all three → ≥20:1, target 30+; "at the very least" 15:1. If at 5–8:1 in a manual model, keep tinkering in one location rather than scaling. Most of his wealth was made at 30:1+.
- Colder markets convert a smaller % and cost more → need more padding.
- Don't obsess about passive income before maxing active income.
- In the swamp he recommends both overdrive and hiring: generate extra (unscalable) profit so you can afford 2–3 shots at a hire.
- Rush is only justified in winner-take-all network-effect businesses (usually already venture-funded).
- Volatile customers → volatile business; agencies serving SMBs at $1–2K/mo rarely reach $100M — big agencies go up-market.

## Playbook steps
1. Log every idea on a growth sheet instead of pushing all of them to the team.
2. When bandwidth opens, score ICE; pick the one with ≥20%+ expected upside.
3. Let working parts of the business "breathe."
4. Measure 12-month revenue retention by cohort.
5. Compute LTGP (gross profit) and CAC; set target ratio by leverage of attraction/conversion/delivery.
6. Use referral tactics (two-sided, timing of ask, events, ongoing programs, unlockable bonuses — from $100M Leads).
7. In the swamp, increase profit to fund multiple hire attempts.
8. Define the ultimate version of your one business; cut the rest.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Chasing perfection with constant changes.
- Assuming everything can grow on word of mouth (his own former belief).
- Using SaaS LTV literature (≈100% margin) for physical/service businesses.
- Scaling manual models at 3:1.
- Adding partnerships/locations/businesses out of rush (nail-salon owner Annie with messy partnerships; TikTok Shop agency owner wanting to start a brand and invest).
- Growing by starting more $3M businesses instead of solving why the current one isn't $10M; calling "inconvenient" not "hard" (owner stuck at $1M for 10 years).
