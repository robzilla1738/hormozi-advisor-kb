# The 3 Fastest Ways I Know To Grow Any Business

## Core thesis
Three (plus a bonus) ways to 10× income, for employees and businesses alike: (1) arbitrage — buy/live cheap, sell into the expensive market; (2) give away genuinely valuable stuff for free to massively grow how many people know you, monetized on the back end; (3) make customers worth more, fastest via pricing (price anchors / profit maximizers). Bonus: hit the list of people you already know with all three. Opens with his hourly arc: $6/hr at 17 → $25 at 21 → $250 at 23 → $2,500 at 26 → $18,000 at 28.

## Frameworks & models
- **Arbitrage** = buy and sell between two markets and capture the price difference. Types: geographic (live cheap, sell to US/Europe rates), network (overpay to be near people with money), expense-sharing (split costs). All profit is arbitrage: skill/information lets you do something for less than it costs the customer to do it.
- **Wealth is a ratio, not a number**: what you make vs what you spend. "Make more or need less." Low personal spend = more capital to reinvest aggressively in the business.
- **Giveaways**: "the two most powerful words in marketing are free and new." Give away real value with a hard, perceivable cost (reciprocity: "jam value into someone else's wallet" so it comes back as money later). Must have a back-end monetization.
- **Favorite business model**: look at what everyone else is buying, make it better, make it free, monetize another way.
- **Lead magnet types** (from $100M Leads): reveal a problem; give a trial; give one step of a multi-step process. Six Week Challenge = trial/one step; Gym Launch Secrets = reveal a problem; ALAN coaching & Prestige S3 = solve one problem that creates the need for the product.
- **"Give away the secrets, sell the implementation."**
- **Employee pricing lever**: ask for more (weakest), take on more risk (comp tied to a metric linked to revenue/profit — strongest), or switch roles (internal switch underrated; lets the business save face vs raising everyone in the role). Pay = value created, negotiation, replaceability, and most of all risk taken.
- **Employee vs 1099 vs business owner**: same "doingness," difference is risk; owner has many bosses (customers).
- **Price anchor**: put a price far above normal to frame the rest of the menu; say big numbers early in a presentation.
- **Luxury vs premium**: luxury — the price itself confers value/status (others must know the cost); premium — significantly more result/experience.
- **Profit maximizer**: what you sell most isn't what you make most on (McDonald's burgers vs fries/Coke). Free/core product drives volume; a high-touch, done-for-you, concierge tier maximizes profit.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Thailand median income ~$2,700/yr vs US ~$50–60K → >10× make-vs-spend gap for geographic arbitrage.
- Competitors offered 14–21 day trials (gym access/workouts only); he gave a full 6-week program → order-of-magnitude difference, not incremental.
- To 10× you can't make incremental changes; you need order-of-magnitude differences (10× customers or 10× their worth).
- Giveaway value test: if you're afraid it's too good, it's probably the right thing to give away.
- Long-term giveaways require a back-end product; you must be willing to wait. "95% of you aren't going to do it."
- Anchor tier ~10× core price ($500/mo → $5,000/mo) lifts takes on the anchor and the old top package.
- Hit your existing list (phone contacts, email contacts, socials) — near-zero added cost since acquisition is already paid.

## Playbook steps
1. Cut personal spend / arbitrage geography or network to free capital and reduce personal risk.
2. Build a free giveaway with real hard cost (reveal a problem, trial, or one step of multi-step).
3. Design the ascension: giveaway naturally creates the need for your paid product.
4. Add a 10× anchor / premium done-for-you tier as profit maximizer.
5. Announce the free thing to your existing contacts/list/socials and route into the offer (e.g., 3-email blast → webinar).

## Mistakes he warns against
- Lame giveaways: too afraid to give value, or giving something without real value.
- Asking for a raise as the main income lever.
- Assuming your best-selling product is your profit driver.
- Not having an email list ("shame on you").
- Network arbitrage by living above means — worked for one founder but "so risky"; he wouldn't do it.

## Examples / case details
- "Zip code tax": contractors bid higher at his Austin mansion.
- Elevator-pitch founder living in his high-rise above his means; Hormozi invested; funded the software company from people in the building.
- Pre-gym: $400/mo spare bedroom, Costco-only food, ~$50K saved, ~$4K/mo from online training.
- Six Week Challenge: pay up front, money back if completed.
- Gym Launch Secrets: ~400-page book with ads, scripts, lobby setup, upsells, pricing packages; sales team asked for more books; led to Gym Launch licensing.
- ALAN: free coaching/course for software users → $0 to $1.7M/month within six months.
- Prestige Labs: free "S3" supplement selling system (held back 2 years); pricing gave trained sellers a discount far below retail; reps trained gym teams free.
- Skool: $100K or a Cybertruck to whoever builds the biggest group in 30 days (three run so far).
- Acquisition.com: free books and a free ~14-hour scaling roadmap (~30 pages per stage).
- Nusr-Et (Salt Bae): ~$90–100 steaks; gold-foil steaks $2–3K (luxury).
- Friend's B2C business: $500/mo core, added $5,000/mo tier; people bought it and more bought the old top package.
- Largest portfolio company: 3-email blast → webinar → $2.3M (list of millions).
