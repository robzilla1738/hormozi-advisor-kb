## Core thesis
A walk with an 18-year-old neighbor (entrepreneurial, struggling in his first college semester) becomes a general decision process: clarify the outcome you want, pick the path that logically gets you closer, then find the emotional reason you're not taking it — usually fear of being seen as a failure. Big goals require trade-offs and "unreasonable sacrifice"; nothing great comes from balance, at least for a season.

## Frameworks & models
- **Logic → emotion decision process**: (1) What outcome do you want? (2) Which path gets you closer? (3) Why aren't you taking it? Name the fear.
- **Socially acceptable shield**: people pick respectable options (e.g., college) to avoid confronting not being good enough yet at the real goal.
- **Out-sacrifice, not out-work**: "I will not outwork everyone, but instead I will outsacrifice anyone."
- **Story/identity**: the story you tell about why you chose shapes who you become (Matrix: "unafraid ... because of the path that lies behind me").
- **Maybe vs. never**: take a maybe over a never.
- **Seasons vs. whole life**: imbalance can be a season, not forever.
- **Rocky montage period**: most hyper-successful people endure a 3–5-year grind of getting hit.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Compare options by which gets you closer to the goal with the same 4 years and ~$200K (college vs. investing it into building a business).
- Failing doesn't make you a failure; it means you failed.
- Write down explicitly what you're willing to give up.
- Ask whether it's reasonable that someone who sacrifices more (codes 16 hrs/day vs. 2) ends up far ahead — then take the reasonable bet.

## Playbook steps
1. Define the desired outcome.
2. Compare paths logically.
3. Identify the emotional blocker; name the fear.
4. List the sacrifices required and decide if you'll make them.
5. Commit for a season and choose the story you'll tell.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Making decisions for socially acceptable rather than real reasons.
- Keeping the real goal as a side hustle so failure doesn't count.
- Chasing balance while pursuing unreasonable goals.
- Letting life make decisions for you by default.
