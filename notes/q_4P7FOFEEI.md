## Core thesis
This is a casual behind-the-scenes conversation before the $100M Money Models launch. It has several speakers: Alex, Leila Hormozi and team members. Alex explains the goal of a $100M launch and how much he prepared: he scaled prep to audience size and personally wrote every asset. He also talks about grinding through a mountain of work and the expected emotional drop after the event. It has little tactical content.

## Frameworks & models
- **Harry Potter / series compounding**: later books outsell earlier ones only if you keep over-delivering. Multiple good books create "surface area" for people to come into contact with the brand.
- **Prep scales with audience**: he'd prep a week or more for a 5,000-person stage, and expected 300–500K live viewers, so he did roughly 100× the prep. He says that's about how much he prepared.
- **Post-climax dip**: expect a physiological "down" after a big event. Spreading the event over 3 days softens it.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Target: $100M launch ≈ 3.3M books. He says that would be the 3rd or 4th biggest book of all time, about double the nonfiction record (figures as stated in the conversation).
- Do the math on vendor capacity yourself rather than trusting assurances.

## Playbook steps
- [inference] For a launch: build the brand and content first, publish several good books as surface area, have the founder own all the copy, and check that fulfillment capacity matches the sales target.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Accepting vendors' "everything will be fine" without doing the math.
- Complaining about being tired while others face bigger burdens, such as a teammate with a 1-year-old.

## Examples / case details
- Alex wrote the Money Models book, an affiliate-gift audiobook (for affiliates who refer 10+ people), a 24-chapter "secret" book, the 12 Playbooks (~600 pages), rewrites of the ACQ closer handbook and ads handbook, all page, email and text copy, every VSL script (affiliate, sales and thank-you pages, onboarding), and the webinar.
- Warehouse: they planned 20 extra workers at $20/hr = $400/hr × 8 hrs = $3,200/day. He pushed back because that's far too little for ~3M books.
- His sleep log was mostly 5–6.5 hours a night during the build.
- Leila says Money Models is like Offers but easier to apply, because you can use it whether or not you already have something.
