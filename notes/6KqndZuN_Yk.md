## Core thesis
A year plan in four steps: **eliminate distractions → get started → get better → never stop.** What you spend your calendar and money on predicts next year's results. Bet your savings on your own skills (education beats the S&P), add reps with an improvement loop, focus by saying no to good opportunities, and outlast everyone. The video was posted at the end of 2024, so it refers to "2025" even though the title says 2026.

## Frameworks & models
- **Father's line**: "Rich people buy time. Poor people buy stuff. Ambitious people buy skills. Lazy people buy distraction."
- **Commitment = elimination of alternatives.** Focus is what's left after you remove everything else (Seinfeld locks himself in a windowless room with only a notepad, so writing happens by default).
- **5 friend questions**: Would you take "you're like them" as a compliment? Is it fulfillment or just a cure for loneliness? Can you be yourself? Do you like who they are now or who they could be? Would you want your child to be friends with them? His filter for close people: the same *scale* of goals and the same dedication to them.
- **Reference group**: he says a Harvard study found the reference group (the people you compare yourself to or want to impress, not the 5 you spend time with) predicts long-term material success. For big decisions, listen to the people closest to your goals, not the people closest to you. Borrow a measuring stick: "Is this the standard Bezos/Jobs would pursue?"
- **One razor**: does this thing, person or belief make it more or less likely I hit my goals? Identity is a lie. Doing is being. (He thought he was bad at math, then used no calculator for years.)
- **Ignorance tax**: a speaker asked a woman earning $50K what she'd pay to earn $1M; she "pays reality $950K/yr" for not knowing how. Skills are the closest thing to buying time: "buy the future at a discount."
- **SM500 > S&P 500**: $10K in the index vs. $10K spent on skills that take income from $50K to $100K. Then spend the new $50K on 10K/25K chunks to go to $250K. (In the video he says 10% turns $10K into $10,900; at 10% it would be $11,000, so the $900 is a slip. [inference])
- **Opportunity curve**: uninformed optimism → informed pessimism → valley of despair (most quit here, or jump to a new thing's optimism) → upside → goal.
- **Declarative vs. procedural knowledge**: knowing *about* vs. knowing *how*. 100 cold calls teach more than 100 books.
- **Hard ends 3 ways**: you quit, it gets easier, or you get better (the gold-mine analogy).
- **When it's easy, do more. When it's hard, do different.**
- **Pull the future forward / micro speed, macro patience**: break work into real hours. Replace "by end of week" with "it takes 3–4 hrs, so done by noon, next by end of day" (two week-long cycles done in one day). Then cut, automate, delegate or delete. "I reject the timeline, but I accept the skills."
- **Culture = the rules that govern reinforcement** (spoken and unspoken). These differ by team depending on who controls the reinforcers.
- **Lazy / losers / legends**: lazy don't know how to start, losers how to finish, legends how to stop.
- **Loop**: find what works → do more → find what prevents more → solve it → repeat.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If it's important for future you, do it today. Don't wait for Jan 1.
- About 20 hours of focused effort gets you decent at any skill.
- Early on, spend your savings on education every year "until you can't even spend the money." Make money while awake before chasing passive income. Passive investing only gets the leftover after reinvesting in skills.
- Keep a monthly **education investment budget** (e.g., cut expenses to free about $1K/mo). Much of the value is the connections in the room. He traded his sales skill for others' skills.
- Be the program's #1 success story. You learn from bad teachers too ("getting good is the removal of bad").
- Move geographically to be near better people. He moved to SoCal, "the capital of fitness."
- Try 10× harder than competitors. Work = output, not effort.
- Focus is measured by the quality and quantity of what you say no to. At $10K/mo you'll be saying no to other $10K/mo opportunities; at $100K/mo, to $100K/mo ones.
- Sustainable pace for him: 10–12 hrs/day, 7 days a week. About 18 hrs/day was too much. He does a "three shifts" sprint about once a year for 6–8 weeks. The goal is total net output over the longest time. Sleep and rest as needed.
- Challenge: work 10–12 hrs/day for 30 days straight once, to learn that you have the gear (he cites China's 996 as the bar).
- "If you don't know why you believe what you believe, it's not your belief."
- Before saying "X doesn't work," restate it as "I don't know how to make it work."
- Ask how you can learn 10 more skills, not for a role that fits your one skill.

## Playbook steps
1. Audit your calendar and card statement. Cut distractions, bad friends and their opinions, and limiting "isms."
2. Phone: grayscale (he says this cuts use about 30%), all notifications off, a do-not-disturb mode where no one gets through, and eliminate whole channels (he doesn't use email).
3. Time-block with a physical timer and pause it on every distraction to see your real working time.
4. Start today. Do the first 20 hours.
5. Invest in skills (coaches, masterminds, conferences). Do reps with feedback ("great, do it again").
6. Say no to shiny opportunities. Double down when it starts working.
7. Never stop. Write down your reasons for sticking with it and revisit them.
- **His 13 rules for a Skool 90-day sprint group** (100 picked from 500+ applicants): follow instructions; Google first; post what you figure out; Rule of 100 daily (100 reach-outs, or a 100-minute post, or $100+ on ads, plus 100 min/day improving ads); excitement ends in a week and that's where the work begins; your work works on you; everything is unscalable at first; the pain of repetition forces improvement ("find the pain"); complain and you're dead to me; thousands have succeeded, so repeat their activities; write down your reasons to stick with it; business is simple but hard, and the hard part is consistency; just win.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Waiting for New Year's, or telling yourself "you're young, you've got time."
- Wanting credit for trying, and entitlement. Underestimating the price tag (it's harder, takes longer and costs more than you think, which is an expectations problem).
- Sprinting a marathon of unknown length.
- Follow-your-passion advice from people who have already delegated everything ("doing what they do vs. doing what they did to get there").
- Betting your last $5–10K on Dogecoin or meme schemes instead of yourself.
- Opportunity-hopping at the valley of despair.
- Mistaking watching videos for knowing how.
- Complaining. Asking the world to meet you where you are.

## Examples / case details
- His father: failed premed in Paris 2 years (no French), succeeded in Belgium, graduated in 7 years, then 2 more years in the US working as a slide courier before residency. He later built his own surgery center as his own general contractor rather than pay about $250K. "Education is the only thing no one can take from you."
- At 23, Alex had $50K saved and paid $10K to join a gym-owner mastermind before owning a gym. His first location was profitable and outsourced by month 9; some members at year 5 still hadn't done that.
- Losses: his first 2 real estate deals, crypto, millions in bad hires, 6 failed businesses, 9 failed partnerships.
- Podcast started July 2017, 90 days after losing everything. It stayed under about 3,000 downloads/mo for about 3 years (300–400 episodes), and later reached 1M+/mo.
- About 3 months into Gym Launch (pacing $300–400K/mo, rent $1,200), he wanted to start a supplement company. An 8-figure founder told him "when it gets easy is when you go hard." He and Leila worked "like someone was trying to put us out of business."
- He took about 10–11 days off in 2024 (a day off = under 4 hrs of work). He left a $5–10K/night resort vacation after 1 day of a planned 2.
- Current skills he's working on: standardizing reinforcement rules across a scaling company, and top-level recruiting ("the best talent is always yet to come").
