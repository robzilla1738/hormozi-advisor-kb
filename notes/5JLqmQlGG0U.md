# If I Wanted to Get Rich and Famous, This Is What I'd Do

Signal: medium

## Core thesis
Personal-brand lessons from Taylor Swift, The Rock, MrBeast, Joe Rogan and Kylie Jenner, each applied to his own path. The common thread is longevity: every one of them is a decade-plus success. Compounding volume, being authentically yourself (you are the niche), monetizing only in ways that reinforce what people already love you for, owning your IP, and leverage. It closes with Munger-style inversion: the ways to guarantee a brand fails.

## Frameworks & models
- **Longevity/compounding**: don't compare your year 1 to someone's year 10. 52 vs 520 videos means both more assets bringing people in and more reps. "Start vs get serious": shrink the gap between starting and committing; on-and-off years don't count.
- **Scale the unscalable**: simulate a 1:1 experience at scale (Swift at fans' weddings; his live Q&As advising someone; free books and courses).
- **Vulnerability → relatability**: tell failure stories (money stolen twice, shut 8 of 9 businesses). Shared flaws build a fan army that defends you.
- **Ownership**: keep your rights rather than taking an upfront publisher check. Conditions change, and early creators sign rights away too soon.
- **Shifting identity/eras**: the brand should track who you really are. Forcing a persona makes you feel like a fraud as you drift from it.
- **Expanding niches**: widen step by step (Rock: football → WWE → physique movies → Disney). His version: fit guy → trainer → gym → gym licensing → businesses. Each move happened because someone handed him money and asked.
- **Monetize as "more of what you already are"**: products must reinforce existing associations (Rock + tequila). Off-brand high-ticket items (a suit line for him) destroy trust.
- **"Make the clickbait true"** (MrBeast): make the crazy claim, then back it up with something even crazier. Same for marketing.
- **Packaging**: the first 5 seconds must confirm the thumbnail. A/B test thumbnails on both CTR and watch time (20–30% watch-time swings from framing). Tie topics to timely hooks (luxury pricing → Arnault becoming the richest man).
- **You are the niche** (Rogan): a personal brand's niche is your unique mix and proportion of interests. Audience strength = how many of your interests a viewer shares. A business brand is instead built around the product and avatar. Teach, don't preach.
- **Leverage** (Kylie): proximity to people with exposure, media (record once, seen by millions), scalable products (a lip kit, not a tax service), and outsourcing weaknesses through partners. "I'd rather own 20% of a $20B company than 100% of a $1M company."
- **Direct distribution**: platforms cut out middlemen, so creators capture the economics. Audiences also give access to other people (CEOs say yes).
- **Inversion: how to guarantee failure**: talk about things you don't know; be inconsistent or never start; sell too early; sell the wrong thing (off-niche sponsors); associate with the wrong people; wait for perfect conditions; never change. Flip each one to get the recipe.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Choosing between reputation and money: always reputation. "You can't file bankruptcy on a reputation."
- Only promote what requires minimal change in your behavior. Limit "brand bullets" to about 2–5 interests.
- Provide far more value than you ask for before selling.
- Align with what the platform wants (engaging content), not algorithm hacks, follow-for-follow or giveaways, which exploit flaws that get fixed.
- Commit to a long horizon. He told his first YouTube hire he'd do it 10 years.
- Learn platforms by paying someone who knows them to teach you.
- Big goals: don't take advice from people projecting their smaller goals onto you.

## Playbook steps
1. Pick the topics you know and care about (your unique mix). 2. Start now with what you have (webcam). 3. Post consistently for years and reinvest in the compounding vehicle. 4. Package well: thumbnail promise confirmed in the first 5s; split test. 5. Share failures. 6. Give value far beyond any ask. 7. Monetize late and on-brand, keeping ownership. 8. Associate with aligned creators. 9. Adapt to new platforms; let the brand evolve with you.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Comparing yourself to someone's 10th year; the "overnight success" bias.
- Trend-hopping and algorithm hacks.
- Selling early or off-brand; shilling 100 things.
- Waiting for perfect conditions; not being willing to suck at the start.
- Building a persona you'll outgrow.
- Creators with national brands picking low-leverage vehicles (e.g. a restaurant).

## Examples / case details
- YouTube subs: ~88K in year 1, 600K+ in year 2, 1.1M+ so far this year. Podcast: ~2K downloads/month after 2 years, ~2M/month at year 6 (first podcast July 2017).
- Wrote ~6 hrs/day for almost 2 years on $100M Leads. Early career: worked 4–10am on his own building, then ran the business.
- Went from consultant (on his way to Harvard) to a minimum-wage trainer: "a season of learning not earning."
- Renamed "Gym Rescue" to "Gym Launch" because owners' egos rejected being "rescued."
- Brand eras: neon fitness look for a ~70% female audience → mustached "Gym Lord" for a ~90% male gym-owner audience → utilitarian owner/investor.
- MrBeast reinvests all profit into videos and monetizes elsewhere (Feastables). His first viral video was counting as high as he could, which cost nothing.
- Rock's ~30% Teremana stake; Rogan's Onnit exit; Rogan reportedly turned down sponsors he didn't believe in (the FTX example is Alex's speculation).
- Stats he cites: Goldman Sachs projects a ~$500B creator economy by 2027; YouTube ~2.6B MAU, IG 2.3B, TikTok 1.6B; YouTube adds $25B US GDP and 400K+ jobs.
