## Core thesis
A talk to gym owners (Gym Launch event). He spends five minutes on branding and says it won't make most local businesses more money; the rest is strategy. Strategy means setting priorities: find the one constraint and the move with the most return for the least effort. Then fix the product ("your sandwich") before pouring on marketing, raise your standards for people, do the Core Four at volume, and build simple, high-return locations. Captions read like a machine translation (odd phrasing), so the wording in quotes is approximate.

## Frameworks & models
- **Brand = bouquet of associations**: a brand is the intangible ideas linked to an unknown name (clean, friendly, giving first). One dead flower changes how the whole bouquet looks. Others try to attach their associations to yours. For local businesses, brand ≈ reputation ≈ consistently *meeting* expectations: make promises and keep them.
- **Magic wand / emotion diagnosis**: name the income gap. Anxiety = too many options and no priority, which is a strategy problem. Sadness/hopelessness = you don't see options, which is a knowledge problem you can solve by getting informed.
- **Strategy = priorities**: allocating limited resources among unlimited options. The most expensive thing is the "unknown unknowns," the options you didn't know existed.
- **Volume × Leverage = Result.** Low effort needs huge leverage. Other people's hours are leverage.
- **Theory of constraints**: one thing holds back growth. If you work all day and aren't growing, you're working on the wrong thing. Ask "why can't we grow this 10×?"
- **Three filters for any activity**: does it (1) get more customers, (2) raise LTV, or (3) reduce the risk that 1 and 2 stop, i.e. raise enterprise value? If none, throw it out. Pick the one with the highest probability and least effort.
- **What → Who → How**: list what must be done, then who on the team does it, not "me."
- **More → Better → New**: do more of what works, then do it better; only start something new once those are exhausted.
- **Impact × Ease ranking**: number the list; do high-impact/easy first; consume no new info until it's done.
- **Owner vs asset**: two businesses at $5M revenue/$2M profit. The team-run one is worth $10–20M. Going from $2M to $3M profit adds ~$10M of net worth. "You get rich by what you own, not what you do."
- **Intelligence = rate of learning; learning = same conditions, new behavior.** Training = recreate the condition and require the new behavior.
- **Lead sources**: the core four (warm outreach, cold outreach, paid ads, content) plus four kinds of "other people" who can do them for you: customers (referrals), partners/affiliates, employees, agencies. He says "eight ways."
- **Rule of 100**: every day, $100 in ads, OR 100 warm/cold contacts, OR 100 minutes of content.
- **Location economics**: compare opening cost to annual profit per location (e.g. $100K cost → $100K/yr = 100% cash return). He prefers a simpler model making $250–500K per location. Minimum footprint; can 6 staff become 3?

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Local business: build the brand through product/service quality, not daily Instagram nutrition content.
- If the same person has had the same task for 12 weeks (a quarter) and it isn't done, the doer is the problem.
- Underperformer: have the direct conversation and put them on a 14-day improvement plan. About 1 in 5 turn around; the other 4 you "walk out."
- If someone takes up mental space and isn't making you money, that's a cost (he says ~25% of your bandwidth).
- Don't rebrand or change the logo when nobody knows you yet.
- Fix retention/product before acquisition: "until people stop leaving," don't worry about new customers.
- LTV (lifetime gross profit) over everything. Whoever can spend the most to acquire a customer wins.
- Don't depend on one acquisition channel; all platforms get expensive. Safe = you'd be fine for 3–4 months with no new customers.
- Expect ~2 years of doing the known "shoulds" to build an exceptional business. Chasing 10× in 90 days is what kills people.

## Playbook steps
1. Name your income goal and gap. 2. Diagnose: options problem (strategy) or knowledge problem (learn). 3. Find the constraint ("why not 10×?"). 4. Filter tasks: customers / LTV / risk. 5. Rank by impact × ease; assign who. 6. More, then better, then new. 7. Fix the product, experience and retention ("eat your own sandwich"). 8. Run the Core Four at Rule-of-100 volume, then lever through customers, partners, employees and agencies. 9. To scale locations, simplify to a minimum-footprint, high cash-return model, then build launch teams.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Thinking marketing is the problem when the product is mediocre ("Have you tasted your sandwich?"). Marketing a mediocre product just tells everyone you're mediocre.
- Ego tied to the business, so the owner can't see obvious flaws.
- Tolerating mediocre staff and avoiding hard conversations. Excusing people for "good intentions."
- Chasing new and shiny things (TikTok, magic bullets) over doubling what works.
- Optimizing taxes on income instead of building asset value.
- Trying to scale or franchise a model with bad unit economics.

## Examples / case details
- Acquisition.com put out ~250 pieces of content/week, heading toward ~1,000.
- Photo studio agency charged ~$5K to help studios add ~$400K/yr. Instead they owned studios: 77 locations, growing from ~$1.5M/yr to ~$2.5M/month over ~2.5 years.
- PR firm: 85% of clients left within 2–3 months. They sold only to the best 15%, narrowed the message and raised price 10×. The business sat near zero for ~9 months, then grew ~10×.
- Dental buying association (savings-pooling model): idea to pool practices and sell the consolidation mechanism at a 10× multiple instead of a ~$20–30M sale. [inference: the numbers in the caption are garbled]
- YouTubers with 40M+ subscribers wanted better media. He said they were "97/100 on media, 0/100 on products"; the constraint was having nothing to sell.
- Gym Launch went from ~$3M to $37M because of Leila. It grew ~40% after the sale under CEO Cale through many small improvements, e.g. doubling cold calling instead of trying TikTok.
- $100M Offers: sold at $0.99 with one post to ~20K IG followers. ~25K copies/month (growing each month), ~14K five-star reviews, no ad spend.
- GMAT: 4 hrs/day for 16 weeks of practice problems. Volume of problems correlated with score.
- Partner idea: chiropractors and massage studios within 5 miles give their clients a free month.
- Franchise with ~20 locations (6 corporate, 14 franchise): ~$1M to open, ~$1.3M revenue, ~$150K profit (~11.5% margin). It had tried for 10 years to market the franchise. His fix: cut a 500-item menu to the top 15, consolidate suppliers, find +1% profit every month for 12 months.
