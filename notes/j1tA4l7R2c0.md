# How To Close Prospects By Saying "No" First (ALEX HORMOZI)
id: j1tA4l7R2c0 / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j1tA4l7R2c0 / duration: 494s (~8 min) / tags: 05-sales-process, 02-offer-design, 10-money-cashflow
Signal: medium

## Core thesis
For small add-on upsells, frame the recommended add-on as already included, then ask a negative question: "You don't want anything else, do you?" When the customer says "No," they are agreeing to the add-on. People are wired to resist being sold, so this assumptive close uses that reflex. Alex says typical upsell uptake is 20–30%, while this technique gets 90%+.

## Frameworks & models
- **Yes-by-saying-no (assumptive close)**: state the recommended add-ons as part of the plan, then ask "you don't want anything else, do you?" That "no" closes what you just recommended.
- **Variant**: "You don't want to pay full retail for that, right?" → "No." → "Cool, I'll send it with this discount to the address on file."
- **Fries & Coke**: small, high-margin bolt-ons drive profit and liquidate acquisition costs. "You don't make your money on the burger."
- **Free hook that carries a paid service**: the free item needs paid storage or conditioning, which covers its cost.
- **Inspection add-ons**: during inspection, point out small fixes ("want us to sew this seam?"), then repeat the "anything else?" question.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Use it for non-complex, hand-to-hand bolt-ons and upsells, not core-offer or complex sales.
- Benchmark: 20–30% upsell take rate is normal. 90%+ is possible with this tactic (his claim).

## Playbook steps
1. List the add-ons that pair with your core offer (supplements, meals, orthotics, braces, special toothpaste, night serums, a scarf with the outfit). 2. Present them as part of what they need. 3. Ask the negative "anything else, do you?" question. 4. Treat "no" as yes and proceed. 5. Add inspection-based extras and repeat.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Leaving small upsell profit pools untouched, which is common in brick-and-mortar service businesses.
- "My business is different." He says winners ask how they could use an idea, and losers say it wouldn't work for them.

## Examples / case details
- Story from Richard Schwarz of Manos Schwarz furriers, a fourth-generation Baltimore furrier that dominated the Maryland/Pennsylvania market by doing the boring everyday work better. At 17–18, Alex brushed coats in their warehouse and did back-of-napkin math on the business.
- Summer storage promo: free real-fur earmuffs if you store your coat. David (the son) would say "we'll store and condition your coat, you don't want anything else, do you?" Conditioning was an upsell that added a couple hundred dollars per coat across thousands of coats.
- The earmuffs then also needed paid storage and conditioning, which covered their cost.

## Notable lines
- "They don't know they're being sold."
- "You're getting them to say yes by saying no."
- "Winners always think, 'Man, I wonder how I could use that in my business.' And losers think that wouldn't work in my business."

[inference] This is a high-pressure framing tactic. Use it for add-ons the customer genuinely benefits from.
