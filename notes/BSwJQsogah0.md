## Core thesis
"How to make a million dollars" comes down to reverse math. Turn the annual goal into a weekly revenue target, divide by LTV for the units you need, then work back through close rate, show rate and outreach rate for daily activity. "Fuzzy targets don't get hit." The lower your LTV, the harder the math, so starting out, sell premium ($5K–$15K) and over-deliver. He is also candid that a high-paying job is underrated next to entrepreneurship.

## Frameworks & models
- **Goal decomposition**: $1M/yr → ~$83K/month → he uses ~$21K/week (≈$83K ÷ 4) → ~$3K+/day. [inference: exact math is ~$19.2K/week and ~$2.7K/day; his rounding is conservative.] Weekly is the best cycle until ~$10M+/yr; daily is too volatile.
- **Revenue vs take-home**: at 40% net margin (very good), $1M revenue gives $400K profit, which is roughly the US top-1% income.
- **Same $1,000 LTV, different shapes**: a $1K course; $100/mo gym × 10 months; $250 skincare order × 4 months. Starbucks LTV is ~$8K.
- **Funnel back-math (service)**: $21K/week ÷ $1K LTV = 21 units/week → ~5 deals/day (5-day week, overshoot) → close 1 in 3 = 15 shown calls/day → 66% show = ~22 set appointments → 50 outreaches per appointment ≈ 1,000+ outreaches/day. E-commerce: 1% conversion → 500 clicks/day for 5 sales.
- **Deal size as the main variable** for $1M/yr: about nine $10K deals/month, one $100K deal/month, ~83 $1K deals, or ~833 $100 units. Low price is far harder.
- **Salesperson version**: replace price with your commission. At $1K commission, $1M takes 83 units/month. Every role has a time cap.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Know your exact math; if you don't, "you have no chance."
- A $1K deal isn't worth hand-to-hand sales at that outreach volume; it needs to be closer to ~$10K.
- Starting out, aim for the $5K–$15K sweet spot, deliver tremendous value, and don't worry about scalability yet.
- Making a lot of money below a ~$1K LTV is "super super rare." Raise LTV with something unique, expensive, sticky and habit-forming; ticket price can stay low if repeat purchases make LTV high.
- Low-ticket ($10–$100/mo) needs heavy operational and support skill for volume, which is a different skill than acquisition.

## Playbook steps
1. Choose the goal (revenue or take-home; apply margin).
2. Convert it to a weekly target.
3. Divide by LTV for units per week, then per selling day.
4. Apply close rate → shows; show rate → appointments; outreach-to-appointment rate → daily outreach (or conversion rate → clicks).
5. If the activity is unrealistic, raise deal size or LTV.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Fuzzy goals with no unit math.
- Starting with low-ticket, high-volume models that need ops skill you don't have.
- Underrating a high-paying job. He says knowing the risk, he's not sure he'd start a business again.
