## Core thesis
"The single most valuable skill set you can have is getting other people to do things for you." When someone doesn't do what you asked, find which of five causes is behind it and fix that cause. Turn vague labels ("be charismatic," "stop being a dick," "motivated") into observable behaviors. Most training fails because it isn't broken down far enough. ## Frameworks & models
- **Management Diamond: 5 reasons someone didn't do it**: 1) didn't know what you wanted → communication; 2) didn't know how → training; 3) didn't know when → deadline; 4) not incentivized → motivation/reinforcement; 5) something blocking → remove the blocker. There can be more than one; fix all of them. This also works as a script for hard conversations ("I just want to figure out the reason so we can solve it").
- **Delegation vs abdication test**: if it gets better or stays the same after you hand it off, you delegated. If it gets worse, you abdicated.
- **Bundling / unbundling terms**: labels like charismatic or patient bundle many activities. Give the activities instead (vocal range, pause for emphasis, use names, eye contact, nod). Soft skills are just skills described in bundled language.
- **Checklist diagnosis**: score recorded calls against the checklist (Sarah does 3 of 11, Caroline 9 of 11). If someone does all 11 and still underperforms, the checklist is wrong, and that's on you.
- **Reinforcement**: behavior is driven by what happens *after* it. Motivation comes from deprivation (whatever the person lacks, e.g. praise). Money is a generalized reinforcer. Best performers find the work itself reinforcing. Roles with fast feedback loops (sales, editing) motivate themselves; managers must add feedback loops to long or complex jobs.
- **BS detector: Logic / Evidence / Utility** = "What does this mean? How do you know? So what?" (worked through "I'm stressed because I'm lonely").
- **Observable-only reasoning**: drop feelings, intentions and beliefs as explanations; reason from what you can observe (e.g. lead stages in the Leads book vs "seven stages of awareness").
- **Second-order response**: in a heated moment, ask "what do I want to have happen?" Counter-punishing reduces your influence.
- **Instruction specificity scales inversely with skill**: low-skill people need precise instructions; experts can take vague directives ("Market Skool").
- **Prerequisite skills / return on effort**: customers or employees who succeed already have the prerequisites. Teaching missing basics may not be worth the effort compared with selecting people who already have them.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Always attach a deadline when assigning work.
- Give feedback as "do these N things / stop these N things," never as a character label.
- If a high performer has a behavior problem, give them specific observable instructions before firing them.
- When training fails, assume you took a missing skill for granted.

## Playbook steps
1. Missed task → go through know-what / know-how / know-when / incentive / blocker. 2. Fix each cause found (write it down; don't say it once on Zoom and expect 100% retention). 3. Turn desired traits into behavior checklists. 4. Audit recordings against the checklist; fix the checklist if top compliance still underperforms. 5. Add feedback and reinforcement loops to slow-feedback roles. 6. Make instructions more or less specific depending on skill level.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Hiring "a human being that has a pulse," dumping info verbally once, then yelling two weeks later.
- Directives like "stop being a dick," "be patient," "be nicer."
- Insulting or punishing in the moment.
- Accepting confident statements ("there are only three ways...") without evidence.

## Examples / case details
- Editors worked from home because home upload speed was faster. He paid Google Fiber to run a line into the building, which removed the blocker.
- A high-potential employee acting "dickish" had already had talks with 4 leaders. Alex gave specific examples of behaviors to stop (e.g. at the start of calls), and within a month people said he'd "completely turned around."
- Onboarding checklist: greet by name, review notes and state the goal, no bed or pink bunny poster in the background.
