## Core thesis
Lessons from a mastermind of 8 founders doing $500M+/yr combined: the biggest ones sold simple, required zero of the founder ("scale zero"), went after oceans not lakes, focused on one business/one customer, and sold high-gross-margin, high-value offers.

## Frameworks & models
- **Scale Zero:** get the business to require zero of you; "for me to get bigger I must get smaller within the company." Test: if the business doubled, would your workload double? (20 hrs/week → 40.)
- **Outsourcing ladder:** admin → back-end customer service → sales → marketing → messaging/positioning → only the biggest decisions remain.
- **Oceans vs. lakes:** big players target big markets. Niching to a lake is right when starting (his gym niche), then expand once skilled (they later served agencies in 100+ niches).
- **One vehicle:** nearly everyone who made big money did it in one vehicle; owning pieces of others is fine but they're not active — hired CEO/exec team with equity.
- **"Decide" = to kill off** other options (-cide root).
- **High margin + high value:** all but one sold very high gross-margin offers; the two lowest-margin were the smaller businesses.
- **Talent ladder:** doers → managers → leaders → decision makers; hire people who've "already won the Olympic gold," "batteries included."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Being CEO of multiple businesses isn't feasible "for just about every human besides Elon Musk."
- Starting out → a lake (niche) is fine; at scale → ocean.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Running three businesses at once — he says it cost him ~2 years of development.
- Low-margin offers.

## Examples / case details
