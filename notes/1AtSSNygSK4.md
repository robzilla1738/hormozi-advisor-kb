## Core thesis
Business segment: if you own respected IP and a fan base (Stan's Vertical Diet), don't just sell to consumers. License the IP B2B through a certification for fitness professionals. That gives a high-margin front-end product at no incremental cost, and certified coaches become an affiliate army that brings clients into your recurring product (meals).

## Frameworks & models
- **Work backward from the highest value you can provide** to a person.
- **IP → certification → affiliate army**: license IP to hundreds of pros at no incremental cost. Possible structure: ~3 months on how to coach, ~3 months on business acumen to help them sell clients. They then sell your recurring product (meals) to their clients.
- **B2B certification economics**: his rough claim is better LTV:CAC because cost to acquire is "a fifth." Captions are garbled here.
- **Four promotion methods**: one-to-one outreach, list blasts, organic posts (YouTube/Instagram/TikTok), paid ads.
- **"Simplest business model possible"**: recurring meals plus a high-margin licensing front end, so you need far less volume.
- **Retrofit YouTube descriptions**: old videos still get daily views, so change every description's above-the-"see more" text to a CTA ("Attention fitness professionals who love the Vertical Diet… personally certifying a handful… click here"). Then a ~30-minute webinar qualifies them before they book a call.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Suggested cert price: ~$10K.
- Fitness (Stan): lots of low-fatigue, higher-rep work (15–20 reps), short rest, knee-dominant range, concentric/sled work. You keep size and much strength with less fatigue to sleep and well-being. [Stan's training views, not business advice.]

## Playbook steps
1. Package the IP into a certification. 2. Change the CTA in all existing YouTube video descriptions to point at it. 3. Send people to a ~30-min webinar, then a call. 4. Sell the cert (~$10K). 5. Certified pros sell your recurring product.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Comfort after past success (Stan's wife's critique, which Stan relays).
- Overtraining / chronic fatigue harming quality of life (Stan).
