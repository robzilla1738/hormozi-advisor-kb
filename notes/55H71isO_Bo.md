## Core thesis
Sleep is a business input. Poor sleep weakens impulse control (he says a sleep-deprived brain performs like a drunk one: more reactive and emotional) and intelligence (learning, memory, consolidation). The result is worse decisions and less money. He shares 50-plus personal sleep habits in four buckets: beliefs, environment, intake, habits. He frames them as 80/20 options, not doctrine. Personal-performance content, not business strategy. He credits the presentation to one given with Dr. Trevor Kashey [caption: "Trevor Cashy"] at a licensee meeting. "Not medical advice."

## Frameworks & models
- **Why it matters for entrepreneurs**: (1) impulse control; (2) intelligence. He claims as little as 2 hours of sleep deprivation cuts the ability to store, learn and recall almost in half, and that poor-quality sleep impairs learning even when hours are enough.
- **Four buckets**: Beliefs (8), Environment (9), Intake, Habits (13).
- **Remove "have to / must / should"**: each implies "…or what?" Spelling out the threat shows the belief is silly. Superstition about sleep creates the anxiety that ruins it.
- **"90% of the benefit is from being in a dark room with eyes closed"**: a belief he holds to cut sleep anxiety (presented as a belief, not a cited fact).
- **Peaks and troughs**: work and activity hard enough during the day to create high peaks, so the night trough is deep. The worst case is groggy all day and half-asleep all night.
- **One-time fix, permanent benefit**: environment changes are done once and pay forever. He draws the parallel to business: frontload work for enduring, compounding returns.
- **Caffeine half-life ~6 hours.**

## Decision rules & thresholds
- 80/20: improving 10–20% of 365 nights from one 20-minute video is a win. Pick one to four tactics, not all 55.
- Treat weekdays and weekends the same; his most productive work happens on weekends.
- Room ~67°F (typical 68–72). No LEDs or screens; phone out of the room if possible. Pitch black with blackout curtains and/or an eye mask. Weighted blanket ~10% of body weight. Invest in the mattress (a third of your life). Use white noise. Buy several pillows and keep the best. Consider separate bedrooms.
- No caffeine after 10 a.m. Last meal 2 hours before bed. No alcohol within 4 hours of bed. No liquids 3 hours before. Frontload water in the morning. Salt tabs (with Gatorade Zero) to reduce night urination. Eat at the same times daily. Avoid sleep-disrupting meds.
- Morning sun 20–30 minutes; ~1 hour of total sun daily; walk after meals. No workouts within 2 hours of bed. Screens off 2 hours before.
- Use a go-to-bed alarm, not a wake-up alarm; bed between 9 and 11. Naps no later than 2 p.m. and no longer than 30 minutes (he sets 20).
- Use rooms for single purposes (sleep room for sleep and sex) because activities anchor anxiety to a room. Short-term violations (hotels) are fine.
- Stop work after dinner (his own rule). Hot shower or bath before bed; meditate or read to unwind.

## Playbook steps
1. Drop superstitious beliefs and sleep tracking if it makes you obsess. 2. Make one-time environment fixes (cold, dark, quiet, mattress and pillow). 3. Adjust intake: caffeine cutoff, meal, alcohol and liquid timing, supplements by the bed or toothbrush so you don't forget. 4. Habits: sun, walks, bedtime alarm, wind-down. 5. Add more tactics only if you still struggle; drop the low-yield ones.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Treating the list as doctrine and judging yourself ("bad sleeper, therefore bad person").
- Obsessive sleep tracking. He stopped using rings and apps because competing for "sleep records" made him sleep worse.
- Thinking heavy caffeine users "pass right out" just fine; it means tolerance, not no effect.
- Late drinking: he says the hangover comes more from wrecked sleep than from the alcohol.
- Spending millions on a house and about $1,000 on a mattress.
