## Core thesis
"The reason you're not making money is because you're not that good — and that's okay," because you just started. You can't expect a top-1% income after a year in a game others have played for 20. The fix isn't a hack. It's deep, nuanced skill built through reps ("time under the bar"), which he calls a "Rocky cut scene" of 2 to 6 years. Speed it up by studying people who already succeed: replicate before you iterate. An older Gym Launch-era video, with captions in lowercase.

## Frameworks & models
- **Eskimo words for snow** (his hearsay, and he invites correction): experts see delineations that novices miss. "There's only free and non-free offers" vs "19 types of ways to monetize the word free." You only get that nuance from volume.
- **Vehicle viability test**: look for others in the industry doing what you do. If they succeed, the vehicle is viable and the problem is your skill.
- **Rocky cut scene**: every successful entrepreneur has a 2-, 4- or 6-year stretch nobody talks about, doing the same thing over and over until mastery, then moves the skill to a new vehicle and "blows up overnight."
- **Skill pyramid**: skill is the foundation. A six-month foundation caps how high you can build, while a deep one supports a skyscraper. "No one can take my experiences."
- **Conviction**: only two ways to get it: "be a sociopath and lie, or have done it."
- **Replicate before you iterate**: copy the successful person's system exactly until you match them; only then have you "earned the right to iterate."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Before you switch industries, check whether others succeed in yours. If they do, stay and get better.
- Learn from others' mistakes, and better still from their successes. Making every mistake yourself is "possibly the stupidest thing you can do."
- Don't change anything in a copied system until you perform as well as its source.
- To learn from an operator, offer real value (front desk, cleaning), not "I'll work for free to be around you." Pick people closer to your level, not Bill Gates.
- The market can tell trench knowledge from pretending.

## Playbook steps
1. Confirm the vehicle is viable (others are winning in it).
2. Find your best performer or customer and study them in detail.
3. For the real-estate agency example: fly out to the client doing $250K/month off your leads and record everything. Call frequency and intervals, first words, intonation, follow-up, where and how soon they meet (in person, Zoom or phone), the contract conversation, positioning in the room, the order of house showings, and how the deal is closed and paid.
4. Replicate exactly, put in the volume, then iterate.
5. Move the mastered skill to a new opportunity.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Quitting or pivoting industries after about 18 months because you're "not good yet."
- Shallow understanding ("he just calls them up").
- Hunting for the shortcut. His answer to "how do listeners shortcut it?" was "you're not."
- Arguing with experienced people from a position of no reps: "this is your first winter; I've lived in Alaska 20 years."
- Feeling owed success.
