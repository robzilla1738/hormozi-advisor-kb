## Core thesis
Don't build a sprawling menu to fit every budget. Sell one core thing (0–$1M) and later two (front end + back end, $1–10M). Make the offer solve the dream problem, then fit buyers' budgets with payment terms, not product tiers. Sales becomes "how would you like to pay," not "which one do you want."

## Frameworks & models
- **Accommodating buying curve**: richer buyers spend more, poorer still buy. Get there with payment terms, not more SKUs.
- **Stage rule**: $0–1M = one product/service, one avatar, one channel. ~$1–3M heading to $10M = add a second product line (front end + back end).
- **People look at the monthly cost, not total contract value**; for the owner, contract value matters most.
- **"The bigger the head, the longer the tail"**: the more paid up front relative to the recurring payment, the stickier the customer (e.g. $99/mo vs $5,000 down + $99/mo). Upfront cash also covers ad cost, sales commissions and onboarding cost.
- **Payment ladder for one offer** (e.g. 6-month implementation): pay in full; split pay; 3 payments (~20% higher); 6 payments.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If your employees can't explain all your products and prices, your customers can't either; simplify.
- More payment options → higher conversion ("that's proven"), but reps may default to the lower-cash options, so cash flow might not rise.
- Avoid low/medium/high product tiers chosen by the rep; reps can't decide what to sell. "A confused mind doesn't buy."
- With high gross margins, low monthly payments are fine if the customer stays a long time.
- Scope: aimed at founders below ~$3M/yr who aren't strong operators (his caveat).

## Playbook steps
1. Identify the true problem and avatar; make one offer that solves it and is the most valuable thing you sell.
2. Script sales around that one thing.
3. After the yes, offer payment terms (PIF, split, 3-pay at premium, 6-pay).
4. Push big upfront payments to fund CAC/commissions/onboarding and raise stickiness.
5. Ascend prepaid customers to the second product in a later conversation, solving the next natural problem.

## Mistakes he warns against
- 100 bolted-on products/services creating operational complexity and distracted staff.
- Good/better/best product tiers ("a diet, a medium and a great").
- Trying to sell multiple things in one conversation.

## Examples / case details
- Portfolio company was advised by another mentor to run low/medium/high products; Acquisition.com pushed one offer with payment-term variations instead.
- He says he has sold two things in "virtually every business" he's owned.
