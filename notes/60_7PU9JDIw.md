# My $100M Leads Affiliate Marketing Guide

## Core thesis
Behind the scenes of how he got ~10,000 people (9,000+ at recording) to promote the free virtual launch event for $100M Leads with zero payouts. With affiliates you have two customers — the affiliate and their audience — and the strategy works when it's a win-win-win: you win, the affiliate wins, and most importantly their audience wins. Lead with value, align incentives, make joining effortless, and keep affiliates promoting with reminders, fresh assets and status updates. Partly a promo for his affiliate page.

## Frameworks & models
- **Two avatars / two offers**: the offer to the affiliate, and the offer the affiliate makes to their audience on your behalf.
- **Affiliate types**: brick-and-mortar partnerships (massage therapist gives local corporations' new hires 2 free massages via onboarding packets; chiropractor's patients get a free massage, and refer back); influencer codes (straight paid endorsement ≈ paid ads; revenue split ≈ affiliate); internet-marketing list owners.
- **Two-tier prize design**: scarcity at the top for competitive, status-driven super affiliates (top 10 get a live AMA with him for their audience — scarce because he rarely speaks) + a tiny hurdle anyone can clear (bring 10+ people → two unreleased bonus chapters).
- **Incentive alignment**: prize benefits the affiliate's audience ("click my link so our community gets the AMA") rather than the affiliate ("help me win supercars"), so promoting adds goodwill instead of withdrawing it. Paying cash aligns you and the affiliate but not the audience.
- **Paid vs unpaid affiliates**: paid = money or collab swap; requires tax forms (1099), more friction but higher intent. Unpaid = bonuses/goodwill.
- **Friction dial**: want volume → make signup easier; want quality → harder. B2B/services: add friction to qualify. His mass-market book: name/email/phone only, ~30 seconds.
- **Value equation applied to signup**: dream outcome (become an affiliate), time delay 30 sec, effort = typing details, likelihood = click submit.
- **Whisper → Tease → Shout**: whisper 6+ weeks out (months/years; show you're working on it, no contents — the Godzilla foot poster); tease 6 weeks → 7 days (peel back a page, trailer); shout last 7 days (direct reminders, crescendo). Keep baseline value content constant throughout and layer promotion on top; ads should themselves be valuable like trailers.
- **Missionaries vs mercenaries**: recruit for the cause; mercenaries leave for the highest bidder and start a bidding war.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Shouting for 6 weeks burns people out; save shout for the final week.
- Shout effectiveness is predicted by the value given beforehand; without goodwill you end up shouting harder until no one listens.
- Ask rarely: he emailed his list only ~3–4 times in a year vs ~1,000 content pieces/month, and only for things ~99% will find worth it.
- To the event-attendee audience: 2, max 3 promotional messages over the campaign; assume customers read affiliate messaging (never "we're going to sell a ton of books").
- Set the cadence up front (e.g. 2 emails/week until the event) and keep it.
- Hold some assets back; release a fresh content packet ~3 weeks in so affiliates' promos don't go stale.
- Give affiliates tracking/status (where they stand).

## Playbook steps
1. Define the ideal affiliate and the offer to them + the offer to their audience.
2. Build a minimal opt-in giving a unique tracked link on the thank-you page AND by email.
3. Tiered prizes: scarce status prize at top, easy universal prize.
4. Recruit (two emails to his list → ~9,000 sign-ups; most from the list).
5. Run two concurrent campaigns: to end customers and to affiliates (reminders, small tokens like Starbucks, check-ins).
6. 2 emails/week to affiliates: one content packet, one leaderboard/status update.
7. Final days: extra reminders; after the event, announce winners and deliver prizes.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Spamming the end audience until it loses goodwill.
- Asking without having provided value first.
- Promoting things you can't vouch for to your own audience (he declines reciprocal promos to protect trust).
- Treating affiliates' customers worse than they expect — affiliates hold you to a higher standard than they hold themselves.

## Examples / case details
- $100M Offers launched at $0.99 with a free course to demonstrate the value equation; the Leads launch demonstrated lead-gen methods (incl. affiliates) on itself.
- Putting the link in both the thank-you page and an email: ~25% difference in how many promote; changing the banner above the submit form lifted affiliate sign-ups 60%.
- He guesses ~1/3 of the 9,000 signed up just to watch his process — fine with him.
- Top affiliates had each sent ~1,000 registrants; affiliate pressure led him to reword his own promotion.
