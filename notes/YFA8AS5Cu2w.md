## Core thesis
Advice to Avante, a young father working two low-wage jobs with a 1-year-old. Assume luck doesn't apply to you. Your only asset is time with low skills, so drive costs to the floor, buy back time to learn one valuable skill (sales recommended), trade up to higher-paying jobs through relentless personalized applications, then out-work the top performer for years. "Volume negates luck."

## Frameworks & models
- **Three steps**: (1) cut all costs: food and shelter only, no clothes for 2 years, live with family or another saving family; (2) increase income: at-home phone sales starter roles pay $40K+ with no experience, plus Uber; (3) sell someone else's stuff first, then buy cheap and flip, trading up from cards → furniture → homes, and eventually sell your own.
- **"Sadness comes from a lack of options... this is ignorance, not sadness."** Ignorance is controllable.
- **Two levels of balance**: macro (over your lifespan) and family-unit (one partner leans into career, the other into the kid, for a season).
- **Wallet of money, wallet of minutes**: everyone you spend either on must help you get where you're going.
- **"99% right but 100% wrong"**: naysayers are right about most attempts but wrong about the one that works (like a parent predicting every breakup).
- **Kobe effect**: match the top performer's effort, then add your "get ahead" effort on top. "When he clocks out is when your work clocks in."
- **Rejection asymmetry**: a no leaves you where you were, plus experience; one yes can change your life. You either learn or win.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Use a $5 timer, not your phone: 4 hrs/day of focused study on ONE skill. About 20 hours gets the basics; then spend the time becoming the best applicant.
- Message 4 people per company individually on LinkedIn (recruiter, head of talent, director), plus a custom cover letter tied to the job description. Scale to ~10 companies/day (~40 messages). For roles you really want, visit in person after your shift.
- For low-level roles, hustle matters more than the resume.
- Buy a $5–10K car in cash. No payments.
- If people around you don't have bigger dreams for you than you do, ignore their advice. "The best way to stay poor is to listen to poor people about how to get rich."
- Commit to a ~36-month season.

## Playbook steps
1. Cut costs to food and shelter. 2. Get a sales or higher-pay job; drive Uber in the gaps. 3. Do 4 timed hours/day on one skill. 4. Make high-volume personalized applications plus in-person follow-up. 5. Out-work the top person for 1–2+ years. 6. Trade up: sell others' stuff → flip → sell your own.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Spending on nice clothes while broke.
- Copy-pasting the same message to everyone at a company.
- Listening to broke friends and family.
- Dabbling: "You're going to blink and be 30 and in the same spot but worse."

## Examples / case details
- Alex owned a $5–6K car in cash and paid $400/mo sharing a room in a 6-person house (dogs everywhere) so he could afford courses and seminars.
- He told his 15–16-year-old mentee Jacob "volume negates luck."
- Avante asked to work for Alex for free. Alex: "I have no need for low-skilled labor, but I do have a need for high-skilled beasts."
