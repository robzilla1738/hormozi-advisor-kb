## Core thesis
Half of making more money is making customers more valuable, not just acquiring more. The "Service Delivery Cube" is a creative checklist for repackaging your existing resources ("sawdust"/excess capacity) into new or more valuable offers without new cost or liability: place your current product on each of 6 dimensions and ask "can I move one left or one right?"

## Frameworks & models
**Service Delivery Cube (6 sides)** — for service businesses, not physical products:
1. **Ratio:** 1-on-1 → small group → 1-to-many (unlimited).
2. **Client effort:** do-it-yourself → done-with-you → done-for-you.
3. **Support channel/level:** text, chat, email, expert rep, phone, Zoom. Tier it, e.g. lower tier = email only, higher tier = phone + Zoom.
4. **Consumption format:** live vs recorded × written / audio / video.
5. **Speed & convenience:** "fast beats free". Can you deliver the result in half the time, or answer support faster?
6. **10x / 1/10th thought exercise:** If I charged 10x, what would I include? If I had to charge 1/10th and still deliver more value than now, how? Ideas from both ends can often go into the current offer.
- **Sales-to-fulfillment continuum (mentioned):** the easier something is to fulfill, the harder it is to sell. DIY is hardest to sell and easiest to deliver, DFY is easiest to sell and most operationally complex. More DIY = more of a marketing/acquisition company.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Example tiering: normal support replies in 24 hrs, VIP support replies in 60 minutes or less. Same resources, different expectation, higher price.
- "One of the easiest things in the world": take any service category and deliver the result in half the time.

## Playbook steps
1. List current products.
2. Locate each on all 6 dimensions.
3. For each, try moving one step either way. Note offers that add value at low or zero added cost.
4. Run the 10x and 1/10th exercises and port any ideas back into the current offer.
5. Use it in team meetings about "how can we provide more value".

## Examples / case details
- Lumber mill sawdust → sandpaper and pellets. Uber vs walking, FedEx vs USPS, Spotify vs free downloads, Amazon Prime 2-day → 1-day → hours.
- At the time: $120M in sales across 4 companies over 4 years.
