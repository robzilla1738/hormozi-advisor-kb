## Core thesis
This is a compilation of his sales talks. His core claims: sales is moving belief across a bridge of trust. Selling happens before the ask and closing after it. Prospects give their power away to circumstances (time, money, fit), then to other people (authority), then to themselves (avoidance). A closer peels back that "onion of blame" with questions, stories and logic, and asks again after every resolved concern. The talks also cover team-level throughput: availability, show rate, scripts, conviction, daily drilling, comp, culture. Four portfolio case studies quantify the results: show/close fixes, the diagnostic sale, payment-plan-to-recurring, and replacing an outsourced team with an in-house one.

## Frameworks & models
- **Selling = transference of belief over a bridge of trust.** Both belief and trust are continuums. Cold streaks come from lost belief, not lost skill.
- **Obstacles vs objections**: obstacles come up before price and are easier to handle; objections come up after. **"Kill zombies up front"** (also "defuse the bomb"): confront the spouse, time or money issue before you name the price.
- **Onion of blame** (from Albert Ellis's CBT distortions): circumstances → others → self. Five buckets: Time, Money/Value, Fit, Authority, Avoidance. Each layer down is closer to someone who can decide.
  - **Time**: macro ("busy season": you'll always be busy, so start now while support is highest) vs micro (screen time; good programs cut what isn't working). Also the "when/then fallacy."
  - **Money**: "Is this a lot to you? Good, you'll go all in." Ask absolute vs relative ("worth it if it makes $10K/mo?" means it's belief, not price). You pay with money or time. "Resourcefulness, not resources." The Ferrari-for-$5K and surprise-tax-bill logic: you'd find the money. A price objection means you failed to show the value.
  - **Fit**: new identity, new priorities. Use "change the change" and the pain of staying the same vs the pain of change. Ask "if this were perfect would you do it?" A no means a trust problem; a yes means find the gap.
  - **Authority**: "support, not permission." "What if they say no?" (about 1 in 3 say they'd do it anyway). Divert with "what would their objection be?" Use past agreements ("does he approve of the current struggle?"). Then ask forgiveness rather than permission, or do a **delayed close**: card down, charge Friday, tear it up if the spouse says no.
  - **Avoidance** (past/present/future):
    - Past: "not a fast decision, you've decided for years"; sunk cost; "don't let a bad decision burn you twice"; "another year of almost."
    - Present: the **rocking chair** ("it takes information, not time, to decide, and I'm your only source"); teach the decision with three questions (will it get you there? do you trust us? will it work for *you*?) plus access to funds; "you can only make an informed decision on the inside" (trial/guarantee); "decide" = to cut off.
    - Future: "five more years like this?"; the **options close** (do it + succeed, skip it + fail, do it + fail but guaranteed: only walking out guarantees no result); "closer or further from your goal?"; "the reason you won't is the reason you should."
- **Best case / worst case: "let's play it out."** He says it closed more sales than any other close (the Dave Ramsey envelope prospect). Pair it with "what are you most afraid of having happen?"
- **Looping**: resolve the concern, ask again. You can do it any number of times as long as each concern gets resolved.
- **CLOSER**:
  - **C**larify why they're here
  - **L**abel the problem (they must say it)
  - **O**verview past pain using the pain cycle: what have you tried, for how long, how long ago, how did it work, what else?
  - **S**ell the vacation, not the plane flight. Keep it to 3 pillars told as stories, pitch ≤3 min. Tiers are swim, boat, flight or private jet to the same Maui.
  - **E**xplain away concerns
  - **R**einforce the decision. Selling continues through onboarding, and customers decide in the first 48 hours whether to stay.
  - It works B2C and B2B, from $500 to $100K.
- **Only three things come out of a closer's mouth**: questions, restatements, anecdotal stories. Answer questions with questions ("be like smoke"). Best reps listen about 2× as much as they talk (AI call analysis).
- **Conviction framework**: words are about 10% and tone about 90% (from Jordan Belfort's book). "Conviction corrects your tone." The most important part of the sale is the product.
- **Three jobs of a salesperson**: maximize opportunities, convert the highest %, do it consistently over time.
- **Dogs / horses / tigers** (from Mike Ary): hire tigers who "kill for sport."
- **Diagnostic sale**:
  1. Pre-sale questionnaire that agitates the pain
  2. Info plus card on file ("setting up your profile")
  3. Current state
  4. Desired state
  5. Obstacle
  6. Present the vehicle with price tied to the goal and a guarantee
  7. Prepay incentive
  8. Transition to recurring

  Sales models run transactional ↔ custom ↔ enterprise. The magic is a standard product that feels custom. Let the customer self-identify on a chart ("the scale called you fat, not me"; Yogurtland self-serve).
- **Price presentation**: anchor at the full payment-plan price, then offer prepay discounts (e.g. $4,500 full / –10% half down / –20% prepaid ≈ $3,600). Never quote low and add "interest." Say "prepayment discount," not "paid in full."
- **Step-downs**: prepay → third-party financing → partial with a smaller discount → payment plan/continuity → book the next visit. "Everyone buys something."
- **Six C's for scaling a team**: Closer sequence (question-based script), Consistent daily training + Conviction, Call recordings (game tape), Communication cycles/feedback (one fix at a time), Cut the fat fast, Competition + Career path.
- **Comp**: money plus non-money (recognition, leaderboard, perks, status stripes). "Winners win" ratchets (e.g. keep your job at 50% close, 15% commission at 70%, 25% above 80%; he calls these arbitrary illustrative numbers). Pay setters too.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Benchmarks:
  - **Show rate ≥70%** for appointment businesses.
  - Close rate around 40% for a two-call mid-priced consumer service. In-person low-ticket can reach 80%+; investments over the phone about 5%.
- **Total available time slots** was the strongest predictor of sales across about 4,000 appointments/day at Allan. Offer same-day/next-day slots, not 5+ days out. **Pull appointments forward**, push unconfirmed ones and refill the slot. Send a personalized morning-of reminder (voice or video), double dial, and contact leads fast.
- A +20% schedule or show rate is worth as much as a +20% close rate.
- Track close rate both off shows and off offers. A rep with a low offer rate and near-100% close is cherry-picking.
- Optimize ads for **cost per sale**, not cost per lead or appointment.
- Prep about 10% of call length for repeat calls (60 min → 6 min). Rare high-stakes meetings flip it (about 6 hrs of prep per 1 hr).
- Pitch ≤3 minutes. Never ask for the sale without clarity on why they're there. Don't ask unless you're fairly sure they'll buy (lawyer rule).
- Consumer transactional sales under about $1–2K: handle the spouse on the call. B2B multi-call: bring stakeholders in. Before the appointment, ask "anyone else needed to decide?" and reschedule to include them if so.
- **Upsells should be about 5× the core price**: if ~20% take it, the average ticket doubles.
- Commission target is **under ~10% of revenue** when the company supplies the leads. Self-generating reps cost more.
- **Cut reps who can't close within ~14 days** (1–2 weeks). It's easier to take a 6 to a 9 than a 2 to a 5.
- **No prima donnas**: a rep who triples output but skips the process usually drags the whole team down.
- Hiring:
  - "Hire one to hire 10." Hire the sales director first; a recurring problem under the same person is a who problem, not a what problem.
  - Judge skill by the quality and quantity of metrics a candidate tracks and how they tie them to revenue.
  - Sales leaders are servant-type, even-keeled and repetitive, a different profile from killer closers.
  - Recruit leaders by outreach or recruiter, not ads. Reach out to 100–1,000 people.
- Run competitions on 6-week cycles with mixed teams. Use career-path micro-raises (e.g. $500 → $525 per deal at 50/100/150 deals).
- Keep inbound and outbound setters/closers separate.
- Training: 60 min/day, 5 days/week. That's 25 min reading the script aloud with tonality, 5 min drilling obstacles, and a 30-min call review covering what went right, what went wrong, and what you'll do next time. Drill one correction 3× correctly in a row.

## Playbook steps
1. Script the CLOSER milestones as questions, and memorize 30-second stories plus obstacle handles.
2. Kill zombies before price, ask for the sale, loop until there are no concerns left, and reinforce the decision within 48 hours (personal video, gift, handwritten card).
3. Maximize availability and pull calls forward. Run an off-call SOP. Book a meeting from a meeting (BAMFAM, from Sharran Srivatsaa). Keep a visible kill list of whales. Ask "who do you know who's as awesome as you?" for referrals.
4. Secret shop your own sales process; "you'll be horrified." Find the constraint.
5. Repackage around the goal (diagnostic sale), anchor at the full price, step down, and roll payment plans into recurring.
6. Scaling a team: hire the director → rebuild comp → rebuild the process stage by stage ("100 golden BBs") → recruit from every channel with recruiters screening → run an internal training course → cut fast → retire the outsourced team.
7. Keep conviction high: read testimonials aloud daily, bring the head of customer support into sales meetings, and fix the product instead of blaming customers.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Talking more than listening, pitching features or the "plane flight," and over-educating reps on the product instead of the prospect.
- Surface-level discovery ("how much do you want to make?") instead of digging into intent and why.
- Not asking, asking again without resolving the concern, and winning the argument but losing the sale.
- Letting scripts grow through superstition ("the cough").
- Promoting the best closer to manager; the CEO acting as sales manager.
- Setters both multitasking and nurturing; disqualifying prospects too early by quoting price on set calls.
- Aggressive fake urgency or scarcity, and booking appointments weeks out.
- Reps who blame lead quality publicly (give that feedback 1:1), and treating poor leads as waste instead of practice.
- An outsourced sales team responsible for a big share of revenue: costly, and it hurts sellability.
- Hiring "a pulse" because the house is on fire. If you hire one bad leader, you may have to fire 10.

## Examples / case details
- **Show/close turnaround** (portfolio company, three young founders):
  - Fixes: hired an experienced sales director; replaced a negligent media buyer and retargeted from ages 18–24 to 25–35 (75% of appointments had been getting cancelled); trimmed the team by utilization and raised setter quotas; created a lead-nurture specialist role (three-way intro, morning-of video); deeper discovery; looping.
  - Results within ~60 days: show 49→70%, offer about 83→80%, close 27→41%, cash up front 47→82%, units 56→93 per month.
- **Diagnostic sale at a brick-and-mortar chain** (14→32 locations): revenue per customer went from $200 to $800 with the same delivery. In that product line, recurring share went 9→60%, memberships 0→30%, visits 1→4–6, and revenue $20K→$250K+ per month ("more than 10× recurring").
- **Weight-loss pricing**: 60 lb at 1.5 lb/week = 45 weeks × $99/week ≈ $4,500, with a "work with you free until you get there" guarantee. [inference: 45×99 = $4,455; a caption line reads "$500," which is a slip.]
- **Payment plan instead of monthly**: a friend's $300/month offer churned at about 3 months. Sold as a $10K program on 36-month financing, average tenure rose to about 8 months ($900→$2,400).
- **Outsourced → in-house** (0→40 reps in about 12 weeks, $5M+/month):
  - The outsourced team took 20% commission. Per 100 buyers, the two-call funnel ran 74% show → 53% schedule → 63% show → 80% close ≈ 20% ascension.
  - After the switch: 91% show, 94% schedule, 87% show, 42% close ≈ 32%, at 9% all-in commission.
  - Quarter comparison: $7M kept $5.6M; $12M kept $10.92M. He states +$5.4M/quarter, or $21.6M/year in profit. [inference: 10.92−5.6 = 5.32, ×4 ≈ $21.3M. He also sums the stage lifts to "3.5× volume," but multiplying them gives about 3.0×.]
- **Solar rep**: called every new booking immediately, either closing then or filling a later slot. He sold about 4× the #2 rep, and the practice was adopted portfolio-wide.
- **Referral rep**: asking "who else would you bring?" lifted his sales about 50%. Roughly 1 in 3–4 prospects refer, and referrals close at about 80–90%.
- **Timeshare rep** ($3M/year commission): won an hour with the CEO and asked for the best leads. His income rose 5×; rolled out nationwide, the business grew 5×. Higher top earnings attract more reps.
- **Personal**: about 4,000 one-on-one fitness sales at 20–25 consults/day. "Attach a phone to the funnel" turned a 2–2.5:1 loss into a 10:1 return and raised break-even cost per lead from $10 to $100. 191 signups in 19 days. 36:1 lifetime ROAS on just under $3M in ad spend. Sam Bakhtiar's $10K mastermind at age 22 ("draw a line in the sand").
- **Mortgage-leads rep John**: when asked "how good are the leads?" his answer showed no belief, which is what conviction fixes.
