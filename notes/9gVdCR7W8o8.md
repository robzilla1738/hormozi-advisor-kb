## Core thesis
"Your market isn't capped, your mindset is capped." Owners who plateau, e.g. at about $3M top line and $50K/month in Facebook ads, wrongly add new product lines and complexity. The real problem is that they don't know how to get more customers in their current niche. First pull the 4 lead levers (method, platform, media, volume). Only after truly exhausting them, expand using the 5-direction Pyramid of Markets. And big things take time.

## Frameworks & models
- **4 growth levers**: Method (paid ads, content, outreach, referrals, affiliates) → Platform (Facebook, phone, email, Hulu, YouTube, Google) → Media within the platform (e.g. Instagram DMs, stories, posts, reels) → Volume within each medium (1 a day vs many a day).
- **Market sizing heuristic**: add up the ad spend of all competitors on one platform to estimate available demand (e.g. 100 chiropractor agencies on Facebook).
- **Market selection (from $100M Offers)**: 1) in pain, 2) easy to find, 3) have money, 4) growing (e.g. retirees growing, not newspapers).
- **Pyramid of Markets, 5 directions**: 1) **Up-market**: fewer, higher-spending, higher-quality customers; enterprise, franchisors, multi-location (e.g. salon chains). 2) **Down-market**: about 10× more people (e.g. stylists vs salon owners). 3) **Adjacent**: similar business characteristics, owner avatar and customer (lash/nails; chiro → PT); attack it the same way. 4) **Broader**: the parent category (Beauty: med spas, lash, massage). 5) **Narrower**: a smaller market but not necessarily less money (extensions-only or blowout-only salons).
- **Choose an expansion direction by value**: "Who do we provide the most value to by dollars made?"

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Don't add product lines or offers at around $3M top line just because one ad channel stops scaling profitably.
- Expand markets only after the 4 levers are exhausted, which is "probably not true" for you.
- Niche down if you can make one avatar about 10× more money than the broad avatar. He says they've doubled, tripled, even 5×'d revenue while cutting the target market by about 85%.
- Look at it as an investor would: e.g. you hold less than 1% of a $60B industry.
- Pull the levers one at a time, then eventually all of them. Start with the "should do" checklist (speed to lead, follow-up, more content, cold calling, more platforms).
- About $1M profit is roughly Acquisition.com's minimum to consider an investment.

## Playbook steps
1. Confirm the market passes the 4 checks. 2. Add a method → platform → media → volume, one at a time. 3. Work the "should do" checklist. 4. If the niche is truly saturated, rank the 5 directions by dollars of value you create and expand there, or niche down.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Thinking you've saturated the market and starting a new business or product line, which adds complexity.
- Impatience: "big shit takes time."

## Examples / case details
- A meetup of 8 entrepreneurs doing $500M combined, where he was at about $30M. The others weren't smarter or harder-working; they had been in business longer and chased bigger markets.
- Gym Launch: about 50,000 US micro gyms, about 20,000 already contacted, every channel maxed. The options were down (personal trainers), up (health clubs), adjacent (PT/chiro) or narrower. They chose health clubs, where they could make owners tens of thousands of dollars in month one and double or triple their bottom line via onboarding, sales, retention and upsells.
- He says there are only about 10 self-made billionaires under 30 ("maybe 20").
