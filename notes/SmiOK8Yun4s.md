## Core thesis
A teardown of Ashley, a luxury fashion stylist ($309K revenue, $130K profit, 42% margin, 1,000+ lifetime clients) with three fixes: (1) plug the leaky funnel (8 sales from ~1,800 high-intent clicks) with a lead magnet, a short VSL, BANT qualification, and a better headline, before scaling spend; (2) double down on the hidden gold mine: 6 affiliate partners from 40 cold emails produced a third of revenue, so build the list and send 10× more; (3) fix pricing with a high anchor tier, a default (assumed) continuity backend, and continuous price increases. "More, then better, then new." The caption renders "Alex" as "Alexi."

## Frameworks & models
- **Model**: $8,500 60-day onboarding transformation → $2,000/mo or $4,500/quarter ongoing styling + 10% affiliate commission on clothing purchases.
- **Interruption vs intent traffic**: track metrics separately. They are different people needing different processes and sometimes products. Don't compare cold metrics to warm ones.
- **Lead magnet logic**: solve a narrow problem that reveals the next problem your paid offer solves. Illustration: 1/100 buy directly vs 10/100 take the free thing and 3 of those 10 buy = 3× sales. Content = "hundreds and thousands of tiny little lead magnets" (a pre-lead magnet). Good lead magnet: fast, easy, narrow, solves one complete problem. Favorite example: "How to know if a girl wants you to kiss her on a date" (hair-behind-the-ear test; "always get consent").
- **$100M Leads p.45 checklist**: problem for the right customer → how to solve → how to deliver → interesting, clear name → easy to consume → actually good.
- **Service as lead magnet, gated**: a free personalized style assessment (skin tone, body type, lifestyle), only for qualified prospects ("why would I give you a ride in my free jet if...you can't afford a private jet").
- **BANT** (IBM): budget, authority, need, timing. Asked on the intake form plus an income question.
- **Three Ps**: product, prospect, process.
- **VSL**: high-intent traffic needs only ~3–7 min. Belief: "It only takes a moment to make a decision"; extra calls mean information is delivered inefficiently. Front-load the salient info so 3 calls → 1 and 60–90 min calls → 15–20 min. Build: review common objections → record answers → send to every prospect → append "a couple more questions" cuts over time. Most of his companies' VSLs are first versions.
- **Headline formula**: "how to [yay] without [boo/insecurity]." Example: "Free lookbook to dress for your tone using your existing closet." 80% of the ad dollar goes to the first words seen.
- **Affiliate army** ($100M Leads p.227): find ideal affiliates (adjacent, non-competing businesses that hold your avatar: matchmakers, private social clubs, executive coaches, high-end trainers, divorce attorneys) → make them an offer (e.g. she pays for events for their clients) → qualify → compensate (value to their clients, bundle into their offer, upsell with 100% cash to them, or a revenue %) → launch → integrate monthly. Treat affiliates like customers; farm them (dinners, events).
- **Price anchor tier**: a top tier ~10× price. Even 10% take "effectively doubles revenue" and makes the old top price feel like a deal. Pro tip: make the anchor nearly identical, changing only 1–2 features most don't care about.
- **Default bias**: organ-donor opt-out example (70%+ vs <10%). Present continuity as the natural next step ("you've invested in your transformation, let's make it stick") with an assumed close.
- **Best backend upsells**: more of what they just bought, or more help with it.
- **Veblen good**: for luxury, a higher price can raise desire. Maximum profit sits after the point of maximum conversion: 3× price at half the yeses makes more.
- **Scaling roadmap stage 4**: double down on the top 20% of customers, better free stuff, qualification/friction so volume self-selects, track KPIs.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Fix conversion (the leaky bucket) before increasing acquisition spend.
- Don't fear qualifying. It saves time and turns maybes into yeses ("they serve people only like me").
- Speed over everything: always shorten timelines (qualifying, products, results).
- If two calls close on average and you get it to one, you double sales with the same team.
- If a prospect didn't watch the VSL, hold the frame: have them watch it (7 min) on the call so the script stays uniform. That's why shorter VSLs are preferred.
- Ask: "If I could only do one thing to double my business this year, what would it be? Why am I doing anything that's not that?"
- Keep raising price until total profit (not conversion) drops. Good margins are no reason to stop.
- Sales process must be congruent: high-end ads, lead magnet, qualification, price and product.

## Playbook steps
1. Lead magnet (gated service or asset). 2. Short VSL before calls. 3. BANT + income on intake; auto-cancel non-qualifiers. 4. Rewrite headline around the freebie. 5. Build an adjacent-affiliate list; send 20 emails/day (400 in 30 days vs 40 lifetime). 6. Activate and retain affiliates with value for their clients. 7. Add a ~10× anchor tier. 8. Make continuity the default, assumed next step. 9. Raise prices continually.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Sending cold traffic to a form with no headline or value prop, just "get more info" ("goes from zero to let's sleep together").
- Putting "trash" in the lead magnet: 99% of people never buy, so the market judges you on your free stuff (he says "you want the marketplace to judge you on your worst thing," apparently ironic [inference]). He spends ~2 years per book (his lead magnet).
- Obsessing over making the lead magnet or VSL perfect instead of shipping.
- Blending intent and interruption metrics.
- Chasing new things instead of doubling down on what worked (banner blindness: "your mouth is an inch below your nose, but sometimes you need someone else to tell you your breath smells").
- Being a generalist: then you can't partner with anyone.
- Ignoring affiliates while spending $10K/mo on ads; expecting affiliates to work without farming.
- Grandfathered low prices ($1,500/quarter or less), no price shown or tiers framed before the call: premium delivered like a commodity.
