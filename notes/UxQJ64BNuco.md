# The Alex Hormozi Guide to Haters [REVEALED]

Signal: medium

## Core thesis
Hate comes with being visible, and it is free advertising. Don't attack back. Ignore it almost always; agree, or agree and one-up, when there's some truth in it; and drown it out by getting louder with your own value. "You don't beat hate, you drown it out." You only lose if you get quieter or change your message.

## Frameworks & models
- **1-2-3 response ladder**: (1) **Ignore**, his top pick because it costs nothing and haters are advertising you to their audience. (2) **Agree**: the Eminem 8 Mile move. "Only one person allowed in the angry boat." Agreeing removes the opposing force. (3) **Agree + one-up ("ninja level")**: insult yourself better than they can, because you have more material. He says this handles ~95% of attacks.
- **Extinction curve**: you are the rock. Without reinforcement, a hater fades after one or two attempts.
- **2×2 box: attacker bigger/smaller × lie accusation/other insult**:
  - Smaller, other insult → any of 1/2/3.
  - Smaller, calls you a liar on something core → **ignore + amplify** your message to your own audience.
  - Bigger, other insult → 1/2/3, leaning hard on #3 humor. You want bigger people to hate on you because they send you traffic for free.
  - Bigger, discredits you → don't keep denying it. Make them look petty with humor and get louder.
- **Pink-elephant / association rule**: brand is built on associations. Repeating "I am not X" links you to X and spreads your enemy's message.
- **Law of large numbers**: 500 hateful comments is a tiny fraction of a large audience, but it *feels* like a crowd. Over a 6-month horizon, a few bad days get diluted by steady value. (He cites both ".1%" and "5%" for 500 out of a million followers; 500/1,000,000 is actually 0.05%. [inference] Read it as "a very small percentage.")
- **Reframe insult as compliment**: "you must have faked it" means they think your results are impressive. Example: Leila called "too skinny."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Never attack back: "you never win in direct conflict when it comes to reputation." Even if you win, it's a Pyrrhic victory and you lose credibility.
- True claim → lean into it. False and smaller → ignore. False and bigger → make them seem petty. In every case, get louder.
- Only reply to people bigger than you.
- His real usage: almost all ignore. Options 2 and 3 together are "the world's tiniest slice." 99% of his attention goes to amplifying his message.
- If a hater can get you to respond, they control you, and everyone learns which button to push.

## Playbook steps
1. Classify the attack (true/false; bigger/smaller). 2. By default, ignore. 3. If it's partly true, agree or self-deprecate harder than they did. 4. If it's a lie about something core, don't deny it repeatedly. Tell your own team/audience and turn up value output. 5. Put the anxious energy "into your megaphone." 6. Let the extinction curve play out.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Repeated denial posts that spread the accusation.
- Petty fights with smaller accounts; sending your traffic to them.
- Going quiet, or changing your message, in response to hate. He says that's the only way you actually get "canceled," short of full deplatforming.
- Catastrophizing: we're wired to correct behavior when shamed, but social media scale distorts that signal.

## Examples / case details
- Ted Lasso press conference about Nate: an agree-and-one-up done with humor that ended the drama.
- A top news anchor mocked adult-industry creators. They replied "you should join us next time," and he says it led to record sales the next day: free traffic, no affiliate commission.
- A mega-influencer kept denying a competitor's lie. Alex DM'd them the pink-elephant advice: "only reply to people bigger than you."
- An athlete accused of not being natural. His suggested reply was a self-mocking "you got me, my hair/lashes/makeup are fake too."
- His own critics say he faked his business success and made up numbers. He reports "never once have I made less money" from ignoring them.
