## Core thesis
Behind the scenes of the $100M Money Models book launch (filmed July 16, 2025, ~30 days before the Aug 16 live launch). The launch itself demonstrates the book's idea: build a money model that pulls cash forward so you can spend as much as possible on ads while staying at break-even or better. If customers cost too much to acquire, "make more from each one."

## Frameworks & models
- **Money model** = offering the right thing at the right time in the right way to the right customer; how you collect money and how you charge. He started writing in 2021 on a mentor's advice to document his crystallized knowledge. The 400+ page draft became all three $100M books.
- **Launch economics**: the last launch had no offer to liquidate ad spend. This time the goal is maximum ad spend at break-even or better by accelerating cash conversion.
- **Ad creative iteration**: reuse the best hooks from existing winning ads and top organic content; wait for statistically significant data before the next creative shoot.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If acquisition is expensive, raise value per customer rather than cutting ad spend.
- "Businesses rarely die of starvation. They often die of indigestion." (one of the ad hooks)
- Advertise even when you think everyone knows: "We think we're so important. No one knows."

## Playbook steps
1. Build a money model that self-liquidates ad spend. 2. Pre-orders with free bonuses plus a free registration event. 3. Batch-film ~40 ads using proven hooks. 4. Plan operations ahead of time: three warehouses, pallets pre-kitted, 300+ support staff, AI support, shipping target within 72 hours.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Assuming a business needs a different industry when the real issue is its monetization structure.
- Under-staffing fulfillment. The last launch's 30 warehouse workers "got hammered," and this one was expected to be ~5× bigger.

## Examples / case details
- Target: over 1 million copies. One email already drove large pre-orders before promotion started.
- Sleeping ~5h20–5h45 a night during prep. He likes printed clipboards over AI tools for working sessions.
