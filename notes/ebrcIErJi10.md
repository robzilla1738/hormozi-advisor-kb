## Core thesis
Against "fake it till you make it." Faking success erodes your self-respect and conviction, which are what you're really selling. It overextends you financially and gets the sequence backwards: fame should come from results, not results from posing. Be rich, don't look rich; wealth is spending less than you make.

## Frameworks & models
- **Selling yourself to yourself**: conviction comes from self-respect; lying about success makes you "a fraud in the mirror," and people can feel hollow conviction.
- **"Outwork your self-doubt"**: if you're insecure about a skill, do more of it rather than pretend.
- **Wealth is a ratio, not a number**: live on less than you make.
- **Sequence**: Bezos and Musk are famous for what they did; let actions create fame.
- **Jay-Z rule** (as quoted by him): "If you can't buy something twice, don't buy it once."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Don't buy the Ferrari or house you can't afford for image or "networking"; you can attend those places without buying a $4M house on a few hundred thousand a year.
- Be transparent about numbers in good and bad years; honesty about strengths and weaknesses wins loyal customers and employees.
- Get the sequence right: build cash savings before investing (his side note).

## Playbook steps
1. Decide your values first, then live by them, not by perceived expectations.
2. Keep lifestyle far below income.
3. Report results honestly, including down years, to build credibility for later claims.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Posing on Instagram to get wealthy.
- Overextending to look successful; in 2008 many who were faking it "faked it until they lost it all."
- Constant "we're doing great" stories: everyone hates you, no one cares, and you know you're full of it.

## Examples / case details
- About 5–6 years earlier: first licensee event (28 licensees). He shaved to a mustache from nerves; he arrived in a dented Prius with a cracked windshield. A licensee: "I thought you were supposed to be rich." His reply: "You should see my bank account, not my car… you want to be rich, not look rich." Origin of the "stash."
- At the time: ~$1M cash after tax, already owned 6 gyms (sold) and 30+ turnarounds; he and Leila lived on $1,200/mo rent + ~$400/mo food while taking home $300–400K/mo.
- Bought a Bentley once and gave it back; felt contrary to his values.
