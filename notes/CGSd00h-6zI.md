## Core thesis
Test prep / tutoring sold as an *outcome* (a higher score, a better school) in a **semi-private** (small group) format is a no-capital business that can make top-1% income in 4 hours a day. Later you replace yourself with teachers and keep the spread.

## Frameworks & models
- **Sell the outcome, not the tutoring:** a score increase gets you a better school, and better schools mean a better network, recruiters and salaries. Price against that value (~$100k/yr more earning potential).
- **Semi-private is one of the most profitable service models:** charge about 2/3 of the 1-on-1 rate with up to 8x the people. Students still get personal attention and take turns solving problems.
- **Input→output curve:** research shows SAT/GMAT scores are proportional to the number of problems solved. Make that the core of the pitch ("every 1,000 problems ≈ +20 points") and sell the goal score.
- **Free-pilot loop:** do the first 5–10 students free in exchange for the "big three": feedback, a testimonial, a referral. The next 10 pay half off. After that, full price.
- **Super Saiyan / "God mode":** templatize the process, hire teachers at $50/hr, and keep the spread. Build two pipelines: students in, teachers in (ask teachers for teacher friends).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- 1-on-1 at $60/hr × 4 hrs = $240. Semi-private 1:8 at $35/student/hr = $280/hr × 4 hrs ≈ $1,120/day, about 4.5x more per unit of time. (Opening hook: "$1,120/day profit".)
- Needs ~50 students coming 3x/week. $35/session is the "sweet spot most people could afford".
- He prefers prepay over pay-per-session (per-session ties price too closely to time).
- Service margin target: 80%+. Teacher cost must stay below ~$56/hr of the $280, so $50 works.
- 4 teachers × $280/hr × 4 hrs ≈ $4,400/day, minus $800 teacher pay ≈ $3,600/day. $1M/yr ≈ $3,300/day.
- Accountability: "every day you don't do your homework I will bill you" gives them skin in the game.
- Could take ~6 months to build to ~$1,000–1,100/day.

## Playbook steps
1. 3-day curriculum: class A and class B separately, plus a makeup day (makeups pay the same rate).
2. Run sessions at free public libraries (no rent, no buildout).
3. Outreach: your network, door knocking, DMs to classmates, DMs to parents and adults. Pitch "I moved my score from X to Y, here's the method". Offer a free session that is half assessment, half pitch: give one easy win, then "this is how I teach, do you like that?"
4. First 5–10 free → collect feedback, testimonial, referral → post testimonials with a CTA to DM → next 10 at half off → full price.
5. Other channels: cold outreach to strangers, ads, sports teams that need scores for scholarships (whole team comes after practice).
6. Scale by hiring teachers at $50/hr.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Selling hourly tutoring instead of the outcome. Doing 1-on-1. Shame ("get rid of your shame because you're going to get embarrassed").

## Examples / case details
- Test prep was one of three businesses he almost started before the gym. He bought every GMAT prep book at Barnes & Noble, did problems 4 hrs/day for 4 months, took a practice test every Saturday, and scored above Harvard's average.
