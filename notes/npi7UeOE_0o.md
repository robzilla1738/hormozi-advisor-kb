## Core thesis
Super Bowl ads cost ~$8M per 30s, so he tiers 12 of them to pull out lessons any business can use. The ads that work make the customer the subject, show the product in use through the whole spot, and make it obvious whose ad it is and what to do next. Brand ads work by creating clear associations: "Brand = X, and X = things our avatar already likes." Data beats opinion. Mostly his reactions to each ad, with fewer hard rules than his frameworks videos. The ad audio is mixed into the caption text.

## Frameworks & models
- **6 ranking factors**: grabs attention, makes you care, sells the dream, creates urgency, shows social proof, and "most importantly" tells you what to do next.
- **Brand association equation** (Jeep): Jeep = Freedom; Freedom = the stuff our demo likes; so if you like those things you like Jeep. Pick the associations from customer research. Nike = winning, Nike = doing what people said you can't, and Nike = the athletes you admire, so buying the product gets you closer to them.
- **Pain hook → immediate demonstration** (Ramp): a question hook about what life is like without the product, which the product then solves on screen.
- **Before/after ad structure** (his rewrite of GoDaddy): show 3–5 emotive things a business owner can't do, bring in the product, revisit those things now solved, recap, CTA.
- **Copyable structure** (breast-screening ad): lots of attention → a fact that makes people uncomfortable → a testimonial from someone who avoided the bad outcome by using the service → CTA.
- **Advertising vs persuasion**: advertising lets people know about your stuff; persuasion changes behavior. Judge an ad by the behavior change, not the applause.
- **Ad objectives differ**: pure branding/positive sentiment, reinforcing an existing position, or direct response. Judge each against its own objective.
- **Crowdsourced creative** (Doritos fan contest): ~$1M prize vs agency cost; the audience pre-validates the winner through votes and shares, and customers know the product better than agencies do.
- **Brand elements** (mentioned in passing): influence, reach, directionality.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- "You can't beat data." The best ad is the one that drove the most sales. Let tests decide (he A/B tested the $100M Leads title instead of polling five friends).
- Make the ad about the customer, not about you: "rule number one is that no one cares about you."
- Make it clear early and throughout whose product it is. A 1-second logo at the end costs attribution (Rocket: A+ instead of S tier).
- Put the CTA on screen as text, not only in the audio; many people watch without sound or subtitles.
- Don't open with a brand name the viewer doesn't know yet (Booking.com).
- A call-out has to name the actual avatar. Calling out "actors" in a small-business ad loses the viewer.
- If people have to rewatch an ad to understand it, it failed: they won't rewatch, and the moment is gone.
- Judge an ad as its avatar would; you may not be the demographic.
- A "winning" brand needs losers: "if everybody's a winner, no one is."

## Playbook steps
[inference] When reviewing your own ad: 1. State its objective (brand, reinforce, or direct response). 2. Score it on the 6 factors. 3. Check that the product is shown in use and the brand is clear throughout. 4. Check that the hook speaks to the avatar's pain or desire. 5. Put the CTA on screen as text. 6. Let sales data pick the winner.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Ads about the company itself (On: Federer and Elmo talking about the letters in the name; C).
- Fast cuts that aren't explanatory and leave the viewer unsure where it's going (Booking.com; C).
- Saying the system is built for profit and then pitching your own medication; fear without showing the dream outcome (Hims & Hers: D, his worst).
- Fun ads that create good feelings but may not change behavior.
- Weak attribution to the brand at the end.

## Examples / case details
Tiers he gave: Google Gemini S (a 30-second emotional story arc with the product shown in use throughout and many audiences cut in); Ramp A (15s); Doritos A; Jeep A; Nike A; Rocket Mortgage A+; Breast screening B+ (he also calls it a B); Instacart B (only the last ~5s showing the forgotten-milk use case is strong; maybe a brand co-op); Booking.com C; On C; GoDaddy C; Hims & Hers D. He suggests the screening ad might have changed behavior more as a Gemini-style "moments you'll miss" spot or a Jeep-style association spot.
