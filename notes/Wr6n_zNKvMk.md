# Building a $1,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 69 Minutes

## Core thesis
Live teardown of Philip's residential trash business (Garby Disposal: $642K revenue, -$151K net, -23% net margin, ~2 yrs, 2,500 customers; founder working 16 hrs/day, baby due in 6 weeks). Alex finds that the problem isn't leads. The business is spread across two avatars and about five channels, and its offer collects less cash up front than it costs to acquire and serve a customer. The fix: pick one avatar (scatter homes), one channel (commission-only door-to-door), and restructure the offer so first-transaction cash exceeds fully loaded CAC + COGS (~$208/door), letting growth be debt-free and cash-flow positive.

## Frameworks & models
- **Diagnosis order**: avatar → offer (how to facilitate the transaction) → acquisition channel → ads → sales process → funnel.
- **One avatar, one channel, one product** up to ~$1M+.
- **Fewest need-to-believes**: plan around the single assumption that, if true, solves the business. "Every single new assumption that we introduce dramatically decreases the likelihood it occurs." Here: can 10 commission-only reps each do ~5 sales/day? 50/day = ~1,500/month.
- **30-day cash rule**: "cash in the first 30 days [must] be greater than your cost of goods sold and CAC fully loaded."
- **Upfront cost stack** (per scatter customer): $42 first-quarter fulfillment ($14/mo labor+trucks+dump × 3, since the quarter is prepaid) + $116 bin (incl. freight) = $158; + $50 rep commission = **$208** needed at signup.
- **Rename fees to cover hidden costs**: a "$99 bin" fee really covers first-quarter labor, and an "initiation" fee really covers commission.
- **A/B anchor offer** (final): both get 3 months free (one free month in each of Q2–Q4, so ~$60 billed per later quarter). A = $99 bin + first quarter $89–90 (~$189). B = 12 months prepaid at $30/mo = $360 and the bin is free. "You get the free bin when you commit for the year." Fallback for people who won't buy the bin: finance it over a 3-year term at ~$150 total, with ~4%/yr price increases built in. Bin is taken back if they cancel before term ("another piece of collateral").
- **Blended cash per door**: assume 80% take A and 20% take B. Alex gets to roughly $162 + $72; [inference: correct arithmetic is 0.8×$189 = $151.20 + 0.2×$360 = $72 = ~$223, still above $208].
- **Ugly-QR-code lever** (floated, then dropped from the final offer): put a huge QR code/phone sticker on bins (a free billboard); charge +$5/mo to remove it.
- **Processing-fee trick**: add 3% processing; waive it if the customer gives a second form of payment (protects against declines and service interruption). "Check with your legal counsel."
- **Learning = modeling + doing with feedback**; **Document → Demonstrate → Duplicate** for training reps.
- **Recruiting ad = a real ad**: headline, pain (what they hate about current job/sales job), promise (six figures, work 4 or 8 hrs), simple task (memorize 4 questions), a damaging admission (hot/cold weather), "we don't care about grades, we care about work ethic."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Avoid commoditized auction businesses (HOA low-bid contracts = race to bottom). HOAs are the long-term "efficiency business"; scatter wins short-term with higher price, margin, easy acquisition. Choose scatter; still close inbound HOAs opportunistically.
- 70% of homes in the area are HOA-controlled (~500 HOAs on 3–5 yr contracts). That is not a cold-email play; use handwritten cards, gifts, drive-ups. About 150 come up per year, so one visit every other day keeps you current.
- Door math: ~30% door-open × ~26% close ≈ 1 sale per ~12 doors. A rep can knock ~150 doors/day, so 10+ deals/day is possible; Alex hears "conservatively 10, most guys get 15."
- One-in-eight prospects switching back after their provider matches price is fine: "if we get the deal on seven out of eight, who cares?"
- If cash isn't the constraint, raise commission ($100/deal) to attract an order-of-magnitude better rep ($500/day); top D2D reps run between doors.
- Hiring commission-only reps: low bar, "screen for crazies"; group calls (2 min each), meet the normal ones in person, offer on the spot; expect to hire ~5 to keep 1. Screen for hunger/circumstance (young and hungry, or kid on the way).
- Founder should never sell alone: always have a trainee shadowing. Pay the lead trainer $60/sale vs $50.
- Cut paid ads and other channels; put all attention on hiring ~10 commission reps.
- Don't fix what isn't a constraint (contracts signed without payment at the door still collect ~100% via invoices, so leave it).

## Playbook steps
1. Pick scatter as the sole avatar. 2. Launch the A/B offer (A: $99 bin + first quarter; B: $360/yr with free bin; 3 months free on both). 3. Run Indeed/Craigslist ads written to salespeople and tap your network → group call → in-person → offer. 4. Shadow for a week, then pair off; role-play the opener ("close the door, open the door" drills) and the ~4-question, ~2-minute script plus objections (think about it, spouse, card). 5. Founder keeps knocking with a trainee in tow. 6. Plan for the next truck as capacity (~3,500 customers) nears.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Two avatars and five channels for a sub-$1M business ("I was doing way too much").
- Being the low-cost provider ("I hate being low low cost").
- Not valuing founder time in CAC (cold outreach looked like 700:1 only because Philip does it himself).
- Fronting/financing bin costs instead of collecting them up front; Alex prefers growing debt-free.
- Writing recruiting ads like corporate job postings.
- Leading an ad with self-introduction rather than the prospect's pain/offer.

## Examples / case details
- Metrics: blended CAC $67, blended gross-margin LTV $1,300, monthly churn 0.4%, 217 leads/mo, 72 closes (33%). HOA: 13 customers, $300K revenue, CAC $1,200, LTV $23K (18:1), 23% GM. Scatter: $300K revenue, CAC $51, LTV $1,000 (21:1), GM 26% heading to 40%. Meta: $7,500 total spend, $2.12 CPC, 3,500 clicks, 153 sales, $600/mo now. Door hanger converted 1.2%. Gross margin last year was 8% because of ~$100K in repairs on one truck.
- Old scatter price: $29.67/mo billed $89/quarter with 3 months free; web signups got a $37 initiation fee + $35/mo.
- New rep: 3 weeks in, averages 5 sales/day, hit 7. Founder knocks 300 doors/week.
- Alex suggests QR codes on bins and neon-orange bins for visibility. Philip prefers clean light gray; customers call in about "nice clean bins."
- Best ad was founder-on-camera and authentic. Advice: flip it to pain/offer first and record ~10 variations (shirts, truck, bin, neighbor), but cut ads for now.
- Founder + 1 rep at 10/day combined ≈ 300/month, adding ~1,000 customers to reach capacity in ~3 months.
