## Core thesis
Labels shape experience. Many "problems" are normal swings on human continuums (happy/sad, energized/tired, relaxed/stressed) that we label as bad. That label turns a passing emotion into a "long-standing emotional disturbance." For resilience, watch your labels and your "should/must/need" language, and add "and that's okay." This is a mindset rant with an explicit disclaimer that it isn't advice. It includes opinions on mental-health labels and pharma that are his views, not business tactics.

## Frameworks & models
- **Minds are meaning-making machines**: we assign positive and negative labels to survive. "Brains are meant to keep us alive, not help us thrive."
- **Positive-label persuasion**: label the prospect ("you seem like a loving, caring man who provides for his family"), get agreement, then tie the ask to the label. Cognitive dissonance pushes them to act consistently with it. This can be woven into sales scripts and narratives.
- **Continuum, not good/bad**: you can't have light without dark. Trying to eliminate one side numbs you to the whole continuum.
- **Implied-threat language**: "I must have my coffee" creates a threat ("or else what?"). Listen for should, have to, must and need as signs of broken thought patterns.
- **Frankl (Man's Search for Meaning)**: "Between circumstance and response is choice."
- **Refrain "…and that's okay"**: you can't always control emotions, but you can control the judgment you put on them.
- **"Wisdom begins with a definition of terms"** (attributed to Socrates): most disagreements come from undefined words (happy, healthy, wealthy, success).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Define the outcomes you want precisely; it helps you get them.
- In disagreements or negotiations, agree on terms first.
- Treat a negative emotion as "this will pass, and it's okay, and it's part of being human."

## Playbook steps
1. Notice the negative labels and should/must language you use. 2. Reframe the state as a point on a normal continuum. 3. Repeat "and that's okay." 4. Choose your response in the gap. 5. Define your key terms (success, wealthy) explicitly.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Treating being human as the problem.
- Obsessing over happiness. He says it made him less happy.
- Letting someone sell you that normal states are problems.
