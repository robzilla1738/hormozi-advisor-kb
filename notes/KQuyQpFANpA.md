## Core thesis
Three event Q&A cases share one lesson: they were in the wrong opportunity vehicle and were capturing a fee instead of owning the economics. The cases are an appointment-setting firm, an HVAC marketing agency, and an influencer with 30–40M subscribers. Use the theory of constraints to find the real bottleneck, then move toward owning the result: partner, buy, or build. The pie's size matters more than your slice's shape.

## Frameworks & models
- **Theory of constraints**: a business grows to its constraint and no further. Example funnel 50% → 5% → 50%: improving each by 10 points, taking 5% → 15% *triples* the business while the others add ~20%.
- **"More customers" is usually not the problem**: customers leaving or low-quality customers usually are. Find the avatar that made the most money from your service and niche down to them.
- **Own the result**: an agency doing $5M may be generating $50M for clients and capturing only the fee. The more steps you take toward owning the result, the more "you are the business," so own the economics by learning the business, partnering, or buying one.
- **Atlas boulder vs compounding**: service work restarts monthly. Inside one owned company, 50 outbound reps a month accumulate to 300 in 6 months.
- **Foundation analogy**: the fastest way to $10M ≠ the fastest way to $100M. A 10-story foundation can't hold 100 stories.
- **Slice size, not shape**: the average IPO founder owns ~12.5%.
- **Monetization ladder for creators**: sponsorship/ad revenue is least efficient because sponsors arbitrage your brand. Options: (1) take equity from repeat advertisers (10% of a $100M company you help grow); (2) clone the top-paying sponsor category Amazon-style, poaching execs with equity (he'd keep ~70%); (3) become a creative/ad shop that turns proven viral videos into paid ads ("Madison Avenue agency"). Ad spend has no reach limit while profitable.
- **Decision framework**: (1) What problem are we solving? (2) Over what time horizon? (3) Which problem do we prefer, or are we best equipped to solve?

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Work on the business's constraint, not what you enjoy.
- Partnering is faster but slices the pie. Doing it yourself is slower and may be bigger. Weigh how many new skills each path needs.
- A business doing $50M likely spends ~$5–10M/yr on marketing, which is the size of the partnering opportunity.
- Sometimes take "a half step back so you can take 20 steps forward." Don't try to keep the agency going while buying the operating business.
- A creator at "97/100" in media should go 0→20 on sales, product and customer success, not 97→99.

## Playbook steps
1. Ask what problem and over what horizon. 2. Find the true constraint with funnel math. 3. Identify the best-performing client avatar. 4. Choose: niche down, partner, learn the business, or buy one. 5. Recruit proven execs with equity (Acquisition.com's strength). 6. Own the whole pie where you're best equipped.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Working on what you enjoy instead of the constraint.
- Staying a fee-taker while generating client outcomes worth 10×.
- Building a $10M foundation and expecting it to hold $100M.
- Obsessing over equity percentage instead of pie size.
- Influencers living "video to paycheck" on sponsorships.

## Examples / case details
- Appointment setting: low-quality clients who didn't make enough, didn't stay and lacked skills. The fix was the best avatar → niche down or co-own a $50–100M client business over 5–10 years.
- HVAC agency: ~50 clients across 50 local markets, so it has the marketing skill of a $50–100M HVAC company.
- Influencer: ~100M views/month even with no new posts, an impression base worth a $100M+ revenue company, but no sales, product or customer-success departments.
