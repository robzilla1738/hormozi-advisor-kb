# What I Would Do If I Had My Gyms (Do This in 2020)

## Core thesis
Early-COVID (2020) crisis play: if he still had his six gyms, he'd use the "permission" from government and clients to flip to a more profitable model. That means breaking or renegotiating leases, moving recurring members to 100% remote accountability, keeping only the best 1–2 trainers per site, having trainers do outbound, and personally closing all day while lead costs are down 50–75%. The tactics are time-bound, but crisis-pivot and "sell accountability, not workouts" principles generalize.

## Frameworks & models
- **90% get an 80% win > 5% get a 100% win**: he designs offers and sales processes anyone can run, not ones that need his own skill (e.g. high-ticket phone sales).
- **Crisis = permission to flip**: when the environment encourages change with no repercussions, switch to the higher-margin model (remote fulfillment, which he says he argued for months earlier).
- **They pay for you to pay attention**: workouts are commodities ($10–20/mo apps like Beachbody and Sweat). You win on accountability and service.
- **Lean remote ops model**: each trainer runs one recorded workout a day (12 trainers → roughly hourly classes), checks in on existing clients, and spends the first 2 hours doing 100 cold reach-outs.
- **Owner role in crisis**: do what you do best, "make it rain." Market and take sales calls from paid and outbound leads all day.
- **Everyone is "at the airport"**: prospects start at a negative stress level, and what they want is hope. Spend the first ~15 minutes of the call making them feel good. The vehicle (weight loss) is secondary to how you make them feel.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Get out of leases if possible, or at least negotiate 3–6 months of free rent.
- Keep the most engaged, caring trainer(s), 1–2 per facility. Everyone not generating or retaining revenue is non-essential.
- Trainer quota: 100 reach-outs a day and at least one booked call a day.
- Referral-style outreach script: "Do you know anybody who's trying to stay in shape during the quarantine?" Asking for someone else doesn't feel like fishing.
- Hope comes from optimism about what you're willing to endure; convey that certainty to team, customers and prospects.

## Playbook steps
1. Renegotiate or break leases. 2. Move recurring members to remote nutrition and accountability. 3. Keep the top trainers on ~$3K/month from home. 4. Each trainer records one workout a day and checks in on clients. 5. Trainers spend the first 2 hours doing 100 cold outreaches and book calls onto the owner's calendar. 6. Turn on cheap cold traffic. 7. The owner closes all day. 8. Paint a vision of a better future for the team.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Thinking you'll win on workouts or content.
- Waiting to see how the crisis shakes out instead of taking the opportunity.
- Owners not rolling up their sleeves to sell.

## Examples / case details
- Hypothetical math: 6 gyms × $30K/month EFT = $180K. Assume 25% churn (not owner-operated) = −$45K, leaving ~$135K (he wavers "40ish"). 12 trainers × $3K = $36K, which leaves "~$100K a month... in all profit." Verified: $135K − $36K ≈ $99K, before other costs [inference].
- Gym Launch was launching 16 central remote workouts a day so member gyms could cut overhead.
- Lead costs at the time: "like 50% to 75% off."
- Put new members with your best coach ("Jessica, not Eric").
