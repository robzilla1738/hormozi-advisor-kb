## Core thesis
One "super influencer" partnership can compress years of audience-building into weeks: if you reach a million people for free, any product that sells for more than it costs makes money, so the question becomes "which thing makes the most" rather than "will it work." Three-step "super influencer process": (1) pick the right influencer, (2) get them to talk to you, (3) get them to promote your stuff. You trade a share of the upside for their years of brand-building. Workshop format; includes promotion for Skool and his free trainings.

## Frameworks & models
- **Two partnership models**: (a) you have the brand, find an influencer to promote it (Nike + Jordan); (b) find the influencer and build/deliver a product branded around them (Congo + Logan Paul → Prime). Rule: whoever has the bigger audience owns the brand: if yours is bigger they promote your thing; if theirs is bigger you deliver for theirs.
- **Army of small vs one super influencer**: many ~100K affiliates takes longer but you keep more ownership (Prestige Labs, ALAN); one super influencer is faster/easier to manage but you give up more. He now leans toward super influencers for speed to market and as an "insurance policy" (Skool: co-owns and acts as own influencer).
- **Targeting**: ICP → influencers they follow → pick super influencers that best fit.
- **Contact circles**: influencers you know personally → ask your network who they know (offer a paid intro or cut of the deal) → influencers you follow/have heard of (all channels, mutual connections, bounties, gifts) → formal channels (agents, their website's support email/opt-in).
- **Get them to talk**: Verify yourself (paid check mark — he claims 5–10x response rates; profile bio states you partner with influencers; clear, close, bright face photo; a few real posts) → **Magic message** → **Rule of 100**.
- **Magic message script** (Skool example): "Longtime fan, first-time messenger. I own a company that partners with influencers to start communities. Based on your following it looks like you could generate X–Y/month recurring. Here are screenshots of people who started this month. You get the majority of the profits, we do the majority of the work. Up for a 15-minute call?" Personalize heavily.
- **Close the deal**: over-preparation = profit (mockups of their product, "you now vs you with me" projections: followers × posts/wk × reach × ~1% click conversion → 12-month estimate) → presentation **Promise / Proof / Plan (+demo) / Offer** → "every position has an advantage" → "no's or yeses."
- **Every position has an advantage**: New: personal touch, full attention, flexibility, "you're my only partner," owner mentality. 800-lb gorilla: best practices from thousands of accounts, infrastructure, specialists, track record, durability ("someone who might not be here in 6 months").
- **Default product if you have none**: a paid community for the influencer (fans want more access; paywall the extra access).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Bigger audience owns the brand; offer to deliver if theirs is bigger.
- Send 100 messages/day across platforms (~4 hours/day) until you get replies.
- Superstars cost less than people assume — "a couple hundred grand a year" for some.
- Offer to incur cost (fly out: "I'm out that way all the time"); offer payment relevant to *their* scale, not yours.
- Make their side of the deal look better than yours; a smaller share is still worth it.
- Mid-sized influencers may want growth more than money — offer resources/content team instead of or in addition to economics.
- If they say no, ask why (best feedback) and move on; you only need one yes.

## Playbook steps
1. Define ICP and list influencers they follow; pick the biggest fit. 2. Work contact circles from closest out. 3. Verify and clean up your profile. 4. Send personalized magic messages, 100/day, multi-platform (LinkedIn/email/direct mail/website support stand out vs crowded IG DMs). 5. Share their stories/content beforehand to build affinity. 6. For a dream target: thoughtful gift (their logo, not yours), handwritten card, offer to fly out. 7. Build mockups + projections; present promise/proof/plan/demo; make a "Michael Jordan" offer. 8. Pitch from your position's advantage. 9. Reuse decks made for other influencers as proof of work and as alternative structures.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Spammy, generic copy-paste blasts; looking like a "weirdo" profile.
- Valuing your prep time over the payoff — "you're the one who stands to gain far more."
- Gifts with your logo on them.
- Offering payment that's trivial at their level.
- Assuming lots of people take action because lots of people see something.
