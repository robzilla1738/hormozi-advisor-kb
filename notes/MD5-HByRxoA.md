## Core thesis
Organic content serves one of two objectives. For most small businesses (phases 1–2) it is **lead nurture, not lead generation**: it proves you're legit to prospects who arrive through ads, outbound or affiliates, and it gives sales and paid teams material. Only if you invest in it like paid ads (vendors, then in-house, max volume across every platform, which takes 18+ months) does it become a lead-gen machine. He walks back his old Gym Launch advice to "stop making content," saying it was partially right and partially wrong.

## Frameworks & models
- **5 phases of content**: (1) make something and post it; (2) post consistently on one platform with a cadence (he does all marketing on one day a week); (3) post reliably on ALL platforms, which is about learning each platform's "packaging"; (4) max out what each platform can take (short-form ~5–10/day on TikTok/Twitter/Reels; newsfeed platforms cap ~1–2/day); (5) capture + create, i.e. deliberately produce content such as a call-in show and repurpose it everywhere.
- **Objective split**: phases 1–2 = nurture, fodder for sales, testing hooks. Phases 3–5 = generating new customers.
- **Master sheet of best content**: rank the content that convinces people to buy, mapped to sales-call FAQs. Reps send pieces before calls or between call 1 and call 2 to close.
- **Content as low-risk hook testing**: organic posts that get disproportionate saves/shares → lead paid ads and cold emails with them, then split test vs control.
- **Three costs of content**: time, long-term (patience), money.
- **Narrow pond / "king of a tiny pond"**: define the problem you solve narrowly enough to be the best there ("fastest toilet fixer this side of Tuscaloosa"), then widen step by step: plumbing → local business → marketing → enterprise.
- **Give > take**: mostly value plus a simple CTA. "The more you give, the more you grow. The more you take, the more you shrink."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If paid ads, outbound, affiliates or referrals are paying the bills, don't take your eye off them. Content = nurture.
- Litmus test for nurture content: "you've posted something recently that's not [crap]." Prospects consume 2–4 pieces to check you're legit and still in business.
- Expect ~6 months to learn platform packaging. Acquisition.com took ~18 months before deal flow or growth showed.
- Vendors first (costlier, ~5× faster, they know the mistakes), then bring it in-house once you've learned from them.
- Don't let vanity metrics (views, subs) pull you toward content that isn't core to you. Pay attention only if *nothing* ever hits.
- "No such thing as too long, only too boring." Volume first: "quantity wins over quality" early because reps build quality.

## Playbook steps
1. Post something. 2. Set a cadence on one platform. 3. Build the best-content sheet and FAQ list for sales. 4. Mine top organic hooks for ads and cold email; split test them. 5. If content is to be lead gen: go all platforms and learn packaging via specialist vendors. 6. Max out every platform's volume. 7. Insource the team. 8. Phase 5: deliberate capture formats. Throughout: value + one simple CTA.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Not even doing phase 1.
- Over-pitching a small audience ("oversell the small amount of demand they have"), so it never grows.
- Vendors optimizing for easy-to-quantify metrics: "Just because it's easy to quantify doesn't mean it's important."
- Talking about things you don't have the right to talk about, or trying to compete with Gary Vee / Grant Cardone instead of owning a niche.
- Expecting fast results from organic.
