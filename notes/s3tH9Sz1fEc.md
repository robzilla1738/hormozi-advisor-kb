## Core thesis
"You get paid on what you do; you get returns on what you own." To turn an active business into a passive, sellable asset, separate the founder's labor income from the business's true profit. Take **two steps forward**: delegate what you do now *and* hire someone for what you'd do next, then step back to an owner/chairman role. Profit may drop, but dollars per hour and enterprise value rise. Don't do this too soon.

## Frameworks & models
- **Labor vs return split**: a business "netting" $3M where replacing you takes $1–1.5M of executives (4–5 leaders) really makes ~$1.5M. You're doing ~$1.5M of payroll work. Separate the two.
- **Two steps forward, not one**: one step = delegate current work and do the next thing yourself. Two steps = delegate current work AND hire someone to do the next thing, so you own it while others run it.
- **Entrepreneurship = repeated time arbitrage**: delegate lowest-value hours ($10–15/hr) and refill them with higher-value skills such as marketing, new channels or product.
- **Chairman role**: meet the CEO about direct reports, leadership and vision. Vision rarely changes, so the focus is execution.
- **Outside-in perspective**: you see weaknesses, the market and customer feedback better from outside.
- **Poor/rich/wealthy**: "Poor people do the wrong things, rich people do the right things, wealthy people own the right things."
- **Constraint question**: once out, ask "how can I increase revenue without me doing anything?"

## Decision rules & thresholds
- The #1 mistake is stepping out of the CEO seat too soon; he pushes back on founders at ~$1–3M wanting to "outsource my business." He did it after reaching the wealth he wanted.
- Accept a pay cut (e.g., losing your $1M of labor income) for higher enterprise value. Net worth can rise as business profit falls.
- When you outsource time, refill it with higher-value work if you want growth.

## Playbook steps
1. Delegate the lowest-value tasks. 2. Level up skills to fill the freed time. 3. Price your own role at market (exec cost) to see true profit. 4. Hire for both current and next roles (two steps). 5. Install a management team and become chairman. 6. Push revenue growth without your involvement.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Exiting too soon.
- Investor-mode temptation to make passive things active again to chase returns.
- Outsourcing time and not reinvesting it.
- Personality-brand businesses that can't be sold ("no one's going to buy me").

## Examples / case details
- Gym Launch: ~$17M profit (per quality of earnings, 2018) when Alex was the face, product and CEO, versus ~$8M expected "this year" with ~90 minutes/week of his time. It is now worth more because it's an enterprise with a management team.
- New success metrics: time with his wife, gym, meeting people.
