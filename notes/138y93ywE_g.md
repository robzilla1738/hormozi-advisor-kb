# 3 signs you're going to be a successful entrepreneur.

Signal: medium

## Core thesis
Entrepreneurs at $1M–$50M usually aren't limited by tactics. They're limited by character, meaning behavior patterns. Humans want new, now and easy. Business requires the opposite: **focus** (keep doing the old thing), **patience** (delay gratification), and **frustration tolerance** (keep going through failure without re-questioning whether it's worth it). "All businesses can get very big. Not all entrepreneurs can."

## Frameworks & models
- **Three traits**: focus, patience, frustration tolerance.
- **Most dangerous competitor test**: "What would the competitor I'd least want to face do in my situation?" This exploits the Solomon paradox: we give better advice than we follow because we have no emotional tie.
- **Emotion vs logic**: if an emotion and logic point the same way, fine, but logic is what made the decision right. Taking action relieves anxiety, but that doesn't make the action correct.
- **Surface area of thinking / coats of paint**: revisit the same problem many times, in different moods and through different lenses. Solutions become more complete and elegant over repetitions.
- **Known hard vs unknown hard**: a new business looks easier only because you haven't done it yet. Take known hard.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Before acting on a feeling, ask: is this action meant to make the emotion go away, or is it what the business needs? Usually the right move is to keep doing what you were doing and let the emotion pass ("emotions are like the weather").
- Before starting a second business or revenue line, ask: is anything, logically, stopping the existing business from doing 2×, 5× or 10×, besides it being hard?

## Playbook steps
1. Ask what the most dangerous competitor would do in your place, then do that.
2. Separate emotional itch from business need; acknowledge the feeling and continue the plan.
3. Ladder up to the three traits: kill new ideas that aren't required (focus), give problems repeated thinking time (patience), sit with discomfort and keep taking the actions you planned before your emotions objected (frustration tolerance).

## Mistakes he warns against
- Seeking tactics when the gap is traits.
- Shifting priorities weekly after reading an article or watching a video. This whiplashes the team and customers and confuses the messaging, so "you stand for nothing."
- Starting new projects instead of seeing things through.
- Making changes to the business to change how you feel inside.

## Examples / case details
- He says he meets entrepreneurs running $1M, $10M and $50M businesses at Vegas HQ weekly and can guess their range from how they behave.
- He suggests successful CEOs seem robotic because acting logically, independent of feeling, is what the work demands; it's not how they started.
