## Core thesis
Sales needs two things: **conviction** (measured by degree, not yes/no) and **trust** (from expertise plus rapport). Trust carries conviction from the believer to the non-believer. Juice conviction with fresh customer results, run the same conversation framework every time, and handle objections without confrontation through "childlike curiosity." The personal side: his drive started as "crippling insecurity and needing approval," then became conditioned habit. He accepts that he loves working, and calls other people's idea of balance "irrelevant to me."

## Frameworks & models
- **Conviction + trust**: you can "trick yourself into the right tone" simply by believing. Testimonials from last week's buyers are what really juice the team.
- **Standard sales conversation**: why are they here → what's the problem → what have they tried → where did they fail → why ours differs from what failed → ask permission to explain → explain via the experiences they'll have (not features), using analogies → close (a "no-based" close, e.g. "would you mind…?").
- **Childlike curiosity for objections**: "Huh, that's so interesting, tell me more." Example: "My husband won't approve" → does he want what's best for you? does he know you're struggling? would he be happier if you kept struggling? → "would you be opposed to moving forward today?"
- **Slow your breathing like a fighter**: adrenaline makes you combative, so slow down.
- **Seasons**: entrepreneurial seasons of about 5 years each (about 3 years to see a thing through, about 2 to transition), "kind of like a PE cycle." Framing time as a season makes commitments feel lighter.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Hiring reps: he allows a very short window to fail, because "I've never had a killer salesperson who didn't do pretty well the first week." The result is a team of killers.
- "We've had this conversation a hundred times, they have only had it once": the rep should lead the framework.
- "In arguments no one wins."

## Playbook steps
Stack sales appointments on proof-heavy days (weigh-outs). Drill the framework. Respond to objections with curiosity questions that lead them to their own conclusion.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Chasing a prospect toward an outcome without a framework.
- Pitching features.
- Getting defensive or confrontational.
- Judging yourself by others' arbitrary ideals of balance.
