## Core thesis
"The way to create a fortune in business is to only sell things that people do not stop buying." Marketing and sales tactics can make you a millionaire but won't build a fortune. Fortunes come from retention, driven by **customer surplus**: value delivered far beyond the price paid. Raise value rather than cutting price. Recorded about 30 days after he and Leila sold their eight-figure software company. He says the acquisition.com portfolio does ~$85M/yr.

## Frameworks & models
- **Lifetime-customers thought experiment**: tally everyone you've ever sold × current average monthly price. Imagine that as your revenue, and how much more you could spend on acquisition and how confident you'd be if you never lost a customer.
- **Customer surplus**: value minus price. The test is whether customers would immediately update a failed card (Wi-Fi, phone, Netflix). "Price is what you pay. Value is what you get."
- **Models > methods**: a method is bought once and no longer needed. A model can absorb new methods over time (franchises sell models; the marketing channel changes from Groupon to email to Facebook ads, but the model stays). Move from selling methods to selling models.
- **SaaS metrics apply to services**: "software is just software doing the job that service is supposed to be doing."
- **Promotion → Product → Scale** (credited to "Mark Rober," early HubSpot, now a Harvard lecturer; [inference] likely Mark Roberge, caption error):
  - Promotion (~$0–1M): price high enough that people care, not to maximize profit. "You make sales to get customers," not the reverse.
  - Product (~$1–10M): iterate until people don't leave.
  - Scale: once retention holds, marketing compounds ("glue").
- References his "8 C's" stickiness video. He says customer surplus is by far the biggest of them.
- **"High value," not "high ticket."**

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Target 1–2% monthly churn at most, vs the 10–20% most businesses have.
- Don't leave the product phase until people stop leaving.
- Create surplus by raising value, not cutting price. Cutting price only works when reproduction costs near zero (e.g. Dropbox storage).
- B2B selling ~100 clients/month should be able to reach a $100M enterprise *if* you don't lose customers.
- "If you never lose a customer, you will literally never stop making more money year-over-year."

## Playbook steps
1. Ask what you can sell that people never stop buying once they start.
2. Restructure from method to model.
3. Run promotion to acquire customers at a price that makes them care.
4. Interview and survey customers, study usage and consumption patterns, and build community ties until churn is 1–2%/month.
5. Then scale acquisition into the sticky product.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Stopping at promotion (common in internet marketing) and "obsessing about Stripe account screenshots."
- Selling one-and-done methods.
- Cutting prices to create value.
- Treating "high ticket" as the goal.

## Examples / case details
- Starbucks, Coca-Cola, Apple and Netflix as never-stop-buying products.
- Consulting engagement: a company selling the same number of clients per month as his made $300K/month vs his $4M/month, and he thinks he had ~100× their profit, thanks to better monetization and product.
- Lambo for $20K or a $500K house for $50K: obvious value. The game is building the $500K house for $5–10K and selling it for $50K.
- Over 20 years, a never-churn product gets huge even with mediocre promotion.
