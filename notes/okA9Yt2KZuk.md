## Core thesis
Keynote and Q&A at Taki Moore's 8-figure mastermind; Taki and Leila Hormozi also speak. Part 1: how Gym Launch fixed being dependent on one channel (Facebook/IG) by building a manual outbound machine over about 14 months. It is a three-tier BDR → SDS → closer "farm" with title ladders that cut churn and create closers who were never spoiled by inbound leads. Part 2: lessons from preparing for an exit. Buyers named churn, founder-as-brand and channel dependence as the weaknesses. Fixing them ("fix the house") made the business worth keeping. Each growth stage requires giving up a layer of control: doing → managing → leading → deciding.

## Frameworks & models
- **Outbound ramp is linear, not exponential**: sales per month went 1 → 2 → 4 → ~6–8 → 12 → 16 … → ~45/month at month 14 (~60% of sales volume). Keep going as long as the funnel shows progress.
- **Christmas-tree org**: BDRs (cold callers, the "ad") feed half as many SDSs (sales development specialists, who qualify and deliver value), who feed half as many BCs (business consultants, the closers). A separate team sells the ascension to the $40K product.
- **Outbound pipeline**: scrape lists from Google → Philippine VAs enrich/verify → PhoneBurner with local-area-code masking → 100 dials → ~20 pickups → ~2 shows to the SDS → closers close ~10% of SDS shows. BDRs are paid $50 per show, so cost is ~$500 per close against a $40K LTV.
- **Value-first call sequence**: BDR call ("if gyms don't exist, we don't exist; let us give you what's working from our private client program, free") → SDS 45-min call: 30 min delivering something genuinely valuable (e.g. sales room setup and scripts) + 15 min "switch hats" ("that's 1% of the juice… do you know what we actually do?") → live scripted speakerphone call to the closer, who hops on Zoom only to build rapport and book → next-day close call, framed explicitly as a sales call.
- **Baseball farm system**: AA = BDR, AAA = SDS, the bigs = closers. The training system itself becomes a business asset. Inbound close rate rose from ~20–25% to **60%** once closers came up through outbound.
- **Four-level title ladders** (junior / associate / [base title] / senior) at each tier. Objective qualifiers (e.g. junior BDR: 50 showed appointments, ~8 weeks) are paced at about 8 weeks of production per step, with symbolic raises (~$50 → $55). Senior status earns "the ability to be promoted," not a promotion. Missing qualifiers means relegation.
- **On-target earnings**: BDR ~$40K; SDS ~$55–70K; closers ~$120–220K (top ~$225K). Recruit at $40K by showing a path to ~$200K in 9–12 months with no degree.
- **CLOSER**: Clarify why they're there; Label the problem; Overview past pain (go through everything they've tried until they feel "six inches away"); Sell the vacation (3 points max, each with a ≤30-sec analogy, e.g. brushing teeth = external accountability turning into internal motivation); Explain away concerns; Reinforce the decision (personalized video from the onboarding person within the hour; a "handshake, not handoff").
- **Objection triangle**: decision maker (past agreements: "do they want you to keep struggling?"; a 3–5 day out clause for B2B); price (drill the sales team's conviction, not price scripts); delay (best/worst case; 3-question decision framework: can we help? do you want to work with us? do you have access to the money?). Concession → reciprocity → "fair enough."
- **Sales = transference of conviction**, with trust as the bridge. Conviction comes in degrees, not a yes/no. Stack sales calls on client-results days (gym "weigh-out days").
- **Beta process** (productize innovation without a guru): customers list and upvote problems → pick the top problem → round table with the top 1% (10–20 clients) → write everything down → keep only what *all* of them do (churn: 180 items → 5) → productize → test on bottom-10% and middle clients for 30 days with no extra help → report results, including failures.
- **Name off the door** (~3-year process): specialist subject-matter experts rather than generalists (a generalist becomes a replacement face or a flight risk); recruit superfan ex-clients as head of product; gradually put other people in the ads.
- **Give-up ladder by revenue**: $0–1M stop doing fulfillment; $1–10M stop selling (learn to recruit, train and manage salespeople); $10–30M give up marketing; $30–50M+ give up leading and become an adviser who provides capital and connections. Org levels mirror this: doing → managing → managing managers → leading business units → advising.
- **Business of businesses**: once stuck at ~$30M (citing Mark Ford), move to business units with their own P&Ls around shared services (IT, HR, legal, finance). Pay unit leaders on growth ($300–500K) or they start their own version of your business.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Outbound needs a ticket of at least ~$15K (theirs: $20K minimum, $40K LTV).
- Below ~$10M you needn't worry about channel dependence; at $10M+ add a second channel.
- Cold callers: 4-hour shifts with 100 dials expected. The manager's experience was that 8 paid hours yield only ~5.5 hours of calling, so 4 paid hours yield ~3.5.
- Expect each pipeline step to take 2–4 weeks to figure out.
- New closers get 10 deals / 2 weeks. Close zero of 10 and they drop back down. "I have yet to find a killer who doesn't close in the first two weeks."
- Closers at ~25% or below get bumped down to AAA.
- Hire BDRs with almost no screening; they ramp in 2 days and leads cost almost nothing, so let them prove themselves.
- Phones drive ~75% of outbound results (email/text follow-ups are secondary).
- Use question-based frameworks, not word-for-word scripts, except for ~20-min transactional sales of about $200 or less.
- Hire people who have already built the thing ("already run an Olympic gold"); be very patient when you see progress and impatient when you don't.
- Use a lead magnet only if it's something people genuinely want; do it unscalably first.

## Playbook steps
1. Get the exit-readiness critique from bankers/PE. 2. Build list scraping + VA enrichment. 3. Hire BDRs (low bar, 2-day ramp, $50/show, 100 dials in 4 hrs). 4. Staff SDSs with ex-clients who show domain expertise. 5. Run the value call → switch hats → live speakerphone intro to closer → next-day close. 6. Install title ladders with ~8-week qualifiers and relegation. 7. Promote from senior SDS to closer with a 10-deal trial. 8. Run CLOSER and drill conviction. 9. Send a reinforcement video right after the sale.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Quitting outbound early when the funnel is still progressing.
- Lead magnets that aren't truly valuable.
- Screening too hard for entry-level reps.
- Training "selling the plane flight" (features, Zoom calls, support hours) instead of the vacation.
- Founder-as-guru dependence and generalist replacement faces.
- Hiring people to learn on the job instead of proven builders.
- Believing the sale/exit story. A "$100M" sale ≈ $60M cash + $10M seller note + $20M earnout + 10% rolled equity; after ~4% broker, bonuses, escrow and ~20% capital-gains tax, about $40M actually lands. Buyers also want involvement proportional to what you do now.
- Chasing freedom *from* work instead of freedom *to* do what you want (~9 months bored after replacing himself).

## Examples / case details
- Gym Launch + Prestige Labs were doing ~$1M/week after a best-ever month. Leila wanted to shut it down; a banker said ~$250M if they kept the pace for 12 months ($17M profit in year two). The process took 2.5 years.
- Largest sales team in the portfolio: 26 reps. Portfolio: ~$85M/year.
- Alex couldn't get YouTube to work in the gym-owner niche, so he chose outbound.
- Neighbor: $3.8B revenue, 100% owned, 26% net margins, "doing the same thing for 40 years." Alex states $935M take-home [inference: 26% of $3.8B ≈ $988M; figure as spoken].
- Advice from a friend: "Match the conditions." Make life look like post-sale before the sale and keep the asset. The wealthy "buy, build, refinance"; a 100%-owned asset can carry debt (borrowed cash isn't taxed as income in the US).
- A senior team member told Leila: "It's really nice not having you guys around," and the team is more self-reliant since they stepped out.
- Recommended book: *The Motive*. Mark Ford's book on being stuck at ~$30M.
