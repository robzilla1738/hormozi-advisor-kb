## Core thesis
Gym Launch-era internal training. Track client outcome data and slice it into compelling, compliant hooks that become the central idea of whole campaigns. Everyone promises "we make gyms more money"; true stats make the promise unique and believable, and give the seller conviction. The title's "106M impressions" isn't discussed in the transcript.

## Frameworks & models
- **Three wheels of stats**: (1) **percentage of clients** (average, median, top X%, bottom X%, "1 in N," fraction, or the inversion, e.g. 1 in 5 vs 4 out of 5); (2) **outcome** (revenue, profit, churn, growth %, pounds lost); (3) **time duration** (first 2 weeks, 30 days, 6 weeks, 1 year, 2 years later; or "every X days we produce Y"). Spin them until you find the sweet spot.
- **Four ways to express an increase**: percent increase; absolute increase (+$15,500); total amount (base plus increase); relative increase (what it buys, e.g. months of rent).
- **"1 in N" beats a percentage**: 1 in 5.5 sounds better than 18%. It plays on lottery and survivorship hopes.
- **Averages and medians**: "that means half of people are above this," and everyone thinks they're above average.
- **Long-duration stats ("one year later")**: long-tenured clients usually have great outcomes, and the stat proves sustained value and longevity in the market, which newer competitors can't show. "One year later" was their most successful campaign ever.
- **Frequency framing**: annual count ÷ time = "every week we create another seven-figure gym."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Choose the framing that sounds biggest: "$15,500" over "30% increase."
- In weight loss, slide the threshold (12 lb, 16 lb, 30 lb) against the percentage hitting it to find a strong, true stat (e.g. 70% hit 16 lb in a set period).
- Stay absolutely compliant. Use only substantiated data.
- Track client success data for years; it's a big advantage because most competitors don't measure (often because the numbers are bad).

## Playbook steps
1. Have customer success capture before and after metrics on a schedule. 2. Pull cohorts by tenure. 3. Spin the three wheels for each metric. 4. Express increases four ways and pick the most compelling. 5. Build the campaign hook around the winning stat. 6. Use averages plus "half do better."

## Mistakes he warns against
- Not capturing your own data.
- Generic promises everyone makes.
- Copy alone, without a data-backed story.
