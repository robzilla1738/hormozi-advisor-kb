## Core thesis
Once you know the tactics, the bottleneck is hunger, not knowledge. Owners who "make it" (sometimes just $10K in the bank) get complacent and stop doing the obvious things. If someone's life depended on your results you would cut the noise and execute, so the real work is finding out *why* you aren't doing what you already know to do. Alex says champions are driven by something deep, usually insecurity or fear. If you only want enough to live on, you'll only ever make that. This is a repost of a gym-owner talk from his former private community ("Eye of the Tiger"/sustained victory) after a 3-day mastermind with top Gym Launch sellers.

## Frameworks & models
- **Life-on-the-line test**: if a loved one would die unless you got more clients, would you be available only 1 hour a day? Prospect only an hour a day? Contact members only after a month absent?
- **Coach yourself**: step outside yourself and ask what you'd tell you. You usually already know. Aim all your attention at the reason you don't act.
- **Payroll proof**: whenever you *must* make $10K in 3 days, you do. So you know how, and you're choosing not to the rest of the time.
- **Relief test**: if you feel a big sigh of relief at your first $20K/$40K/$100K take-home month, your motivation isn't a champion's. Money is an output. Focus on the deficiencies that still existed when you hit the number.
- **Chopping wood** (term attributed to Mike Grandon): boring reps every day, like working leads, handwritten cards, weigh-in reminders, an enthusiastic greeting, the same questions every time ("to that person it's day one").
- **Bigger-than-you motive**: satisfying one person or family keeps you small. Serving a community, state or nation requires a bigger drive.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Sales availability must match when buyers are free. One 7:15am slot (or only 9–11am weekdays) can't grow a business. His analogy: a grocery store open only 7:00–7:30.
- If you don't actually want more, say so honestly ("I only want $10K/month") and stop complaining.
- Once tactics are known, more tactics are useless until the motivation and avoidance issue is fixed.

## Playbook steps
1. Audit the obvious holes (availability, lead follow-up, reminders, training, team meetings). 2. Ask "if a life depended on this, what would I do?" 3. Identify why you aren't doing it (headspace, relationships, priorities) and attack that. 4. Do the boring reps daily. 5. After a big month, review what was missing instead of celebrating relief.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Complacency after early success; making only "enough to live on".
- Setting availability around the owner's convenience.
- Swinging between business and personal life: focus on the gym and life falls apart, switch to life and the gym falls apart, because you never learn to prioritize both.
- Looking for a secret or hack when the answer is reps.
- Paying for motivation, or waiting for a crisis to try hard.

## Examples / case details
- OG gym owners near 7 figures complained about show rates. Availability for new clients was one hour, at 7:15am.
- Mastermind: 2 of 3 gyms over 7 figures, the third expected within ~3 months. Ed Turney and Mike Redstone are named. Those meetings were easy: "clean lives", "what do I need to do?"
- Gym Launch "freeing and soul-crushing": once owners know how to build a 7-figure gym, the only remaining reason is them.
- Alex's driver: fear of failing, of not being good enough, of haters being right. "It's not about money."
