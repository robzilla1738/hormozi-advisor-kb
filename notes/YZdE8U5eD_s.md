Speaker note: Interview. Most content comes from guest Harley Finkelstein (Shopify president; the captions give his first name only). Alex hosts and adds commentary and tactics. Attribution is marked below.

## Core thesis
Starting a business costs less to fail at than ever ("effectively $39"), so take many shots. Start by scratching your own itch or by telling people you want to start something. Grow through generous partnerships, referral rev-shares, brand champions and focus. Keep founder mentality at scale with crafter tracks, open disagreement, transparency and a single North Star. Make your partners rich.

## Frameworks & models
- **Cost of failure → near zero (Harley)**: his grandfather mortgaged his home for an egg stall. Gymshark's Ben Francis (2012, dorm room) would have just gone back to class if it failed. The downside is now "a couple less cups of coffee." Alex: risk-adjusted return means endless shots on goal.
- **Idea sources**: scratch your own itch (Alex: "every really good business I've had has come from that"). "Do things and tell people" (Harley): telling everyone he needed a business led to the university promo-shirt idea. Commercialize a hobby or obsession (David's Tea founder → Firebelly).
- **Luck surface area (Alex)**: mentors (ahead of you) + peer community (Harley met Toby at a Friday young entrepreneurs' club). Online communities do this now.
- **Swiss army knife early hire (Harley)**: the non-engineer does whatever adds value to the builders (lease, Rackspace/PayPal deals, PR, legal).
- **Shopify's scaling principles (Harley)**: (1) Focus: win SMBs in English-speaking countries; competitors died running upmarket too soon. (2) Simplicity: complexity reveals itself over time. (3) Tight CAC:LTV. Once it was above 1, raise money and pour it in. (4) Patience: a 100-year business; never pull forward future profits at a discount.
- **Three growth levers (Harley, recapped by Alex)**: (1) Partner/referral ecosystem: app store with an 80/20 rev share (more generous than Apple's 70/30); agencies got ~20% of Shopify's cut in perpetuity, "a distributed salesforce not on our payroll." (2) Build a Business competition (Tim Ferriss's idea, $100K prize) helped them "cross the chasm." Later prizes were experiences (a week with Branson, lunch with Seth Godin). (3) Economies of scale passed to customers: negotiate payments and shipping as an aggregate, keep a few basis points, and give the rest away.
- **Brand champion (Alex)**: find your "Michael Jordan." This is someone who already bought from you organically and has distribution. Let them co-create (Tim felt like a co-founder). Asking them warmly beats cold sponsorship.
- **Local partnership loop (Alex example, chiropractor)**: smoothie shop, gym and yoga studio put in a coupon for a free adjustment. Their offer gets better at no cost to them, and you get free leads. Harley adds doctor-style closed referral loops, plus over-deliver on referrals you send.
- **Founder mentality at scale (Harley)**: an IC/"crafter" track alongside the manager track; encourage disagreement, then disagree-and-commit; share the unredacted quarterly board letter with the whole company; a single North Star ("merchant obsessed") as the tiebreaker for decentralized decisions. Alex: open-book quarterly financials in his companies.
- **"Pointy, not river stones"**: be world-class at one thing and hire around the rest. Partner with people who are pointy in different directions.
- **Positive-sum / grow the pie (Harley)**: TAM framing is flawed. Shopify 2022: $5.6B revenue vs ~$200B merchant GMV; partners made >$6B. Alex heuristic: you keep about 1/10 of the wealth you create for others.
- **Authenticity (Alex definition)**: "behaving as if you could not be punished." Your unique mix of interests creates a category of one (Rogan).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Pay referral partners at least your CAC; a partner referral likely has higher LTV than paid ads (Alex).
- "Never count the other guy's money": judge the deal only on your side (Alex, crediting Leila).
- Give partners customers first (e.g., send 5), then propose the partnership (Alex).
- CAC:LTV above 1 → time to raise and pour in capital (Harley).
- Around 100 employees / $10–30M revenue, mission and values become decision frameworks (Alex). Dunbar's 150 (Harley).
- Be in the weeds on what matters most ("micromanagement is bad is total horseshit" — Harley; Alex: "win in the weeds").
- Ask every key person: manager track or IC track?

## Playbook steps
1. Start something cheap that solves your own problem; tell everyone. 2. Build mentor + peer network. 3. Focus on one segment and keep the product simple. 4. Know CAC:LTV, then scale spend. 5. Build referral partners with generous perpetual rev-share, giving customers first. 6. Create an irresistible competition/offer and a brand champion who co-owns it. 7. Pass scale economics to customers. 8. Keep founder culture with IC tracks, open books, and a North Star.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Waiting for the perfect passion business. The first business teaches you the next step (Alex).
- Keeping your goals secret.
- Losing partnership deals out of greed over what the partner makes (Alex's own mistake).
- Going upmarket too soon. Side quests outside your main quest (Shopify divested its fulfillment network to Flexport).
- Hiding weaknesses. Harley stepped down as COO (2015–2020) because he wasn't the best at it, and Kaz became COO.
- Choosing co-founders who are just like you.
- Inauthentic paid celebrity sponsorships.

## Examples / case details
- Harley's start: DJ business at 13 (hired himself); university promo t-shirts in college. A tip said McGill spent $25K a semester; it was really about $250K.
- Shopify: started as Toby's snowboard store (2005–06); Harley joined ~2009. Series A $7M at ~$25M post (Bessemer). Series B at ~$100M valuation, 50–100 employees. IPO May 2015 at ~$1B after 93 road-show meetings (46M SMBs TAM). Now ~$100B; aggregated US stores would be the #2 online retailer after Amazon.
- 2024 stats cited: ~25% of US consumers are considering starting a business; 15.5M new business applications since 2021.
- Spouse-relationship strength said to have a ~0.71 correlation with subjective well-being (Alex).
