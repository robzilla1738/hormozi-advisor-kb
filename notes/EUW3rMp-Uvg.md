## Core thesis
Mass-market businesses (consumer products, biz-op, mass services: beauty, weight loss, investing, relationships) plateau because three things inevitably compress margins as they scale. The only counterforce is the "invisible hand" of positive word of mouth, so somewhere around $1–5M you must pause scaling and invest in customer experience. Go slow to go fast.

## Frameworks & models
- **Stage sequence:** $0–100k = sell something to someone with unreliable acquisition. $100k–1M = one reliable channel, one product, one avatar → product-market fit. After PMF, "do more of the same" eventually kills margin.
- **Three inevitable margin killers at scale:** (1) CPMs rise over time, (2) you reach colder audiences so conversion falls, (3) infrastructure and fixed costs rise. (Physical-goods economies of scale are diminishing: most savings come in the first two order-of-magnitude purchase increases.)
- **Word of mouth is "quadratic"** (1→2→4→8) and the only acquisition channel that doesn't degrade this way. It lowers CAC at scale and warms cold markets.
- **Negative WoM detector:** if CAC rises faster than CPM (e.g. CPM +10%, CAC +40%), you have negative word of mouth. Unhappy customers tell ~10x more people.
- **Expectation-delivery gap:** set modest expectations and over-deliver. Hype now → negative gap → negative WoM. (Movie analogy.)
- **Brand = what people say about your product behind your back.** It gives pricing power: a 10–20% price bump with no demand change can 2–4x profit on thin-margin B2C.

## Decision rules
- Illustrative margin compression: $1M→$10M takes margin 40%→30%; $10M→$30M takes it 20%→10%.
- At ~$1–5M/yr, pause and build customer success: a CS director or team tracking time-to-value, NPS and customer health scores, all aimed at referrals.
- Neutral word of mouth isn't enough. The three forces still hit, and you end at an equilibrium "massive nonprofit you're 100% liable for".
- Without a brand, you're just media arbitraging.

## Playbook steps
1. Recognize PMF and the 3 eventualities.
2. Pause the "more of the same" scaling.
3. Learn what job customers hired you for, find ways to surprise and delight them, shorten time-to-value.
4. Lower the promise, raise delivery.
5. Track CS metrics and referrals. Then use pricing power.
