# How To Actually Get Rich In Your 20s

## Core thesis
A mindset/life-strategy monologue aimed at young men: when young you have energy but no utility, so trade energy for experience (high volume reps + deliberate feedback), live far below your means to build a stockpile that lets you take bets and think long term, avoid stupidity (drinking, late nights, out of shape, status spending, distracting friends), and judge every person/activity by "does this make it more or less likely I hit my goal?" Little direct business mechanics; manifest category "pricing" is misleading.

## Frameworks & models
- **"Use what you've got"** — resourcefulness, not resources; energy is the resource you have.
- **Broke-defense list (<$100K savings)**: 1) stop eating out, discount groceries only; 2) Goodwill clothes only; 3) only free friend events; 4) spend rest of time (a) applying to higher-paying jobs, (b) side-hustle work, (c) learning skills worth a higher hourly rate; 5) find the hardest worker and double their input. Then wait 36 months.
- **Survival vs Thrive mode**: stockpile lets you be patient and think long term; it's about spend relative to income ($40K/mo earned & spent = still survival).
- **Skill = repetitions × improvement between repetitions.** Volume + feedback loop.
- **Top/bottom review**: look at top 10 vs bottom 10 videos (or top 50 vs bottom 50 of last 2,000 ads), find what winners share, do more of it, build a checklist. Applied to ads, sales calls, content, meetings, businesses.
- **Learning = new behavior under the same conditions** (if the script never changes, you aren't learning).
- **Patient with outputs, impatient with inputs**; patience = figuring out what to do in the meantime; "action alleviates anxiety."
- **Dumb vs inexperienced** (banana story): make mistakes fast without self-judgment.
- **Intelligence = rate of learning**; Munger: avoid stupidity and you become intelligent by default.
- **Reference group**: the ~5 people you compare yourself to shape your outcomes; the "lonely chapter" between groups; hate as a leading indicator.
- **Goal filter**: "is it more or less likely I hit my goal with this person/activity?"
- **Defense to play offense / "your lifestyle is your competitor's opportunity"** — keep cash for bigger deals (e.g. being ready to write a $10M check).
- **Three things to win**: balls to start, brains to learn, heart to never quit.
- **Logic / Evidence / Utility** (attributed to "Dr. Cashy" per captions): define a vague claim ("I have demons") as behavior → what evidence → so what/who cares relative to goals → what to do.
- **Trauma redefined** as permanent behavior change from an aversive stimulus; good or bad only relative to goals.
- **20-hour rule**: before saying you can't figure something out, try for 20 hours.
- **Balance over a lifetime, not every moment**; seasons.
- **Entrepreneurship is Last Man Standing, not best man wins** (infinite game).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If broke with no skills: go fully defensive on spending. If you can earn ~$500/30 min selling, outsource anything worth less than $500/hr (cooking, groceries).
- Double the input of the hardest worker in the room (100 doors → 200).
- Give a new direction ~36 months before judging it.
- Don't ask for the next career move 30 days into a job.
- Delay gratification long enough to get the first feedback-loop reward; that reinforces the habit.
- Many right answers; the only wrong answer is doing nothing.
- Spend on status only if you actually value it; early-career luxury spending only impresses poor people ("impress poor people, outspend them; impress rich people, outwork them").

## Playbook steps
1. Cut costs to the bone (list above). 2. Use freed time for higher-paying job, side hustle, higher-rate skills. 3. Take a high-volume sales role (door-knocking, cold calling, in-person) for years. 4. Out-input the hardest worker. 5. Review top vs bottom outputs and iterate a checklist. 6. Learn from people ahead (pay for a mastermind, ask to be mentored, share what works for you). 7. Sleep on time, get in shape, stop drinking during intense build periods. 8. Prune relationships/activities via the goal filter. 9. Build a stockpile; stay patient ~36 months.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Not starting because you lack capital/connections.
- Posting/working at volume without reviewing what worked.
- Researching for a "better plan" instead of starting.
- Leasing a Ferrari / showing face; being accurately judged ("if you are strong, appear weak").
- Treating vague narratives ("demons," trauma "stored in your body") as reasons not to act.
- Ego: refusing to learn from people you think you're smarter than.
- Letting someone walk out "to get their credit card."
