## Core thesis
A 10-step warm-outreach system for getting your first five customers, previewed from *$100M Leads*. Pull every contact you have, send personal messages (100/day), warm them up, and ask for referrals rather than a sale. Give the first five a free case study in exchange for usage, feedback and a review. Then step the price up as referrals arrive, use real capacity scarcity to reach full or premium price, and keep the list warm. Includes a mid-video ad for the book launch event (Aug 19).

## Frameworks & models
- **Three lists**: (1) all email contacts, (2) everyone you can DM on social, (3) phone contacts.
- **Pick one platform**: start with the one where you have the most contacts.
- **Personalize**: spend 30 seconds on their profile and open with something real ("saw you switched jobs / had a baby").
- **ACA (Acknowledge, Compliment, Ask)**: "you have two kids" → "you must be a super mom" → tie the ask to the compliment and lead toward your service ("how do you have time to eat right and work out?").
- **Ask for referrals, not a sale**: "Do you know anyone looking for X? I'm opening a few slots." A lead is someone you can contact; an **engaged lead** has also shown interest.
- **Free case-study terms**: free if they (1) use the service, (2) give feedback, (3) leave a killer review if deserved.
- **Script**: "Do you know anybody who is [struggle] looking to [dream outcome] in [time]? I'm taking on five case studies for free because that's all I can handle… I just had [name] get [dream outcome]… does anyone you like come to mind?" If they say no: "Does anyone you hate come to mind?" Short version: "I help [customer] get [dream outcome] in [time] without [effort/sacrifice], and I guarantee XYZ."
- **Price ladder**: free ×5 → 80% off ×5 → 60% off → 40% → 20% → full price → can go 20–40% *over* retail if the value merits it.
- **Hidden costs**: if people won't take it even for free, ask why. The reasons are hidden costs. Remove them and you can charge more than competitors who still carry them.
- **Ben Graham / Buffett**: free can still be "overpriced" because of training cost. Free customers still pay with time and effort.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Reach out to 100 people per day.
- Take no more than five free customers ("that's all I can handle").
- Start charging once people start referring.
- He promises that reaching 3,000 people with a free offer will get you five takers.
- At capacity, tell free clients they must match full-price clients' rate to continue. If they decline, ask for the review.

## Playbook steps
1. Build the three lists. 2. Pick a platform. 3. Personalize. 4. Send 100/day. 5. Warm up with ACA. 6. Ask for referrals / invite friends. 7. Free offer for 5 case studies (use, feedback, review). 8. Go back through the list again. 9. Start charging and step up the discount ladder. 10. Keep them warm: check in like a human, provide value, post testimonials and results until they come to you.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Overcomplicating early growth with scaling advice.
- Blasting generic messages.
- Pitching friends directly instead of asking for a favor (referrals).
- Thinking "never work for free": you probably suck and need reps.
- Taking too many free customers.
- Short-term thinking with free customers, who bring reviews, referrals and conversions.

## Examples / case details
- A short-form video editor cold-pitched Alex: "you don't have to do anything," we'll repurpose and post your content. Alex said yes, and the editor later started charging. He had removed the hidden costs.
- Buffett offered to work for Graham for free; Graham said free was too expensive.
