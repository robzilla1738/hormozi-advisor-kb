## Core thesis
If you are exceptional at the product, customers come back and refer, so constant marketing compounds instead of merely replacing churn. If you aren't growing with consistent marketing, the market is telling you the product isn't good enough. Spending far longer to make something exceptional gives the highest return on time.

## Frameworks & models
- **Compounding vs. transactional:** Sell 50/month transactionally and the business dies when marketing stops. If the first 50 come back, the same effort yields 100 next month — linear effort, growing customers.
- **Effort vs. result over a lifetime:** a year or two building something exceptional, then customers advertise it forever — vs. a month making something mediocre and promoting it until you die.
- **"Do what is required, not your best."** Your best isn't the standard; the market decides.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you are not growing with consistent marketing → product isn't good enough.
- If you have an exceptional product and economics still fail → it's usually mispriced; fix the price tag.
- He guesses (explicitly "making up a number") ~95% of businesses could be fixed by improving the product.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Assuming your product is great because it's your best effort.
- Mediocre products that nobody buys twice (his pre-First Form/earlier products).

## Examples / case details
- Best lawyers/investment advisors have waiting lists, no marketing. Charlie Munger: the next customer is the work on the table in front of you.
- $100M Offers: ~1 year, first 6 hours of each day, 160 pages; launched with one post to ~10k followers; no ads; ~20 months later selling 25–28k copies/month and growing monthly by word of mouth. $100M Leads: ~3,500 hours combined with editor, priced at $0.99 ebook.
