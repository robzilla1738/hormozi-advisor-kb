# Building a brand, but it's on easy mode instead

## Core thesis
This is a workshop Q&A with business owners about organic content. Treat early organic content as lead nurture: it makes paid ads more profitable (more shows, higher close rates) and becomes acquisition as it grows. Make content pure "give" and let ads be the "ask." A brand is an association, built through repeated value and through who you appear next to. Line up with the platform's objective instead of hacks, and use volume to learn quality.

## Frameworks & models
- **Traffic + monetization marriage**: one world has money but needs traffic, the other has traffic but no money. Combine them and the money is "obscene." Examples: Logan Paul with Prime, MrBeast with chocolate, and Skool for Alex's own distribution.
- **Content = give, ads = ask**: you don't have to balance a give:ask ratio inside content. The roughly 3:1 ratio still exists across the feed (three content pieces, then one ad). This "protects the brand."
- **Promise / Proof / Path** (intro structure taken from his top 35 videos' first 3 minutes): promise = what's in it for me; proof = "why should I listen to you" (the educator's "Lamborghini," e.g. "I sold my company for $46.2M…"); path = structure and expectations, which drives watch time. Promise and proof are why viewers stay.
- **Brand = association between things known and unknown** (hat example). Three elements: (1) strength of influence, meaning what % of people change behavior (from cattle branding); (2) directionality, toward or away (Trump: strong in both directions); (3) reach. The hypothetical max is everyone moving toward you at once (Taylor Swift).
- "All publicity is publicity": it raises reach, not necessarily strength or direction.
- **Algorithm objective**: platforms want clicks, watch-through, and watching more from you. Align with that. Hacks die when the loop closes.
- **Long-form = strung-together shorts**; there are only three formats: lists, steps, stories, and they can be mixed.
- **Four topic sources**: real-time (tweet dumps / notes), recent past (review your calendar and meetings), far past (lessons that shifted beliefs), manufactured (e.g. "lived on $100 for a month," which is closer to entertainment).
- **Framework for making frameworks**: take in lots of data, list everything, keep only what all the examples have in common, then name it with alliteration or an acronym. Example: the Five Horsemen of retention came from 20 gyms with less than 3% monthly churn over 6–8 months. Each did 20+ things, but only 5 were common to all.
- **Quality power law**: 10× the time on one video can yield 100× the views. Making a video 20% better on AVD or CTR yields disproportionate returns (attributed loosely to MrBeast). Bigger creators post less often.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you run paid ads, start organic now, because it immediately improves ad profitability.
- No CTAs in content ("they kill reach"). Put links in the description and bio.
- Niche vs wide: stay on topic for your avatar. Roughly 3:1 or 4:1 long-form ratio, one going a bit wider as top of funnel. Customers come from niche content even when it gets fewer views.
- Keep packaging congruent with the video's content, and obsess over the first 30 seconds.
- Volume: start with what you can commit to, then ramp up. Volume is for learning quality. His team puts out about 350 pieces a week (about 50 a day) across platforms.
- New platform: start with an agency to "buy lessons and shrink timelines," then hire in-house. Pay twice until "your team beats their team," then drop the agency.
- Finding an agency or creative director: talk to 10 and get referrals. "The best agencies don't need to run ads."
- Be selective about who you appear next to (stages you don't own, podcasts).
- Controversy is basically trending topics: it raises reach and CTR, but not necessarily the other brand elements.

## Playbook steps
1. Make content only as give. Run ads as the ask to the same audience.
2. Capture ideas in real time in one repository (his is tweets; earlier, a yearly email thread to himself of lessons and failures). The team turns tweets into recordings, Instagram captions, and reels.
3. Each week, review your calendar for topics, then mine far-past lessons.
4. Structure intros as Promise, then Proof, then Path.
5. For a new medium: agency → hire an in-house lead (Caleb) to manage the agencies → in-house team replaces them.
6. Summary prescription: find one really smart person, overpay them, and let them hire about 10 people.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Worrying about views in the early days.
- CTAs and "right hooks" in every video, which drive the audience away.
- Growing off hacks (e.g. hashtags) that go to zero when the platform closes the loop.
- Educators copying entertainer intro rules (matching the headline word for word) instead of leading with proof.
- Weak presentation and structure. He tells one attendee better structure could get "10 times the views" on the same content.

## Examples / case details
- He paid an early YouTube agency about $5K/month. They "basically did nothing" but kept him accountable to 3 videos a week, and he still doesn't know how to post on YouTube.
- Workshop room poll: the whole room consumed the case-study / niche content, even though those videos get about a third of the views of the broader "lessons to my younger self" videos.
- He no longer speaks on stages he doesn't own, and rarely appears with others, because of association.
- On cancellation: he says you can only be cancelled if you stop communicating (R. Kelly vs Kanye, who kept making content). He personally avoids controversies.
