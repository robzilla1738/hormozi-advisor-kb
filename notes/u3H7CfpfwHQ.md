## Core thesis
Behind-the-scenes documentary of the *$100M Money Models* book launch, which broke the Guinness record for fastest-selling nonfiction book in 24 hours (beating Prince Harry's ~1.43M copies). Many voices: Alex, team members, Leila and Guinness judges, so not all of it is Alex. The lessons are implicit: the launch itself was built as proof ("proof above everything"); the team pre-mortemed every choke point; a phone sales team supported a low-ticket book purchase with upsell bundles; and goals have to be big enough to motivate.

## Frameworks & models
- **Series as a stack**: the $100M books were written as one book (offers, leads, money models) and split into layers: make the offer → advertise it → monetize it. He launched Offers first because Money Models makes no sense without Offers and Leads.
- **Proof above everything**: "If I said I can double your business... no one believes you." The best way to get belief is to have already done it, so the event and lead-up were designed as a live demonstration.
- **Pre-mortem / choke points**: "Rather than how do we win... how would we fail?" Choke points in order: (1) ad platforms throttle spend, (2) emails and texts fail to send, (3) broken links, (4) Shopify payment processing.
- **Backup ad channels**: when the Google Ads account went down, budget was rerouted to Meta, then TikTok, then LinkedIn.
- **Offer**: "$31 million of consulting work went into this. It's yours for free. You donate 200 bucks." Also a single book or a bundle (staff quoted "$59.98"). The bundle includes a "scan me to scale faster" insert that books a call. 300+ trained phone reps help with checkout.
- **Rehearsal reps**: rehearse the physical flow until it's automatic ("play guitar while singing").
- **Trade-off rule** (Alex clip): "What is the least important thing I'm spending time and money on? Am I willing to trade that for this other thing that I want more? If yes, I make the trade."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Launch mindset: if it's going well, follow the plan; if not, "maybe it hasn't had time... just follow the plan."
- Sales reps: "When we're selling, stay slow... help the person make the purchase."
- One-time live decisions (whether to show a phone number, when, and whether it routes to CS or sales) have to be made in advance and lived with.

## Playbook steps
[inference] 1. Build the offer stack and the proof narrative. 2. Train internal staff across departments on phone sales with role-plays. 3. Pre-mortem the choke points and test every link, payment flow and stream. 4. Line up backup ad channels. 5. Fire the email and SMS announcement. 6. Run a long live event with continuous offers. 7. Keep calling across time zones (they moved on to Australia).

## Mistakes he warns against
- Spinning up a team just for an event; culture can't be manufactured on the spot.
- Goals that are too small to motivate.

## Examples / case details
- Pre-launch, they found many links broken and the Shopify link "sending people to Serbia."
- One buyer bought 10,000 books for his community. Live callouts included 200K books, 1,000 books a second, "2 mill before lunch" and "broke three million." The team said they pushed toward $100M in sales over a multi-day launch. [Figures as spoken in clips; final totals aren't stated clearly.]
- Core value "competitive greatness"; the team treated the launch days as "boot camp for our culture."
