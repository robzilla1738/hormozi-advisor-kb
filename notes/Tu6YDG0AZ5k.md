## Core thesis
This is a vlog episode (San Diego keynote at Real, the real-estate brokerage; Leila appears) built around choosing between two "billion-dollar paths." His framework: size opportunities with a 10× test, avoid irreversible risk (a large share of net worth, or reputation-only downside), and once you pick the big swing, go all in with no half measures. A first-time founder should take the lower-risk path. Much of the runtime is casual banter.

## Frameworks & models
- **10× attention test**: at ~$100M, an opportunity must be $1B+ to deserve his time.
- **Two paths**: one has more upside and more risk; the other is more certain with lower upside. Both are relatively irreversible.
- **What makes a bet irreversible**: (1) a large percentage of net worth or capital: "Never bet the empire for a pot of gold. You bet what you have and need for something you don't have and don't need" (quoting friend Sean/Shiron, president of Real); (2) reputation: "you literally can't bankrupt yourself out of a bad reputation," so avoid downside-only reputational risk (taking a stand that trades one audience for another is a trade-off, not a risk); (3) time and opportunity cost.
- **Asymmetric bets**: Tony Robbins' story of a billionaire storing nickels whose metal is worth more than face value (about 40% upside, almost no downside).
- **Half measures fail**: at a personal-trainer conference he gave 3+ hours of value, then rushed a 90-min pitch into 15 minutes. It was the biggest flop of his life (~2 sales). "Either sell or don't sell... don't mix them."
- **Measure success by actions**: complete the actions that make it unreasonable to fail, and you succeed by default.
- **Sales micro-skills off the phone**: use their name (Carnegie); get people in a good mood, then ask, so they want to help. Examples: the steakhouse dress code, the ice cream shop staying open 5 minutes.
- **Remembering names = leverage** in conversation.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- First time at a crossroads in business: take the lower-risk path with the higher chance of success. You don't hear about the tens of thousands who bet big and lost 5–8 years.
- If you take the big swing: burn the boats, eliminate alternatives, control everything controllable, and only lose to what you couldn't control.
- Don't compete where the audience knows more than you. Frame it as "if I were starting, here's how I'd win."
- Change environment for big decisions; he thinks best on planes.

## Playbook steps
1. Apply the 10× size filter. 2. Check irreversibility: capital share, reputation, time. 3. Prefer asymmetric bets. 4. Commit fully and remove the way back. 5. Turn conference notes into a do-list and change behavior back home.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Half-in/half-out commitment: "not in at all."
- Mixing a value talk with a soft pitch.
- Betting what you need for what you don't need.
- An anecdote of "idiocy": a one-on-one client who only cut back on Netflix while working.

## Examples / case details
- Real keynote: ~350 attendees, 300+ slides finished 20 minutes before. Nerves are "directly proportional to the prep."
- He has enough "singles, doubles, triples" to take a bigger swing now: "I'll either win or I'll learn."
