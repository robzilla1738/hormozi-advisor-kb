## Core thesis
Alex walks through 26 lessons from his first gym (first $100K at 23). Start lean: barter, no ego, learn from people a step ahead. Pre-sell before you open. Compete with a cash-up-front guaranteed offer plus zero-cost upsells so you can outspend rivals. Work your leads hard. Find out what customers actually value before you spend on it. Pair profit-share operators with real authority. Nail one location before you scale it. Then become a teacher instead of the MVP.

## Frameworks & models
- **Barter everything**: go through every expense line and trade services (classes, PT) for cleaning, IT, laundry, videography, food. Ask for less service until it's worth about one membership. He calls it tax-efficient.
- **Market and sell as fast as possible, deliver as late as possible**: pre-sell for about 3 months before opening (prepaid 3/6/12 months, trials that start later). A grand-opening strategy is core to scaling brick and mortar.
- **Pre-sale market test**: spend $500–$1K on ads in each of 5–10 markets and open where leads are cheapest. That's roughly $10K of "insurance" against signing a bad 5-year lease. Never take money for something you won't deliver.
- **Same present, different wrapping paper**: keep the core deliverable and rotate promo names (Lean by Halloween, Slim for Santa, 42-Day Detox, 6-Week Challenge). Locally he made 20–30 ads a week. The creative now does the targeting ("like attracts like").
- **Guarantee formula**: "If you don't achieve X by Y, I will Z." The "or what" is where the power comes from. Two he used most: a service guarantee (we keep working with you until you hit it) and an unconditional one ($500 of value within 6 weeks or your money back, regardless of results).
- **6-Week Challenge**: lose 20 lb in 6 weeks (or 10% of body weight under a BMI cutoff), win your money back, and convert to a year-long membership at the end.
- **Best case / worst case close.**
- **Sell the vacation, not the plane flight**: talk about their current pain and what the result feels like. Signing up is the process.
- **Bonuses, not discounts**: "we don't negotiate with price terrorists." If they ask for a discount, he answers "I could do it for more." If they still can't, change the terms: a discount in exchange for 3 referrals or reviews. Build a bonus for each objection (travel meal plan, budget meal plan, shakes guide, home or away workouts) and match bonus to objection in 1:1 sales.
- **Job to be done**: ask customers open questions without leading them. For his gyms it was community, accountability and a "third place," not the equipment or the periodization.
- **Proof system**: collect proof (engineer wow moments like weigh-ins) and display it (framed reviews and before/afters in the sales room). Ask "How much proof would you need?" to reach the fear underneath. Flywheel: customers → results → more customers.
- **TEAM partner test**: a partner should bring Time, Expertise, Assets or Money that you don't have.
- **Operator profit share (OTE math)**: half of target pay as base, the other half as a % of location profit set so an average location pays exactly OTE.
- **Pair responsibility with authority**: give spending authority (e.g. $1,000, or up to 100% of a customer's last month to fix a complaint). The operator's deal is "this phone doesn't ring."
- **Zero-cost upsells**: sell products alongside the service (supplements, about $200–300) so their margin covers ad spend and all service revenue is gravy. You can also sell the guarantee (+10%). He says about 1 in 10 buyers claim it.
- **Internal plays**: run a quarterly promo to existing customers on one narrow slice ("Big Booty Bootcamp").
- **Agency as tutor**: hire an agency to learn from it and eventually replace it. Ask "What would it take for you to teach me one-on-one?"
- **Absolute vs relative return**: total profit matters more than ROAS.
- **People**: "grind or pay" to find talent. Platinum rule: treat others the way they want to be treated. Friend hat / boss hat.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Don't care about a second location until the first runs without you. Don't open it until the first has grown without you for 6 straight months. Test with a 1-month vacation.
- Don't scale a leaky bucket. If churn is high, fix retention or your customer selection first.
- If customers love it and it's profitable, don't change it, open more. If they love it and it isn't profitable, fix the price, not the product.
- Never discount the core service. Lead magnets are a separate case.
- Give away as little early equity as you can. Splits don't have to be equal; slice by risk. If you're the working partner and they fund it, settle a salary first.
- Rely on one reliable channel early, but never one another business controls (Groupon). He says one channel can scale to about $1M a month before you need to diversify.
- Lead nurture moves results more than close rate. If speed-to-lead gains are worth more than one dedicated caller's salary, hire the caller.
- Services should run 80%+ gross margins.
- Unscalable beginnings are fine (his $4K/mo PT client) as long as you're selling time at a high ticket, not building a second business.
- Keep training minimal: his whole gym sales training was 1h15m (CLOSER).

## Playbook steps
1. Barter your expense lines and ask for help openly instead of posturing. 2. Pay to join peers who are ahead, and shadow their businesses. 3. Ad-test 5–10 markets and pick the cheapest leads. 4. Pre-sell about 3 months with a grand opening. 5. Launch a guaranteed challenge offer plus a zero-cost upsell. 6. Call leads within 60 seconds. 7. Sell the vacation, handle objections with bonuses. 8. Collect and display proof. 9. Run internal plays quarterly. 10. Train others (CLOSER), give operators profit share plus authority. 11. Grow 6 months without you, then expand.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Opening first and marketing second.
- Overspending on location two ($250K vs $34K, about 7×): same revenue, much worse return on capital.
- Asset rich, cash poor, and an artificial rush to prove yourself. He went 2 years living on about $30K/yr with no salary.
- Fixing a packed product to fit capacity: halving session times churned about a third of members ("capacity tail wagging the business dog").
- Obsessing over ROAS. Wanting to be the MVP instead of the teacher. Overcomplicated sales systems. Solving problems only you think are problems. Relying on an agency or one platform. Being friends with employees with no line. Assuming staff share your motivation (his best manager chose to step down for half the pay).

## Examples / case details
- Saved $50K: $10K on a mastermind, $34K to open, $6K left against $5K rent. First month's cash collected was $4,972, equal to the rent.
- First challenge: 15% hit the goal. Later about 80%, with the same workouts and meal plans. The difference was accountability, specific questions ("How many days out of 7 did you eat all three meals to plan?") and simpler color-coded plans. His physician dad: "they're just not adhering."
- Unconditional guarantee: only 2 refund requests over the years.
- Took about $500 up front plus $200–300 in supplements 48 hours later, against rivals' $21-for-21-days. Competitors needed about 3 months to collect what he got in the first transaction.
- Gym staff who used to sell tanning closed 2 of 5 ($750) while he was away at Christmas. That's when he "tasted freedom."
- Big Booty Bootcamp: 30 × $300 = $9,000 for six 90-min sessions ($1,500/session). Trainer cost about $180, so roughly 98% margin. Getting 30 new customers would have cost about $6K. Average micro gym: about $360K revenue, ~12.5% margin, owner takes home ~$30–40K. Four plays a year (~$36K) roughly doubles that.
- Paid an agency owner $750/hr weekly (about $6K total) instead of $3K/mo retainers. Alex ran the computer himself and learned national and retargeting campaigns.
- Competitor spending $15K/mo at 4:1 = $60K, netting $45K, vs Alex at $1.5K at 10:1 = $15K, netting $13.5K.
- A business owner closing 55% of *leads* (2–3/day) with one full-time person whose only job is calling every lead immediately. Most in his position close about 10%. "391% increase" when you call within 60s, which is about 4.9×.
- Chick-fil-A operators as the profit-share model.
- **Math check [inference]**: in the OTE example ($150K OTE, $75K base, $250K average location profit), Alex says "12.5%… I don't know what the math is." The correct share is 30% ($75K ÷ $250K). At 30%, a $350K location pays $75K + $105K = $180K and a $100K location pays $75K + $30K = $105K. His spoken $185K and $87K don't follow from either percentage. (12.5% gives $118.75K and $87.5K.) The method he describes is right; use 30%.
- Close rate 50% → 80% is a 60% lift. Show rate 10% → 50% is 5×. His numbers check out.
