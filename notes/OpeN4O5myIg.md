## Core thesis
Your existing email list is an underused "investment account." A 7-day challenge sent to only ~15% of an old list (mostly non-buyers) produced $2.4M in 5 days for a portfolio company. He says it added over $100M in enterprise value. Run a "quarterly cleanup": re-engage the list with a challenge or lead magnet that has a deadline and a big promise, test it on a small segment, and rename the wrapper each time.

## Frameworks & models
- **Email = 401k, ads = paycheck**: ads and outbound are daily work that pays back directly. The list compounds over time. Email CAC is $0 once you have the lead, so the gross profit drops to the bottom line.
- **Why email raises enterprise value**: email sales add straight to profit (and most businesses trade on a multiple of bottom line). The list size doesn't change overnight the way ad performance does, so email smooths out volatility.
- **Lead magnet**: "a complete solution to a very narrow problem," which ideally creates the next problem that your offer solves. Challenges fit the value equation: a clear outcome, a high likelihood of success, a fast time frame, and they feel easy.
- **Challenges "approximate brand"**: they "microwave" the prospect, cramming a 6-month relationship into 7 days. The weight-loss example: "7 lbs in 7 days," then talk about long-term goals.
- **Offers ∝ money**, except that every ask spends goodwill. Keep a healthy give:ask ratio. Email counts as content: one-to-many and free.
- **Same offer, different medium**: webinar, phone, workshop or email reduces offer fatigue. Price also varies by medium (book < course < live virtual < in person; digital vs. in-person college).
- **Re-wrapping**: the same deliverable gets a seasonal or pain-point name (gym examples: 42-day fix, slim for Santa, lean by Halloween, six-week booty blast).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Run one re-engagement promo per quarter, with different names each quarter. Provide value in between.
- Test on ~10–15% of the list for 1–3 runs before mailing the whole list. Split-test subject lines, openings, CTAs, landing pages and the challenge name.
- It's okay to ask again; people get busy.
- Local businesses have small audiences, so they fatigue faster and need more variety.
- Once you have 4 tested annual promos, reuse the calendar every year.
- Trust smart team members' ideas when the downside (brand damage) is low. If you shoot down every idea, your team stops bringing them.

## Playbook steps
1. Take a piece of your product that already works and make it a mini-outcome challenge (e.g., 7 days).
2. Send it to a test segment of the list (buyers and non-buyers).
3. Funnel: email open → click → register → challenge → buy → upsell (ascension).
4. Rerun the play on another segment, then the full list, using what you learned.
5. Repeat quarterly with a new wrapper.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Treating leads as "dead" after the initial follow-up.
- Sending an untested promo to the entire list.
- Fear of making the second ask.
- Rejecting every team idea.
