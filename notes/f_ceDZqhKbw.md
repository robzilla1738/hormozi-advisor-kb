## Core thesis
Every recurring business has a **hypothetical max**, where monthly churn equals new sales. Work it out on a napkin from new sales/month, active clients, revenue, price and cancellations. The cap shows where you'll stall and what to fix. Often cutting churn is far easier than 10×-ing acquisition. Business grows to its nearest constraint, so find the biggest one and remove it. (Captioned name "Alex Rosio"; portfolio said to be ~$85M/yr.)

## Frameworks & models
- **Six numbers**: new sales/month, current revenue, price, churn, LTV, gross margin. Most owners don't know churn or LTV.
- **Churn** = cancellations per month ÷ active clients. Example: 13/week ≈ 50/month ÷ 380 = ~13%.
- **LTV** = price ÷ churn = $1,000 ÷ 0.13 ≈ $7,700. **LTGP** = LTV × gross margin (90%) ≈ $7,000. This is the maximum you can spend to acquire a customer and stay profitable.
- **Hypothetical max** = new sales × LTV (equivalently new sales ÷ churn × price) = 100 × $7,700 ≈ **$770K/month (~$9M/yr)**. The video once misspeaks "77K"; [inference] the 770K/month figure is correct. Growth slows asymptotically as churn eats new business.
- **Churn lever**: at 3% churn, LTV ≈ $33,000 and the cap becomes ~$3.3M/month (math checks: 100 ÷ 0.03 × $1,000). Compare 10×-ing sales to 1,000/month for $7.7M/month; ask which is easier.
- **Buyer valuation variables**: growth × stickiness × profit, multiplied together.
- **PE retention benchmark**: ~80% yearly retention for enterprise value. 13% monthly churn keeps <20% of customers by year-end (0.87^12 ≈ 19%, checks out).
- **Theory of constraints**: businesses are organisms that want to grow; remove the biggest constraint.
- References his "8 C's" stickiness video (cracking Netflix's recurring model) for cutting churn.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- You should be able to calculate these numbers in your head; relying on finance reports to make directional calls makes you weak.
- If you don't know your hypothetical max, "you are fooling yourself."
- Keep acquisition cost below LTGP.
- When churn is high, fix retention before scaling acquisition.
- Do the fewest things that get the most returns.

## Playbook steps
1. Collect new sales/month, active clients, revenue and price. 2. Count monthly cancellations → churn %. 3. LTV = price ÷ churn; × margin = LTGP. 4. Cap = new sales × LTV. 5. Compare: what does halving churn do vs doubling sales? 6. Attack the biggest constraint.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Not knowing churn or LTV.
- Defaulting to "get more leads" when churn is the cap.
- Being blindsided by a plateau that the math predicted.

## Examples / case details
- A prospective portfolio company: 100 new sales/month, ~$400K/month revenue, 380 clients at $1K/month, 13% churn, >90% gross margin (<$100 to fulfill), growing fast but heading toward an asymptote.
