# I Confronted a Stranger With a Failing Business...

Signal: high

## Core thesis
Live teardown of Alexi Omar's Latin-music legal business. It made $300K revenue at −$100K profit, with about 6 months of cash left. Revenue split across three units: services $131K (≈+$10K profit), education $131K, software $40K (burning ~$150K/yr; roughly the whole loss). The fix uses no new assets. Kill the money-losing unit (software), treat education as a charity/lead nurture, point every traffic source at the one thing that already makes money (services), sell it as an outcome instead of by the hour, and raise the price from $250/hr to a $5,800 package plus 5% of deals. "You literally have everything in place. They were just organized wrong."

## Frameworks & models
- **Supply & demand + leverage** ("two most important concepts in business"): when demand outstripped his capacity for services, he launched cheaper courses and templates instead of raising prices. Low supply plus high demand means price goes up.
- **The money path**: one offer that makes money. Every eyeball source (YouTube, Instagram, Skool group) gets forced through the most efficient monetization vehicle. Old way: one traffic source split three ways into money-losing units. New way: three traffic sources pointed at one.
- **Four YouTube CTA placements**, all saying "DM me on Instagram" because that's where sales already happen ("I'm not trying to break it"): (1) a 30-second ask at the 30% mark of each video, (2) the description above the fold, (3) a pinned comment (go back and add one to your biggest videos), (4) the channel profile. Old videos act as "little mini ads that are always running" whose CTAs you can redirect.
- **Follower DM qualification**: DM every new follower with "here for free content/entertainment, or do you want help?" Anyone who says help is a lead. At 800 new followers/mo with a ~5% close at $6K, that's 40 sales ≈ $240K/mo of added potential.
- **Funnel**: DM → free booked call ("it's not a consultation, it's a sales call") → a 5–7 min VSL (process, past outcomes, reviews) → sales call qualified by **BANT** (budget, authority, need, timing) → sell the outcome. Unqualified or lying leads get triaged to a downsell.
- **Outcome pricing vs hourly**: hourly billing puts your incentives against the client's ("we're incentivized to take as long as humanly possible"). Charge for the outcome.
- **Price anchor with two options**: $5,800 + 5% of the deal, or $25,000 with no percentage.
- **Phone-call price multiplier**: "typically you can triple the price with a phone call of the same thing." So the $500 checkout course becomes a $2–3K education downsell on calls, used only to "liquidate your time."
- **Six big small-business mistakes** (scored on Alexi): too many avatars; focus (more than one business; overexpansion); overpaying staff (compensation); underpriced/mispriced; single product (no front end/back end).
- **Gross margin waterfall**: start from $100 revenue. 80% gross margin leaves $80. Marketing, sales and admin may eat ~60, leaving ~20% net. Aim to run those at 30 to get to 50%. That's why 80% GM "isn't greedy."
- **Opportunity cost = hard + soft**: hard is lost sales; soft is lost focus, distraction and hassle.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If a unit loses money and there's "not a specific reason we can't shut it down," shut it down. Treat unprofitable "impact" products as charity, or even run them like a nonprofit.
- Service gross margin ≥ 80% minimum. Delivery cost ≤ ~$1,100–1,200 on a $5,800 sale. Price at least ~5× labor cost. Example: at $100/hr lawyer cost, delivery must take under 11 hours.
- Qualify on income/ability to pay, not on vanity signals like Spotify listeners. "Our job is to enable the opportunity," not to pick who will make it.
- Unfinished software with B2B interest: rather than funding the build, offer big companies the whole thing (e.g., $100–200K) instead of $25K/yr subscriptions. The alternative is burning the other units and going all in, which he calls "more fantasy than reality."
- Don't take on demand-generating activities (speaking, stages) when you're not demand constrained. If you do speak, price it at your daily sales value: 3 sales/day × $6K × 2 days = $36K + travel.
- Sequence: step 1, fix monetization and pricing; step 2, route new followers into DMs; step 3 later, use the book as a lead magnet (for now, give it to unqualified leads as an elegant "not yet").
- Expect the fix to "break the business" on delivery. Use the new cash to hire more lawyers. The next constraint is supply.

## Playbook steps
1. Get the P&L for each unit (revenue and profit). 2. Kill or freeze the units that lose money. 3. Find where sales actually happen (here, Instagram DMs, which they never asked for) and redirect all CTAs there. 4. Book free sales calls, with a VSL beforehand to set expectations. 5. Qualify with BANT. 6. Sell an outcome package at a 5–20× higher price with a percentage-or-flat anchor. 7. Downsell education to unqualified callers. 8. DM every new follower. 9. Move the community to a platform with discovery (Skool) and offer each member a free onboarding/setting call. 10. Hire sales capacity (everyone gets their own calendar link), then delivery capacity.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Answering excess demand with a cheaper product instead of a higher price.
- Spreading attention across more units while losing money.
- Charging for consultations up front at $250/hr.
- Sending traffic to a website that doesn't convert when the sales come through DMs.
- Assuming 80% gross margin is greedy.
- Speaking and stage tours that take focus away when demand isn't the constraint.

## Examples / case details
- Funnel numbers: ~7,000 site visitors → 150 opt-ins for a free course (2%) → ~9 buyers of a $500 lifetime course + $500 lifetime upsell. 800+ students, all on lifetime access.
- Services: 524 hours sold at $250 ($131K). About 9–10 consults/month; ~3 of 10 buy one-time deliverables (trademarks) and ~3 buy ongoing (~$1,000/mo). Services are already ~$150K this year. Commissions run 5% on deals over $500K; the last two deals averaged ~$40K+ in fees.
- Software: $1,000/yr templates with churn (people download a contract and leave). Burning $7K/mo after layoffs.
- Price negotiation: Alex wants $5K and offers to start at $3K. Alexi already sells 3 months for $4K. They land on $5,800.
- Assets: 90K YouTube subscribers, 23K Instagram followers, a bestselling Spanish-language book in the niche, 3 people who can take sales calls. Sales capacity could reach $18–50K/day.
- Note: Alex first agrees the constraint is "demand" (misdirected, mispriced). He then argues they're really supply-constrained and shouldn't add demand [the transcript says both; the final position is supply].
