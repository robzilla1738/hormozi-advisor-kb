# Why old friends keep you poor...

## Core thesis
Alex lives in 3–5 year "seasons" and sheds friends who no longer add value or who reinforce his old identity. Relationships are exchanges, and you love someone in proportion to what you'll give up to keep them. Winners and losers want the same goals; what makes winners unique is that they're willing to pay the price. That includes shrinking your circle, then rebuilding it through communities of people with the same goals.

## Frameworks & models
- **Life in 3–5 year seasons**: the friend group changes with each one.
- **Same goals, different willingness to pay the price.** "You don't buy everything you see in the mall."
- **Love = what you'll sacrifice to maintain it.**
- **Relationships as reinforcement or punishment**: a relationship's strength is how long you'll wait for the next positive reinforcement.
- **Identity labels**: old friends "beat that label into you." Answer "You've changed" with "Yeah, you haven't."
- **Lunch tables on steroids**: join a community, put in volume over time, rise in status, sit with the top, then leapfrog to the next community.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Rank friends by how much you'd sacrifice for each, not just by whether you love them.
- To phase someone out, slow the cadence (weekly → biweekly → monthly) and accept fewer invitations. If they're close enough that it would be obvious, have the honest conversation.
- Honesty test: say to someone's face what you'd say behind their back.
- "You can't expect to do the same thing everyone else is doing and somehow achieve a different outcome."

## Playbook steps
1. Audit relationships for mutual value and for whether they reinforce who you want to be. 2. Taper or confront. 3. Fill the vacuum by joining goal-aligned communities. 4. Rise through volume and time. 5. Leapfrog communities.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Keeping relationships out of a societal "bad friend" story.
- Staying around people who retell stories of who you used to be.
- Scattering limited time, focus and energy.
