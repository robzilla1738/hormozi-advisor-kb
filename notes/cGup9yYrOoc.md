# How I CRUSH copycats, thieves and competition.

Signal: medium

## Core thesis
Copycats are inevitable and a sign you have something desirable. Responding to them, especially by changing your business, only hurts you. Never acknowledge them. Get louder about your own mission and pour the anger into customer value. "Beat them in the field."

## Frameworks & models
- **Predict behavior by self-interest** (from an early mentor): assume people act in their own best interest, and align their interest with yours to harness it.
- **Mental reframes:** (1) they need me, I don't need them. A copy is a subset of the source and can't outgrow it. (2) Copiers admit they're followers. (3) They operate from scarcity and focus on you, not their customers ("where attention goes, money flows").
- **Never acknowledge** ("Coke never acknowledges Pepsi"). Naming them elevates them to your level.
- **Whoever gives the most to customers wins.** HubSpot founder (quoted): you don't have a customer until someone offered them a cheaper version and they declined.
- **80-year-old test:** act only in line with the values you'd be proud of at 80–90, never from malice.

## Decision rules
- If they get loud → you get louder about yourself, your solutions, your mission.
- Don't restructure the business in response to a defector. He did once and only hurt his own business, while most customers never knew anything was happening.

## Examples
- 22 clients or employees in 5 years tried to repurpose his licensing material, often bad-mouthing him and soliciting clients and staff. "22 for 22." They fade away and blame the industry.
- Quotes Yellowstone: anything worth having, people will try to take.

## Notable lines
- "You'll become astonishingly good at predicting the behavior of others if you always assume they will act in their own best interest." (mentor)
- "If people are talking smack in the stands, beat them in the field."
