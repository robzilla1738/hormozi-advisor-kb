## Core thesis
His fastest growth (Used Alan ~$1.2M/mo, Prestige Labs ~$1.5M/mo, Gym Launch >$2M/mo by end of year one) came from a set of growth levers: get out of obscurity with volume, out-shout competitors instead of fighting them, write clear copy, lead with proof, focus on hooks, do more of what already works, protect word of mouth, reuse your own winners (70/20/10), invest in "give" content for high-information buyers (70/30), state facts and claim what's unique, target the right list, and track leading indicators the way masters do.

## Frameworks & models
- **Obscurity is problem #1**: under $1M revenue, basically nobody knows you exist. Spend the first 4 hours of every day on the core four (warm outreach, cold outreach, content, paid ads). Pick one and go all in.
- **BOOM (business order of magnitude)**: advertising can 100× reach. Optimizations (close rate, opt-in %) are capped at 100%. Avoid the "optimization mousetrap."
- **The market is bigger than you think**: a gym needs ~200 members out of a 1M-person city, so new competitors barely dent it.
- **Shrink the competition**: either grow so big no one hears them (Eminem's "Who?") or kill them with kindness. Claim your own flaws first (8 Mile). Answer hate with "You're right…" plus a compliment.
- **Repetition is fine**: "People need to be more reminded than they need to be taught." Before his book launch, 19 of 20 people who stopped him in Vegas didn't know about it, even with an audience of ~7M.
- **Clear, not clever**: his best ads used low grade-level language. Target below 5th grade, 3rd if you're great. Match analogies to the audience (cars for mechanics, houses for realtors, food/sleep/driving for broad audiences). Complexity hurts speed and ease in the value equation.
- **Proof > promise**: 11,382 reviews at 4.7 beats one 5-star review with a bigger guarantee. "Show, don't sell." Do epic things first, then talk about them.
- **Hook > everything**: the proof only gets seen if the hook works. His hook formula is proof, promise, plan. Look at both the visual and the verbal hook.
- **More / Better / New**: more is usually the highest risk-adjusted return. Change guarantees the cost of change but not the benefit.
- **Volatility = low volume**: take your yearly activity and do it every day ("flip the calendar vertically"). People who are crushing it often do ~1000× more.
- **Negative word of mouth**: Disney study cited as 37 tragic moments to undo one magic moment; good experiences are told to ~5 people, bad ones to everyone. If CPM is flat but CPL rises 2–4×, suspect negative word of mouth.
- **Steal from yourself (70/20/10)**: 70% carbon copies of what works, 20% adjacent variations (reworded hook, same hook with a new background or shirt), 10% brand new. Most founders have this flipped. Building vs demolition analogy: only one arrangement works.
- **Buyer information continuum**: he replaces "emotional vs logical" with low-info vs high-info buyers. High-info buyers are orders of magnitude more numerous. Schwartz's 5 levels of awareness: unaware (broad curiosity), problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware (just make offers).
- **70/30 give:ask**: New Balance CMO Chris Davis flipped the budget from 30/70 to 70% brand/endorsements and 30% "buy shoes," and it took 18 months to pay off. ~3.5:1 give-to-ask is cited (TV ad minutes, 1 ad per ~3 feed posts). Corporate execs who flip the ratio "suck the well dry."
- **B2B "give" ladder**: your own aspirational outcomes (2 degrees of separation) → people like the avatar you helped (1 degree) → helping the prospect directly with free content and products (0 degrees, strongest, slowest).
- **Marketing laws**: state the facts and tell the truth (change reality instead of exaggerating). Show only what you can show; say what only you can say. Get narrower to be unique and be "the best in a puddle."
- **The list / targeting**: winter coats advertised to South Florida fail no matter how good the offer. Put someone who looks like the avatar in the ad.
- **Masters have more ways to win**: more and better metrics, leading indicators over lagging ones, and "directional correctness rather than binary."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Under $1M: the first 4 hours a day go to one core-four channel.
- Before a new space or product, get beta users free in exchange for testimonials, reviews, feedback and referrals. Keep early negative word of mouth "concentrated and quiet," and don't take money from people likely to be unhappy.
- Test at real volume. His mentor tested flyers at 5,000, then ran 5,000/day for 30 days (150K), versus Alex's 300.
- Copy readability: below 5th grade, ideally 3rd.
- Effort: 70% proven, 20% adjacent, 10% new. Budget and impressions: ~70% give, 30% ask. Expect brand returns in ~18 months.
- If nothing comes back at all, suspect targeting first.
- Hiring: judge masters by the quality and quantity of metrics they use (time to fill, cost to acquire talent, two-sided 10/10 fit at day 90).

## Playbook steps
1. Choose one core-four channel and give it 4 hours a day.
2. Collect proof through free beta users and fix the product using their feedback.
3. Rewrite copy to a 3rd–5th grade level.
4. Find hooks that work (proof, promise, plan) and cut any dead lead-in.
5. Scale the winners: more reach-outs, budget and posts.
6. Run 70/20/10 on creative.
7. Shift toward a 70/30 give:ask ratio.
8. Narrow your messaging to what's unique and true.
9. Check targeting and avatar match.
10. Track leading-indicator milestones.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Shiny object syndrome ("a cancer"), and dropping hooks because you are bored with them.
- Tests that are too small, then concluding "it doesn't work."
- Attacking competitors or arguing with critics.
- Big words that "feed my ego, not my bank account."
- Exaggerated stories about ordinary things.
- Pure direct response that caps growth; pulling demand forward for quarterly numbers.
- Binary lagging metrics only ("revenue's down").
- Believing you have no negative word of mouth: "half of you are below average."

## Examples / case details
- 3-second trim: cutting the first 3 seconds before the real hook took a video from ~40K to ~780K views (~19×).
- Changing a speaker's 1–2 minute intro changed their NPS for the same talk.
- Rewriting email follow-ups to a lower grade level gave +50% conversion.
- Sales math: raising one closer from 30% to 40% vs hiring 3 more closers (4×, a 300% gain). He calls 30→40% a 25% lift; it is actually ~33% [inference: arithmetic slip in the video; the point stands].
- Portfolio company: a new ad director's fresh ads failed; reusing the 3-year-old hook worked. Nike kept "Just Do It."
- Martial arts "one of six double black belts in the nation, none local"; Mad Men "It's toasted."
- Rockefeller overpaid for Cincinnati's biggest refinery to claim "biggest," then did 20+ deals in 30 days.
- Gym Launch thank-you page: Leila's photo got far higher response and show-up than Alex's. His audience is ~89% male.
- Outbound: after ~4 months with 1 sale, execs staged an "intervention." He worked through lists → enrichment → local numbers → script hook → offer → second-call shows. "Heart surgery looks like murder" to outsiders.
- The channel worked early because he had just sold a company for just under $50M (proof).
