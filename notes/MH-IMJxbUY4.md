# Building a $12,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 25 Minutes

## Core thesis
Live teardown of Joel McDonald's "Just Get Out of Town" (travel-hedging course/coaching; ~$6.4M TTM, ~30% margin, ROAS 4.5:1, LTV:CAC a "skinny" 1.4:1, ~85% of customers from a Meta book funnel, spend capped ~$100K/mo). First ask: supply- or demand-constrained? It's demand-constrained, so the options are spend more, new channel, or lower CAC (CRO, better creative, offer). Spending more hit a wall and a new channel is "a little too hard for right now"; the offer already converts. So attack two angles: the ad side (better creative via a UGC loop + kaleidoscoping winners) and the conversion side (lead scoring + parallel dialer + more reps + damaging admissions). Both lower CAC and break through to a bigger audience pool. Guest Joel speaks for much of the setup.

## Frameworks & models
- **Supply vs demand constraint decision tree**: demand-constrained → new channel / lower CAC (CRO, creative, offer) / spend more.
- **Creative cap**: spend usually caps because the creative isn't good enough. Meta serves the most interested people first, then CPA rises as you push into the bigger, less interested pool. Old Spice "guy on the horse": if everyone could buy the product, there's one ad good enough to convert everyone.
- **UGC loop / "decentralized content machine"**: incentivize customers to post trip montage reels (<60s) in exchange for a held-back checklist or extra training plus permission to use them. Aim for 20–30 community videos a week, blast them all, find winners, then "juice them."
- **Kaleidoscope**: take a winning creative and make 20–30 variations with AI (black & white, sepia, a 3-second video from a still, cartoon/Ghibli styles, re-films).
- **Copy vs creative**: winning copy rarely needs to change; creative changes all the time.
- **Video vs static**: "good video beats images, but images beat bad videos." Same for content: good content works, bad doesn't.
- **Organic → ad double dip**: post shorts organically, and when one wins, add a 5-second CTA and run it as an ad.
- **Avatar likeness** [his own "bet with no informed data"]: the algorithm shows ads to people who resemble the person in them, so a diversity of avatars in the ads opens more audience "circles" even at the current creative quality. AI avatars are "just about good enough" for TikTok-style 15–30s testimonial-style videos that end at the book funnel.
- **Damaging admissions front-loaded**: "before I tell you about the good, let me tell you about the bad." Removing the "too good to be true" feeling makes the good believable and lets prospects self-qualify ("you can have what you want, just not when you want").

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If the offer already converts, don't mess with it; work on creative and conversion.
- When you find winning copy, keep running it (Skool Games: one copy for 6 months, a second for 3; another company spending a couple hundred thousand a day has run 3 hooks over 5 years).
- Lead scoring on the two qualifying factors (credit card spend ~$5K/month and travel spend ≥$5K/year): call 2-for-2 first, 1-of-2 second, zeros "if we're bored." Capture both at opt-in.
- ~300 prospects/day (≈250 book sales + ~20 paid-group joins + ~30 Facebook group members) → about 6 reps on a power/parallel dialer, KPI ~$300/day each. A parallel dialer calls 10 numbers at once per rep; at 5% pickup that maximizes talk time.
- Make the phone number required, or trade it for a free travel assessment.
- A new channel is a later move when the current channel still has lots of headroom (he thinks this broad offer could scale to $100K+/day in spend).

## Playbook steps
1. Classify the constraint (supply vs demand). 2. List the levers; eliminate those already tried (spend more) or too hard now (new channel). 3. Build the UGC loop with an incentive for community reels. 4. Hire a platform-native Gen Z short-form person to model top viral travel pages (e.g., "7 hidden gems… for under $1,000," pinned comment). 5. Run organic posts, and turn the winners into ads with a 5s CTA. 6. Kaleidoscope the winners with AI; keep the copy. 7. Add avatar diversity / AI avatars. 8. Sales side: lead score at opt-in, parallel dialer, scale the team, front-load damaging admissions.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Concluding "video/content doesn't work" when it's just bad video or content.
- Changing winning copy too often.
- Polished, professional-looking ads on short-form instead of iPhone selfie style with trending audio.
- Reps wasting time waiting for connections, or dialing unscored leads.
- Jumping to new channels before maxing the current one.

## Examples / case details
- Metrics above; a book funnel loss leader with a ~6-month breakeven even including the back end; spending $150K in a month yielded only 10% more book sales.
- Inbound/outbound sales about 60/40, with outbound started a month earlier.
- Joel's example itinerary: ~$70K of first-class flights (or ~7M points) done for ~1M points / ~$1,800 out of pocket.
- Joel noted retirees/business owners convert. Alex's guess is that it's partly because Joel himself, the face of the ads, fits that profile.
