## Core thesis
Hard decisions are hard because you've already made all the easy ones. What's left is obvious-but-risky trade-offs. Speed of decision-making comes from clarity, so **spell out the trade** (what you get now vs later, what you pay) and pick. Wanting everything gets you nothing.

## Frameworks & models
- **Spell out the trade:** option A and its cost vs option B and its cost. The cost of not choosing is staying stuck. Ask: "How can I make today feel worse so tomorrow feels better?" Most of his big leaps came from present pain, not vision.
- **Step back to step forward:** an investment means the bank account goes down before up. Muscles shrink, then supercompensate.
- **Second/third-order chain for supply constraint:** more demand than you can handle and a thin team → raise price → more cash flow → pay and advertise higher comp → hire talent → no longer supply-constrained.
- **Relative vs absolute ad return:** spend $10k → $100k ($90k profit) vs spend $1M → $2M ($1M profit). Lower ratio, far more profit. Don't cap ad spend at breakeven: "everything above that is the gravy."
- **Focus multiplier:** spreading thin gives up aggregate upside that is "almost always 10x bigger". The more capable you are, the more tempting multitasking is.
- **Avatar repositioning (case):** to move from beginner consumers to business owners, change the ad language ("tired of your job" attracts employees), change the lead magnet (a book about your business, not about quitting your job), and change the price, because acquiring a business owner can cost 5–10x more than a consumer.

## Decision rules
- "Make more money" is a bad goal because everything makes money. Define the outcome you're optimizing for.
- Want better customers → add friction and disqualify, qualifying your avatar to the same degree.
- The more irreversible decisions you make, the better you get at them.

## Examples
- Entrepreneur 18 months in: attracting beginners while 80% of revenue came from a segment he liked less.
- A woman running 5 businesses at ~$1–2M/yr who got bored easily. Trade: novelty now vs a big business later.
- Alex: ~9 things at once (launch business, 2 agencies for chiro/dental, 6 gym locations) → consolidated into one → roughly 10x income (his rough numbers).
- Gym owners capping spend once they hit breakeven member count.
