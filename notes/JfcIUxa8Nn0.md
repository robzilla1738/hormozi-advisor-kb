## Core thesis
"Don't be cute." He got the phrase from a mercenary PE CEO with four exits. Fundamentals ("put the two fat guys in the middle, run to the right") give consistent yardage, while fancy plays fumble. Newer entrepreneurs fail to repeat successful actions, both their own and those of a system or advisor they paid for. Replicate first, and change things only after you match or beat the teacher. Short vlog format, recorded in Austin.

## Frameworks & models
- **Two failures to repeat success**: (1) something works and you move on to something else instead of doing more of it; (2) you pay for a system or advice and don't follow it.
- **Replicate → match → then iterate.** The teacher has the same goal and the same constraints and has already balanced the tradeoffs.
- **Optimize conversion, then shift attention to traffic**: once the conversion numbers are optimized, stop tweaking, treat it as a successful action, and push attention to filling the top of the funnel.
- **Above the fold dominates**: the first 1–2 things someone reads (headline, first sentences, one image) swing results most.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- "Until I'm doing as well or better than the person who taught me this thing, do not change it."
- If you're well below target, ignore everything below the fold. About 90% of optimization attention belongs above the fold.
- If you use someone's templates, use them as they are.
- "To not take advice is akin to literally burning your money" (when you paid for it).

## Playbook steps
1. Buy or learn a proven system and run it exactly as outlined. 2. Track results until you match the teacher. 3. Optimize conversion on headline and above-the-fold content first, and test deleting what's below the fold. 4. Once conversion is optimized, lock it and redirect attention to traffic volume.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Ego: "we think we're special… a snowflake."
- Fancy plays instead of fundamentals.
- Spending hours and weeks optimizing below-the-fold bullets and images.
- Early wins you can't explain. A first-try home run doesn't teach you why it worked.

## Examples / case details
- An 18-year-old neighbor bought a real-estate flipping system. Advice: follow it exactly until you out-earn the person who taught it.
- Two agencies doing about $3M/yr finally did it "the way that you had outlined" and saw better results.
- In about 3 weeks, the team tested over 100 elements of the funnel.
- A headline change on the agency partner landing page for their software tripled throughput. Removing "you finished this process" from the scheduling page (step 3) 5×'d throughput. Doubling to quadrupling from top-of-page changes is routine. Deleting everything below the fold sometimes beat having content there.
