# The SEASON OF NO (What it Takes to Win)

## Core thesis
Big growth periods came from a "season of no" (3 months to 3 years) of saying no to almost everything except the one thing that matters most. Every no is a yes to the big goal. Achieving it reopens most of the doors you closed, and new doors appear.

## Frameworks & models
- **Yeses = withdrawals from your energy account; nos = deposits into your goal account.**
- **Buffett 25/5:** write 25 goals, rank them, circle the top 5, discard the rest. Put full effort on #1 and define the *minimum* needed not to slide backward on the others ("like bodybuilding, one weakness at a time").
- **Five categories to say no to:** (1) social life and alcohol, (2) opportunistic side deals (e.g. equity-for-services from agency clients), (3) work below your pay grade, (4) "family obligations", which are really family *trade-offs*, (5) business contacts' "got 15 minutes?" calls.
- **Context-switching cost:** cites research that switching between two tasks takes ~4x as long. Count the time thinking before and after a call too.
- **Sacrifice list:** write what you're willing to give up, not just a to-do list.
- **Change the environment, not yourself** (Seinfeld: during the writing block he may do nothing else but write).

## Decision rules
- Most plans taken to their natural end are good (a dry cleaner run for 40 years without distraction ends up with multiple locations, maybe national).
- If a task eats 10–15 hrs/week (≈2 full days) and someone else could do it, hand it off even if it isn't a full 40-hr role.
- Side deals bolted onto the core business signal "an undisciplined entrepreneur" to investors.
- You'll fail to reach a goal if you do the thing too few times, for too short, or the wrong thing.

## Examples
- 3-year season: quit alcohol, no social events, skipped every event of a ~$35k/yr paid mastermind for 2 years. Gym Launch (~$50M exit) was built in that season.
- Going from 6 to 10 gyms at 80–100 hrs/week, gave up football and Netflix, then sold the locations and moved to licensing on a mentor's advice.
- Calls the supplement company (Prestige Labs) one of his bigger mistakes, a distraction from the main thing.
- Portfolio CEO doing a 10–15 hr/week role himself to protect cash flow.

## Notable lines
