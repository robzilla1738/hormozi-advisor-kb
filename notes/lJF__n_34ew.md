## Core thesis
Day-in-the-life vlog. The substantive thread is Acquisition.com's operating philosophy, "praise over punishment": reinforcement unlocks discretionary effort, while punishment-based cultures get only the minimum needed not to be fired. Also covers his deal pipeline and his "builder" investing style.

## Frameworks & models
- **Discretionary effort:** effort required not to be fired + discretionary effort above it. Punishment cultures → law of least effort. Firms like McKinsey/Goldman raise the bar so only anxiety-driven high performers survive (and burn out). Reinforcement cultures capture the discretionary effort.
- **Reinforcement timing:** reward crying with a cookie and you teach crying. Punish a sneaking-out teen when they walk in the door and you punish coming home. Reinforcement that comes too late teaches the wrong thing.
- **Progressive reinforcement** (Dr. Trevor Cashey): a Skittle per 3 words read, 2 Skittles for beating the previous record. By the end of the first session the kid was reading entire pages.
- **Branding = conditioned association:** value from the book → rewards → associated with Alex → associated with the logo. A teammate wearing an Acquisition.com shirt got photos, drinks and access.
- **Bettors vs builders:** bettors buy mispriced companies (Buffett style). Builders deploy templatized playbooks/SOPs to 3–10x companies. Acquisition.com leans builder. ~95% of deals reaching pipeline review are declined.
- **Internal vs external vision:** team, vendors and customers vs the greater community (mission: make real business education available to everybody).
- **Audience trains the creator:** clicks and watches decide content. Higher production isn't "selling out" if it reaches more people without diluting the message.
