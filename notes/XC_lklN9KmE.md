## Core thesis
Most service businesses' goals (e.g., a 300–500 member gym) are capped by inflow, not churn. Service is "easy in, easy out," so expect churn and use the service advantage: fast scaling of inflow. Work the funnel math backward from the goal, from churn to sales to conversations to appointments to leads to ad spend, and then block calendar time and staff to match. "Either switch your goal or switch your head." This is a Gym Launch / GLX-era video (gym examples, but he says the metrics apply to any service or B2B business).

## Frameworks & models
- **Inflow cap**: at a steady state, members = new adds ÷ churn rate. Accept "acceptable metrics" (service churn rarely gets below 1–2%/month). Under ~1–2% usually means pure organic referral growth, and getting massive needs organic, outbound and paid.
- **Two sales in service**: the "try it" sale (front-end/trial) and the "commit" sale (a longer-term membership). Even people signed straight onto recurring billing (EFT) must be resold to stick.
- **Backward math (his worked example)**: goal of 300 members at 10% monthly churn means 30 new members/month just to stay flat. Converting 50% of front-end buyers means 60 front-end sales/month (15/week). Closing 1 in 2 conversations means 120 conversations/month (~30/week). A ~50% show rate means ~60 booked appointments/week. Leads needed ≈ 15/day; at $10/lead that's $150/day ≈ $4,500/month in ad spend. Double the volume to actually grow beyond flat. (Math checks.)
- **Alternative check**: losing 20/month at a 50% trial conversion needs 40 trials/month; at the ~35% industry average it needs ~60.
- **Time math → roles**: 30 consults/week × 30 min = 15 hrs, or ~3 hrs/day for one person. Add conversion consults (~3 hrs/day) and 2 hrs/day of retention outreach to existing customers, and that's one role. Plus a full-time salesperson meeting ~10/day (5 hrs).
- **Inflow never stops**: don't run ads for two weeks and then pause to service those people. Sign up and fulfill at the same time, with calendar blocks for outbound/retention reach-outs, onboarding, orientations, sales, and conversion meetings.
- **"She wished for a weapon but not the skill to wield it"** (the Burning Wish card): owners want growth but aren't operationally ready for it.
- **Champions have short memories**: if you must meet 200 people at a 50% close rate, 100 will say no. Prospects' excuses and limiting beliefs are why they need you, so it's not about you.
- **Priorities**: there are no truly conflicting priorities, only an order. Schedule them.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you can't sign ~20 new clients per week, you won't reach a 200–500 member facility.
- At 10 trials/week max, reaching 300 members requires ~75% conversion, which most don't achieve.
- Selling only 2 days a week caps volume; spread consults across 5–6 days.
- Don't wait for conversion or retention improvements before scaling inflow; orders-of-magnitude improvements won't come.
- If needed, the owner works double time to afford the hire who unblocks the bottleneck.

## Playbook steps
1. Set the target size and know your churn.
2. Compute the required monthly adds, then front-end sales, conversations, appointments, leads and ad spend.
3. Convert the meeting counts into hours and staff roles.
4. Block calendars daily (retention outreach, onboarding, sales, conversion).
5. Run inflow continuously.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Goals disconnected from the required activity.
- Pausing the service because there are "too many leads" (10 GLX gyms paused in one week for this).
- Getting emotionally derailed by "I have to talk to my husband" objections.
