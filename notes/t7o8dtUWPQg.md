## Core thesis
He lists seven disciplines everyone, especially entrepreneurs, must learn. School teaches only the first, and knowing the full list exposes your gaps. Philosophy is listed last but matters most: improving everything else without knowing why doesn't make you feel better. His own meaning is learning, and he learns best by teaching (why he makes free content).

## Frameworks & models
- **Seven disciplines (pyramid)**:
  1. **Foundational**: reading, writing, math. The gateway to the rest; school does it decently.
  2. **Physical**: eating, training, sleep. Don't be superstitious about routines. "Everybody has every wish except when sick, then only one."
  3. **Professional**: career skills. Most are learned by doing; "the real school for entrepreneurship is getting into the game."
  4. **Emotional**: understanding your own thoughts and feelings; naming deficiencies and being OK while having them.
  5. **Relational**: communicating, influencing, being likable and trustworthy (character over hacks).
  6. **Financial**: first personal finance (spend far less than you make; "wealth is a ratio, not a number" between earning and spending), then multiplying money (his focus at the time).
  7. **Philosophical**: the values you live by. He calls it the most important.
- **Language reveals growth potential**: "I am / I'm not good with money / I don't play well with others" hardens into reality. Reframe as "I have a deficiency" or "I'm not good at this yet, and that's OK."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Audit your time spent learning each discipline. Can you read a balance sheet and cash flow statement?
- Increase wealth by cutting expenses as well as raising income.
- In hiring and evaluation, listen to people's language patterns to predict how likely they are to grow. [inference: he applies this to employees and partner businesses]

## Playbook steps
1. Rate yourself on all seven. 2. Pick your deficiencies. 3. Fix your self-talk ("yet, and that's OK"). 4. Learn steadily over your life. 5. Anchor on your philosophy and "why."

## Mistakes he warns against
- Assuming business success will fill the "hole in your soul."
- Absolutist self-labels.
