## Core thesis
A rapid list of money rules for both boom and bust. The themes: leverage comes from not needing the deal, reputation and trust compound, focus on one game, protect principal and peace of mind (an emergency "FU fund"), slow down on FOMO, and measure savings and cash flow rather than income. Intro claims: he sold two companies last year for $46.2M, and the Acquisition.com portfolio does $200M+/yr.

## Frameworks & models
- **Power and leverage**: (1) he who gives the money has the power (banks). (14) Money flows to whoever needs it least. (22) Leverage comes from needing nothing: monks get there by renouncing, the rich by satisfying their needs.
- **Compounding**: (2) never trade reputation for money. (3) "Money loves speed, wealth loves time, poverty loves indecision." (27) Trust beats a bigger return because it speeds up deals. (28) Money isn't zero-sum, so get on the same side of the table. (38) Buy forever (Munger: "the money is made in the wait").
- **Risk**: (5) Fortunes are made with a lot of risk on a little money and kept with a little risk on a lot of money. (25) Don't bet the empire for a pot of gold; go all in on attention, not money. (26) Do a small starter deal with new partners. (35) Use house money: recoup principal, then get aggressive. (36) Know exactly how you'd get your money back, step by step. (40) If you can't afford to lose it, don't use it. (41) Peace of mind can be bought and sold. (42) Diversify vertically by capital stack position, e.g. a bank as preferred creditor gets paid first.
- **Focus**: (6) money flows where attention goes, the magnifying-glass idea. (32) Diversification is a hedge against ignorance (Buffett); play one game to its conclusion.
- **Decisions**: (9) It's easier to buy than to sell, so go slow on the buy. (16) "Amazing opportunity, but not our amazing opportunity." (21, 39) Mistakes love a rushed decision: "FOMO means slow go." FOMO only exists because your time horizon is too short. (23) Markets take longer to adjust than you expect, then move faster than you can imagine (The Big Short).
- **Earnings formula** (20): pay = value created × ability to negotiate ÷ how hard you are to replace.
- **Ignorance tax** (19): not knowing how to make $1M costs you $1M minus what you make, so $950K/yr at $50K.
- **Investing standards** (30–31, 34): his billionaire friends seek 50%+ annualized, not 10–20%. Think in time-to-double or triple, not IRR. Returns are in the terms: he says he could pay a billion for your business "on my terms" and not lose money.
- **Control the money flow** (17): payment processors and banks sit upstream and hold the power.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- (13) Think once before investing and twice before spending. Investing raises earning capacity or yields a return; spending is consumed.
- (18) Build an "FU fund" (the caption garbles it as "no fund"/"oh fund" [inference]) of 3–6 months to start, then grow it so you can play offense.
- (29) Never take the standard deal. Ask about each concerning term and be willing to walk; counterparts often come back 3–4 days later.
- (37) Track monthly savings, not monthly income.
- (7) Home money rules match business money rules (no personal debt, no business debt). Don't live lavishly while reinvesting.
- (8) Ignore money advice from people poorer than you want to be.

## Playbook steps
1. Build the FU fund. 2. Pick one game. 3. Measure savings and personal records ("bank account PRs"). 4. Do starter deals with new partners and keep an exit path. 5. Negotiate terms and walk if needed. 6. Recoup principal and play with house money. 7. Pause whenever you feel FOMO.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Scarcity- and fear-driven decisions. Belief #4: "we can always make more money than we need."
- Keeping the all-in risk behavior once you have something to lose.
- Tying self-worth to net worth (15). His family lost everything in the Iranian Revolution: "we make our money, our money doesn't make us."
- Constantly trying to one-up partners, which kills compounding relationships.
- Spreading across stocks, real estate, crypto and side hustles.
