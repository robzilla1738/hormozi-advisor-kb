## Core thesis
This is an interview-style clip (the interviewer's questions are implied). Alex steers by what is observable: stimulus, outcome and a feedback loop. He does not steer by feelings or by imagined mental states. Without a "capital-M meaning," he says, you build your own small meanings, treat beliefs as revisable assumptions, and drop shoulds and musts. He accepts emotions as weather rather than judging them, and for him that is what makes unconventional action possible.

## Frameworks & models
- **Observable > internal states**: if a stimulus reliably produces an outcome, you can adjust the variables. He calls marketers' invented awareness-stage funnels "fictitious steps" with no evidence behind them [his view; he doesn't name a specific model].
- **Capital-M vs little-m meaning**: religion is "meaning off the shelf." Creating your own is harder but more rewarding, and it can change by season.
- **Beliefs = assumptions**: calling them assumptions separates them from your ego, so you update when the evidence changes.
- **Remove "should / must / need / have to"**: it frees you to act in ways others would judge.
- **Emotions as weather**: labeling feelings good or bad causes more suffering than the feelings do. His mental refrain: "and that's okay. Period."
- **Measure externally**: for example, define love by what you'll endure to keep it, which he frames as an external consequence such as staying married.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Feelings matter little for accomplishment or fitness. Act on observable feedback loops instead.
- When new data materially changes the expected outcome, change your belief.

## Playbook steps
[inference] Define the observable outcome → run the action → read the feedback → adjust the variables. Ignore the mood about the work.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Spending effort diagnosing how you feel so you can judge yourself.
- Tying identity to beliefs.
- Making yourself miserable because you think you "shouldn't" feel what you feel.
