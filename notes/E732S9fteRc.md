## Core thesis
He lists five lenses billionaires use that millionaires don't: (1) size of the opportunity, (2) product over promotion, (3) an army of smarter people over a "genius with a thousand slaves," (4) long time horizons, and (5) brand as reputation. The master lens is the time horizon, because the other four follow from playing the game for a very long time. (Context: the business was ~$85M/yr at the time.)

## Frameworks & models
- **Size of opportunity / TAM**: niching down to start is fine, but there has to be a path to something enormous.
- **Product > promotion**: the billionaire question is "will people never stop buying this?" Promotion matters more for one-time big-ticket buys like solar. Most businesses are recurring, so the product has to keep people buying to earn outsized returns on marketing.
- **Genius with a thousand slaves vs. army of smarter people**: Schwarzman's second criterion is "can I find 10/10 people who want to pursue this?" Big talent only chases big opportunity.
- **Talent scale**: 10 = best in the world; 9 = best you can attract given your company; 8 = very good, takes initiative, outsized returns; 7 = functional but needs direction; 6 and below = don't want them. Most businesses are run by 6s and below because the opportunity is too small.
- **"Play games where if you wait you win"**: success is an inevitable result of time.
- **Brand = reputation**: "you only get one name." Integrous long-term decisions compound, and people at the top seek others with similar character.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Ask whether time helps or hinders you in this opportunity, and prefer the ones it helps.
- "I would rather get rich for sure and get rich slowly than never get rich at all."
- Small and big goals are equally hard (Schwarzman), so pick the big one.
- Judge decisions by asking whether you'd be proud of them looking back 80 years from now.

## Playbook steps
- [inference] Choose a large-TAM market, even if you enter through a niche → make a product people never stop buying → set a big vision that attracts 8–10/10 talent → plan on a decade-plus horizon → protect your reputation by never trading integrity for short-term dollars.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Chasing "small fries" with no growth potential.
- Doubling down on promotion because it made the first money.
- Doing it yourself or underpaying a few people.
- Thinking only about the next 90 days, campaign or payroll.
- Shady short-term moves that leave a "so-so" brand.

## Examples / case details

- He cites Grant Cardone: "don't listen to millionaires, listen to billionaires."
