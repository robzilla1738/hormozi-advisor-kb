# The Biggest Mistake Everyone is Making

## Core thesis
The #1 mistake: chasing "easy money on the table" (new product lines, side offers, second businesses) when the core business gets hard. Strategy is knowing what you say no to. Cut to the 20% that makes 80% of revenue; "simple scales, fancy fails." Only add new businesses once you've transitioned from CEO to owner, and then make the portfolio your "one thing."

## Frameworks & models
- **Woman in the red dress** (The Matrix): attractive distractions/quick wins that are really "agents" pulling you from the long-term game. The higher you climb, the sexier she looks (a $1K/mo offer is easy to refuse; $50–100K/mo is tempting).
- **Ladder of beliefs / character traits / skills**: you climb until a limit in one of the three; instead of building the next rung, people jump to a neighboring ladder with rungs already built, but it still tops out below the first ladder's limit.
- **Pareto 80/20**: across multiple businesses/product lines, ~80% of revenue comes from ~20%.
- **CEO vs owner test**: you're an owner when the business grows while you spend <5 hrs/week on it for 6 straight months and never have to jump back in. Being out of fulfillment/day-to-day but still driving it = still CEO (still a job).
- **Owner language**: you don't "start a business," you "assemble a team that pursues the opportunity on your behalf" or acquire a stake and help that team.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Default when entering a business: get the entrepreneur and business to do *fewer* things.
- Turn down adjacent demand (e.g. a $4–5M digital marketing owner being asked for general business coaching: say no).
- Don't start a second business until you pass the owner test; do things in sequence.
- If you're not at owner stage, be fine running your own business; don't copy what Alex does now.

## Playbook steps
1. List all businesses/product lines and their revenue. 2. Identify the 20% driving 80%. 3. Cut everything else and remove the operational drag. 4. Repeat successful actions with "aggressive patience"; let compounding work. 5. Install a CEO; pass the <5 hrs/wk × 6 months test. 6. Only then treat the portfolio as the one thing and assemble teams for new opportunities.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Taking quick wins for the dopamine hit when growth gets hard.
- Fancy, complex businesses with multiple teams/lines.
- Starting a second business too early (his own case below).
- Filling personal deficiencies ("don't want to be alone with our thoughts") with busy activity that messes up a running business.

## Examples / case details
- Entrepreneur with 3 businesses × 3 product lines: confirmed one main thing drives revenue; did the rest because "it seemed like a good idea at the time."
- Same type of owner said ~80% of his time went to the other things' operational drag.
- Alex gets ~2 DMs/day offering $10K for an hour; he declines.
- Started Prestige Labs too early; says it basically flatlined growth for ~2 years.
- Buffett/Munger: spend time making good decisions, not working much.
