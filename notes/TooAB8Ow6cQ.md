## Core thesis
Growth comes from the four levers: traffic, conversion, price and churn. Everything you do should feed one of them. Pick the biggest constraint, then go "more → better → new". Price on earnings per click (conversion × LTV), not headline price. Separate one-time value (charge upfront) from ongoing value (charge monthly). Communities retain through member-to-member ties and less content, not more. Hire for vibe and pattern recognition ("what good looks like"), and say no to the wrong avatar and the wrong money.

## Frameworks & models
- **Four levers**: traffic, conversion, price, churn. LTV = price / churn. Optimize conversion × LTV = **EPC (earnings per click)**. "If it doesn't ladder up to one of these four, why are you doing it?" (Alex)
- **Worked pricing example** (Alex, live): $77/mo at ~33% churn → LTV ≈ $231; $137/mo at 50% churn → LTV ≈ $274. Ratio 274/231 ≈ 1.19 (a caption/room voice says "1.86", which is wrong). Alex's conclusion: $137 wins unless $77 converts ~20% more. [Checked: the comparison is on LTV, not price, so ~19–20% is correct.]
- **More → Better → New** (Alex): first argue why you can't just do more of what works; then make it better; only then try something new. "Scale until something breaks, fix it, scale again."
- **Big head / long tail pricing** (Alex): charge one-time value (education, checklists, blueprints) upfront and ongoing value (community, calls, daily drills, ad testing) monthly. If you spread one-time value over a 12-month plan, value drops to zero once they've learned it and they cancel. Example: a $30K "price" at $2,500/mo with 25% monthly churn collects ~$10K. $7K + $500/mo at 4% churn (~25 months) ≈ $7K + $12.5K = ~$19.5K LTV, and it gets 2–3× more buyers → roughly 4–6× EPC. Front-load payment plans.
- **Churn ceiling model**: adding 8/yr with no churn → 16 → 24 → 32, versus a business stuck around 20 because churn eats the adds. [inference: steady state = adds / churn rate]
- **Guru vs network community** (Sam/Alex): ties only to the leader are fragile. Members tied to each other stay when one tie breaks.
- **Culture = rules governing reward and punishment** (Alex). Categories signal what gets rewarded.
- **Maker vs manager time** (Alex): makers get ~14 blocks a week (AM/PM), managers get ~20 fifteen-minute slots a day. Taking one maker slot kills 1/14 of the week. Alex: no meetings in the first 6 hours of the day (4–10am when he had a gym), and now all meetings on Mondays.
- **Talent funnel = customer funnel** (Alex): generate applications → nurture → sale = interview → onboard → retain/ascend. "Scaling is when the inflow of talent matches the inflow of customers." Brand counts double: once for customers, again for talent.
- **Avatar selection = LTV:CAC × TAM** (Alex): pick the front end with the biggest arbitrage times market size, smash it, then cross-sell and ascend. Run one front end, not two.
- **Training needed is inversely proportional to the skill they come in with** (Alex/Sam).
- **Free → paid Skool funnel** (winner): content/outreach → free group → auto-DM → pinned start-here → classroom = Perfect Webinar in ~5 short modules (intro, 3 stories, offer) → book call → close.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Solo operator: start with traffic (the lifeblood). Once it's consistent, find what members actually want and delete the rest. (Alex)
- Low churn + good LTV:CAC → just spend more on ads and make them better.
- Skool data (Sam's team): free groups ~80% about-page conversion; $10–30 ~5%; drops sharply above $100. Churn at low ticket: 5–10%/mo good, ~20% average, 50% too high.
- High-ticket price changes move conversion less than low-ticket ones. Portfolio test at $50K/35K/25K/15K gave the same revenue → Alex prefers more customers at the lower price (word of mouth, value gap) if margins hold.
- ProfitWell data (cited): firms that review price more often (quarterly > annual > never) are more profitable.
- If restarting Gym Launch: ~$10K/yr and focus on 80–90% renewal. #1 investor metric = how many of last year's customers still pay you.
- Classroom: keep it under ~2 hrs. Skool is hiding the classroom for new communities, because building a course doesn't correlate with retention and is a churn reason. "Overwhelm is the main cause of churn."
- Promotion vs delivery: you can never promote too much, but you can put too much inside the community. Move extra lives/posts outside, as promotion.
- Hire a mediocre delegate if the time saved, reinvested (e.g. into YouTube), beats the quality gap. (Alex)
- Videographer/close-proximity roles: vibe > skill. Editors: test skill first (same raw file, 48 hrs; 1,200 applicants → 60 submissions, ~5%), then vibe.
- Lifetime deals: one-time income with forever delivery. Avoid them. To exit an avatar ethically, refund.
- Refund anyone who asks. Alex's observation: people who got in FTC trouble refused refunds → disputes → AG complaints → kicked off Stripe first ("canary in the coal mine").
- "Can't come back for a year" cancel penalty: Alex has seen no effect. Use a friendly goodbye (Planet Fitness, ~5% monthly churn, cycles the whole town).
- Don't build software unless you can do it right (Sam delayed Skool a year to find his CTO).

## Playbook steps
1. List your three post-event actions; one will matter disproportionately.
2. Map each to traffic / conversion / price / churn and pick the biggest constraint.
3. More → better → new.
4. Price test: compute LTV (price/churn) × conversion per click for each price and pick the max EPC.
5. Fix churn: interview people who cancelled and people who stayed, amplify what stayers did, remove what leavers did. Add a weekly group onboarding call.
6. Simplify: fewer details, each perfect (Skool Games = 8 videos < 45 min, one call a week, one Mastermind a month).
7. Listen ~90% of the time, validate problems with power users, fix the #1 problem, announce "I made this for you."
8. Record every call: delivery + content + marketing at once.
9. Cut low-value personal tasks (cleaning, laundry, food ≈ 96 hrs/mo on US averages).

## Mistakes he warns against
- Doing work that doesn't map to a lever (e.g. reviving a dead 14K free group when ads would do more).
- Finding something that works, then getting distracted by six new things.
- Spreading one-time value over monthly payments.
- Adding content to fix churn.
- Widening content to off-topic subjects: Alex's views rose with the wrong audience. Back to "100% business", and site conversion, book sales and portfolio applications all went up while average views fell.
- Two front ends at once, and not saying no to money.
- Interviewing top talent with "sell me why I should hire you".
- No-refund policies.
