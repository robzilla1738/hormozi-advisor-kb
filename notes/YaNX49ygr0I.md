# How to get what you want...

## Core thesis
Winners and losers have the same goals; the activities they commit to separate them. Don't write down goals. Write down who you want to become, and at every decision point ask "What would this type of person do?" (a billionaire, a wise man, someone 10× smarter, Solomon, Buffett and Munger). Repeated actions are votes for an identity. Once the behavior becomes who you are, you keep playing indefinitely instead of easing off after a good month. This is a mindset video from when ACQ was ~$85M/yr.

## Frameworks & models
- **Be → Do → Have**: the external result, the behaviors that produce it, and the identity of a person who does those behaviors (via James Clear's *Atomic Habits*).
- **Desire = a contract to be unhappy until you get what you want.** Writing goals gives direction but doesn't separate winners from losers.
- **Advertising = making known** (applied to "get more customers"): private one-to-one communication (cold calls, DMs, emails) or public one-to-many (posts, email lists, radio). Do more of these activities.
- **The identity chorus**: a single refrain ("What would a billionaire / wise man / person 10× smarter do?") replaces a 50-point checklist and covers situations you didn't predict ("What would Jesus do" as the model).
- **Identity = "repeated beingness"** (his etymology). Actions are votes; affirmations without evidence don't work.
- **Comparison treadmill**: as you level up you just change who you compare yourself to (via Jordan Peterson).
- **"We need to be reminded more than we need to be taught"**: most people know what to do. The kidnapped-family test shows you'd act if it mattered enough.
- **If you truly don't know what to do**, step the question down: what would a millionaire do, or someone becoming a millionaire (e.g., talk to millionaires)?

## Decision rules & thresholds
- "Committing to the activities is the goal." A big month achieves nothing by itself.
- People doing it for an outcome ease off when they hit it; people doing it because of who they are keep going.
- "The people who achieve the really extraordinary things do the uncommonly obvious thing for an inordinate period of time."

## Playbook steps
1. Write down the type of person you want to become.
2. Put the question on a post-it where you work.
3. At each crossroads, ask it and act accordingly.
4. Accumulate evidence (actions) that reinforces the identity.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Relying on goal-writing or affirmations.
- Hunting for "secrets" instead of doing the known obvious things.
- Easing off after short-term success.
