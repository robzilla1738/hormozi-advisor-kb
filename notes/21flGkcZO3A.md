## Core thesis
If you use media to get customers rather than to sell ads, optimize content for revenue, not views. The algorithm tells you what the most people like, not what the most valuable people like. In Alex's data, his six most-viewed videos last quarter made zero sales, and his top revenue videos had modest views. Make content for your buyers (the top 20% by spend), add "vertical value" when going broad, track with UTMs, and accept lower views for higher sales. (Context claimed: 3B impressions, +4.5M subs, 35K pieces of content in a year; $106M in one book-launch weekend.)

## Frameworks & models
- **Media company vs business using media**: media businesses sell views to advertisers who price on audience size. Businesses should chase buyers.
- **Niche beats reach**: a registered dietitian with <6K Instagram followers and ~9–20 likes per post made $1M+/yr teaching RDs how to bill insurance.
- **Two tests**: (1) a quarter of broad top-of-funnel content broke view records, but book sales, leads and portfolio applications fell. (2) Last quarter: top-viewed videos (1.2M, 1M, 800K, 500K, 500K, 350K) made 0 sales, while #1 revenue was a ~270K-view video on where the money is and segmenting customers. Cash Cows deep-dives (100K–250K views) were also top earners, and a straight service-business Q&A was #6.
- **Shrinking audience**: only ~9% of Americans own businesses; cut by ~2/3 for those over $100K/yr.
- **51-to-1 rule**: half the audience has $2 and the other half has $98. Don't fight for the poor half.
- **"Qualume"** (quality + volume) is the nirvana. If forced to pick, choose revenue.
- **Vertical value**: broad videos should help both the starter and the $100M founder (e.g., "how the 1% think about money" earned revenue and views). Advanced "1→10" videos tank on views but bring buyers with spending power.
- **Strategy fork**: serve everyone at the lowest cost (Walmart) or serve the high end (where services usually go).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Measure content by attributed revenue, leads and applications, not views or subscribers.
- Don't fear deep, advanced content. Low views mean you're serving a different audience, and the algorithm routes it to the right people.
- To find buyer topics: take the top 20% of customers by spend, find common traits, messages and problems, and make content solving those.

## Playbook steps
1. Decide: media company or business? 2. Pull the top 20% of customers by spend and list their problems. 3. Make topics for them; keep broad videos vertically valuable. 4. Put CTAs to a lead magnet in videos, with UTM links in descriptions. 5. Attribute revenue per video. 6. Expect views and subs to drop and sales to rise.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Taking views as your strategy signal (he's made this mistake multiple times).
- Broad, beginner content that feels impactful but doesn't sell.
