## Frameworks & models
- **5-step content machine**: (1) **Test** ideas on Twitter, which replaced his private email-to-self brain dump; Twitter and TikTok allow ~10 posts/day. (2) **Record** the winners: short tweets become shorts, threads become long videos. (3) **Add CTAs** at the beginning, middle and end, plus extra mid-rolls on long pieces. Write ~5 variants of each CTA type (book, review, share/tag, event, lead magnet) for ~30 total, 5–10 seconds each, to avoid "banner/audio blindness." (4) **Contextualize** per platform (Dolly Parton meme): no TikTok watermark on IG/YouTube; thumbnail and text fit each platform. (5) **Distribute at volume**.
- **Rule of 100**: 100 primary actions a day (calls, emails, DMs, or $100/day in ads), or 100 pieces of organic content a week. "Your problem will not be demand generation."
- **"How I" vs "how to"**: say "how I built…", not "how you can build…". "You should" presumes authority you may not have earned, while experience can't be challenged. To pivot, talk about what you did for named clients.
- **Give away the secrets, sell the implementation**: paid value is implementation, accountability, troubleshooting and outside perspective (e.g. a coach reviewing your sales calls).
- **Reciprocity and the value ladder**: "people make their purchasing decisions based on the value they've already received." The top of your value ladder is set by the last thing you delivered *in excess* of its price. If a $100K offer only returns $100K, you have a satisfied customer, not a raving one, and the relationship ends. Make your free stuff better than competitors' paid stuff. Free still costs time and attention.
- **Leverage** = the ratio of output to input. Content costs the same for 1 viewer or 1M, but the returns flow back to one person. Money is the unit of exchange for time.
- **Micro time mastery**: make "end of day" the default deadline instead of end of week. A project with ~7 back-and-forths shrinks from 7 weeks to days.
- **Macro time mastery**: are all activities aligned and compounding over decades? "Play games where if you wait you win." The wealthy "talk in decades not days."
- **Three traits of the ultra-successful** (a study he cites): a superiority complex, deep insecurity/fear of failure, and impulse control. The third is the one people miss: refusing shiny objects.
- **The $30K/year contract**: most people would sign a deal to live on $30K a year for 5 years in exchange for $1M a year afterward, but they don't live that way. Sacrifices are trade-offs, or prices you've chosen to pay.
- **Sales team = Recruit → Train → Manage**: picking matters more than training (it takes 3x the time to get someone from a 2 to a 5 as from a 7 to a 9; "championships are won in the draft"). Train on the **prospect, not the product**: describe their pain better than they can and the solution is assumed, and products can be swapped. Manage toward **conviction**. A rep who goes from hot to cold lost conviction, not skill. Fix it with testimonials and live clients ("remember Johnny you sold?"). Conviction is a degree, not a yes/no (the "sell your past self on Bitcoin" thought experiment).
- **Simple question-based script**: Why are you here? What have you struggled with before? Why didn't it work? Then "I think you might be a good fit, can I tell you about it?" → X, Y, Z → "Does that make sense?" Questions are a home base for bringing a wandering prospect back.
- **Objection onion, peeled outside in** (people give their power away):
  - (1) Circumstances: time ("it's priorities"), money ("not resources but resourcefulness"; you find money for rent, tax bills or car repairs but not for yourself), fit ("that breakfast got you that body").
  - (2) Other people: "you're asking for permission when you should be asking for support." In two years, who will you blame?
  - (3) Avoidance ("I need to think about it"): now you're talking to the person in charge.
- **The point of the sale is to get a decision.** "Decide" comes from Latin for "to cut off": "which future are we cutting off today?"
- **Past / Present / Future** for avoidance:
  - Past: "burned twice" (did you swear off dating after middle school?); "you've been making this decision for 5 years, today's just the first day of action."
  - Present: the three questions. Will it solve the problem? Do you want to work with us? Do you have, or have access to, the money? Then: "What are you most afraid of having happen?" → worst case → "What would you need to feel you won't fail again?"
  - Future: "if you'll do it eventually, why not now?"
- **Skeleton-key closes**: the **reason close** ("that's the very reason you should do this") and the **hypothetical close** ("if time weren't the issue, would you do it?", then recreate the hypothetical yes in real-world conditions). Related: the **1–10 close** ("what would make it a 10?" / "why aren't you a 1?") and the **unicorn close**.
- **Failure**: just a step in the sequence, "a requisite for success." The labels we put on it were given to us as kids.
- **Content standard: valid + useful (+ entertaining)**. "Some things work, some don't" is valid but useless. The "7 stages of awareness" may be useful but unvalidated.
- **Lead definition** (from the draft leads book): "information that you can leverage to create a transaction." A YouTube subscriber is not a lead; an Instagram follower you can DM is. He made 58 broken models before the "advertising cycle."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If results are normal, output is normal. The volume gaps that matter are 100–300x, not 2–3x: his mentor tested with at least 5,000 flyers and then ran 5,000/day vs Alex's 300; a "Rule of 100" follower made 100 reach-outs in 8 weeks instead of ~56,000 [inference: the transcript says 7×8=56 days ×100 = 5,600, but he says 56,000, which looks like a math slip; 56 days × 100/day = 5,600].
- Don't post on topics you lack the authority for (the 18-year-old relationship coach, a $10K/month business owner teaching business). Business influencers didn't need their following to make their money.
- Hire an experienced sales manager who has built a team before. Otherwise, learn to sell yourself first. Match rep type to the cycle: high-volume transactional (hunters) vs enterprise long-cycle (farmers).
- Keep sales systems simple: "the more complexity you add to the system the easier it is to break."
- Default deadline: end of day unless stated otherwise.
- Acquisition.com (at the time) worked with businesses doing $3M+ and aimed to take them to $30M+, a result claimed six times.

## Playbook steps
1. Brain-dump ideas publicly on Twitter/TikTok and see what performs.
2. Turn winners into shorts (from short tweets) and longs (from threads).
3. Insert short, varied CTAs at the start, middle and end, drawn from a bank of about 30.
4. Re-package each piece natively for every platform.
5. Raise volume about 10x (he went from 7 to 80 posts a week).
6. Frame everything as "how I" or "what my clients did." Give away more than feels comfortable.
7. For sales: recruit well, give reps a question-based script, train them deeply on prospect pains, run daily huddles and role-play, and rebuild conviction with client stories when a rep slumps.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Blaming the strategy when the real problem is execution volume.
- Fear of mean comments keeping output low.
- Preaching "you should" without proof. Rewarming others' content (e.g. GaryVee's) "from a position of weakness."
- Hiding your best material behind walls, or putting out weak free content and then deciding "content doesn't work."
- Squeezing out the gap between price and value at the top of the ladder.
- Training reps on product features and competitor differences.
- Shiny objects that break the long-term plan.
- Reposting TikToks with the watermark and identical captions.
