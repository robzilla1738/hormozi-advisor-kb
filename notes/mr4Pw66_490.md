## Core thesis
"A business that requires you isn't a business" — it's a job you can't quit, and it's worth little to anyone else. Removing yourself (self-inventory → delegate with rules/scorecards → hire people who teach you → systemize marketing so it's not your face → take 90 days off) turns profit into a sellable asset, which is where wealth is made (multiples, not taxed income). ## Frameworks & models
- **Frustrated Fred vs Wealthy William**: identical $10M revenue / $2M profit. Fred works 80 hrs/wk: $2M → ~$1M after 50% tax → ~$500K saved/yr. William's team runs it; worth 6× profit = $12M. +$500K profit: Fred saves +$250K/yr; William's value +$3M ($12M→$15M). He calls it roughly a 20× difference.
- **Self-inventory + time study**: spreadsheet, timer every 15 minutes, write one word for what you did (~a week). Also use on key-man employees.
- **Project / Process / Person**: assign each task to a one-time project, a repeatable process, or a person.
- **Red / Yellow / Green**: Green = can hand to someone now; Yellow = need to build a one-time project/process I know how to do; Red = don't know how / need a person I don't have. Do greens → yellows → reds.
- **If-this-then-that decision trees** for common scenarios, plus a **spending authority box** (e.g. under $500 / $1K / $10K / $100K by company size), checked with monthly financials.
- **"How does your role make the company money?"** — reverse each role's possible mistakes into KPIs (camera operator: footage saved, named, in the right drive, timestamped, lighting/camera set correctly, on time).
- **Graduation test**: can someone 80% as good as you get 100% of the result?
- **Shadow → Supervise → Support**: they watch you; you watch them; you're available but not involved; decisions migrate to them.
- **Leverage ladder**: trade ~200 hrs/month of doing for ~20 hrs of managing (10×), then hire a manager and trade again.
- **Litmus tests**: baseline "does it burn down without me?"; better "leave a month, come back and it's better"; ultimate "3 months off and it grows" (the "phone test").
- **Ways to get marketing without the founder's face**: screenshot praise in the community every week → ads; give rewards for testimonials (credit, unlocks, a trial of a higher tier = upsell too); lifecycle ads (stitch recorded sales, onboarding and checkpoint calls into a before/after timeline); record key emotional moments (reveal, weigh-in, financials); pay support reps $5 per mini chat testimonial; turn existing reviews into ads; joke about 1-star reviews Liquid Death-style to push away bad fits; affiliates; TikTok Shop whitelisting.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Accept 80% (sometimes 60%) as good at handoff if there is a clear path to improvement.
- Judge employees by how fast they improve, not where they start; "intelligence = rate of learning."
- Hiring leaders: if you aren't learning from them in the interview, it's a waste of time. Leaders should teach you.
- Judge candidates by the quality and quantity of metrics they use to describe their role.
- Interview more people than feels reasonable; group interviews for frontline up to manager.
- Brick and mortar: don't open a second location until the first passes the 3-month-away test and grows. If it's flat, it drops when you look away. He calls overexpansion one of the top reasons businesses die.

## Playbook steps
1. Time study (15-min increments). 2. List every task. 3. Assign project/process/person and mark R/Y/G. 4. Write if-then rules plus a spending limit. 5. Scorecard/KPIs tied to how the role makes money. 6. Graduation test (80% as good, 100% of result). 7. Shadow → supervise → support. 8. Hire for the reds (interview a lot, hire people who teach you). 9. Systemize marketing away from your face. 10. Take 90 days off; fix what breaks; repeat until nothing breaks.

## Mistakes he warns against
- "No one can do it like me" / never outsource sales, marketing, money.
- Thinking you can outwork everyone: "a mountain of work, but not Mount Everest."
- Ego-driven key-man dependence, especially the founder as the only source of customers.
- Weak proxy metrics (low refunds ≠ good product; no lawsuits ≠ good culture).
- Expanding too early.

## Examples / case details
- Prestige Labs support team: tickets reduce to if-then rules (change card, flavor, billing cadence, cancel, refund).
- Hotel credit for a bad stay: decision limit set in advance.
- First strong HR hire asked about cost to acquire talent, time to fill, and 90-day two-way fit %; he knew none of them.
- Sam interviewed ~600 developers over about a year to find CTO co-founder Daniel.
- Hired ~5 sales managers (6–12 months each) before finding a good one, which built pattern recognition.
