## Core thesis
Who you marry is the single most important net-worth decision, more than business, industry, market, pricing or lead gen. You can't change your spouse into Leila. Instead, decide whether they are a "cheerleader" or a "fullback" and manage the closeness-vs-distance dichotomy. Alex and Leila married "for logic," built love through action, and grew from ~$400–500K/month to millions per month by dividing roles. Mostly personal and relationship content, with some business-partner role lessons.

## Frameworks & models
- **Cheerleader vs fullback** (credited to GaryVee): a spouse outside the business must be a supportive cheerleader who gives perspective and context and never asks you to quit when the game is on the line. A spouse in the business is a fullback, blocking and carrying in rotation. Anyone doing neither is detracting.
- **Dichotomies to manage, not solve** (Esther Perel): closeness/familiarity vs distance/intrigue. In business, micromanagement vs abdication is the same kind of tension. Too close and you're "roommates"; too distant and you're strangers.
- **Create space to be missed**: separate offices on opposite sides of the house, avoiding the same meetings. When one partner isn't in the business, build a shared reality and grow in the same direction.
- **Division of roles**: a mentor circled ~80% of the tasks (operations) for Leila and left marketing, sales and product to Alex. That split took them from ~$400–500K/month to millions per month.
- **Fear evolves**: early on you fear being poor; in the middle you fear losing status.
- **Love logically**: love can be created through actions.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Don't try to change your partner; figure out the yin/yang arrangement.
- If the business is your only thing, all your chips are on it and you get more emotional and erratic, so a cheerleader's perspective improves your decisions.
- Keep nothing "sitting between you on the couch." Voice unhappiness with radical candor.

## Playbook steps
1. Identify your spouse's role (cheerleader or fullback). 2. If you work together, clearly divide responsibilities, with operations and growth handled by different people. 3. Diagnose where you sit on closeness/distance and lean the other way. 4. Create a shared reality if they're outside the business. 5. Hold regular candid check-ins about how life is going.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Asking "how do I make my wife like Leila."
- Both partners doing everything with no role split.
- Silently grinding while unhappy because neither wants to let the other down.

## Examples / case details
- First date: 15-minute froyo, then a 4-hour walk with no chemistry. Leila was a top salesperson at 24 Hour Fitness.
- After his first three gym launch turnarounds, they processed $100K in contracts in an hour. She asked "is it legal?" and then left her training clients to join him.
- Proposal: a logical pitch (young, Persian backgrounds, fitness, business). They bought a ring 45 minutes later, married at church that Wednesday after less than a week's engagement, and told their parents afterward.
- December 2019: after ~$30M profit in 24 months, both were unhappy and fear-driven. They prioritized the marriage in 2020, and he says it "probably tripled" in quality despite COVID hitting their brick-and-mortar businesses.
- At the time, he says Acquisition.com did "85 million a year."
