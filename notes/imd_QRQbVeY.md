# Negative Emotional Motivation

## Core thesis
Name your emotions (e.g., envy vs. jealousy) to create space between emotion and action, then judge whether the emotion is adaptive. Envy doesn't make you happy but can make you successful; judge actions/outcomes more than motives.

## Frameworks & models
- **Envy vs. jealousy:** envy = wanting what someone else has; jealousy = fearing someone will take what you have.
- **Name → space → evaluate:** 1) name the emotion, 2) naming increases time between emotion and action (better decisions), 3) ask if it is pro-adaptive or harmful.
- Prefer people who do the right thing for the wrong reasons over the wrong thing for the right reasons.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Wanting is reason enough (Dan Sullivan), provided it doesn't hurt others or yourself.

## Examples / case details
- Dinner with Nick Bare ("Billion Dollar Brotherhood"). He told a friend running a weight-loss company "I am envious of you" — conversation became "let me help you."
- Navy SEAL former EMT who cared about his stats/being best, not compassion — you'd want him on your heart attack.

## Notable lines
- "Envy absolutely does not make you happy, but I do think envy will make you successful."
- "Wanting is reason enough." (attributed to Dan Sullivan)
