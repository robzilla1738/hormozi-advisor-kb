## Core thesis
"Scale the unscalable." Things look unscalable early only because you lack money, skill and context. "It's not unscalable, you are unskilled." Small businesses (under $10M) win by doing what big companies can't: calls, handwritten cards, 1:1 onboarding, personal outreach. You learn more than you earn, then step it down through 2.0/3.0 versions that keep ~80% of the effect at 10× the output. "You can't be busy and broke. Pick one." Launch new offers top-down: done-for-you → done-with-you → do-it-yourself.

## Frameworks & models
- **Unscalable = unskilled**: unless physics forbids it, "it requires people" just means it's a service business (culture plus training).
- **Don't refuse the step because you fear the extreme**: like "I don't want to get bulky," founders refuse to start what won't reach $100M while making $0.
- **Unscalable → 2.0 → 3.0**: Andy Frisella (First Form) wrote handwritten cards to every buyer, then signed them, then had others write them. They still do it at hundreds of millions in revenue.
- **Two magic customer questions**: (1) "If I eliminated all features except one, which would you keep?" (2) "If I eliminated one feature and it changed nothing for you, which?" Across ~100 customers this gives a power ranking. Cut and distill.
- **Talk to customers** (Paul Graham): every marketing or product problem is solved by talking to customers more.
- **Onboarding scale ladder**: 1:1 → answer the same ~6 questions with a deck → 1:6 → 1:30 (six 1:30 sessions = 180 people/day for one person) → train someone else on the deck. A portfolio company scaled group onboarding to $100M+/yr.
- **Overbooked sales calendar**: he did 4,101 closes (not consults) for his gyms, with 2–3 people booked per 30-minute slot to cover show rate.
- **Every business incurs debt**: VC companies take financial debt to hire talent at market rates; bootstrappers take "management debt," working the job of 2–4 people (the swamp).
- **Context-appropriate advice**: rich people's current behavior (Buffett flying private) is the result, not the path.
- **Leverage small-company advantages**: personal iMessage voice notes and videos from a real phone rather than green-text automations, even at companies doing tens to hundreds of millions.
- **Gary Halbert's "one letter" exercise**: start with the maximum-effort version (handwritten envelope, licked stamp, lumpy object). Then find which 20% of elements drive 80% of results, and scale those with cash from the early version.
- **Sales-to-fulfillment continuum** ($100M Offers): the easier something is to sell, the harder it is to fulfill. Technology is the main exception.
- **Top-down pyramid ("Tesla model")**: start with expensive done-for-you (easy to sell, hard to fulfill, max revenue per customer with low lead flow, best results). Build SOPs → done-with-you at ~1/3–1/5 the price, roughly 10× the customers since support is ~1/10 of doing it → DIY at ~1/10 of done-with-you. The top tier also price-anchors and brands everything below it.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you're broke, get busy with unscalable work: take calls even for $10–20/month products (gym memberships are sold in person at the front desk too).
- Be more efficient per lead when small; pull out all the stops.
- Accept losing efficiency at scale; start with the most effective process.
- Prefer top-down over bottom-up product sequencing (least operational drag when customers are fewest). Bottom-up isn't wrong, just not his preference.

## Playbook steps
1. Do the maximum-touch version (calls, onboarding, handwritten notes, personal messages).
2. Ask the two feature questions repeatedly; refine offer and messaging.
3. Document the recurring questions/steps into a deck or SOP.
4. Increase the ratio (1:6 → 1:30), and then hand it to trained staff.
5. Fund leverage (e.g., phones for reps) from early cash.
6. For new products, sell done-for-you first, then productize downward.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Calling something unscalable to avoid hard work.
- Copying advice meant for a different stage.
- Starting with the hardest-to-sell (cheapest, DIY) offer.
- Generic automated outreach that reads like a bot.
