## Core thesis
Live Q&A with business owners (callers speak; Alex answers). Recurring answer: stop asking for guarantees and set goals on inputs you control (e.g., 4 hours/day of promotion); commit to one path; stack knowledge in one vertical; start with a narrow problem you can solve with your resources; promotion volume (content + outreach + partners) is what solo service businesses lack.

## Frameworks & models
- **Input goals over outcome goals**: you can't control how many buy; you can control actions that make failure "unreasonable." Judge success by adherence to committed actions. Outcome = skills learned, not just the number.
- **"Die if you don't hit 10x" test**: ask what you'd do if your life depended on it → that's the plan.
- **Four ways to let people know** (core four): talk 1:1 (outreach), content, ads, and others (affiliates/referrals/agencies/employees).
- **Talk to the marketplace, not the universe**: the market always answers when you knock.
- **Give in public, sell in private**: keep public content value-only; when people DM/respond to stories, define scope and offer a call; light CTAs in comments/stories.
- **Stack knowledge in one vertical** for compounding returns; richest people he knows stayed in one space ~40 years.
- **Strategy is prioritization**: allocating limited resources against unlimited options → narrow the problem, solve it completely, then go adjacent (Amazon → books, Facebook → colleges).
- **Second-location test**: if you can step away from the gym for a year, you can do the next thing; either path starts with delegating the first location.
- **Content as lead nurture early, not lead gen**: prospects you reach out to check your page; content earns the reply.
- **Friend test**: do they make me better, and do they have my best interests at heart? "Lonely middle" when outgrowing the old group.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Commit 4 hours/day to promotion (content + outreach; ads if you can afford).
- Pursuing multiple businesses at once "virtually guarantee[s]" you don't succeed at any; pick one and commit amid uncertainty.
- Brick-and-mortar owner: either scale the location (second location, systems/incentives/training for a 5/5 experience) or reduce your time there to near zero and go all in online — not both.
- If you lack income, get your head above water with a simple business before attempting a world-changing, well-capitalized arena.
- Solo bookkeeper/CFO: ~10,000 reach-outs → business you want in ~6 months; or ~1,000 B2B partners (HR, recruiting) for referral/kickback swaps.
- One post a day; message everyone who engages.
- Judge yourself by what you do despite how you feel.

## Playbook steps
1. Replace outcome goal with daily action commitment. 2. Run core-four promotion 4 hrs/day. 3. Find people with your audience (adjacent businesses/creators) and reach out at scale. 4. Use content to nurture outbound leads. 5. Delegate the existing business before starting another. 6. Narrow the problem to what your resources can solve; expand adjacently later.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Analysis paralysis / wanting a guarantee before acting.
- Splitting focus between in-person and online (traction bounces back and forth).
- Believing you must go online to avoid being in person (he owns ~70 stores and is in none).
- Mission mismatch: "impact 1M lives" with a 200-member gym.
- Solo service owners relying on referrals: one new client every 2–3 months while one churns.
- Expecting content to be the main early lead source.
