# How to buyback your time as a business owner

## Core thesis
The personal side of scaling is about control. You first jump from no control (a job or your parents) to complete control. That same need for control then stifles growth, because there's only one of you. Each stage requires buying back your time and giving up control of the thing you currently identify with, while keeping full accountability. His rule of thumb for staying close enough: "know where the bodies are buried." (Early video: "three companies… over $110M in sales.")

## Frameworks & models
- **Buyback principle** (credits the term to "Dan Martell" [inference: the caption reads "denmark tell us"]): keep buying back your time as the company grows.
- **Sequence of control to relinquish**: low-skill support/doing (an assistant; for gyms, training classes) → admin (billing, collections, contracts) → right-hand operator and money tracking → selling → building and managing a sales team (recruit, hire, train, compensate) → leadership/culture through a small group of leaders.
- **Revenue markers**: up to ~$1M/yr you can sell yourself. Past $1M you must scale sales. The sales team carries you to roughly $3–10M. $10M → $30M requires delegating leadership (lead leaders).
- **Identity question**: "What am I trying to control, and what part of my identity am I giving up by giving it up?"
- **Know where the bodies are buried**: if you can't name a department's flaws or problems, you're too far away. That means you need more transparency, not necessarily more control. Hearing only good news means you're too far away.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If your work is worth $100/hr, don't do work the market pays $15/hr for.
- The sales model is broken if it only works at the founder's close rate. It should work with reps closing half as many as you. Over time, reps should beat you because selling is all they do.
- Relinquish control but keep full accountability and responsibility for results.

## Playbook steps
1. Hire support for the lowest-value doing.
2. Hand off admin (billing, contracts).
3. Hand off money tracking to someone better at it (still review it).
4. Founder sells until ~$1M, then hires and scales sales.
5. Build and manage a sales team.
6. Lead through a small leadership group; embody the culture.
7. Audit each department: can you name its problems? If not, get closer.

## Mistakes he warns against
- "No one can train/sell like me": ego and identity attachment.
- "I don't trust anyone with the money."
- Letting trust issues from your past cap the business ("get over it"); blaming others instead of taking responsibility.
