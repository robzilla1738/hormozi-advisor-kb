# The SIMPLE Managerial Framework that changed my business
id: IMowPVgcWbA / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMowPVgcWbA / duration: 650s (~11 min) / tags: 07-hiring-management, 08-ops-scaling

## Core thesis
When someone doesn't do what you want, there are only three reasons: they didn't know you wanted it (communication), they don't know how (training), or they don't want to (motivation/incentive). Diagnose which box it is. That way you attack the process, not the person. He built this on Andy Grove's two-reason model (High Output Management) and says he "stole ruthlessly" from Leila.

## Frameworks & models
- **Why-people-don't-do-it triangle**: (1) Communicate the expectation (the peak); (2) Training: show how to do it, how you want it done, and how to report it; (3) Motivation/incentive, both internal and external.
- **Andy Grove's base model**: an employee fails to perform because they either don't know how or aren't motivated, so the boss's job is to train and motivate. Hormozi added the third reason: "they don't know that you want them to do it."
- **Communication cadence = perceived importance**: how often you talk about or report on a metric tells people how much it matters. Twice-daily meetings say very important. One line on a quarterly slide says less. Never mentioned, and it very likely won't get done.
- **Communicating expectations has three parts**: communicate it, repeat it, document it (in writing, ideally in several places, and in daily/weekly checklists or reports).
- **"Being advanced is simply never not doing the basics."** Businesses plateau because they stop doing the basics at scale. Service and smiles drop as teammates get added.
- **The "why"**: tie the task to the organization (e.g., CRM notes help finance collect and help customer success personalize onboarding) and then to a personal benefit (the notes screen out tire-kickers so the rep's close rate goes up).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you want something done, make them report it to you daily or weekly.
- "I was busy" can mean low motivation (would they do it for $10K by tomorrow?) or a lack of training in prioritizing and managing their schedule. Check both.
- External motivation: if the task isn't in their comp or bonus while four other things are, expect it not to get done. Realign the comp.
- Once all three boxes are addressed and it still doesn't happen, it's a motivation issue. Have the real conversation.
- Moving an underperformer to another role: he says "nine times out of 10" it's usually not the right call. Hire the person you normally would for that seat, because "a track record of failure does not make a high likelihood candidate." [Transcript wording here is muddled; this is the gist.]

## Playbook steps
1. Open with "Hey Johnny, you're underperforming and it's really unlike you." The compliment protects their ego while you attack the behavior.
2. Ask "What's going on?" through the three lenses: Did I not communicate it? Do you not know how, in general or given your workload? Are other priorities more motivating?
3. Isolate the box and fix it: re-communicate and document; train by doing it together, recording it, and setting how it gets reported; or realign incentives and explain why.
4. If it still doesn't happen, name it as motivation and discuss fit.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Assuming people know what you expect (the sales director who never got told to do call reviews).
- Asking once and never measuring it or putting it in writing.
- Asking for work that isn't compensated or measured.
- Assigning activity with no purpose: "activity without purpose is what they do at prison camps."
- Moving failed performers into other roles by default.

## Examples / case details
- New sales manager: a few weeks in, no call reviews. "I was unaware that you wanted me to do call reviews." Hormozi never told him. The fix was doing two reviews together, recording them, and defining the notes, the communication to the team, and the reporting.
- CRM notes rationale: helps Judy in finance collect, helps Cindy in customer success personalize onboarding, and helps the rep close more by screening leads up front.
