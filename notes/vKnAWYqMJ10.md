# I Spent $312,900 Living Like The 1% (Here's What I Learned)

## Core thesis
"Never be impressed by money" (his dad's line). The "ultra-wealthy" Instagram lifestyle (private jets, exotic car, penthouse, designer clothes, fancy dinners) costs only ~$300K/yr (~$550K pre-tax) — achievable from a business netting ~$40K/month — so it is not a signal of real wealth or of someone worth taking money advice from. Wealth is an income-to-expense ratio, not a number. Keep lifestyle under 10% of income and save the rest. Personal finance video, little operational business content.

## Frameworks & models
- **Lifestyle cost breakdown** (his numbers): private jet ~$4K/hr × 24 hrs/yr (6 round trips of 2 hrs each way) ≈ ~$100K; exotic car lease ~$4K–4.5K/mo ≈ $50K/yr; penthouse $10K/mo = $120K/yr (~$5K/mo gets a very nice place outside NYC/LA/Miami); clothes ~$3K/yr; $400 weekly date night ≈ $20K/yr. Total ≈ $300K/yr after tax → ~$550K pre-tax (top 1%).
- **Trimmed version**: drop the jet and use a reasonable home → ~$150K/yr while still having the car, clothes, restaurants.
- **Wealth = income ÷ expenses** ratio. 10:1 (e.g., live on $30K, earn $300K) = very wealthy, no stress.
- **Happiness = haves ÷ wants**; drive wants toward zero and happiness → infinity; decreasing desire increases abundance and the income/expense ratio.
- **Wealth tiers** (his definitions): millionaire $1–5M; high net worth $5–30M; ultra-high net worth $30M+ investable. At $30M earning 10%, passive income $3M/yr ≈ 10× this lifestyle's cost.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Spend <10% of income on lifestyle; save ~90%. He says at that rate you can be fully free within 2–5 years.
- Only buy the luxury lifestyle when it's a small fraction of income (or passive income covers it many times over).
- Don't take money advice from people whose lifestyle consumes 100% of their income.
- Primary residence: treat as an investment if possible (e.g., live in one side of a duplex, rent the other).

## Playbook steps
1. Write your dream-lifestyle list and price it out per year (useful exercise he recommends).
2. Gross it up for taxes to get the real "dream number."
3. Cut items with low enjoyment return (car, jet, expensive home).
4. Raise income so lifestyle is <10% of it; bank the rest.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Being impressed by visible spending/Instagram wealth signals.
- Chasing "a zillion dollars" when the actual number needed is far smaller.
- Exotic cars: he owns two, regrets it; hedonic adaptation within 1–2 weeks.
- Overspending on where you live.

## Examples / case details
- Bought the car after Brad Lea called him cheap on a podcast; he and Leila had shared one car until recently.
- A rented Chevy Suburban's interface beat his ~$350K Bentley — mass-market products get more product testing.
- Prefers Chipotle, Chili's, Cheesecake Factory, BJ's to $500 steaks; value of fancy dinners is dressing up.
- Ultra-wealthy people he knows almost all concluded "almost none of this stuff is worth it." Flying private is nice but he still feels guilty.
