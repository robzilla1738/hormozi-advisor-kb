# 8 Entrepreneurs Compete For $100,000

## Core thesis
This is a "Scale or Fail" competition-show finale. Eight owners who got a personalized roadmap 90 days earlier compete over three rounds for $100K plus a year of guidance from Acquisition.com. Round 1 is judged on growth, round 2 on adherence to the plan, round 3 on a head-to-head pitch. Alex's lesson is that a clear plan for your current constraint, plus "violent" commitment to it, can produce years of results in 90 days. Per Alex, contestants averaged 2.4x revenue growth and together added >$1M revenue and >$500K profit. Speakers: Alex, Leila Hormozi, CEO "Shanza" (caption spelling), and the contestants. Many lines come from contestants or the other judges, not Alex.

## Frameworks & models
- **Diagnose the single constraint, then 2-5 levers** for each business. The levers reveal Alex's standard prescriptions:
  - Founder bottleneck → a system so the team answers repeat questions; raise prices on 1:1 time; cut a 3-4 hr pitch to a tight 45-min webinar; be clear about teaching vs. selling.
  - Marketing → build referrals into every visit; give away something big instead of discounting; sell where rich customers already gather (events).
  - Not enough customers → spend far more on ads; replace many cheap products with one higher-priced annual offer.
  - Competing on hourly price → flat rates plus a VIP tier; sell to the rich.
  - Cash crunch with a small team → pick one grand slam offer and go all in; use a giveaway for leads and fast cash.
  - New roofer → take the small repairs competitors ignore, raise prices, increase volume, use the "menu close" script (Alex's favorite 4-step script).
  - Capped by one clinician → hire more providers; shift the founder's identity from best practitioner to best teacher. A 90% close rate means prices are too low.
  - Made-up prices / thin margins → a cost-basis pricing formula ("profitable by design rather than by accident"); reps book meetings and the founder closes; B2B event sponsorships put you in front of hundreds of ideal customers at once.
- **Judging order**: growth → adherence → sales. Why adherence: "the fastest way to build a $10 million business isn't the fastest way to build a hundred million dollar business"; build it fast and right.
- **Sales is the central entrepreneurial skill**: you sell investors, employees, customers, and through marketing.
- **Investor lens (Alex)**: reverse-engineer what the prospect wants. Investors want fast-growing, highly profitable, defensible businesses. Both finalists missed this.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- A ~90% close rate signals underpricing. Raise prices aggressively.
- Timing of the upsell: "It's not just how you sell, it's when you sell." Tina moved her upsell to the middle of a shorter program (month 3 of 6) instead of the end of 12 months.
- A VSL is "an insurance policy" on a big-ticket sale ($41K). It cuts doctor time (3 conversations → 2, 1 hr → 30 min) and raises close rate. "You cannot educate a customer more and have them be less likely to buy" (unless the product is bad).
- When choosing what to invest in, he weighs scalability, margin and cash flow, recurring revenue, LTV:CAC, and the founder's commitment (staying through COVID shutdowns as a "limit test").
- Leila: "watch what they do, not what they say." Alex: telling the judges you went out partying the night before was a bigger judgment error than the partying itself.
- Acquisition.com motto (Leila): test and learn; you learn by executing, not by debating.

## Playbook steps
1. Name the one constraint. 2. Pick the 2-5 levers that attack it. 3. Implement all of them fast, and break the beliefs that say "channel X doesn't work." 4. Track clean metrics. 5. Pitch by starting from what the buyer or investor wants.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Splitting focus across several offers with a small team.
- Discounting instead of giving away something big.
- Competing on hourly price.
- Unstructured pitches that ignore the audience's goals.
- "We've always done it this way" beliefs, such as assuming patients won't pay upfront.

## Examples / case details (contestant-reported)
- Tina (autism/ADHD parent coaching): 13 weekly webinars, 232 enrollments vs 75 before; run rate $918K → $1.84M. Dropped a ~$150 option for a $2,500 six-month program.
- Dr. Zad (concierge medicine): country-club events via existing patients at almost no CAC; run rate ~$4M → ~$4.5M. Eliminated.
- Mim (sticker-making courses): went from ~$850-900K to $1.76M projected after more ads and one annual offer. Eliminated despite ~$800K of annualized growth.
- Nas (moving company): flat rates, biggest move ever at $15K; $360K → $1.03M on track. Eliminated.
- Paul (Boulder adventure park): had put his 401k in; a giveaway brought in 3,700+ leads but conversion struggled. Run rate $4.4M → $4M. Eliminated first.
- Caleb (roofing, 6 weeks old at the start): repair ticket target raised from $2,500 to $5K; hired a repair tech; recruited salespeople with $1M+ prior production. Says $370K → $2M. Eliminated on adherence.
- Hussi (dental, 3 locations): hired 2 associates; collected cash upfront; closed a $70K case paid upfront; run rate $1.73M → just over $4M. Not running VSLs yet. Says he posts 100x/day.
- Joey (Utopia Experience, events; winner): a $15K sponsorship produced $670K closed. Alex then cites "just under 3 million" at a "200x return," apparently counting the pipeline [inference]. Multi-year contracts took average contract price from $44K to $200K (~4.5x) while close rate went from 44% to 48%. Revenue $1.3M (2025) → $4.1M pace, plus $2M in pipeline. Pros: LTV:CAC, recurring revenue, $4M already booked for next year, stayed through COVID by pivoting to virtual production. Cons: lower margin and cash flow. The vote was 2-1 (Alex was the deciding vote).
