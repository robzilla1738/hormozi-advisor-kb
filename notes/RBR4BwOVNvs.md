## Core thesis
After selling his businesses, cars and Austin house, he lived out of one bag in high-end rentals for ~9 months. He found that he overcorrected: no home base, no familiar stuff and no social circle was worse. He landed on "function over form" as a unifying rule for spending (he misspeaks as "form over function" before correcting himself). He recommends pre-made decision algorithms to save bandwidth. This is mostly a personal lifestyle video with little business content.

## Frameworks & models
- **Life in 3–5 year seasons**: college/consulting → running gyms → licensing gyms plus three bigger companies (sold) → now investing in service and e-learning businesses with ~$1–10M profit.
- **Location checklist**:
  - Within walking distance: nature, restaurants, a great gym, a grocery store.
  - The home: an ocean/lake/mountain view, fast Wi-Fi, hot tub/pool, office/studio, 3+ bedrooms, big balcony.
  - The area: Amazon Prime reach, a zero-tax state, ultra-wealthy neighbors, an entrepreneur scene, a central airport with direct flights, lock-it-and-leave-it flexibility, a market that attracts talent, an office within 15 minutes, weather.
- **Submarkets over cities**: ~90% of life happens within walkable distance. Judgments about a city rest on a few people and places, and high-end submarkets look alike. (He visited 33 cities in 18 months earlier on.)
- **Tax as tiebreaker**: if there's no marginal difference in living, pick the lower-tax option. This matches his other videos on not deciding by tax alone.
- **Relative cost**: at $10M/yr vs a ~$50K family income (200×), a $10K private flight "feels like" $50 (math checks). Flying private turns travel from a negative into a positive experience: no lost recovery days, and more willingness to travel.
- **Function over form**: pay up when it improves function (well-fitting flannels), go cheap when cheap works better (Amazon 5-pack cotton tanks).
- **One-in-one-out wardrobe**: the closet stays the same size while utility rises. When something fits, buy it in ~6 colors.
- **Decision algorithms**: if-then rules made in advance, so bandwidth goes to new decisions instead of remaking old ones.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Prefer a home base (eventually 2–3 homes in different climates and vibes, stocked with your stuff) over pure nomadism.
- Don't buy status items. The Bentley Flying Spur (~$300K+) earned better service but wasn't worth it.
- Work without windows if they distract you (he records in a closet).

## Playbook steps
1. Write requirements before choosing where to live.
2. Judge by submarket and walkability.
3. Adopt a function-based buying rule and a one-in-one-out rule.
4. Codify recurring choices as if-then algorithms.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Overcorrecting from too much stuff to nothing.
- Judging cities from a weekend.
- Expecting novelty to last (hedonic adaptation). He never once swapped his pooled-lease car, despite paying ~$1K/mo for the option.

## Examples / case details
- He says he sold a $12M business, two businesses for just under $50M, two exotic cars and a $4M Austin house.
- Portfolio at the time: ~$85M/yr. Says he has taken home over $1M/month for the last half decade.
- Rentals ran ~$50–100K/month (Cabo, Scottsdale, Flagstaff, Bear Lake, Vegas, SoCal). Cabo oceanfront mansions are ~$5M vs ~$20M in the US.
- Moved to remote work over the past year. Bought an 8-figure mountaintop home.
