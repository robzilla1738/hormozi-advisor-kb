## Core thesis
Teardown of Corey's ProShine (HVAC/duct cleaning, ~$1.25M trailing 12 months, $479K profit, 38% net margin, $60K debt). There's "nothing majorly wrong," so it's a game of incremental improvement ("no Hail Mary's, consistent yardage"). The order: raise price → pay off debt → fix the landing page → fix ads → scale spend → retargeting + branded terms → reactivation emails → affiliate offer / outbound HOA events. One year later: on pace for $2.3–2.5M, leads ~120/mo → ~200/mo and higher quality.

## Frameworks & models
- **Supply- vs demand-constrained**: if you doubled leads, could you handle them? If yes, it's demand-constrained, so add leads. If not, build capacity first. Don't fix a problem in a way that makes the existing problem worse.
- **Raising price can raise close rate** (non-Veblen case): a higher price raises perceived likelihood of achievement (value equation). Virtuous cycle of pricing ($100M Offers p.48): more emotional investment, perceived value, results, less demanding customers, more money to deliver.
- **Funnel step loss**: each added step typically loses ~50% or more. Ads should go to a standalone conversion lander, not the home page.
- **ROAS logic**: "If for every dollar you gave me, I gave you $5 back," you'd give as many dollars as possible. If you're not sure of the return, that's a data/attribution problem.
- **Seasonal pain cycles**: one or two seasonal peaks, messaged pre/during/post, give year-round coverage (gym: New Year's and summer; HVAC: pollen in April, mildew when heat kicks on). Run one theme across emails, ads and PPC for ~2 months.
- **Reactivation as mini-campaigns**: about monthly, each a 2–3 email sequence for a segment. Angles: "I owe you $175 / a free checkup" or "we messed up, can we make it up to you?"; energy savings with before/after bills; allergies; case studies as follow-ups.
- **Affiliate lead-magnet upsell** ($100M Leads p.223, versions on pp.237–239; he uses version two): partner sells the $175 low-ticket, high-margin service and keeps 100% of the money; you deliver it and upsell your core offer. CAC = delivery cost ÷ upsell conversion rate. Example: $25 cost at 1 in 3 = $75. With Corey's real ~$100 cost at a conservative 50% = $200 CAC, "who cares" on a $3–4K job.
- **Reciprocal referral limit**: "Referrals everyone promises, no one delivers." Swapping referrals doesn't scale, because you can't feed 20 partners enough business. Partners need a real offer.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- A close rate above ~80% means there's room on price.
- Proposed +10% price (~$1,650/unit): it pays as long as close rate stays above 65%. He estimates ~+25% net profit (~$100K+/yr at last year's volume). Holding revenue flat alone would need ~74.5% close (82%/1.1); his 65% line presumably counts the lower delivery cost of fewer jobs.]
- Sub-$3–5M businesses: charging more is often among the biggest levers.
- Debt: he leans Dave Ramsey. Debt increases risk, and risk over a long horizon "tends to come due" (Chick-fil-A runs debt-free). Pay it off.
- Keep raising spend month over month while ROAS/CAC hold. 13:1 upfront ROAS on an unoptimized page means underspending; it could go from $5K to ~$30K/mo.
- Order: fix the funnel and ads before raising spend. That alone may 2–3× leads and create a supply constraint.
- Bid on your own branded terms even if "they'd find you anyway" ("get bigger problems"); otherwise competitors scoop them.
- Pick ONE of affiliate partners or outbound events, not both. Each needs a full-time owner.

## Playbook steps
1. Raise price 10%. 2. Keep paying off debt. 3. Build a standalone ad lander: tiny logo/hamburger menu, headline above the fold, offer ("Free 18-point inspection"), form, phone, locations, FAQs; point all site buttons to it. 4. Ads: text the ad to your phone to judge size; lead with the offer, less text, put the location in the headline, run ~40 color/image variations (customers smiling by their unit); take best organic posts and add a 5-second CTA (cheapest testing = post organic first). 5. Increase spend. 6. Cross-platform retargeting + own branded search terms. 7. Reactivation email mini-campaigns. 8. Outbound to HOAs (get their event calendar, book the next event at each event, aim for 1–2/week) or affiliate offer.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Sending ads to a home page with nav and clutter; designing ads on desktop.
- Emotional fear of high ad spend when the metrics are known.
- Underpricing so far that you look like a "seedy duct tape operation."
- Repeating identical content (looks cheap).
- Relying on "I'll send you business" referral swaps.

## Examples / case details
- Pricing: $1,575/unit (~2 units/house), 2-year growth-free guarantee, $175 dryer vent cleaning; customer portal with photos (useful when selling the home).
- Channels: ~60% paid (Google $3K→$5K/mo, Facebook $650), 15% local SEO, plus affiliates. One partner sends ~$30K/mo; Corey sends back ~$40–45K of higher-ticket replacement work.
- 99% show rate, 82% close rate, ~$7,200/mo marketing. An earlier +23% price increase raised both close rate and revenue.
- Renaming "free assessment" to "18-point inspection" performed better. One event booked 55 inspections.
- His projections: landing page ~2×, ads/organic 2–3×, retargeting ~10–20%, reactivation 20–30% ("all profit," compounds).
- Scaling roadmap stage 5 "Productize" (10–19 staff). Includes a plug for acquisition.com/roadmap.
