## Core thesis
"F*** end of week." He says top-1% entrepreneurs differ in how fast they execute after a decision. Power is the gap between a thought and reality, so shortening the OODA loop (observe-orient-decide-act) means more iterations and faster growth. The owner sets the organization's cadence: replace "end of week" with "end of day" or "in two hours." (Older Gym Launch-era recording, re-released; portfolio cited at ~$85M/yr.)

## Frameworks & models
- **Power = gap between thoughts becoming reality**: the closer to instant, the more potent you are.
- **OODA loop speed**: faster loops let you pivot, seize opportunities, and find failures sooner. A competitor with a faster loop beats one who was first to market.
- **"Not how old the car is but how many miles are on it"**: it's the number of iterations that matters, not years.
- **End of day vs end of week = ~7× faster org**. A 10am → noon deadline lets the loop run three times a day instead of over three weeks.
- **Speed ≠ rushing**: "speed isn't doing things fast it's basically just not being distracted by [stuff] that doesn't matter." It comes down to prioritization.
- **Follow the tested playbook** rather than turning motivation into your own untested experiments.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- When someone says "end of week," ask: "What are you doing today/in the next two hours that's more important than this?" If there's no real answer, do it now.
- Estimate the real hours a task needs, find a slot today, and start at the moment of decision.
- Measure your business by time-to-improve, for example how long from knowing churn is high to running the "five horsemen" retention fixes.

## Playbook steps
1. Set deadlines in hours or end of day, not end of week. 2. Push back on excuses and require a real priority reason. 3. Help people re-prioritize toward what drives revenue and retention. 4. Look at your schedule, identify the bottlenecks, and start them now. 5. Repeat. Growth is "a million small turns."

## Mistakes he warns against
- Procrastinating "for no reason when the problem will not go away."
- Consuming irrelevant education or motivation instead of implementing the proven playbook.
- A long delay between getting information and acting on it, which he saw among lower-revenue owners.

## Examples / case details
- He sent books to friends doing $5–15M. All read them immediately and sent back notes and action plans.
- Joey, the top gym in Gym Launch, implemented every Legacy system within his first 8 weeks.
