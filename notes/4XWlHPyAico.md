## Core thesis
The three biggest growth levers, in order of power: market > offer > persuasion (marketing/sales). A bad market beats a great entrepreneur. Check them top down. Offer is the easiest and fastest to fix; persuasion is the hardest and slowest to build.

## Frameworks & models
- **Three levers, top down**: 1) Market, 2) Offer, 3) Persuasion. Rate each as plus / neutral / negative.
- **Offer definition**: what you give the customer in exchange for money, plus the terms, price, and what they must do to redeem it.
- **Grand slam offer**: rare and very good; unlike baseball (max 4 runs), in business it can "win for life."
- **Persuasion**: learning to market and sell, and acquire customers through multiple channels.
- **Self-rating**: market great/normal/shrinking; offer compelling/normal/bad; persuasion "clients at the snap of my fingers" / semi-consistent / "no idea what I'm doing."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- As long as the market isn't negative (neutral or plus), move on to the offer.
- In a normal market with a normal offer, the only path to hyper-success is building persuasion skill.
- A great market (toilet paper during COVID, hot dogs outside the stadium after the game) makes money regardless of offer or persuasion.
- Most businesses (dentists, chiropractors, people in pain) are in normal markets: not growing, not shrinking.
- Sequence for most entrepreneurs: fix the offer first → develop persuasion → then pick a market where you can apply both and "crush it."

## Playbook steps
1. Confirm you're not in a shrinking market.
2. Audit the current offer: what you give, the terms, the price point.
3. Repackage, reposition, rename, and change the terms into a grand slam offer (about half a day to a day).
4. Build marketing and sales skill (takes volume, practice, failure).
5. Then choose a better market for those skills.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Examining every part of the business except the market itself.
- Believing a great offer or great salesmanship can outpower a bad market.

## Examples / case details
- Friend Lloyd's tech company sold an ad product exclusively to newspapers. He had a great zero-risk rev-share offer and was a great salesman, but it plateaued and declined because the newspaper market was shrinking ~25% a year over the 8 years he had the business.

## Notable lines
- "If you put a great entrepreneur in a bad market or a bad entrepreneur in a great market, the market will usually win."
- "You will not be able to outpower a bad market."
- "Overnight you can take a normal business to an extraordinary business by doing this one thing."
