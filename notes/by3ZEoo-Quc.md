# How to Buy Anything You Want Without Going Broke
id: by3ZEoo-Quc / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=by3ZEoo-Quc / duration: 609s (~10 min) / tags: 10-money-cashflow, 12-mindset-leverage, 08-ops-scaling

## Core thesis
There are four sources of money for a purchase. The wealthiest people he knows buy discretionary or "grandiose" things with **new money**: income generated specifically for that purchase from existing, underused resources. They don't touch savings or normal income, and they avoid debt. This lets them enjoy spending without draining their base, and the specific goal motivates the extra work.

## Frameworks & models
- **Four ways to buy anything**:
  - Past money: savings.
  - Income money: monthly earnings.
  - Debt money: future earnings.
  - New money: extra income created for this purchase.
- **"Sawdust money"**: use your existing resources that are currently underutilized (time, space, skills, clients) to generate new money for a specific project.
- **"Write yourself a swimming pool"**: Paul McCartney story, via Chiron Cervatza. He had the money but wrote a song to pay for the pool instead.
- **Create a vacuum / "make yourself poor"**: a defined purchase raises your demand for money and makes you resourceful. Real wealth comes from raising income while keeping your baseline lifestyle low.
- **Take the delta**: choosing between a sensible and a sweeter option (office, machine)? Commit to earning the difference separately.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Debt is acceptable only if you will pay it off within a year, with no prepayment penalty and a clear plan.
- A bigger office was OK for a client if he committed to paying it off in a year (about 2.5× the price of the sensible option).
- Don't raise your lifestyle relative to income. Raise new money relative to each new purchase.
- New-money efforts can be time-boxed (overtime for 1 month / 6 months / 1 year).
- If you're broke, skip the splurge and focus on new money only.
- If money itself is your goal, don't spend it. If it isn't, use money as a tool.

## Playbook steps
1. Name the specific thing you want. 2. Don't use savings, income flows, or open-ended debt. 3. List underused assets (time, spare room/Airbnb, building, skills, one-off client or 1:1 projects, Uber shifts). 4. Set a defined period to earn it. 5. Buy it once it's funded; the purchase becomes guilt-free and low-regret.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Funding consumption from your nest egg or core income.
- Taking on liabilities for things that won't pay off.
- Having money but never enjoying it out of fear or scarcity.

## Examples / case details
- His 36,000 sq ft building. Leila suggested 5,000 sq ft. The rationale was that portfolio companies spent about $4M/yr on event spaces. He promised it would make more than it cost, and the advisory services were spun up partly to use the building to cover its cost (plus a home gym).
- A gym client, a single mom of four, drove Uber one day a week to afford the membership. She became a long-term member and lost 100 lbs.
- Example: an employee works one extra day a week for a year to buy a boat or car.
