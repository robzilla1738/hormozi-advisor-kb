# Don't Start a Business Until You Watch This...

## Core thesis
Alex's biggest shift of 2020: think like an investor/shareholder on a ~5-year horizon, not a CEO on weekly/quarterly targets. When you extend the horizon, time stops being a threat and becomes an asset that compounds for you ("fast vs. inevitable"). Practically, this led him to invest in slow inbound channels and to manage growth by one leading indicator: sales-qualified applications.

## Frameworks & models
- **Fast vs. inevitable**: choose repeated actions that, over a long enough horizon, make the goal inevitable.
- **CEO → investor/board-member mindset**: monthly fluctuations look like stock-market noise; over years the line reflects true economic value.
- **Time as asset vs. liability.**
- **Leading indicator of growth**: "what non-revenue thing, if I tripled it, would make it unreasonable for the company not to triple?" For him: sales-accepted / sales-qualified leads (showed up, qualified, right business size, interested). He already had a churn leading indicator (customer activation) but never one for growth.
- **Three acquisition buckets**: manual outbound (cold call/email/DM), paid ads (still "outbound"), and inbound/pull "water hole" (SEO, content) built over years.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Use a 5-year horizon (a mentor who sold for ~$3.4B told him to use 100 years; 5 is the one he can believe).
- Ask: "if I did this consistently for 5 years, would it be unreasonable not to have built a reliable channel?" If yes, invest.
- Target +5%/month in qualified applications rather than +5%/month in sales — fewer short-term variables.
- Fund small-but-reliable inflows (e.g., ~20 new clients/month) rather than rejecting projects that won't double lead gen.
- For long-horizon initiatives, start now but don't measure ROI until next year.

## Playbook steps
1. Pick a 5-year goal (e.g., triple the company).
2. Find the leading indicator that drives it (qualified applications).
3. Drive it with consistent activity: more outreach people, more content channels, more ad platforms.
4. Start compounding inbound assets even if they won't pay off for 12+ months.
5. Judge initiatives on long horizons so teams don't quit after a week.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Using an early outlier result as the benchmark (his ~$20M-profit second year "ruined" him).
- Comparing to peers on short horizons.
- Only urgent, short-term work; skipping important long-term investments.
- Killing initiatives after a week of no results.
- Tying ego to monthly fluctuations.

## Examples / case details
- Others' 5-year inbound asset generating ~3,000 applications/month for their software.
- Gym Launch inbound initiative expected to yield ~20 new clients/month.
- 2020 profit roughly halved: ~$13.4M prior year to ~$6M (as he states, books not final) due to pandemic hitting gym clients; he expected recovery within ~3 years.
