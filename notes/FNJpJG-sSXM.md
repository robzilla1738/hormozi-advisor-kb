## Core thesis
Alex reacts to the Biden administration's proposed tax plan (circa 2021, when ACQ was ~$100M/yr). He considers the 20% minimum tax including unrealized gains the scariest provision, because it taxes founders on value they haven't realized and kills the incentive behind the American dream. Practical takeaways are limited: weigh C-corp vs LLC by how you redeploy capital, consider timing equity realization around expected tax changes, and don't let taxes distract from building equity. This is a dated political/tax commentary, and his reading of the proposals was explicitly broad-brush and unverified ("I couldn't find" the formula).

## Frameworks & models
- **Proposal list as he summarized it**: top rate to 39.6%; capital gains and qualified dividends taxed at ordinary rates for income over $1M; gifts and transfers at death taxed as dispositions; corporate rate to 28%; buyback tax; 20% minimum tax including unrealized gains; carried interest taxed as income; 1031 deferral capped at $500K per taxpayer; limits on easement deductions, donor-advised funds and generation-skipping trusts.
- **C-corp vs LLC**: a C-corp makes sense if you keep retained earnings to redeploy (M&A, capex) to grow enterprise value. Taking dividends means double tax (28% corporate, then ~40% personal). For most small businesses with little to redeploy besides M&A, an LLC usually works better. Have your accounting firm run a side-by-side.
- **Wealth is built in equity growth**, not income. Don't waste excessive time on tax versus building the business.
- **His unrealized-gains example**: a company with $20M EBITDA at 10× = $200M grows to $25M EBITDA (= $250M). 20% on the $50M gain = $10M, plus 40% on $25M income = $10M, so $20M of $25M in tax is an 80% effective rate (math checks). Second example: $100M net worth, 15% appreciation ($15M) gives a $3M minimum tax vs $2M income taxed $0.8M, leaving $1.2M against $3M owed. [inference: his "$1.8M every year" shortfall assumes paying the full $3M in one year; he also notes the proposal spread it over 9 years, which he argues still stacks up.] If the company later fails, you paid tax on gains you never got.
- **Private sector allocates capital ~10× better than government** (his claim).
- **His "unpopular" alternatives**: (1) ~0% income tax with a very high capital gains tax; (2) low income and capital gains taxes plus a ~90–100% death tax on transfers down generations, so the wealthy give their money away privately while alive.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you expect capital gains tax increases, consider realizing equity before they take effect. He says ACQ exited three companies that year partly for this reason.
- With large unrealized gains and pending tax changes, consider not realizing them and waiting.
- You'll still invest despite higher taxes, since there's no better alternative.

## Playbook steps
1. Model your entity choice against how you'll redeploy capital.
2. Factor expected tax changes into exit timing.
3. Then return focus to growing enterprise value.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Taxing unrealized gains, which forces founders to sell or borrow against equity.
- Spending more time on tax minimization than on building the business.
