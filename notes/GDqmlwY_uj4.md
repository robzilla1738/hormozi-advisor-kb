## Core thesis
Strategy = the one or two big decisions that set your next several years, and the biggest lever is the *opportunity vehicle* itself. Each step up in vehicle leverage added "about a zero per month" to his income (4 → 5 → 6 → 7 figures/month). Many skilled but stuck entrepreneurs keep reinventing their current business when the problem is the opportunity, not the model. Focus hard, but after years of no real progress, use discernment to switch to a better vehicle.

## Frameworks & models
- **Schwarzman's (Blackstone) 3 criteria** as Alex recounts them: (1) potential to be really big; (2) can be run by 10/10 talent other than him, probably better than him; (3) makes economic sense.
- **Alex's 3 variables for "really big opportunity"** (also used for acquisition.com investments):
  1. Potential number of units sellable at scale (TAM).
  2. Potential profit per unit at scale: value provided, price sensitivity, cost of goods at scale (contribution margin per extra unit can be high even with big upfront costs, e.g. a factory).
  3. Supply/demand economics in the market: is demand vs. entrenched supply in your favor as a new entrant?
- Earlier version: leverage of market × leverage of deliverable.
- **Quitting = changing your mind with increasing data**; ability to change your mind is a sign of intelligence.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Avoid huge markets with entrenched supply (e.g. competing with Verizon in telecom) even when TAM and per-unit profit look great.
- Favorable supply/demand comes from (a) niching into a sliver with less competition, or (b) a market where demand grows faster than entrants (e.g. demand +20%/yr vs competitors +10%/yr).
- Focus on one thing; pursuing multiple paths makes success at any of them very unlikely.
- If 1, 2, 5 years in with no significant progress (one step forward, two back), ask whether this is the best vehicle for your skills, traits, beliefs; don't leave entrepreneurship, change the vehicle ("wrapping paper").
- When switching, don't run a bridge doing both at once: "neither of them will work." Decide when to cut bait.
- Weigh against compounding: the second 5 years usually returns far more than the first.

## Playbook steps
1. Score current vehicle on TAM, profit/unit at scale, supply/demand. 2. Score alternatives the same way. 3. If stuck for years and a clearly superior vehicle exists for your skills/access, plan a clean switch rather than a parallel effort. 4. Otherwise stay focused and persistent.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Stubborn persistence in a weak vehicle to "figure it out."
- Reinventing the wheel inside the business when the opportunity is the problem.
- Pursuing several paths at once; straddling old and new.
- Reading biographers instead of billionaires' own accounts of how they choose opportunities.
