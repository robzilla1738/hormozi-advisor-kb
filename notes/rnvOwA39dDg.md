# How To Retire Early (and be in the top 1%)

## Core thesis
The top 1% by assets is reachable through spending discipline and time, not a huge income. People in the top 1% by income often aren't in the top 1% by assets because they spend everything. The percentage of income you spend, not the amount you make, sets how fast you can retire. Alex says he and Leila have lived on under 5% of income since they started making money (about $30K/yr until income passed $1M). Low spending also lowers anxiety and keeps "dry powder." This is a personal-finance video from the early Gym Launch era.

## Frameworks & models
- **Wealth pyramid (US, investable assets, his figures)**: high net worth $1–5M ≈ 1 in 69 (~1.5%); very high net worth $5–30M ≈ 0.3% (1 in 338); ultra-high net worth $30M–$1B ≈ 0.07% (the caption's "1 in 13 83" is garbled; 0.07% ≈ 1 in ~1,400); billionaires ≈ 1 in 468,000. He says top-1% income is ~$400–440K/yr while top-1% assets are ~$1–1.5M, so "people spend all their money."
- **Relative cost**: judge purchases against income. His Bentley cost less than a week's income at the time (he regrets it anyway).
- **Live on ≤5% of income**: when they had their first $100K, he told Leila it was 3 years of living at ~$1,500/mo each.
- **Simplest retirement calculator**: years of freedom bought per working year = savings ÷ annual spending. Earn $100K and spend $20K, saving $80K: 4 years of runway per year, ~$800K after 10 years before returns. Earn $400K and save only 10%: about 1/9 of a year per year, so they may never retire.
- **Live on 10%, earn 10% returns**: about one year of saving funds your spending indefinitely (roughly checks: 0.9 × 10% ≈ 9% of income vs 10% spending).
- **Spending vs anxiety**: lower expenses equal lower anxiety and more resilience to sudden change (he references the prior year, i.e. COVID).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Spend under 5–10% of income if you can. Most people should live on $30–40K.
- Upgrade lifestyle only after crossing wealth tiers, and only when the cost is negligible relative to income (his studio).
- If you'd rather spend, fine, but then "don't yearn for more."
- Aim for $100K/yr first before chasing $1M/yr.

## Playbook steps
1. Track spending as a percentage of income.
2. Cut spending to a fixed modest level regardless of income growth.
3. Invest the surplus monthly in the S&P 500.
4. Keep large cash reserves.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Instagram flexing (Lamborghinis, Bentleys); lifestyle creep after early success (a former gym-owner client's spending worried him).
- Believing more spending brings more happiness.
