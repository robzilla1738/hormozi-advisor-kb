## Core thesis
"Seven investing concepts" (plus a bonus) for judging any business with only addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division: LTGP, CAC, the LTGP:CAC ratio, return on invested capital, payback period, sales velocity × LTGP (hypothetical max revenue), sales velocity ÷ churn (hypothetical max customers), and TAM × LTGP ÷ risk. LTGP:CAC is "the fundamental economic unit of the business." All profit comes from this ratio. If it's bad, he tosses the business out without looking further.

## Frameworks & models
- **Lifetime**: purchases per customer (e.g., Shopify's "buys 4.3 times") or months retained. Back of the napkin: of the customers at the start of the month, how many remain 30 days later (ignore new sales). 100 → 90 = 10% churn; lifespan = 1/churn = 10 intervals.
- **Gross profit** = price − cost of delivery. Service example: an employee costs $4K/mo and handles 10 customers paying [caption says "$11,000"; the math uses $1,000] → $10K − $4K = $6K/mo (60% gross margin) → $600/customer/mo × 10 months = **$6,000 LTGP**.
- **Use LTGP, not LTV/revenue**: pen sells for $10, costs $9, 10 purchases. "Spending $50 to get a $100 sale" is actually spending $50 to make $10 of lifetime gross profit. The LTV habit comes from Silicon Valley software, where margins are ~100%.
- **CAC = all acquisition cost (labor + software + ads) ÷ customers acquired**: $50K labor + $50K ads/software = $100K ÷ 83 customers ≈ $1.2K (he says $1,224; [inference: 100,000 ÷ 83 ≈ $1,205]). $6,000 / ~$1.2K ≈ **5:1**.
- **ROIC at the location/machine level**: total cost to open a node (buildout, launch marketing, hiring, recruiting). Track payback, one-year return, and profit at capacity. Example: $100K in, breakeven at month 6, pacing $250K/yr at month 12, $500K/yr by year 2 → open five more.
- **Payback period levers** (pull cash forward): (1) first + last month up front; (2) mandatory onboarding/enrollment/setup fee; (3) optional upsell; (4) third-party financing (give the risk away for a fee). Example: CAC $1,200 with $600/mo takes 2 months to break even. Add a $600 fee and you break even on day one, +$600 after month 2 → "more resilient in a downturn."
- **Hypothetical max revenue = sales velocity × LTGP (or LTV)**: 100/mo × $10K LTV = $1M/month at steady state. A business doing $500K/mo with these metrics will double without changing anything. One doing $2M/mo will shrink to $1M (usually because delivery dropped and churn rose).
- **Hypothetical max customers = sales velocity ÷ churn**: 100 ÷ 10% = 1,000 customers. Use it to plan headcount (cut if built for 2,000; scale ahead if heading from 500 to 1,000).
- **Bonus: opportunity size = TAM (potential units) × LTGP ÷ risk**. Risk on units: trend/tailwinds (e.g., fertility), platform dependence, key-man. Risk on LTGP: competitors undercutting, strength of position/premium, economies vs shrinking margins at scale.
- **Knowable × important (Buffett/Munger)**: TAM is important but not very knowable, because companies expand their niche (Amazon books → everything; Facebook college → everyone). Focus on knowable risks.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- LTGP:CAC must be **> 3:1**, and 5:1 is great. Below that you're too constrained to scale. Exceptional ratios can excuse weaker metrics elsewhere.
- Active investments (your time): want at least 3:1 and ideally 5:1 annual return on capital (≈300%+/yr) vs ~10% for passive stock market returns, because time and headspace have an opportunity cost.
- Brick and mortar: overspend on opening to fill capacity fast (e.g., $40K launch budget instead of $10K).
- Average churn/LTV over trailing 90 days / 6 months / 12 months. Daily numbers are too volatile for small businesses.
- Churn 5% → 10% halves lifetime revenue. 10% → 5% doubles it.
- Prefer service businesses for low capex (expand by hiring).

## Playbook steps
1. Compute churn and lifespan. 2. Compute gross profit per customer → LTGP. 3. Compute fully loaded CAC → ratio (≥3:1). 4. ROIC/payback per new location or machine. 5. Shorten payback with up-front fees, upsells, financing. 6. Project the hypothetical max (revenue and customers) and compare to current. 7. Size the opportunity and discount for risk. 8. Keep one sheet that tracks these automatically every month.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Measuring ad spend vs lifetime revenue instead of gross profit.
- CAC that excludes labor.
- Not knowing these numbers in a business you own or invest in.
- Expanding from 5 to 20 locations without a new-market launch strategy.
