# My 4-Part Investing Model (with real example)

## Core thesis
Judge every investment through four lenses, each scored by degree rather than yes/no: capital preservation, yield, tax advantage and equity growth. A "wealth advisor" pitch (startup debt at 10%) fails on every lens. His own $2.5M short-term bridge loan on a foreclosure building, at 4% per month with a minimum carry, passes because the downside is heavily protected and the yield is high enough to beat ordinary-income taxes.

## Frameworks & models
- **Four lenses of investing**:
  1. **Yield**: monthly cash flow.
  2. **Capital preservation**: how likely the money comes back.
  3. **Tax advantage**: capital gains vs ordinary income.
  4. **Equity growth**: whether the asset appreciates.
- **Lending asymmetry**: when you lend money, "all you get is guaranteed cash and you participate on the downside," so the downside protection has to be excellent.
- **Benchmark against the index**: compare a deal to the S&P (he guessed ~20% "last year"), which offers liquidity and upside.
- **Hard money / bridge lending**: borrowers pay high rates for speed and liquidity when they've found a steal they must close fast.
- **Minimum carry**: interest is charged in whole months with a floor, so early repayment still pays.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Startup debt at 10% for 12 months, paid as a lump sum at the end: low capital preservation, no upside, and taxed as income. 10% × (1−45% tax) = 5.5% effective, "barely enough to cover inflation." Run from it, and question the ethics of whoever pitched it.
- Ordinary-income yield deals need a very high rate for top-bracket earners. His 4%/month note is ~27% after tax for him [inference: 48%/yr × ~0.55 ≈ 26%], which "still makes sense."
- Capital protection checklist used: independent offers well above the loan (four offers at $5.1–5.6M on a $2.5M purchase), a top buyer with $300K earnest money, and personal guarantees from both borrowers.
- Better deal flow comes from doing more deals: people learn you have capital and bring you more.

## Playbook steps
1. Score the deal on all four lenses. 2. Verify the exit or collateral value with real documents (he had the offers sent over). 3. Stack protections (earnest-money buyer, backup offers, personal guarantees). 4. Price the yield for your tax bracket. 5. Add a minimum carry (2 months) and charge in whole-month increments (90-day term; day 65 rolls into month 3).

## Mistakes he warns against
- Lending to startups with a balloon repayment and no upside.
- Ignoring taxes on interest income.
- Trusting advisors who pitch bad deals: "either they absolutely have no idea what they're doing or they're expecting that you have no idea."

## Examples / case details
- He wrote a $2.5M note to secure a building out of foreclosure. The borrower's planned hold was 60 days and he allowed 90. 4%/month = $100K/month. The 2-month minimum carry means at least $200K even if repaid in a day; about 65 days → $300K. The borrower makes roughly $2.1M minus fees if it sells at $5.6M. The rate was stated as "48 to 96 per year" [caption unclear; 4%/mo = 48%/yr].
- A portfolio company CEO's advisor pitch: $450K to a startup at 10% paid at month 12 ($495K total).
