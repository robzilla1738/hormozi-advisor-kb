## Core thesis
Wealth comes in three layers. (1) Global: rich countries invest in **education + technology**, which raise output per unit of time. (2) Individual: production comes from three skills, **Build, Sell, Lead (BSL)**, all **multiplied by Risk** taken on. (3) Environmental: the money BSL creates gets wasted, stored, or **multiplied** by buying assets or lending. The top 0.1% max all three skills, keep their risk and upside, and reinvest. The best investment is buying the skill that's currently your constraint.

## Frameworks & models
- **Inputs everyone has are equal: time and bodies.** Technology lets you (a) do more of what you do, (b) do it better, and (c) lowers the skill needed to do it. Everything traces back to output per unit of time.
- **BSL × R**: Build (mechanical), Sell (verbal), Lead (social), multiplied by Risk. Each skill is a lever with degrees, not binary. Build = many people use it once it's made; Sell = reach many at once; Lead = many people act for you.
- **Tripod of business**: you need all three legs. Sell + lead without build = "lying at scale" (Wolf of Wall Street). Build without sell = a product in the garage nobody knows about. Lead without build/sell = people organized around nothing. Lead is the meta-skill: it gets you access to everyone else's skills.
- **Build tactics** (from $100M Offers): trim & stack; the **delivery cube** (personal attention, effort expected, environment/medium, consumption, speed of response, plus 10× price and 1/10 price versions); **psychological vs logical solutions** (Rory Sutherland's elevator mirrors); the **problem→solution→new problem cycle** (pretzel → drink → cookie) leads to an ecosystem (Apple).
- **Sell = everything from unaware to paying** (advertising, marketing, sales, branding, promotion, conversion). The **call out** matters most (Ogilvy: the headline spends 80 cents of your ad dollar). Framing: intros set perception. Cocktail-party examples: dropped tray (everyone), "mom" (all moms), your name (you).
- **Risk multiplier examples**: Jobs' 15% of Apple at IPO would be worth ~$465B; Gates' ~49% of Microsoft would be ~$1.5T. Selling and diversifying lowered their risk and their upside.
- **What money can do**: waste, store (gold, Buffett's ball of gold vs all US farmland + ~13 Exxon Mobils), or multiply (buy or lend). Lending = selling money for more money later; buying = owning a % of others' BSL.
- **Three places to invest, by risk**: a piece of a company (highest), hiring people (middle), yourself/skills (lowest risk, best return).
- **Buying a skill** = paying someone for feedback on an activity until you can reproduce their outcome without them.
- **Constraint diagnosis**: the business is limited by product (build), not enough people coming in (sell), or inability to scale due to people (lead). Fix the binding one.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Every new hire must sell more, make the product worth more, or support those things; otherwise waste. "Every new hire is a guaranteed cost but not a guaranteed return."
- Start narrow, go broad (Facebook: college → high school → adults). A specific call out converts a higher percentage of the audience.
- Starting from scratch: figure out what to sell, then learn to sell it. Logo/branding/website are neither, unless websites are what you sell.
- Communication that changes no behavior is waste. Ask "what do you want to have happen?" and "what should we start/stop doing?"
- Research/measurement that doesn't change what you do isn't needed.
- Always spend as much as you can buying the most valuable skill that constrains your growth.
- If you don't understand what someone is saying, "don't assume that you're dumb, assume that no one knows what's going on." Seek clarity over agreement.

## Playbook steps
1. Find your constraint among build / sell / lead.
2. Invest education and technology into that pillar (AI coding tools, ads/personal brand for one-to-many selling, project management/predictive hiring for leading).
3. To sell: get attention → get contact info → explain value → deliver. Ask customers what they want; mine competitors' bad reviews for pain points.
4. To lead: strip wasted communication; define behavior to start/stop; shrink roles to the actions that drive economic activity, then do more, faster, or better.
5. Reinvest profits: back into the company, into acquisitions (Acquisition.com model, compounding at a %), into people, and most of all into yourself.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Obsessing over logo/branding/website before selling.
- Meetings that are "noise at each other for 30 minutes" with no behavior change; corporate jargon.
- Hires who don't build, sell, or support those.
- Not using technology; he says his own hesitation with it has limited his success.
- Storing money (gold) rather than multiplying it; giving up upside by selling equity/diversifying (framed as a trade-off).

## Examples / case details
- Luxembourg highest GDP per capita (~$151K); Singapore second (~$148K), where teachers are prestigious and well paid.
- Zuckerberg: started mostly build, added promotion inside the product, then developed leadership.
- Jobs "didn't build the iPhone, he built the team that built the iPhone."
- Director who wanted to "bridge the gap" couldn't say what behavior should change; an employee's research changed nothing, so the role was narrowed.
- First business: gave out email and phone, emailed personalized nutrition/workout plans; no CRM, no website.
- Family fled Iran during the revolution and had their assets seized; relatives with skills rebuilt, those with only assets struggled.
