# Leila Hormozi's Brutally Honest Advice About Relationships

**Speaker: Leila Hormozi (not Alex), interviewed on the Acquisitioncom channel.** Treat these as Leila's views, not Alex's.

## Core thesis
Many struggling couples are two good people who lack communication skills. Fix it by taking ownership and changing yourself, since one person can change the dynamic. If you chose your partner soundly (same mission, shared values, shared interests), the problem is a skill gap, not the person. For couples in business, treat your spouse exactly like any other business partner. If they're the wrong person for the business, removing them saves the marriage long term.

## Frameworks & models
- **Partner-selection logic**: same mission + shared values + shared interests means no reason it can't last.
- **Mirror test**: when you want your partner to change, ask what that says about you. Where am I unskilled? What does their strength expose about my insecurity?
- **Reinforcement / training**: you teach people how to treat you. One person changing removes old reinforcement and adds new reinforcement, so the other person adjusts.
- **Spouse-as-business-partner test**: complementary skill set? Equity split (50/50, 30/70)? Roles and responsibilities? Commitments? What would end the partnership?
- **"If they weren't your spouse, would you have started a business with them?"** If no, you have your answer.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Couples working together "works until it doesn't," usually when one is incompetent at work and the other is afraid to say so, and the business plateaus.
- Make deficits openly known (to each other and the company) so they can be worked on; ask "is there something I'm doing or not doing that's keeping us stuck?"
- Don't ask "how do I get my wife to work with me?" Ask what she wants for her own life.
- She suggests a relationship coach or counselor for communication skills; she doubts talk therapy helps, because it's a skill gap rather than unresolved issues.
- To show treatment is unacceptable, leave temporarily (days, a week, a month).

## Playbook steps
1. Select a partner on mission, values and interests. 2. When friction arises, assume a skill gap and own your part. 3. Model the behavior you want. 4. Ask for feedback on what you're doing that blocks growth. 5. For a business partnership with a spouse, formalize equity, roles, commitments and exit terms. 6. If they're the wrong fit, remove them from the business despite short-term pain.

## Mistakes he warns against
(Leila's warnings)
- Being courteous at work but treating your spouse as "an exception to the rules"; they should be the person you practice with most.
- The insecure-partner/secure-partner cycle, where staying no matter what teaches mistreatment.
- Blaming the partner and never taking ownership.
- Keeping an incompetent spouse in the business to avoid a hard conversation; short-term thinking kills both the business and the marriage.

## Examples / case details
- Three people contacted her within four days about relationships that made both partners' lives worse.
- Gym Launch era: the #1 question from men was "how do I get my wife to work with me?"
- Leila was autonomous and career-driven before meeting Alex (left her hometown, moved to California, built her own business). She wasn't coerced; Alex asks "is there something I can do to be a better partner?"
