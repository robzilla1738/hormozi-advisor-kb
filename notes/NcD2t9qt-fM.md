## Core thesis
Recorded CoachingCon keynote on "selling with logic." The goal is a *decision*, not a purchase: help the prospect take back power they've handed to outside sources. Objections are distortions of reality (based on Albert Ellis's three core irrational beliefs: circumstances, others, self). Peel them back like an onion (time → money → fit → authority → avoidance) until the prospect confronts the real decision. Emotion lowers the action threshold, but logic makes the purchase stick. Ends with "invest in yourself / time tax of ignorance."

## Frameworks & models
- **Yes-I'll-decide vs yes-I'll-buy**: be genuinely happy with either outcome so the pressure goes away; measure yourself by "did I help them."
- **Emotion vs logic** (Munger: "high moral responsibility to be rational"): emotional buyers call 2 days later with regret; logic holds after the emotion fades.
- **~21 sales beliefs** (highlights): people want to believe and buy, so help their logic justify it; selling happens before the ask, closing after; obstacles (raised before the ask) are easier than objections (raised after); expect no ("if they could decide alone they'd just send the money"); no gasp at the price = not high enough, and anchor high ($100K first makes $1K a rounding error); selling is the first step of coaching; prospect over sale; childlike curiosity ("huh, tell me more"); a dance not a fight, sell from your back foot; **selling = transference of belief over a bridge of trust**; belief and trust are continuums; closers ask hard questions; the person who cares most about the prospect wins; record all your sales.
- **Hot/cold streaks** come from losing conviction, not skill. Refilling conviction is "one of the easiest and best ways to increase closing percentage."
- **Red-zone analogy**: the top 5 NFL red-zone offenses made the playoffs 90% of the time over 5 years. Closing makes up for many other weaknesses.
- **10/80/10**: 10% never buy, 10% always buy (don't screw it up), fight for the middle 80%.
- **Prerequisites to buy**: they want the goal and believe (1) the product gets them there *the way they want to get there*, (2) you and others will support them, (3) it will work for *them*.
- **Ellis's 3 distortions → onion of blame**: circumstances → others → self. They show up as 5 excuses: **time, money/value, fit** (circumstances), **authority** (others), **avoidance/stall** (self). The layer tells you how close you are to the decision maker.
- **Time**: *macro* ("busy season": you'll be busy again, so learn it while busy so it lasts, and that's when you want the most support); *micro* (story: wife pulled out his phone's screen time; a good program removes the ~90% of activity that isn't working; Bezos has the same hours); *when-then fallacy* (from Jason Fladlien): "I'll save money once I'm rich."
- **Money**: (1) "a lot is good" → you'll go all in; the question is whether *you* work; (2) absolute vs relative ("if it adds $10K/mo is it worth it?" → then it's belief, not price), plus a comparison anchor (a 4-year degree at $50–200K); (3) "what's money good for": you'll pay either with money or time, 12 weeks or 12 years, buy other people's mistakes (Edison); (4) resourceful, not resources (Shoe Dog: Knight asked vendors to cover payroll; you found money for that surprise tax bill).
- **Fit**: *new identity → new priorities* (Sephora story: the clerk told teen girls women budget for makeup every month; "we vote with our dollars"); *change the change* (pain of staying the same vs pain of change, rock bottom differs per person); *hypothetical* ("if this were perfect would you do it?" A no means they don't trust you; a yes leads to "what's different between perfect and this?" Swap broccoli for green beans).
- **Authority**: isolate the absent partner ("what do you think they wouldn't like?"); "do they approve of your current struggle?"; reverse the roles; **support, not permission**, or you'll resent them; a 3-day no-sweat guarantee helps.
- **Avoidance, split into past / present / future**:
  - Past: "not a fast decision, you've been deciding for 6 years"; sunk cost ("six inches from gold"); "what are you most afraid of having happen if you buy?"; **don't let it burn you twice** (bad past program ≠ all coaches); "is waffling why you're here?"; "tired of another year of almost?" (the cost of inaction).
  - Present: **rocking chair** (you won't sit and ponder, life will decide for you); decisions take information, not time, and "I'm the only source of information you've got"; **3 questions**: believe it'll help? trust me to deliver? think it'll work for you? → then "do you have or have access to the money?"; informed decision only from the inside (trial/guarantee, e.g. 30-day money back); *decide = de-caedere, "to cut off"* (which future are we killing?).
  - Future: "how do another 5 years sound?"; **consider the options** (do it + result; don't + no result; do it + no result but guaranteed). Walking out is the only option guaranteed not to get there; "you'll fix it eventually, faster or slower?"; *closer or further* (be directionally right, not a sniper).
- **Bonus close**: "the reason you're telling yourself not to do this is the reason to do it."
- **Time tax of ignorance**: not knowing how to make $1M/yr costs you $1M/yr. Invest in skills that compound and can't be taken (his parents fled Iran and lost their property; his father rebuilt from $1,000 with skills). Skills are bricks on a bridge.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If they don't gasp at the price, go higher, then walk down using the anchor.
- If they say no to "if this were perfect would you do it?", stop and fix trust ("does my breath smell?").
- Proof threshold: "how many people would you need to see for it to be more unreasonable not to believe? 10? 50? here's a thousand, tell me when to stop."
- Don't get stuck at the surface layers. If they want the result and you believe in the product, you can walk them to the core.
- If they believe yes to all 3 questions, it's your "moral obligation" to help them find the money (Ferrari for $5K; landlord needs $10K).
- Ask: "Is this decision going to get you closer to or further from your goal?" Closer = go.
- Fortunes are made by taking a lot of risk with a little money and kept by taking a little risk with a lot.

## Playbook steps
1. Handle obstacles before the ask (e.g. "just want info" → "do you go to lots of programs for info, or is there a problem you're solving?").
2. Confirm the 3 beliefs (the way they want, support, works for them); use proof.
3. Solicit, expect no, stay curious ("what are you thinking about?").
4. Diagnose the layer: time → money → fit → authority → avoidance. Use one angle; if they come back, use another (principles, not scripts).
5. Empathize from the inside ("I was stuck like this for years") instead of attacking.
6. At the core: rocking chair → 3 questions → money access → guarantee/options framing → closer/further.
7. Record every sale; review hot streaks; use the recordings to train new reps.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Closing for yourself; prospects sniff out intention.
- Emotional-only selling that leads to regret and refunds.
- Arguing with the prospect instead of asking questions.
- Memorizing scripts or screenshotting slides instead of understanding principles.
- Talking past a ready buyer ("just take the card").
- Letting a bad past purchase block a good one, and waiting for a savior or perfect program.
