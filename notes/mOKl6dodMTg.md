## Core thesis
Gym-specific (COVID reopening with capacity limits), but the logic generalizes: price must match the service actually delivered, and capacity/staffing should be set from real recurring revenue at a ≥80% gross margin. Start small and add sessions, never inflate overhead to serve everyone at old prices. A forced reset is the chance to fix price, cadence, hours and session structure at once.

## Frameworks & models
- **80% minimum gross margin** on services (fulfillment hard cost ≤20% of revenue; excludes rent, admin, marketing). His long-time Gym Launch rule of thumb; 85–90% is fine.
- **Margin points matter**: average gym ~12.5% net; going from 75% to 83% gross adds ~8 pts to the bottom line (12% → 20%, "almost twice as much").
- **Session math** (worked example): 100 returning clients × $100/mo = $10K MRR → 20% = $2K for fulfillment payroll → at $20/session = 100 sessions/mo → ÷4.3 weeks ≈ 23 sessions/week → e.g. 4/day Mon–Fri + 3 on Saturday.
- **Supply/demand**: returning clients who want back "desperately" put demand above capacity, which supports higher prices.
- **Five reset levers**: (1) unlimited → 2–3×/week main offer (doubles/triples capacity); (2) weekly/bi-weekly/28-day billing = 13 billings/yr vs 12, ~8% more top line (~12% → ~20% margin); (3) price to match service; (4) drop space-hungry exercises; (5) cut sessions from 60 to 45 min (more capacity, less payroll).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If capacity forces ~1-on-8 service, $100/mo unlimited pricing no longer makes sense; semi-private-level service needs ~$300–400/mo.
- Base the schedule on revenue that actually bills, not promises. If you can't bill first, take prior revenue net of cancellations and cut another 10%.
- Start with as few sessions as possible (2–3/day, even 90% margin) and add; cut low-attendance sessions.
- Price floor he recommends: $167 per 28 days (~$39/week); most at $49–69/week; use .99 endings (e.g. $49.99/wk → $199.99 per 28 days).
- If rent is too big for the real MRR (e.g. $6K rent on $10K), you won't start profitable: consider a big pre-sale/trial push; cash matters.

## Playbook steps
1. Contact all past clients; bill those who return. 2. Compute MRR × 20% = fulfillment budget. 3. Divide by cost/session, then by 4.3 → weekly sessions. 4. Lay out schedule. 5. Apply the five levers. 6. Communicate changes (he points to a letter template in Gym Launch Secrets, section 2 "profit levers").

## Mistakes he warns against
- Reopening at old prices with smaller groups.
- Adding sessions/overhead (e.g. 10/day) to see all 100 clients at a 10-person cap: "there goes your margin forever," and cutting back later is painful.
- Treating 75% gross margin as close enough.

## Examples / case details
- 150 prior clients, 100 return, $100/mo (he says he doesn't recommend that price).
- Fewer people per session framed as added value (more personalized), not lost sessions.

## Notable lines
- "Don't go off of promises. I'm begging you. Go off of reality."
- "You're not cutting sessions, you're adding value by making it more personalized."
- "Just trust the math."
