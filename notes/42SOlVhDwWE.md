## Core thesis
Belief isn't binary — it's a continuum. Sales is a transference of belief; conviction (built by deeply understanding the prospect's problem) is a bigger lever than technique, and it counts twice because it also drives work ethic/follow-up.

## Frameworks & models
- **Belief Continuum:** ask "to what extent do you believe" not "do you believe."
- **Close rate = Skill (1–10) x Conviction (1–100).** A skill-2 rep at 100 conviction will close.
- **Units sold = close rate x work ethic (activity, follow-up speed/frequency).** Conviction counts twice (raises close % and follow-up).
- **Time-travel test:** if you could tell your past self to put 100% into Bitcoin/Amazon/Tesla, how many times would you follow up? That's conviction.
- Conviction comes from understanding the prospect's needs, so you know the product matches their problem.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Sales training should focus mostly on the prospect and their problems, not the product.
- If reps aren't "drinking the Kool-Aid," that's the problem — fix belief first, then skill.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Reps who don't listen and just pitch (can't be convinced it solves a problem they don't know).
- Over-focusing on tonality/scripts while ignoring belief.
- Entering a market where you aren't the avatar without studying the prospect.

## Examples / case details
- Founders who solved their own pain sell well because they deeply understand prospects. Religious movements: don't train skill, instill wholehearted belief.
