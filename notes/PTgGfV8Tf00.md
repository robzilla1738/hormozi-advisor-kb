# Hard work won't make you rich.
id: PTgGfV8Tf00 / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTgGfV8Tf00 / duration: 488s (~8 min) / tags: 12-mindset-leverage, 07-hiring-management, 08-ops-scaling
Signal: medium

## Core thesis
"No one can outwork me" brags about a low-level activity metric. Everyone has the same hours, so total output = volume of activity × leverage of the activity. Once your hours are maxed out, the only lever left is *what* you work on. The entrepreneur's path goes from player, to specialist in one function, to coach who recruits and builds the team. The CEO's value is the gap between what players would do with no coach and what they do with the best one.

## Frameworks & models
- **Output = volume × leverage**: volume is a "newbie measure"; after you hit capacity, leverage decides output.
- **Calendar + org chart audit** (Acquisition.com CEOs): you can't work more, so swap low-leverage activities for higher-leverage ones. Hand low-leverage tasks to someone on the org chart. If a large share of tasks belong to a role that doesn't exist yet, hire that role.
- **War party / sports team**: build complementary roles (healer, mage, paladin), not 10 of the same.
- **Business roles**: marketer (making things known, story, brand, value to an audience); sales, which splits into low-ticket direct response (ads, sales pages, sales letters) and high-ticket person-to-person (sell → train sellers → lead trainers); product (the "mage", e.g. Shopify's founder who isn't CEO); customer experience/success. Each is a different skill, and the bigger the company, the more specialized each role gets.
- **3 levels of the entrepreneur**: 1) player; 2) specialize in your best function (CMO/CSO/CPO); 3) coach: recruit top executives and build a farm system that feeds the company, for a "dynasty" and lasting edge.
- **Value of the CEO** = performance with the best coach − performance with no coach. The CEO needs the highest return on time in the organization.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Brag about the high-leverage skills instead: "can't be out-recruited, out-managed, no one builds culture or leads like me."
- Newbies who can't get off the couch should first build volume; then shift to leverage.
- A CEO's time trade works as long as the new activities build the business more than the old ones did.

## Playbook steps
1. Max out your volume. 2. Audit your calendar for low-leverage activities. 3. Map them to people on the org chart, or hire the missing role. 4. Pick the function you map to and specialize. 5. Move to coach: recruiting pipelines at every level (graduates, business school, executive), culture, and standards.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Superstition about inputs (waking at 4am, sleeping little) instead of leverage.
- Playing star player: an all-star with a poor supporting cast loses to the better team ("they lose to Apple because Apple's got a team").
- Assuming great marketers can sell, or great salespeople can market.

## Examples / case details
- Warren Buffett: two good decisions a year is a good year; reads 5–6 hours a day.
- Writing a book is a high-leverage activity because it sells copies forever.
- Google's layered recruiting (graduates, business school, executive).
- He says a CEO can turn even a behemoth around in 2–3 years through standards and values.
