## Core thesis
Three "evil Es" cause most entrepreneurial mistakes: **Expectations** (arbitrary outcome deadlines), **Emotions** (urgency-driven, flip-flopping decisions), and **Expenses** (hiring and spending ahead of growth). They form a self-reinforcing loop: unreasonable expectations → missed goals → scarcity emotions → rushed decisions → undue expenses. Expectations are the deadliest. From the ALAN/Prestige Labs/Gym Launch era ("$120M in sales in the last four years").

## Frameworks & models
- **Process-based expectations**: be intolerant of anything but excellence in the process, not in outcome deadlines. "Good process drives good results" (a mentor's line).
- **Emotion detection**: if the same data produces different decisions on different days, emotion is driving. When you feel urgency, slow down. Most "decide today" deadlines are fictitious.
- **Sleep on it (24 hours)**: the world often looks different after a night's sleep.
- **Decide from contentment**: make big, complex, multivariable decisions when well-rested and grateful (vacation clarity, playing with your kids), then stick with them through later setbacks. If you're not in that state, don't decide.
- **Expenses = liabilities/risk**: entrepreneurship is increasing upside and decreasing downside.
- **Growth pays for people**: get the growth first.
- **Call beliefs assumptions** (from a coach): they become malleable and open to new data, unlike "musts" and "shoulds."
- **Virtuous/vicious loop**: expectations → emotions → expenses.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Don't hire "in anticipation of growth." About 99% of the time growth should pay for the expense. The exception is commission-only sales reps.
- If demand outruns capacity, briefly delay something to shore up infrastructure. "It's much easier to solve problems with cash in the bank."
- Felt urgency is a red flag, so extend the time between the feeling and the action.

## Playbook steps
1. Rewrite goals as process standards. 2. Notice flip-flopping or urgency and pause 24 hours. 3. Schedule big decisions for content, grateful states. 4. Add headcount only after the revenue shows up. 5. Reframe "expectations" as "assumptions" to stay open to data.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Arbitrary goals with arbitrary timelines that lead to shortcuts and high-risk bets ("10 steps backwards").
- Speeding up decisions when emotional.
- Throwing bodies at problems instead of fixing process. Morale, culture, efficiency and customer experience all suffer.
- Decisions driven by unmet childhood emotional needs.

## Examples / case details
- An entrepreneur at $100K/month asked for a guarantee of $1M/month within six months.
- Prestige Labs: hired 25 support reps in anticipation of growth two years earlier. Now 4 people handle more volume than the 25 did.
- He observes that Bezos, Munger and Buffett stay even-handed in decisions.
- "Making the right call is basically my only job now."
