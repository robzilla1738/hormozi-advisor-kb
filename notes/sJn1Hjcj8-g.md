# The Dark Side of Entrepreneurship

**Speaker note:** This is NOT Alex. It is Leila Hormozi being interviewed by "Nate" (Acquisitioncom channel). Alex is only mentioned.

## Core thesis
Self-doubt doesn't go away, and caring a lot produces it ("the downside of the upside"). Don't wait to feel confident. Take the actions a successful person would take regardless of your thoughts; feelings follow behavior, and confidence is stacked evidence of doing things you didn't believe you could. Once your strategy is confirmed as sound, the main failure is impatience and giving up. Growth comes in steps, and everything takes longer and is harder than you think.

## Frameworks & models
- **Downside of the upside**: negative "I'm a sham" thoughts are a byproduct of caring. People who don't care don't have them, so treat them as a gift.
- **Commit to the process, not the dialogue**: at 23, running an eight-figure business with no management experience, her inner dialogue said she'd fail. She followed the plan and took the actions someone without those thoughts would take.
- **Calorie-deficit analogy**: in a deficit you lose weight whatever you believe. Behavior, not thought, dictates most results.
- **"Prove yourself wrong"** is her mantra: act despite not believing you can.
- **Act as your future self / role model**: ask what Alex (or the person you want to be) would do. You can think and feel differently and still get a similar result with the same actions.
- **Evidence → confidence**: feelings come after the behavior (her first stage talk: expected a panic attack, was fine, then believed she could speak).
- **Bias toward action over thinking**: overconsuming content wasted time. You learn best by doing; "get so busy trying things that you don't have time to doubt yourself."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- When "the evidence says it isn't working": it may work in six months. People have a skewed sense of how long things take, and you may be one skill away.
- Keep going once someone a few steps ahead has confirmed your strategy is sound. She knows few people who don't eventually achieve it if they keep trying. [inference: validate the strategy first, then persist; don't persist blindly on an unvalidated one.]

## Playbook steps
1. When doubt hits, don't argue with it; ask what a successful person would do and do that. 2. Collect evidence by doing things you think you can't. 3. Swap consuming content for trying and failing. 4. Get your strategy checked by someone ahead of you. 5. Keep executing and expect a timeline longer than you want.

## Mistakes she warns against
- Waiting to feel or believe a certain way before acting.
- Overconsuming content / bias toward thinking.
- Impatience: expecting results on your timeline.
- Believing the perception that business is easy and sexy.

## Examples / case details
- "All the money left the account" period: she was 23 and had never managed people.
- First stage speech: dry mouth and sweating, then fine.
- She is impatient too ("why are we not at 10 billion yet").
- Seeing hundreds of companies' financials a week, most go "[mess up], grow, [mess up], grow." Growth comes in steps.
