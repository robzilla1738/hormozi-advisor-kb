## Core thesis
To learn a new platform/skill fast, spend money to save time: use agencies as paid teachers, not permanent vendors. Phase 1 basic agency forces cadence; Phase 2 premium agency on a 6-month "teach us" contract while you document and train in-house; then downgrade to consulting and cut. This grew his audience from <10K to ~5M in under 24 months while avoiding the "agency trap" and building enterprise value.

## Frameworks & models
- **Three shortcut options**: celebrities (too personalized), coaches/gurus (often outdated tactics, e.g. "hashtags on YouTube videos"), agencies (chosen).
- **Agency tiers (one brand, one platform)**: basic ~$3–5K/mo; advanced ~$15–30K/mo.
- **Two-phase agency playbook**:
  - Phase 1 basic agency (3–6 months): commit to cadence (him: 3 YouTube videos/week; agency trimmed, added intro + thumbnail). Goal: fundamentals + audience context. Part ways when your demands exceed their capacity ("no business owner should change their business for one client").
  - Phase 2 premium agency: 6-month learn-from-you deal + pay extra for them to explain decisions → document into internal processes → co-create → in-house matches/exceeds → downgrade to lower-cost consulting → cut after 1–2 months if calls teach nothing, amicably.
- **Why in-house wins once expertise gap closes**: more time (fully allocated) + more brand context; you're one client of many for them ("renting fractional labor with expertise at a premium"). Agency needs margin; he covers cost and gets ~5× output.
- **The agency trap** (from *$100M Leads*, 11 steps): excitement → valuable onboarding → best senior rep assigned → results → senior rep moved to newest client → junior rep → results suffer → complain → senior rep pops back occasionally → results never recover, cancel → find new agency, repeat → wonder why.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- More money or more time? No money → learn it yourself (takes time); master one platform, get cash flow, then pay to learn others faster ("pay down your ignorance tax"). Have money → always worth paying.
- Set a clear transition deadline (e.g., $6K/mo × 6 = $36K commitment): gives agency known LTV and pushes your team monthly.
- At the deadline, if in-house is worse: change the people, or the agency isn't teaching everything; otherwise (complex platform) extend until matched.
- Expect a period of paying twice (agency + team); it builds an asset you own instead of dependence (risk of being cut off or price-gouged).

## Playbook steps
1. Hire basic agency, commit to cadence. 2. After 3–6 months, move to premium agency with the explicit script: "I want to do what you do… work with you for six months so I can learn… I'll pay extra for you to break down why… then train my team… then change to a lower-cost consulting agreement. Are you opposed?" 3. Document their decisions into SOPs. 4. Co-create. 5. Transition at deadline; consulting as insurance; cut.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Agreeing to the agency's default "pay until you die" terms.
- Permanent reliance on an agency that can cut you off or raise prices as they see your revenue.
- Following outdated guru tactics.

## Examples / case details
- YouTube: ran both phases.
- TikTok: inbound agency repurposed existing content into shorts with zero work from him; traction came. Then his director of brand, who came from a premium agency, brought SOPs, skipping phase 2; output scaled within ~6 months. Still on good terms.
- Same playbook used for LinkedIn, podcasts, paid ads.
- First agency was good; ~10 subsequent agencies terrible (the trap).
