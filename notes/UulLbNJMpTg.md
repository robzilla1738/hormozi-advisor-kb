## Core thesis
Dissatisfaction comes from wanting the benefits of paths you didn't take while keeping the benefits of the trades you made, with limited resources. Demanding everything amounts to choosing to be dissatisfied. Fix it with high commitment and low expectations: eliminate alternatives, then let reality exceed expectations. This is a life/relationship reflection that applies to big business choices (committing to one business). [inference]

## Frameworks & models
- **One tank of gas**: you drove to Toledo and now want Orange County. You spent the resource on the choice you made.
- **Untaken paths**: the benefits are overestimated, and the costs are unknown and possibly worse.
- **Stages**: (1) blame others; (2) learn to get things, then crave the other path too; (3) be happy with your decisions.
- **Three decision skills**: make decisions → make good decisions → stay satisfied with the decisions you made (don't move the goalposts).
- **High commitment + low expectations** (from studying lasting marriages with Leila). Commitment = eliminating alternatives.
- **"Not wanting something is as good as having it."**
- **Irreversible big doors** (marriage, age, which business; you can "divorce" a business, but wind-downs are messy). Many people stand at the door forever waiting for more information about an inherent contradiction and get the benefits of neither path.
- **"You are the problem"** refrain: find the arbitrary expectation and remove it. He thinks he'd be about as happy in any of three alternate lifetimes, which reduced his anxiety.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- All three spouse models (stay-at-home, parallel careers, working together) can work. Pick one and stop wanting the benefits of all three.
- Discount end-of-life regret advice: it usually means wanting another life's benefits plus your own.

## Playbook steps
1. Name the trade you made and say "I'm OK with this trade." 2. When tempted by an alternative, list its costs. 3. Commit by eliminating alternatives. 4. Lower expectations for the chosen path.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Expecting one partner, or one business, to be 10 things at once (Esther Perel's point about expectations rising).
- Indecision at irreversible doors.
- Changing what you wanted after you get it so you can stay dissatisfied.
