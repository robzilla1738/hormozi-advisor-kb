## Core thesis
Echoing Munger, the first $100k is the hardest money: a $100k goal confines you to time-for-money vehicles, and with no capital you have no leverage, so you must do everything yourself. Once you have it, buy back your lowest-value time and reinvest it into higher-value work. Skills stack and raise your "basement" (income floor).

## Frameworks & models
- **Goal size dictates vehicle:** you can't 100x a $100k method into $10M. Ask "what would it take to make $1M / $10M?" to see different vehicles.
- **All business is arbitrage** (e.g. buy labor, train and systematize it, sell it higher).
- **Buy back time in reverse order of market value:** outsource the task you can buy cheapest (e.g. scheduling/phones at ~$10/hr), then **refill the freed hours with higher-value work** or you just pay someone and earn the same.
- **Skill-stack basement:** each skill raises your floor. Sales → he'd never make under ~$250k (could sell cars for $200–400k). Sales + marketing → "demand at will", floor ~$3M/yr (his estimate).
- **"Your work works on you more than you work on it."** Failures are lessons that increase your value.
- **Skill speed = volume × deliberateness**, faster with mentors. "Every valuable skill I've acquired, I've paid someone to acquire."
- **"You can't be busy and broke."** Busy should mean money, broke means you have time. Trade one for the other.

## Decision rules
- Look for vehicles you don't know exist, e.g. a one-day phlebotomy certification for ~$25/hr. Only get certifications that are required.
- $100k saved = ~3.5 years of runway for him and Leila, a 100x jump from ~$1k. Matching that would take $100k → $10M.
