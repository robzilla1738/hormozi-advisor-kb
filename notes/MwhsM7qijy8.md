## Core thesis
A coaching founder pitching to Acquisition.com said "there's no way we can make our customers more valuable." Alex calls that patently false. You grow a business in two ways: get more customers, or make each one worth more. He gives six ways to raise lifetime value (LTV) in any coaching, service or info business. A higher LTV lets you spend more to acquire customers on the front end. (The title doesn't match the content; the video is about the six LTV levers.)

## Frameworks & models
- **Two ways to grow:** more customers, or make them worth more.
- **Six LTV levers:**
  1. **Raise price:** sell the same thing for more. Pricing tiers can also convert more people.
  2. **Lower cost of goods (COGS):** keep the ratio but outsource part of the delivery to cheaper labor (e.g., abroad), or keep the coaches and raise the coach-to-client ratio. Going from 10 to 20 clients per coach halves COGS.
  3. **Increase purchase frequency.** In recurring revenue this means lowering churn so people stay and pay longer.
  4. **Cross-sell:** a different add-on that goes with the core purchase ("fries with your burger"). Build add-ons around execution bottlenecks that some clients have (ads, sales-team drilling).
  5. **Upsell:** more of the same thing ("bigger burger"), such as more coach meetings, more access, or a small-group or semi-private tier.
  6. **Downsell:** used on the front end for disqualified leads and on the back end against churn.
- **Customers are fractal (80/20):** about 1 in 5 customers will pay roughly 2× the core offer. Of 100 people, about 20 will pay about 5× the base. Apply 80/20 again inside that 20 (4 of them pay another 5×), and so on (80/20 → 95/5 → 99/1). Offer a much more expensive version to the top 20%.
- **Information declines in value:** knowledge loses value the day after you learn it, so info and coaching products churn. Back-end downsells keep the consumable parts: community, troubleshooting, technical support, accountability.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Add-on math: 20% of $1K/month clients take a $1K/month add-on, so the average goes from $1,000 to $1,200 (+20%).
- A small-group or semi-private tier above a large group should reasonably cost about 4–5× as much.
- Only downsell disqualified leads. Anyone who meets the core-offer requirements should never be shown the downsell. Keep it black and white for sales: "if they are this sell this, if not this sell this," based on what's best for them, not their budget.
- Before adding a downsell, track the core-offer close rate. The downsell must add sales without reducing core purchases.
- The front-end downsell should be low-touch or no-touch, e.g., a do-it-yourself at-home program so the lead can prove motivation and move up later. It recovers part of the acquisition cost you already paid.

## Playbook steps
1. Audit price. 2. Cut fulfillment cost through ratio or labor cost. 3. Attack churn. 4. List client execution bottlenecks and turn them into add-ons. 5. Build a premium tier at about 4–5× for the top 20%. 6. Add a DIY downsell for unqualified leads, gated by predefined criteria. 7. Add a back-end continuity downsell (community or accountability) for people leaving. 8. Reinvest the higher LTV into acquisition.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Believing customers can't be made more valuable.
- Sales reps downselling people who would have bought the core offer, especially with gray-area rules.
- Delivering "a diluted version" of the promise to fit a budget.
