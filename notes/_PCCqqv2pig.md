## Core thesis
Persuasion is one of the highest-leverage skills: you get what you want by getting others to get it for you. He walks through Cialdini's six principles from *Influence* and how he applied each in his businesses (then $200M+/yr combined).

## Frameworks & models (Cialdini's 6, as he applies them)
1. **Reciprocity:** the gift must be proportional to the person you're giving to. Retail stores hand out cold water at the door.
2. **Consistency/commitment:** get a small public yes first, then ask them to stay consistent with it. In sales: "Are you the type of person who takes action and invests in yourself?" At the close: "Since you're the type of person who..." This is why lead magnets and front-end offers lead to bigger purchases.
3. **Social proof:** people copy others (Amazon reviews, laugh tracks).
4. **Liking/rapport:** people say yes to those they like and want to be like. Flattery works even when people know the complimenter is paid, and there's no cap if it's genuine. **ACA framework:** Acknowledge what they said, Compliment it (tie it to a positive trait), Ask the next question.
5. **Authority:** people put up fewer filters around trusted sources, so the message persuades more, independent of content. Show credentials, awards, a track record ("life resume"), third-party accreditation.
6. **Scarcity & urgency:** urgency is about time (promo ends tomorrow), scarcity is about units (100 left). Use limited time, limited supply, one-time offers. Use loss language ("you don't want to miss out") because loss aversion beats desire for gain.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Waiter mint study (from Cialdini): 1 mint → +3% tips, 2 mints → +14%, 1 mint then turning back with a 2nd → +23%. Look for "$5 bags of mints" in your business.
- Scarcity must be true. "If you can handle 20 people a week, tell people your limits." Real limits sell more.

## Examples / case details
- Gym Launch era: donated $1M to Arnold Schwarzenegger's charity → sat down with Arnold multiple times (Arnold didn't care about endorsing the book; nothing else had worked).
- A pushy networker he told to go away sent expensive gifts (Cutco knives, custom cakes) every month for 9 months. It worked on Alex even though he knew what was happening ("turns out still a weirdo").
- Gym lobby: every 5-star Yelp review screenshotted and framed floor to ceiling, other half before/after photos. Objection handle: "How much proof would you need... at what point is your requirement for proof more than reasonable?"
- 20+ in-person consults/day bouncing on a mini trampoline. Earlier did a cartwheel before every handshake at the gym.
- Trainers: paid real certifications and also certified them internally, so all were "double or triple certified" (he notes US certifications aren't accredited).
- De Beers diamond scarcity. Luxury brands don't tell stores how many bags they'll get, and kept COVID lines at the door.
- Small-sign → big-sign "drive slowly" neighborhood study.
