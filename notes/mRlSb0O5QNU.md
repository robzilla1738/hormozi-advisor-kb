## Core thesis
"Scale or Fail" consult with Joey Goon (Utopia Experience, St. Louis event planning; ~$44K avg deal TTM; 30 staff / 12 full-time), with 90-day follow-up clips. Diagnosis: demand-constrained AND mispriced (thin margins, "make-believe" line-item quotes). Order of operations: fix the offer → fix price → fix sales motion → increase demand. "We don't want to jam more demand through something that's thin margins. So let's fix the margins of the offer, and then jam as much demand as we can." (Opening line says "$55 per year business", likely a caption error.)

## Frameworks & models
- **Cost-plus pricing generator**: cost true delivery per level (variable costs that scale with headcount, e.g. vendors; fixed costs, e.g. 1/4 of a planner who can handle four events = $15K), hidden from the client; price at 5–10× cost (5× minimum, since estimates can run over). Build as a dynamic if-then sheet or AI tool so sales can quote live on the call.
- **Zip code tax**: identical service costs 2–3× more in rich zip codes; where you land in the 5–10× range depends on how rich the buyer is. "Everyone else is doing this too. They're just not being organized about it."
- **Price lock guarantee**: fixed price as long as scope doesn't change (headcount within ±20%, same date, same venue, same services); otherwise a new conversation / true-up. Pitch: "nickel-and-dimed, or one price within your budget?"
- **Prepay / multi-event discounts**: 10% off to prepay (pull cash forward; small businesses earn more on capital than markets); 10% off for committing to all events this year, with a "pay the difference" clawback if they leave; another 10% for prepaying all. Q4 angle: prepaying moves spend into this year's budget/taxes (as described in video).
- **B2B event play** (where attendees are your ICP): get a booth, a 15-min speaking slot ("20 mistakes people buying event planning make", with proof under each), and a QR "want the slides?" survey close asking "do you host events, how many/yr?" → lead list; one sponsored email to the host's list with rev-share. Trade discounts on your high anchor for these ($90K → pay $5K booth fee → $95K, etc.).
- **"How many events per year?"** asked early: bid for all of them (avg ticket $80K → $240K), book the first, the rest at the same rate if it goes well.
- **VSL before every conversation** (×3: before call 1, before call 2, before the post-event rebooking call): answer FAQs/objections ahead; include one CTA ("text me your headcount/date") to prove viewing and boost show rate; if they didn't watch, play it on the call. Goal: one-call close.
- **Weekly 1:1 structure**: personal → short-term fixes (scripting) → if crushing KPIs, long-term goals/growth (shadow calls). Role-play forever.
- **Avatar narrowing**: clear avatar → templatized solution → higher margin, scale, lower CAC. Later: segment customers by profit (e.g. 25 of 100 events = 75% of profit) and retarget messaging to them.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Price at 5–10× delivery cost, never below 5×.
- Never present an invoice/proposal by email; always live, so you can resolve concerns and close.
- Don't sue a client who can't pay (in bankruptcy): call it a cost of business.
- When the owner converts ~10× better than reps and volume is low, make reps setters (SDR/BDR, still commissioned) and fill the owner's calendar: 4 appts/wk doubles the business, 8 quadruples (at ~40% close).
- Daily start-of-day and end-of-day check-ins with sales when sales is the constraint, not weekly.
- Reps clone the owner's proven outreach and max platform limits daily; "if they're not doing that, either they're the wrong people or you're not good at training."
- Stick with the channel with the best LTV:CAC (LinkedIn here); don't add Facebook. Do more of what works.
- Quote fast ("I'll have it to you first thing tomorrow"); speed raises close rates.
- Speak at as many B2B events as life allows, using a survey close.
- "Offering people to buy more of what they just bought almost always" raises LTV.

## Playbook steps
1. Build the auto-quote generator (variable / fixed / headcount → cost → 5–10× range). 2. Add the price lock + prepay / multi-event discounts and referral/promo incentives. 3. Make reps setters and run daily check-ins + role-play. 4. Record VSLs ×3. 5. Raise outreach: max LinkedIn limits, AI-scrape numbers, feed a dialer. 6. Pursue B2B conferences (booth, speaking, survey close, list email). 7. Later: segment by profit and narrow.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Making up line-item prices; not knowing profit per event.
- Emailing proposals and waiting days on quotes.
- Weekly-only sales management; telling instead of role-playing ("saying it at them will do literally nothing").
- Trying to do every idea/channel at once: "none of them will work if you try to do them all"; "do more of fewer."
- Scaling demand into thin margins.

## Examples / case details
- 90-day results (Joey): live pricing module on discovery calls (more consultative, objections handled live); $15K booth sponsorship → line all day, expecting to close a ~$500K deal; reps limited to prospecting/booking → 200% more pipeline volume; the events-per-year question turned one-year clients into three-year clients; top-of-funnel VSL ("like you knew me") plus a bottom-of-funnel VSL for the CEO/CFO who skips the call.
- Alex's verdict: "scaling". Get the two reps productive plus the offer generator; potential from ~1 deal/wk to 2–3 at $70–100K → $300–400K/month run rate.
