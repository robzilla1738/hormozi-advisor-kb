## Core thesis
Skills are the one asset no government, divorce or crisis can take. That's why entrepreneurs who hit zero bounce back: after losing everything, he collected $110K cash in the next 30 days. With savings, consider buying an existing small business from a motivated seller using seller financing instead of a down payment on a house. "Agree on price, then agree on terms," and try to get a cash-flowing asset for little or nothing. Wealthy people are more risk-averse because "you only have to get rich once."

## Frameworks & models
- **House vs business with $50K saved on $60K/yr**: a $500K house at 10% down gets you a mortgage liability. Instead, find a small local business with $250K/yr profit and buy it at ~2.5× earnings = $625K.
- **Example stack**: seller financing for most of it (over 3–5 years, pushed as long as possible), a bank/SBA loan for ~$200K, with your $50K as the 25% down on that loan. Income goes from $60K to $250K. [inference: his figures don't reconcile. He says "three quarters" (~$469K) and "~$437K," but $625K − $200K = $425K seller-financed. The structure is the point.]
- **"Agree on price, then agree on terms"** (early mentor's tactic). Settle price first, then negotiate seller financing and duration.
- **Brokers**: contact them to learn local businesses and price ranges, but buying through a broker means paying retail. Off-market motivated sellers are better. He hears owners making $1M/yr profit say they'd take $200K just to be out.
- **Buying vs starting**: buying skips the client list, equipment, zoning, permits, licenses and fees.
- **Rich vs poor risk**: the poor buy lottery tickets (likely to go to zero, tiny upside). The rich buy things that can't go to zero, ideally for zero. "Any number multiplied by zero" can erase 30 years of good decisions.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Prefer guaranteed small returns with no ruin risk over long-shot upside. He skips crypto 20× bets because the downside upsets him more than the upside excites him.
- Be patient. Spending six months finding a near-free deal is fine over a decade.
- "To the undisciplined, everything looks like a spending opportunity."

## Playbook steps
1. Build sellable skills. 2. Save. 3. Scan local businesses you know or like (ask brokers for market intel). 4. Find motivated off-market sellers. 5. Agree on price, then push terms (seller financing, longest duration, minimal cash). 6. Let the business pay for itself; cut waste and take over roles to grow profit.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Overspending on build-outs. Gym 1 cost ~$40–50K; gym 2 cost $250K and made no more money.
- Impatience and paying retail.
- Taking ruin-level risk once you have money.

## Examples / case details
- 5th gym: bought from an owner in a family crisis for ~$40–50K with expensive equipment, paid over 12 months (he asked for 24), $0 out of pocket. It did $51K in sales in its first 30 days and paid for itself. He later sold it for ~1.5× the purchase price after meeting Russell Brunson and pivoting to teaching. He calls it a cash-flowing asset acquired for nothing.
- At his lowest point (after six gyms) he had $1,100.
