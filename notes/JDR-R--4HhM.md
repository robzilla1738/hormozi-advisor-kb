## Core thesis
Twelve internal copywriting "hacks" (plus a humor bonus) that his portfolio uses on every ad, email, script and page. It's an 80/20 mental checklist rather than 12 books. Headline first, then specificity, proof, trust and simplicity, with clear one-step CTAs.

## Frameworks & models
1. **Headlines come first**: quoting Ogilvy, the headline is 80 cents of the ad dollar. Going from 1% to 3% CTR triples everything downstream; a killer hook can beat control 2–5×, while other funnel tests win 20–50%. "Curiosity is king, different is ideal, sexy works, never run an ad without a headline." Model the first 3 seconds (visual + verbal) of organic content and ads; "steal like an artist" from other industries. Template: *How to [yay] without [boo] even if [biggest fear/insecurity].*
2. **Say what only you can say**: proof beats promise. "Competitors can copy your offer, but they cannot copy your proof." You choose the pond you're one of a kind in. AI avatars can't have done things in the real world, so story and verification matter more as trust erodes.
3. **Call out who it's for and, more powerfully, who it's NOT for** (e.g. "below $250K/yr, this isn't for you"; "only for people with 30+ lbs to lose"). Write to one person. Use **awareness levels**: unaware (curiosity) → problem aware → solution aware → product aware → most aware (offers). Specificity: describe *moments* ("does it look like a bomb went off behind your bar?") rather than "are you overweight?".
4. **Always have a reason why** ("because"; Langer-style line-cutting study). The "frat party planner" bar for reasons is low (birthday, dog's birth month, anniversary). Bonus if it ties to urgency/scarcity. Each line sells the next line; each step sells one next step. "A confused mind does not buy."
5. **Damaging admissions**: "Statement BUT statement." Whatever comes after "but" is what's believed and emphasized ("our websites are ugly as hell, but they convert"). Reverse the order and you emphasize the flaw. "What you'll sacrifice in promise, you'll gain in trust." Eminem owning his flaws. Gym example: bad parking, flaky AC, "but our sessions are more fun."
6. **Show don't tell**: describe the experience ("running out of order forms because customers read card numbers too fast"; line around the corner). Describe the future problems they'd *prefer* to have.
7. **Tie benefits to status**: who awards the status (family, friends, colleagues, rivals) and when (past/present/future). Status differs by subgroup (a mom vs. a 20-yo hustle bro). Cross-references the *Leads* book p.140 what/who/when.
8. **Urgency (time) vs scarcity (quantity)**: the only secret is that they're legitimate. Say no after the deadline or cap. "Dig your well before you're thirsty"; undersell demand. (*Offers* book p.104 scarcity, p.112 urgency.)
9. **Implied authority**: only-one claims, counts (5,000–6,000 gyms over 7 years), longevity, aggregate experience ("20+ years combined"), small local awards ("best boot camp in Vegas"). Volume of proof: 4.6★ with 4,000 reviews beats 5★ with 3.
10. **Always a PS** (2nd most-read, after the headline; PPS is 3rd). Email: fun/jokes trains readers to read it, then use it for a strong CTA. Ads: a "who it's not for" disclaimer or a one-sentence recap of the main persuasion points.
11. **Clear CTA**: spell out the steps and what happens next. When what you said happens, you gain "say-do correspondence" (Martha Stewart recipes). Make promise → keep promise in a loop. Acquisition.com named so the name is the CTA.
12. **Third-grade reading level**: assume the reader is rushed, uncaring and distracted. "Fancy equals friction, simple equals sales." Short sentences, small words, big promises and proof. Fewer adverbs, better verbs. Simplicity trumps concision. Ninth-grade copy loses ~2/3 of readers. He runs every ad through a free reading-level tool.
- **Bonus — humor**: entertainment goes broad, education goes deep. Old Spice was cited as the highest-converting ad he knows of. Comedians build sets by testing and front-loading the minutes that work. Humor is the first thing to cut if needed.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- A CTR jump of 1%→3% = 3× downstream; good headline tests 2–5× vs. typical 20–50% lifts.
- Keep a winning hook until you can beat it.
- Don't sell after a stated deadline or cap, to protect future scarcity.
- Ordering: want comes first, then believability. Authority/proof sit after desire in the checklist.

## Playbook steps
1. Write/model the headline (first 3s from organic, competitors, other industries). 2. Add unique proof. 3. Call out who it's for and not for at the right awareness level, using specific moments. 4. Give a reason why. 5. List every flaw/complaint and claim them as damaging admissions with "but." 6. Describe experiences. 7. Tie to status (who/when). 8. Real urgency/scarcity. 9. Authority markers. 10. PS (+PPS). 11. One clear CTA with steps. 12. Edit to a 3rd-grade level. Optional: humor, tested.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Vanilla, "everything to everyone" copy; fear of saying no to customers.
- Marketing-speak/lingo ("are you overweight?").
- Fake urgency/scarcity, or selling after the deadline.
- Putting the strength before "but" (emphasizes the flaw).
- Complex words/sentences; leaving the next step to guesswork.
