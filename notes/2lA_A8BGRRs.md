# How To Build Large Sales Teams (Starting from 0)

## Core thesis
Scaling a sales team is a repeatable system with five parts: hire for coachability, onboard reps by immersing them in good calls and a question-based script, manage through daily call review and drilling, hold culture through competition, KPIs and cutting the bottom, and keep conviction alive with testimonials. "Sales management is a skill of tolerance": the manager who tolerates the most has the weakest team.

## Frameworks & models
- **Interview tests:** (1) Response speed tests work ethic, since you're a lead to them. (2) Intelligence, because how they sound is how your brand sounds. (3) Ability to listen: you want an ambivert who is socially aware, not a "motor mouth." (4) Coachability: role-play, give feedback, have them try again. They should take feedback without ego and improve on the second try.
- **Prospect knowledge > product knowledge:** who you sell to matters more than what you sell. If you can describe their problem exactly, they believe you understand them and will buy.
- **CLOSER script (question-based):** Clarify why they're here, Label them with the problem, Overview past experiences and pains, Sell (ask "can I tell you about it?" and explain in short analogies how you solve 2–3 of their problems), Explain away concerns, Reinforce the decision.
- **Boil the decision down to one unit,** e.g., B2B: "can we help you more than you could on your own, yes or no?"
- **14-day onboarding:** listen to about 40 good recorded calls (if you have none, sell well yourself and record) and study the script between calls. Do a role-play every morning and pass a role-play test. Then go on the floor on a half schedule and review your own calls in the second half of the day. Meet the manager twice a day.
- **Feedback session (about 1 hour per rep):** about 30 minutes listening to 1–2 calls at 2× and taking written notes, then pick the 1–2 highest-impact fixes. About 5 minutes to communicate them, then about 25 minutes of role-play on those fixes and objection drills ("I have to think about it," "talk to my spouse," "that's a lot of money"). The next day, check whether they did it.
- **The manager's job = set goals + motivate + train.**
- **Three culture levers:** a visual, always-current leaderboard; clear KPIs plus a bonus competition every 4–6 weeks (optimizing for cash up front worked best across his portfolio companies); cut the bottom 10% every quarter.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- A trainee gets a full schedule only after hitting the minimum close rate or quota.
- Below KPI for 2 weeks puts a rep on a PIP (back to trainee-level attention). No improvement after 2 more weeks means they're fired. Set this expectation at hire: "this is a dangerous place to work."
- If you don't cut the bottom, you're telling the team it's OK to suck.
- Non-negotiables apply even to the best closer: be on time to calls, clean pipelines daily, put notes in the CRM, attend training.
- The best salesperson is rarely the best manager. Managers should be more giving and emotionally even ("the rock") and lead by example.
- Cadence: trainees 1–2 times a day for 14 days. After graduation, weekly one-on-ones (maybe every other week after a year for a top performer). Daily morning training huddle (mandatory for trainees and anyone on a PIP) and a short end-of-day huddle. Weekly and monthly team meetings.

## Playbook steps
1. Interview for speed, intelligence, listening and coachability (role-play, feedback, retry). 2. Run 14-day onboarding: ~40 calls, the script, daily role-plays, a role-play test. 3. Half schedule plus twice-daily review. 4. Full schedule after hitting quota. 5. Weekly 1-hour review per rep with written notes. Keep a tab per rep of the two things they're working on, and send them the key points by message after each call. 6. Leaderboard, a 4–6 week competition, quarterly bottom-10% cut. 7. Protect conviction with testimonials.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Asking "do you have any questions for me?" again and again. That invites more reasons not to buy and shows the rep can't close.
- Talking for 30 minutes and then dropping the price with no structure or fact-finding.
- Management that is just a weekly pipeline check-in.
- Selling from ego ("be number one") instead of genuine empathy. You want empathy, not "nice guy."
- Tolerating bloat.

## Examples / case details
- When the team "got bloated," he cut more than half of it. He told the rest they could sleep on it and quit in the morning, no harm done. The new standards included 5 a.m. one-hour drills, being on time to every call, and daily pipeline cleaning.
- NFL analogy: the same players can go from losing to nearly undefeated under a new coach because of culture and standards.
- Conviction, in order of strength: have past clients join a sales call, play testimonial videos at the morning huddle, or read testimonials aloud. Ideally the rep who sold that client reads it.
