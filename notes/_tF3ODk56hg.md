## Core thesis
2020 address announcing Gym Launch's pivot from in-person gyms to remote/online coaching. Market beats operator: with 25–30% of independent gyms closed (he projects 40–50%) and consumers retrained to other fitness solutions, the in-person market is shrinking. "Gym Launch is loyal to gym owners, not to gyms" — so align with the vehicle that makes customers the most money. Highly era- and niche-specific; the transferable lessons are market selection, sunk cost, and choosing scalable, low-skill acquisition models.

## Frameworks & models
- **Strong operator vs strong market — the market wins** (newspaper example): growing vs static vs shrinking markets.
- **Loyal to the customer, not the vehicle**: the business is an "opportunity vehicle" to package skills; switch vehicles when a better one exists.
- **Evaluate a model on**: (1) lead gen volume/cost/consistency, (2) ease of implementation, margin remaining, owner lifestyle, (3) fulfillment quality for the end user, (4) whether it scales without a superior skill set, authority or following.
- **Why he rejected 3 common online models**: outbound messaging (works but 2–4 hrs/day effort, doesn't scale); webinars (fatigue, ~$100 applications, experienced operators ~1–2:1 upfront ROAS on high ticket); 5-day group challenges (effortful, clumpy, stop-and-go).
- **GLX remote model**: free-trial front end (low risk → low-skill sellers) → supplement upsell → "abandoned hope" offer → continuity (~$39–59/week, ~$2,000 customer value); ALAN software handles lead nurture at fixed cost; app automates ~80% of fulfillment; only roles = trial seller, coach, owner.
- **Same $ test**: with the same $5,000 marketing budget, spend it on the superior vehicle — you net more even if still paying the old gym's rent.
- **Sunk cost fallacy**: past investment is irrelevant to the current decision.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Prefer models a low-skill person can sell; "try outsourcing a $3,000 sale" — high ticket is hard to delegate.
- Pick acquisition where you can't fatigue the market: a 5-mile radius fatigues; national/50-mile doesn't. In-person clients cycled copy/creative at 3–4× the rate of remote.
- Front-end offer should break even or near it on first transaction; profit by day 30 via continuity.
- People decide emotionally and justify logically — speak to both.

## Playbook steps
1. Assess market direction honestly (growing/static/shrinking).
2. Score vehicles on leads, implementation, margin, lifestyle, fulfillment, skill required.
3. Ignore sunk cost; options for the old business: exit the lease (he argued 2020 was the time) or let the better vehicle's profit cover it.
4. Commit publicly ("line in the sand") and focus all company effort on the chosen model.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Clinging to in-person out of emotion/sunk cost.
- Choosing effort-heavy acquisition models that most people can't execute.
- Owner tied to location (he took no vacation for 5 years; came back from Christmas after 2 days for 3 years).

## Examples / case details
- Average in-person lead cost ~$30 for a free offer (his figure, across markets).
- Community members built $3–4K/month recurring in 30 days; Marcia and Ross ~$40K recurring each.
- Some top seven-figure gym owners sold gyms and went 100% online.
- He bought two or three $10K cold-messaging courses and packaged the best into a $37 course.
