# 30 Minutes of sales training that will explode your business in 2022

## Core thesis
A talk at the War Room mastermind (run by Ryan Deiss, Perry Belcher and Roland Frasier). Sales skill can be copied into other people with three frameworks: CLOSER (a question-based script), Conviction (belief fixes tone), and the six Cs of scaling (closer sequence, consistent daily training and conviction, call recordings, communication cycles, cut fast, competition and career path). He says the team he references grew from 14 to almost 30 reps on this process. Captions are rough in places.

## Frameworks & models
- **CLOSER**: **C**larify why they're there ("What made you reach out? Goal? Why that number?") → **L**abel them with a problem ("we can't cure cancer unless they admit they have it") and confirm: "What I'm hearing is X, does that sound right?" → **O**verview past experience with the **pain cycle**: what have you tried / for how long / how long ago / what else, repeated until they've "tried everything" → **S**ell the vacation → **E**xplain away concerns → **R**einforce the decision. He added R later; it improves LTV and cuts churn, refunds and chargebacks.
- **Sell the vacation, not the plane flight**: sell Maui, not TSA, modules or macros. Pitch in **under 3 minutes**: three pillars (fitness / nutrition / accountability; leads that are exclusive / timely / qualified). Get a commitment on each, and if they hesitate, use a memorized ~30-second story (e.g. Game of Thrones: you don't need motivation for something you look forward to; brushing teeth: external accountability becomes an internal habit). Every service tier goes to Maui; the tiers only change the ease, speed and quality of the trip, and arrival is assumed.
- **Objection logic**: price = value ("if we were giving away Ferraris for $5K, would they find the money?", so it's always the rep's fault). Decision maker = circumvent → past agreements → ask forgiveness. Stall = teach them how to decide (can it meet your needs? greater or lower likelihood of success with us? do you want to work with us? do you have access to funds or know someone who does?). Fallback: take a card, delay payment to Friday, and offer to tear up the contract if the spouse really argues against their health.
- **Decision-maker sequence**: "What if they say no?" (about 1 in 3 say they'd do it anyway, so start) → "What would their biggest objection be?" (handle it directly) → past agreements ("Does your partner approve of struggling sales? Then why would he oppose fixing it?") → forgiveness over permission.
- **Hidden dialogue / tone** (from a book he doesn't name): words reach the logical brain, tone reaches the emotional brain, which decides. **Conviction corrects tone.** If you believe, you'll say it right.
- **Six Cs of scaling**: Closer sequence; Consistent daily training & conviction; Call recordings; Communication cycles & feedback; Cut the fat fast; Competition & career path.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Scripts must be questions, not paragraphs, so a rep who gets lost can find the next question. Reps memorize only the ~30-second stories and the objection handles.
- Pitch under 3 minutes.
- Daily training 60 min/day, 5 days/week: 25 min reading the script aloud with correct tone (restart on a mistake), 5 min drilling objections, 30 min reviewing a recorded call (what went right, what went wrong, what you'll do next time).
- Give one correction at a time (golf swing analogy).
- One-on-ones weekly for new reps, every other week for experienced ones; a daily wrap-up to keep morale up.
- If a rep can't close in 1–2 weeks (unless the sale is very complex), cut them. Taking a 6 to a 9 is easier than a 2 to a 5. Don't wait six months for someone to "turn the corner."
- Competitions in **6-week cycles**: a 10–15% group goal, small teams of about four, best paired with worst.
- Career path milestones: e.g. 50/100/150 deals each raise pay per deal slightly ($500 → $525 → $550).
- Keep inbound and outbound separate, with separate setters and closers for each, once lead flow allows.
- Train reps on the prospect, not the product.

## Playbook steps
1. Run your current script through CLOSER and add any missing milestone questions.
2. Write the three-pillar pitch plus a 30-second story for each pillar.
3. Script objection handles for price, decision maker and stall.
4. Reinforce after the sale: a personal CEO welcome video. The customer decides in the first 48 hours whether they like you.
5. Build conviction daily: read testimonials aloud, fix the product, keep improving it, and bring the head of support into sales meetings when sales dip.
6. Record every call (he recommends Gong) and use hot-streak calls as training footage.
7. Run daily training, feedback cycles and 1:1s; cut fast; run 6-week competitions and a career ladder.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Paragraph scripts that leave reps "free balling."
- Long pitches that list features and deliverables.
- Reps who don't believe in the product. Bad reviews or angry callers kill their tone.
- Blaming customers for lack of results instead of fixing the product.
- Training reps on the product instead of the prospect.
- Not recording calls (it's also a compliance issue).
- Piling many corrections on a rep at once.
- Routing every lead type to the same reps.
- Getting fired up after a seminar, then backsliding after 2–3 days without process.

- He cites about $110M sold over the phone by reps who were nearly all under 27.
- Six Cs used to build a 3,000-person affiliate base for the physical products company, adding $35M in sales.
