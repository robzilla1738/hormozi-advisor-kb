# Leila Hormozi Explains Acquisition.com's Investment Strategy

Speaker: Leila Hormozi (not Alex), interviewed by a host who helped build Kevin O'Leary's YouTube channel. Clip from a longer podcast.

## Core thesis
Acquisition.com adds value to portfolio companies through **Who / What / How**: it recruits their leadership (Who), fixes their strategy or adds network and brand leverage (What), and supplies subject-matter experts for as-needed implementation (How). They only invest where they can provide outsized value. She describes them as more of a conglomerate than PE.

## Frameworks & models
- **Who**: an in-house recruiting function recruits all portfolio leadership teams. She personally interviews leaders.
- **What**: three investment theses: (1) the strategy is off or "ten percent away" and they know which direction to take it; (2) the company would benefit from the portfolio network; (3) the company would benefit from their brand or audience.
- **How**: SMEs in marketing, sales, CRO, HR and finance work on projects with founders and leadership while permanent hires get recruited, so the company isn't broken by "adding seven people at once."
- **Strategy cascade**: what the company wants to become, then 3-year, 1-year and this-year plans, then the how.
- **Better vehicle for the same skill**: information sold as a business opportunity becomes a licensing model.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- They promote a business on their channels only if all three hold: (1) they own 50% or more, (2) they absolutely believe in the product, (3) it can sustain the inflow, judged from their impression metrics. That favors e-commerce and SaaS over service businesses.
- Don't take a deal unless you can provide outsized value. Then 51% isn't "grinding" the founder.
- Be very selective about who appears on their channels, with no hard-sell format.

## Playbook steps
Diagnose the strategy → recruit leadership → bridge skill gaps with SME consultants → (later) feature the company in show-style breakdowns or interviews without a sales pitch.

## Mistakes she warns against
- Overloading a company with many hires at once.
- Promoting a business that can't handle the demand.

## Examples / case details
- Photography studio education business: it sold a roughly $2,000 info product on opening studios (about $10–15K down, around $250K average first-year revenue). They converted it to licensing, now about 40-some licensee-operated locations, audited at over $60M.
- Leadership gender balance is unintentional. With a female CEO, more women apply.
- The host's aside: Shark Tank sharks often restructure deals after the show to be fair to founders (e.g. avoid taking 51%). Rob Dyrdek runs a similar model.
