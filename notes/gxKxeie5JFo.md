## Core thesis
A case study of a hard-money (private) loan follow-up. As the lender, you set the deal structure, so you control the upside and the downside. Getting rich is about avoiding downside and "making fewer mistakes," not chasing upside. The bigger lesson was trust: the borrower flagged an invoice error in Alex's favor that his own lawyer told him to keep quiet about. (Captions render his name as "Alex Rossi"; the portfolio is said to do ~$85M/yr.)

## Frameworks & models
- **Downside-protection layers** for a $2.5M note on a distressed 334-unit Memphis building bought from foreclosure (condemned, squatters):
  1. Personal guarantees from both buyers, each with net worth over $2.5M.
  2. Collateral value: three offers at $5M–$5.6M, plus an accepted buyer with $300K earnest money down.
  3. First position on the title. His cash paid off the prior lienholder, so no other liens sit ahead.
- **Payment terms**: 4%/month ($100K/month on $2.5M). The rate doubles to 8% ($200K/month) if the loan runs 4+ months, priced for the higher risk. A **two-month minimum carry** means a minimum of $200K (8%) even if repaid immediately.
- **Three circles for vendors and mentors** (from his earlier video): skill, ethics, hard work. His lawyer failed on skill; the borrower's lawyer failed on ethics.
- **Trust compounds**: each loan repaid as agreed builds trust, which enables bigger deals ($10M, $50M).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- In private lending you set the terms; structure them to limit downside, and let the other party choose whether to accept.
- Make sure you are actually on the title, in first position. "This is how a lot of deals get messed up."
- Lending returns are taxed as ordinary income (~50% for him), so he needs high returns. He'd still do it again for at least a 10–20% after-tax return.
- Fire vendors who make skill errors on what you pay them to double-check, and advisors who make ethical errors.

## Playbook steps
1. Layer protection: guarantees → verified collateral value (offers, earnest money) → first-lien title position.
2. Set a monthly rate, a step-up if the loan runs long, and a minimum carry.
3. Verify payoff invoices yourself; don't rely only on lawyers.
4. Build repeat relationships with borrowers who prove trustworthy.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Thinking only about upside ("everybody who's poor buys lottery tickets").
- Not confirming lien and title position.
- Trusting counsel blindly. His lawyer under-billed the payoff ($275K stated vs the agreed amount).
- Taking short-term gains at the cost of your reputation: "you only get one name."

## Examples / case details
- The deal was meant to close in 60 days but took ~100+ days. Agreed payoff: $300K plus ~$3,333/day after, for about $350K of interest. They got back ~$2.85M on the $2.5M principal. After ~50% tax he nets ~$175K.
- He says this is why he doesn't sell masterminds: moving money earns ~$400K for doing nothing.
