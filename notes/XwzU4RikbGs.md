## Core thesis
You're busy and broke because you're working on too many things ("four parties on one Friday"), so nothing reaches critical mass. Strategy is prioritizing limited resources against unlimited options, and a priority means one first. Change costs ~20% up front, so most tests shouldn't be run. Leave what's at benchmark alone, attack the real constraint (often the part you dislike and outsource), and accept that execution, not ideas, is the bottleneck.

## Frameworks & models
- **Magnifying glass / four parties**: limited network and resources split across ventures means none hit critical mass, word of mouth or brand. Entrepreneurs got reinforced for quitting once (job → business) and over-learn "start new things."
- **Strategy = prioritization of limited resources against unlimited options**: what you will and won't do. "Priority" is singular; "commitment is the elimination of alternatives"; decide = "to cut off."
- **Biggest productivity hack**: do nothing besides the task. Productivity systems are often "creative ways to procrastinate."
- **One-year certainty test**: of your priorities, if you could guarantee only one a year from now, which gets you furthest? Contingencies reveal the true priority.
- **Linear beats parallel**: one company-wide focus finishes faster because everyone feels responsible for the bottleneck.
- **Cost of change**: about a 20% guaranteed drop before any uncertain upside. Rule: only take a shot with at least 20% upside, given that the gain isn't even guaranteed. Constant changes keep performance below the do-nothing baseline.
- **Leverage at the ends of the funnel**: either a product so good people talk about it endlessly, or marketing so good everyone knows. People obsess over the middle (conversion). "You can only convert 100%."
- **Benchmark rule**: if the metrics for the thing you love are at or above industry benchmark, leave them alone.
- **"Make the details perfect; limit the number of details"** (Jack Dorsey). Raising Cane's: tenders, fries, Texas toast, slaw, sauce, drinks. He says the owner owns every location and is a billionaire in his 40s.
- **Patience = knowing what to do in the meantime**: keep a big "ridiculous ideas" list (his has 200+ items across AI, traffic, CRO, LTV, ascension and renewal). He has pulled 3 in 18 months. Moving the to-do list into the idea list relieves anxiety.
- **Buffett's 20-punch card** → one punch per year: the one optimization you'd choose.
- **Small business paradox**: most ideas, least capacity.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- More than one priority means no priorities.
- Don't run tests (e.g., small CRO hacks) that aren't the constraint between your current level and the next (e.g., $3M → $10M).
- Stuck at $1M/$3M/$10M for years usually means changing too much and working on the wrong thing.
- Customers have zero context. You tire of your business before they notice it.

## Playbook steps
1. List current initiatives; apply the one-year certainty test.
2. Pick one; focus the whole company on it, in sequence.
3. Freeze everything at or above benchmark.
4. Put all other ideas on the idea list.
5. Re-run proven processes without novelty (e.g., events with "zero new things").

## Mistakes he warns against
- Running multiple ventures or brands at once.
- Fiddling with the part you're good at (sales) while neglecting the real constraint (top-of-funnel marketing).
- Outsourcing your constraint to a cheap contractor.
- Perfectionism across too many details.

## Examples / case details
- His first company stalled at ~$3M/yr despite working every waking hour.
- Kira, a single mom of five, ran publishing, a TV show, events and women's business coaching, making a few million combined: "three or four mediocre parties."
- A founder kept re-engineering her pitch and conversion every 1–2 months while organic was outsourced at $3K/mo. Conversion stayed flat while leads fell. Her team kept relearning systems and her positioning got confused.
- A shorts editor focused on one platform got more views than he had previously gotten from two platforms combined.
- Monthly ACQ events: changing nothing produced the best NPS four times in a row.
- Promo: monthly ACQ events.
