## Core thesis
Master the **give:ask ratio**. Every ask (commercial) costs goodwill, not money. Give until the audience asks you, give in public and sell in private, and under-ask so the audience, which is the real asset, compounds. Holding back asks makes later asks 10–100× bigger. The video previews $100M Leads.

## Frameworks & models
- **Give until they ask**: his tweak on Gary Vee's "jab jab jab right hook" / give-give-ask. When people ask you, the audience is ready for an offer.
- **Give in public, sell in private**: goodwill is deposited at scale (reciprocity) and funnels back to one person.
- **The audience is the asset, not the content**: a post disappears in days but adds people permanently, provided you don't over-ask.
- **Media benchmarks**: TV runs ~47 min content to 13 min ads per hour; Facebook ~1 ad per 4 posts, about 3:1, which he treats as the *maximum* ask rate.
- **Two ask types**: *Integrated* (mid-content plug, best for long-form like books, videos, podcasts and blogs) and *Intermittent* (a separate post between content, best for short-form).
- **Declaration of business**: a first light-ask post telling people what you're doing now and offering something free. Your existing goodwill from friends, college and work counts.
- **Patience operationalized**: "figuring out what you do in the meantime." You don't actually wait.
- **Supply/demand lever**: content shifts demand up while you control (cut) supply → pricing power and profits. Aim for far more demand than capacity.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Never exceed ~3:1 give:ask. His preference for long-form integrations is ~98% give / 2% ask.
- Short-form intermittent asks: no more than ~3%, roughly once or twice a month if posting multiple times a day.
- If you must ask for income: ~30:1 give:ask ("10 times" the 3:1 ratio).
- Always under-ask. Accept "leaving money on the table" early, because asks held for 12–24 months can be 10–100× bigger.
- Turn down early endorsement and partnership deals because your audience will be much larger later. Example: he declined mentoring requests when he started getting early engagement.

## Playbook steps
1. If you have no content yet, show what you've been up to. 2. Post a declaration of business with a free offer (light ask). 3. Give value for free to a small group to get proof and testimonials. 4. Keep giving publicly with integrated or intermittent asks within the ratios. 5. Sell in private (DMs). 6. Keep demand above supply and raise price.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Learning the wrong lesson from one successful ask and asking more and more. A friend's podcast lost goodwill, listenership dropped and revenue fell.
- Draining the audience early ("fully right hook").
- Trading future equity or reach for an early deal.

## Examples / case details
- His April 9 declaration post (~10 years earlier): a free personal training project for almost a year where clients donated $500–$1,000 to a cause of their choice so they'd value it. That built transformations and a website, and the post got his first 20 paying clients.
- $100M Leads launch: all eight advertising methods were used; ~200,000 registered for the launch live (Saturday, Aug 19).
- Tequila founder turned down The Rock asking for two-thirds. The Rock's own brand (Teremana) is said to be worth ~$6B. Alex reads it as possibly better for The Rock to have waited and built his own.
- Content for Acquisition.com was a test to attract $1M/$5M/$10M *profit*/yr businesses to invest in, and it worked.
