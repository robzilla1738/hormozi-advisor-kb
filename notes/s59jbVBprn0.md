# Entrepreneurs, stop starting new businesses. Fix the one you've got.

## Core thesis
When a business plateaus, starting a new business or product line is the wrong answer. The real reason is almost always "I don't know how." Businesses grow to the level of the entrepreneur's incompetence, so the next business will cap at the same level. Find the single constraint, phrase it as a solvable question, and put all your effort there (theory of constraints).

## Frameworks & models
- **Theory of constraints**: a system grows until it hits a constraint. Remove it and it grows until the next one. At any moment there's really only one true constraint.
- **Only two legitimate plateau reasons**: (1) you've saturated the total addressable market (and even then you'd hop verticals, e.g. poodle food → other dog breeds); (2) the market itself shrinks (a friend's newspaper ad-translation business while newspapers shrank ~25% a year, compounding, down to ~5% of the market size 10 years earlier). Everything else is "you just don't know how."
- **Businesses grow to the level of the entrepreneur's incompetence**: a $4M entrepreneur builds a $4M next business. The cap is set by beliefs, traits and skills; change one of those or stay capped.
- **Better question**: "What's stopping us from being at 5x the current run rate?" instead of "Why am I not growing?" He opens conversations with portfolio CEOs this way.
- **Phrase the deficiency as a question**: "How do I learn to recruit, manage and train a sales team?" / "How do I acquire more customers from a channel I'm allowed to use?" A question turns it into a solvable problem.
- **Sequential de-bottlenecking**: scale the sales team → uneven rep performance → onboarding/training → consistency → not enough leads → focus on leads.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Until you can clearly answer "what's stopping us from 5x?", nothing else matters.
- Ignore non-constraint improvements (pricing, messaging, LTGP) while the main constraint is unsolved.
- A new venture in a saturated market, with no personal track record, a new acquisition system and an undifferentiated value proposition = no interest.
- Test: "If we tripled customers in your current business, would you still want the new thing?" If no, fix the current business.
- You can't be CEO of two companies (operators of public companies essentially never are; Musk is the exception as founder, not operator).

## Playbook steps
1. State the current run rate and ask what's stopping 5x.
2. Rule out TAM saturation or market shrinkage.
3. Name the one constraint and phrase it as a "how do I…" question.
4. Put all effort on it; refuse other topics until it's solved.
5. When solved, find the next constraint and repeat.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Starting another business or product line because you're stuck. It feels good but won't grow past your skill level.
- Adding complexity ("three times as complex") when one business is already hard enough.
- Diluting focus across many possible improvements.

## Examples / case details
- An owner doing ~$550K/month (clinics → telehealth, grew in 2 years) wanted to start a physician-consulting business. Alex had no interest and asked why not take the current business to $5M/month. Answer: he got stuck. His real constraint was acquisition through alternative channels, because the product can't be advertised on Facebook.
- A weight-loss info-product creator doing ~$4M/year planned apparel, supplement and cream lines. Alex called it "a terrible idea": make the $4M thing make $10M.
- An owner doing ~$150K/month: the constraint was an unscaled sales team. Two weeks later he hadn't done it, and Alex said "we don't really have anything to talk about." It took 2–3 reinforcements before it landed.
