## Core thesis
Most strategies work. Progress stalls because people switch paths up the mountain whenever they hit the unknown, and each switch sends them back down. "We need perfect conviction about our plan more than we need a perfect plan." When a business isn't executing, diagnose culture, talent or training instead of changing strategy.

## Frameworks & models
- **Mountain paths**: mission = the summit; strategy = the path. Switching paths means going back down. You re-clear early levels fast, then get stuck at the same unknown level. The hard part is not knowing, and that's where people quit.
- **Fallacy of the perfect pick**: waiting for the perfect plan. This happens at every level, e.g. a $5M/yr owner.
- **Dull knife**: there are many ways to skin a cat; the sharpness of your knife is your ability to execute.
- **Execution failure = culture + talent + training.**
  - **Culture = the rules of reinforcement**: what gets rewarded and what gets punished. You reinforce it through (1) who you hire and fire, and (2) which behaviors you let go unrewarded or unpunished (someone 5 minutes late with no comment teaches everyone lateness is fine). Feedback format: **Start / Stop / Keep**.
  - **Talent**: your bar is probably too low. "Great arbitrage": a 50th-percentile hire at ~$75K vs a 90th-percentile hire at ~$115K, whom he says is ~5× more effective for "only 30% more." [inference: $115K vs $75K is actually ~53% more; his conclusion still holds.] Hire rainmakers.
  - **Training**: translate abstract values into observable behaviors. "Accountability" becomes a morning text to the team, weekly reviews with each person, end-of-day kudos, and asking strugglers what's hard, then drilling the skill.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Interview question: "What do you do in your off time / what are you obsessed with?" If their off-time obsession isn't the role's skill, they'll always be moonlighting.
- Higher roles: hire for actual ability. Lower roles: hire for potential. Prefer intelligence plus work ethic over experience.
- Stick with something for a year, or be willing to suck at it for 100 days in a row, and you beat most people.

## Playbook steps
1. Commit to a sensible strategy. 2. When stuck, ask whether the problem is culture, talent or training. 3. Culture: list the 20–30 behaviors you want; reward and punish consistently; use start/stop/keep. 4. Talent: raise the bar, pay for the 90th percentile, screen for obsession. 5. Training: define values as specific actions and check them.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Running nine businesses at once. He was CEO of nine (dental agency, chiro agency, launches, six gym locations) and made no money.
- Changing strategy instead of figuring out the unknown.
- Values workshops with no behavioral definitions. A solar sales company's two-day offsite produced six pillars and no behavior change.
