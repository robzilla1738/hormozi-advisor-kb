## Core thesis
Announcement/mission video (older, ~$85M/yr portfolio era): why Acquisition.com gives education away free. Thesis: the education system is broken, demand for niche high-value skills is unmet, and technology "democratizes consumption and consolidates production" (quoting Naval). Free content builds trust with founders, and the business model is to invest in them once they reach ~$5M+. Light on tactics; mostly a roadmap of his course sequence.

## Frameworks & models
- **Business-building sequence (course order)**: 1) Offer → 2) Lead generation → 3) Money models (client-financed acquisition) → 4) Sales (script/choreograph) → 5) Scaling sales teams (recruiting, comp, management, training) → 6) Pricing & increasing lifetime gross profit per customer. "Teaching these in sequence for how you would do it to improve and build your business."
- **Client-financed acquisition**: use marketplace money to fund growth; a "negative acquisition cost," collecting more than CAC within 30 days so acquisition stops being the bottleneck.
- **Value = surplus**: people pay for the gap between what they pay and what they get. "A good deal has nothing to do with the price, but everything to do with the value."
- **Free-content-to-investment funnel** [inference]: give skills away for free, then partner with or invest in businesses at $5M+ that want to reach $30–100M.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you struggle to sell or get leads, check the offer first: "usually your offer is poor."
- Build the offer first, before leads, sales or scaling.
- Backend informs frontend: more money per customer lets you spend more on acquisition.
- Release standard: ship when it's better than what the audience already has.
- Investment threshold: around $5M/yr revenue.

## Playbook steps
1. Fix the offer. 2. Generate leads. 3. Structure the money model so 30-day cash covers CAC. 4. Script sales. 5. Scale the sales team. 6. Raise lifetime gross profit per customer.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Thinking success is about marketing: "anyone can sell anything to somebody once." Product determines success.
- Getting customers to make sales instead of making sales to get customers.
- Expensive degrees that add no value and debt you can't escape (an "irreversible decision").
