## Core thesis
At each level, opportunities get sexier ("the woman in the red dress"), so you must relearn saying no at a bigger size. Bezos's "1-in-10 shot at 100x, always take it" is right mathematically but misses that most entrepreneurs have limited capital, time and team bandwidth. Use the three questions from *The Road Less Stupid* and assume the downside will happen.

## Frameworks & models
- **Three questions (The Road Less Stupid, as he uses them):** (1) What's my upside? (2) What's my downside? (3) Can I live with it, assuming the downside happens?
- **Casino with $30:** $10 bets, 1-in-10 chance of $1,000 → with only 3 bets you're unlikely to ever hit. Your bets are limited.
- **Bet rarely, bet big, on no-brainers** (Buffett/Munger: ~15 winning bets over ~40 years created their wealth).
- **Cost of change:** half-trained teams + simultaneous changes → no definitive answer and no result, yet you paid the full cost of change.
- **1% better every day on fundamentals** vs "1% better for two weeks then change everything", which kills compounding.

## Decision rules
- Testing a new follow-up sequence against a proven control: upside maybe +10%, downside maybe −50% → can't live with it → no.
- Optimism is the entrepreneur's Achilles heel: we overestimate the odds and underestimate the cost of the bet, especially when cash-constrained and hoping for a Hail Mary.

## Examples
- Several of his hardest lessons came from saying yes to bigger opportunities over ~1.5–2 years. Recorded during COVID at Bear Lake, mentioning "explosive growth" in the prior 90 days.
