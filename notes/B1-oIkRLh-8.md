## Core thesis
This is mostly a life and mindset video. Alex shares his father's three rules and his reactions to ~20 "dad advice" lines distilled from 600+ Twitter replies. The business-relevant threads are: ownership over victimhood, focus over being everywhere, decisions under imperfect information, emotion-free timing for big choices, "good opportunity vs *your* good opportunity," avoiding catastrophic mistakes (×0), judging people by actions, and the longest time horizon wins. It has little tactical business content.

## Frameworks & models
- **Dad's three**: (1) "Whatever you do, be the best." Your best is further than you think (Little Giants "is that your best?"). The downside is constant comparison and some resentment. (2) "They can never take away your education." His father fled Iran with ~$1,000 and no English and became a doctor. (3) "God gave you the power to ignore — use it": ask "Can I just ignore this?"
- **Fault ≠ responsibility**: it may not be your fault, but it's your responsibility.
- **Good opportunity vs YOUR good opportunity**: you have ~4–6 entrepreneurial "seasons" and few shots on goal. Praise what you don't choose instead of trashing it, including exes and past partners.
- **Big-decision protocol**: wait out the emotion ("never eaten as hot as it's cooked"). Decide with a full belly and a good night's sleep, ideally somewhere different. He cites a Persian saying: 3 days sober, 3 days drunk, decide on day 7.
- **String of extraordinary numbers × 0 = 0** (Buffett/Munger): avoiding mistakes beats making great decisions. Spot and remove threats, and you fail your way to success by default.
- **Love/hate vs apathy**: the opposite of love is apathy. You can flip between love and hate in an instant.
- **Strong beliefs loosely held**: commit fully, detach when a better idea appears. "Hate an idea, love a person."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- By the time you have all the information, the opportunity is closed. Decide with imperfect information.
- If you try to be everywhere on the field, you won't be where you have to be. Pick the fork and accept the roads you won't take.
- If it's worth doing it's worth doing well, which means most things aren't worth doing.
- Judge people by actions, not words or intentions.
- About 20 minutes of prep beats most people in an interview or meeting.
- Measure progress against yesterday, not Tiger Woods ("good miss").
- Think from principles, not social proof. Impress yourself, not them.

## Playbook steps
1. Before a major decision, sleep on it, eat, change your environment, then decide.
2. List the catastrophic downside risks and avoid them first.
3. Filter opportunities: is this *my* good opportunity for one of my few seasons?
4. Prep ~20 min before every important meeting.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Victimhood or blaming circumstances.
- Trying to please everyone.
- Waiting for perfect information.
- Making decisions while emotionally charged.
- Half-assing favors: overdeliver or don't offer.
- Trashing past partners to justify the present.
- Identifying with beliefs.
- Seeking validation before acting.
