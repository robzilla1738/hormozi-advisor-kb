## Core thesis
Live consult with a concierge-medicine owner (Precision Health, Sarasota; bought 18 months ago at ~$1.8M rev, now $3M, $600K profit ex-comp, goal $15M in 3 yrs). For a high-trust, high-ticket local service the fastest growth is (1) engineered referrals — give members something valuable to bring a friend to at every touchpoint — and (2) event-based marketing (scan + giveaway + VSL + close) instead of a cold call funnel. Know your math and be willing to give things away.

## Frameworks & models
- **Referral offer that is "social capital positive"**: a $500 credit means little to wealthy members; instead give a real $1–2K treatment the friend gets (cost to you far lower). Referral-only, not run as ads, so you don't attract coupon-seekers.
- **Bring-a-friend perk built into membership**: e.g. the $5K membership includes ~5 one-off treatments you can bring a friend to; can't bring the same person twice.
- **BAMFAM + "who are you bringing?"**: book a meeting from a meeting, and at every booking ask who they want to bring — "we're not asking for referrals," it's framed as how we do it (doing health things together works better).
- **Wow-moment checkpoint**: find the point where results are universally felt (here ~day 90) and ask for referrals there. Touchpoints: close, 30-day, 90-day.
- **Newsletter PS CTA**: low-pressure P.S. promoting a quarterly giveaway (win a year of VIP service); everyone who's referred/enters gets something regardless. Giveaways let you show the price with no sticker shock and upsell non-winners to the $5K/$10K tiers.
- **Event play (V1/V2)**: attend local events with high-net-worth older attendees; offer the expensive scan (normally $150–$500+) free + raffle "a year of integrated health." While scanning (~20 min), they watch a VSL; close right after. V2 if it feels too fast: scan + VSL → schedule appointment → close.
- **Scalable version — own workshops**: Meta ads → one-day/half-day health workshop (charge ~$99 to filter and get a card on file) including the scan → VSL → in-person close. Same model as "diabetic dinners"; in-person workshops win high-trust sales.
- **Event calendar compounding**: try events; ~every third becomes a winner → put it permanently in the calendar (sponsor every year).
- Scaling-roadmap stage (10–19 employees, "productize to optimize"): coming problems = quality with new docs, sales training at events, emerging high-value cohorts.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Warm process (phone → 15-min Zoom with doc → close for ~$10K/yr) works for warm referrals; cold Meta/search traffic would need more friction/steps.
- Losing $50–55K on a bad agency is "cost of doing business" — you have margin so you can try a few until one works; one bad agency doesn't mean agencies suck.
- Math: 300-person event → ~1/3 get scans (100) → 25% convert = 25 sales ≈ $2.5M LTV per event.
- Spending $10K (or $50K sponsorship) on an event is fine if it can do $1M.
- Cadence: push from one event/month to one per week; target $200–400K from ~10 events in a quarter.
- With ~475 patients, giving away one $20K membership won't matter to the business.
- Around 1,500 customers expect clear cohorts; some will pay $50–100K/yr — create an offer for them.

## Playbook steps
1. Build a referral offer (real treatment, not credit) + bring-a-friend perks in membership. 2. At close, 30 and 90 days, BAMFAM and ask who they're bringing. 3. Add newsletter P.S. with a quarterly giveaway. 4. Attend one event per week: free scan + giveaway + VSL during scan + same-day close. 5. Keep winning events on a permanent calendar. 6. Then scale with Meta ads → paid one-day workshop → VSL → in-person close. 7. Systemize sales training so others can close at events.

## Mistakes he warns against
- No CTA in the newsletter / being too "low pressure" with a product you believe in.
- Weak referral incentives (cash-like credits) for affluent customers.
- Physician traps: thinking in small numbers, risk aversion ("golden handcuffs"), fear of marketing/selling/pricing, "no one can do it like me," ego.
- Being afraid to give things away in marketing.
- Making your own events before using existing ones ("two steps ahead") — the owner later did exactly that due to Sarasota seasonality (May–Sept slowdown).

## Examples / case details
- First event: spent ~$10K, made ~$70K; Alex thinks it could have done ~$400K with the right offer.
- Previous open house: $500 credit for member and new signup + raffles.
- Agency hired after interviewing 5 cost ~$50–55K with no progress; SEO fixed by next hire now converts well.
- Update: owner built a lecture series at venues near ideal clients instead of weekly third-party events.
- Alex's bull case for healthcare: wealthy aging population, supply constraint, competitors either massive or tiny, classic marketing/sales can yield 10–20× not 20–30%.
