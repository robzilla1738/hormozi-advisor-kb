# How wealthy people avoid paying taxes..my new plan

## Core thesis
Don't sell your business to escape pain or to de-risk. Exits are messier and smaller than the headline number (earnouts, seller financing, rolled equity, transaction costs, ~20% tax). Instead, treat the business like a rental property: borrow against its equity (recaps), take tax-free loan proceeds, diversify them, keep 100% ownership and keep refinancing as value grows. "The wealthiest people... buy and they build. And most times they don't sell."

## Frameworks & models
- **Story vs reality of an exit**: transition documents → earnouts → seller financing → rolled equity → bonuses/distributions/financing and transaction costs → ~20% tax → "X," often very different from enterprise value.
- **Business as a self-built house**: you built it with 100% cash equity and filled it with tenants (customers) that throw off monthly cash flow. A real-estate investor would finance about 80%; you can do the same.
- **Debt options**: minority or majority recap, dividend recap, and more.
- **Refinance at intervals** as value grows; this is how public-company CEOs live (loans against stock they don't sell).
- **"Kill a mosquito with a cannon"**: selling the whole business to fix a pain that one or two hires would fix.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you want to sell, check whether you're in pain. He wants to sell most when in pain and least when things go well. Fix the pain (hire) instead.
- Small-business sellers usually can't get a full exit and must roll some equity.
- Realistic loan size in his example is about 70% of value (30% equity kept), not 80%.
- Holding excess cash with no leverage ("negative leverage") was criticized by a PE managing partner as wasting redeployable equity.

## Playbook steps
1. Before selling, diagnose the pain and solve it with hires. 2. Value the business. 3. Take a loan or recap against the equity (~70% in his example). 4. Put the proceeds in other assets to de-risk. 5. Keep growing the business and refinance as its value rises.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Treating an exit as the aspirational goal.
- Selling during pain.
- Keeping most of your net worth concentrated in one high-risk asset (the business) without de-risking.
