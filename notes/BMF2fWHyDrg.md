## Core thesis
Live consult with Cody Schulson, the third-generation owner of Trophy Outlet (~40 years old, St. Louis area): $1.72M revenue, ~$110K profit (6.4% net), 40% gross margin, everything made to order. Gag gifts sell under a separate brand on Amazon, Etsy and TikTok Shop; one viral video drove $250K in December. The goal is $3.5M. Alex sees three levers: **consolidation, pricing, media**. With a 6.4% margin, small price moves double profit. Cody must also **pick one model**: (a) a gag-gift media/e-commerce business (faster, higher pricing power, always chasing cheap media), or (b) a stable B2B/league repeat business (slower, harder, worth more at exit). Cody chose the TikTok/gag route. Alex then adds an offer in the box and email to turn first sales into repeat customers.

## Frameworks & models
- **Litmus test for pricing**: about $530K/yr comes from repeat/internal customers, which is essentially free acquisition. If the business still barely profits, it has either high fixed costs or a pricing problem. Here fixed costs were "not too bad," so the problem is pricing. Explains fixed vs variable costs.
- **Cost-plus → value-based pricing**: "every business I know that does a cost plus model doesn't make a lot of money." Price on willingness to pay, not separate from vendor costs.
- **Two price-test paths**: (A) blanket ~5–6% price increases every month (at a 6.4% margin, +6% ≈ doubles profit; the math checks [inference]); (B) split-test your highest-volume items (5–6 ft trophies), optimizing **gross profit × units sold**. "You will usually make more money before you make less money." Elasticity example: double the price, margin triples or quadruples, 25% fewer buyers → do it.
- **Segment price sensitivity**: car-show/B2B buyers are price-sensitive; gag-gift buyers are impulse buyers ($8 vs $16 barely matters to them, but it's huge for the business). Test the gag line first, and +6% on "boring" trophies.
- **"Decide" = to cut off**: the "no man's land" of serving two masters. Leila's framing: "Which problem would you rather solve?"
- **Acquisition red flags** (value acceleration method scorecard): key-man risk from the retiring owner, 70% of revenue from one customer, and "why bother?" Don't buy a business just to get a supplier connection. Alex says he wouldn't buy it.
- **Allocation**: "a machine you put $1 in and get $13 out," so max out SEO (13:1 LTV:CAC through the agency, little of Cody's time). Add intent-based Google ads, at least on your own brand terms.
- **Posting volume**: no real cap ("you can post 100 times a day"). Each post reaches 1–2% of your audience. A Bollywood page posts 100 a day. The limit is quality.
- **TikTok flywheel**: post 1–3 a day on trends and memes ("hire a 17-year-old to scroll"), repost to IG; put ad spend behind winners; send winners to affiliates/influencers as briefs; model their winners. Focus on hooks and reuse winning hooks. "Go where the fish are": when you see blood in the water, go all in.
- **Offer in the box**: replace the 10%-off insert with BOGO / buy-1-get-2-free (fix the base price so it works) + QR code + top 3 gag gifts. Works because most orders are fulfilled by the merchant, not FBA.
- **Email**: mature e-commerce gets 30–50% of revenue from email. On a list of ~10K he estimates ~$1.5M is "sitting there." Two types: behavior **flows** (browse, abandoned cart, ~5-email sequences) and **long-term nurture** (start at 1/week, raise if domain health and open/click rates hold). Plain text, link to images (curiosity + deliverability). Value is entertainment, like newspaper "funnies." Positioning: a trophy is a vehicle for **recognition/status/cheap compensation**. Write all 52 emails in ~2 days. Onboarding: "reply back for funniest trophy of all time" (works as a double opt-in), then a clickable picture with a buy CTA.
- **ICE prioritization** (impact, confidence, ease).
- **"We make sales to get customers, we don't get customers to make sales."**
- $100M scaling roadmap: stage 5 ("product-ize"). Needs niche-down marketing: pick one avatar.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Made-to-order with many SKUs is fine: cut SKUs only when inventory or operational strain exists.
- Close any break-even/losing location that isn't the future of the business. Bring the best people over and "release" the rest.
- Older businesses that haven't raised prices 50%+ through recent inflation are leaving a lot of money on the table.
- If a price test goes too high, lower it back. "Not testing your price is the most expensive thing."
- Owner chooses the path. Exit value → recurring retention (e.g., 5,000 leagues × 12-year tenure). Fast cash → media arbitrage.

## Playbook steps
Final ordered list: 1) shut the losing retail location (~$50K rent saved ≈ +50% profit); 2) site price tests (≈2× profit or more; maybe +25% on some items → 4–5×); 3–4) SEO max-out + PPC/brand terms (one agency call; ~+50% / +10–20%); 5) offer in the box; 6) email flows + weekly nurture; 7) TikTok content 1–3/day + repost to IG; 8) TikTok affiliates/influencers + ads on winners. Easy fixes fund the bigger TikTok bet.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Cost-plus pricing with no pricing discipline, only raising prices when vendors do.
- Being afraid of price changes ("it's all between your ears").
- Pursuing two business models at once.
- Capping ad spend on a channel with a 13:1 return.
- Never emailing past customers.
- Weak 10%-off inserts.
- Buying a business with customer concentration or key-man risk.
