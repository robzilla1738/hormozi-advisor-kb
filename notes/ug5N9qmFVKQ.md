## Core thesis
Owners at $1–5M who are "bored" and want a new challenge usually break a working business to satisfy personal needs (novelty, status). The business exists to serve customers, not your inner deficiencies. Redefine growth as persisting at the same thing. Channel the urge for novelty into new front ends, and create challenge with constraints instead of new businesses.

## Frameworks & models
- **Trash-business neighbor:** sold his trash company, waited out the non-compete, and is starting the same business to sell back to the same buyers. "Why would I learn something new?"
- **Match the market and compound:** growing ~10%/yr for decades makes you extremely wealthy. Most people chase beating the market and break things. His math: $85M portfolio at ~10%/yr doubles about every 7 years, so ~28 years to $1B.
- **Where to put novelty-seeking:** new hooks, creative, ad ideas and front ends (which attract new avatars), or new product lines inside the business. Not new businesses ("lots of businesses and no money").
- **Multiple metrics of success + constraints:** once marginal utility of money is low, add constraints to the game. Can I hit $1B portfolio revenue working 2–3 days/week, in competitive shape, training 1–2 hrs/day?
- **Learn to sacrifice before you can choose not to:** "I won't outwork everyone, I'll out-sacrifice them." Buddha's middle path came after having riches.

## Decision rules
- Bored at the top of your game → don't enter a new arena where incumbents have a 10-year head start. Three internet-marketing operators ($10–20M revenue, ~$3–5M profit) each told him they'd start software companies because they were bored.
- He made the "business solves my personal problems" mistake for ~4 years.

## Examples
- College pledging forced 3 hrs/day of study hall → ~3.83 GPA. Afterward he spent all free hours on gym, library and class, never worked past 9pm, and went out most nights.
- Acquisition.com at ~$85M/yr at the time, goal $1B/yr.
