## Core thesis
Leveling up changes nature: first acquire skills to proficiency, then deepen through repetition, then build character traits (focus, grit, saying no) to stay the course. People get stuck by assuming growth is linear; at higher levels, the skill is often doing less.

## Frameworks & models
- **Three stages:** 1) fill holes — get proficient (not expert) in each needed skill to make the first dollar; 2) amplify via repetition ("get punched in the face"); 3) character traits to stick with it.
- **Woman in the red dress (The Matrix) = opportunity:** she gets more attractive the higher you go; discipline must level up.
- **Minimum effective dose vs. growth threshold:** like muscle, there's a no-man's-land between maintaining and breaking through; most work in the middle.
- **Grit (Duckworth):** stick-to-itness.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Proficiency in most skills takes ~2 hours of study then trying.
- You learn more in your first 20 sales than in 20 hours studying sales.
- If income has been flat for years, your skills are flat — usually a character-trait gap.
- "You can be successful at anything you want, just not everything you want." → pick one business.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Shiny objects; adding "one more thing."
- He'd likely not start the supplement company (Prestige Labs) again; Leila convinced him to delay it ~1.5–2 years after Gym Launch.

## Examples / case details
- Prestige Labs paid ~$600k/month in commissions to gym owners at the time.
- High school friend running contracting, roofing, and house flipping: "Pick one" — each could be a billion-dollar business, not all three.
