## Core thesis
"Recurring vs not" is binary thinking; the real question is *how recurring* is your business. Adding recurring revenue is, for most businesses, the single greatest way to increase enterprise value (he says it 2.5×'d LTV in their physical products business). He gives the **8 C's of recurring revenue**, each a continuum to push further along, not a checkbox. He explicitly sets aside the ethics of some levers and discusses only their economics.

## Frameworks & models
- **The 8 C's** (stickiness levers):
  1. **Consumption** — get people to actually use/eat/watch it (Netflix, Spotify have no contracts; better onboarding, more value, more habit-forming).
  2. **Collateral** — you hold something of theirs: storage units, Dropbox/Google Drive, CRM data.
  3. **Cost of switching** — time, money, effort, sunk cost. "The bigger the head, the longer the tail": a $5K upfront fee before $100/mo makes cancelling much less likely.
  4. **Choice** (lack of it) — patents, trade secrets, being the only one "on the island," differentiation, niching down.
  5. **Control of the money flow** — get paid first / off the top, less overt charges (Uber takes its fee first; payroll tax withheld).
  6. **Cause** — identity alignment (churches/tithing, charity, political donations, TOMS shoes); build movements.
  7. **Community** — leaving feels like leaving a community and your identity (commitment & consistency).
  8. **Contracts** — enforceable commitments; collateral like credit.
- **Every business is recurring over a long enough time horizon**: realtor wants every house the client buys/sells; the bank, escrow, agent earn repeat revenue on "one-time" home purchases; auto shops too.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Package a monthly offer as a fixed-term commitment: $12K program paid as 6 × $2K (then month-to-month) vs. $2K month-to-month; if month-to-month sticks ~4 months, the contract version likely gets 1–2 extra months; he claims this can 2–3× LTV / lifetime gross profit.
- People who *say* they'll stay 6 months stay longer than those who don't commit, even if not all do.
- Trade-off: contracts and big upfronts make the sale harder — balance them.
- Don't aim to max all 8; move further along each continuum.

## Playbook steps
1. For each of the 8 C's, ask "to what extent do we do this?"
2. Increase consumption (onboarding, value, ease, habit).
3. Hold collateral (data, stuff, files).
4. Raise switching costs and add an upfront fee.
5. Reduce alternatives through differentiation/IP/niche.
6. Get paid off the top where possible.
7. Build a cause and strengthen community ties.
8. Sell term commitments; sell to higher-quality prospects.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Saying "I'm not a recurring revenue business" (e.g., real estate, auto repair) — that's a short time horizon.
- Treating levers as yes/no checkboxes ("I have a community, check").

## Examples / case details
- Physical products company: recurring revenue 2.5×'d LTV.
- Netflix/Spotify (consumption), storage units/Dropbox/CRMs (collateral), drug patents (choice), Uber/taxes (money flow), churches/TOMS (cause).
- He says ACQ portfolio ~$85M/yr at time of recording.
