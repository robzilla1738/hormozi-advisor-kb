## Core thesis
Live keynote to (mostly gym) business owners. The way to make the most money is strategy, meaning prioritization: find the one move or constraint that gives the most return per unit of effort. Every action should get more customers, raise customer value, or lower risk/raise enterprise value. Do more and better before anything new. Fill the team with A players and have the hard conversations. Fix the product and back end (the "sandwich") before pouring on marketing, because LTV funds acquisition and great products compound through referrals.

## Frameworks & models
- **Magic-wand gap**: name your target monthly income vs current. Too many options → anxiety (lack of strategy/prioritization); no options → hopelessness (ignorance, solvable by getting informed).
- **Strategy = prioritization**: "how you choose to allocate limited resources against unlimited options." The costliest things are unknown unknowns; the best moves are often "off the page", where outside eyes help ("your mouth is an inch below your nose, but you still need somebody else to tell you you have bad breath").
- **Volume × leverage = output**: "If you're lazy, you better have a lot of leverage."
- **Theory of constraints**: every business has one limiting constraint. If you work all day and aren't growing, you're working on the wrong thing. Ask "why can't we 10× this business?"
- **Three objectives filter**: does it (1) get more customers, (2) increase lifetime gross profit, or (3) reduce the risk those stop (raise enterprise value)? If not, cut it. Then rank by likelihood of working vs effort.
- **Owner-dependent vs team-run**: two $5M/$2M-profit businesses. The team-run one is worth ~$10–20M (10–15×, sometimes 20×), so going from $2M to $3M profit adds ~$10M of net worth. "You become wealthy from the stuff you own, not the stuff you do."
- **What / Who / How**: list the whats, assign a who. If the same who has had the same what for 12 weeks (a quarter) and it isn't done, the who is the problem.
- **More → Better → New**: exhaust doing more of what works (e.g., double the cold-call team) and doing it better (call leads in <5 min instead of 30, better scripts, faster follow-up) before trying new (TikTok). "100 golden BBs," no silver bullet.
- **Impact × ease ranking**: number your list; high-impact/easy first; stop consuming information until it's done.
- **Branding** = consistently associating the unknown (you) with known/intangible ideas you want prospects to believe (clean, friendly, caring).
- **Compounding vehicle**: an exceptional product spreads by referrals, which are exponential. LTV/lifetime gross profit over everything: "If you print cash on the back end, you can get silly on the front end."
- **Intelligence = rate of learning; learning = same condition, new behavior** (also how to train teams).
- **Inputs → outputs**: GMAT problems solved correlated with score; he did 4 hrs/day for 16 weeks.
- **Core Four + four lead getters**: warm outreach, cold outreach, paid ads, content; plus customers (referrals), affiliates, employees, agencies doing them for you. **Rule of 100** daily: $100 in ads, 100 cold outreaches, 100 warm outreaches, or 100 minutes of content.
- **Location economics**: cost to open vs profit per facility (cash-on-cash). Seek a model with fewer, higher-profit units. Simplify: minimal square footage, fewer staff, less operational drag. Build "the machine that builds the location" (launch teams pre-fill, then hand to management).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- A mediocre person you think about first thing in the morning takes ~25% of your bandwidth. Start a 14-day performance improvement plan; say to their face what you say behind their back. ~1 in 5 turn around; exit the rest.
- "Whenever you're qualifying that someone's heart's in the right place… it's because it's obvious they suck."
- Big businesses differ from small ones in their number of A players and lower tolerance for mediocrity.
- Don't rebrand or chase new channels when nobody knows you yet.
- Don't spend on acquisition until retention holds: you want a business where you'd still be fine for 3–4 months with zero new sales.
- Don't depend on one acquisition channel; attention moves and every ad platform gets expensive.
- 100% cash-on-cash (e.g., $100K to open, $100K/yr) beats the stock market but still carries operational drag. A $1M store making ~$150K/yr is barely better than buying stock.
- Delayed gratification is "the single greatest skill" — gym owners have it for cookies, not money.

## Playbook steps
1. Name the gap. 2. Identify the constraint ("why can't we 10× this?"). 3. Filter ideas through the three objectives. 4. Rank by impact/ease. 5. Assign whos and fire or replace whos who don't deliver. 6. More → better → new. 7. Fix product, onboarding, and churn first. 8. Then run the Rule of 100 and recruit lead getters. 9. To scale locations: simplify the model, raise profit per unit, build launch teams. 10. Margin fix: find 1% of profit back every month for 12 months.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Chasing a 10× in 90 days, then jumping to the next shiny thing.
- Working nonstop on non-constraints (e.g., 40M-subscriber YouTubers optimizing media while having nothing to sell).
- Tolerating mediocre staff because they're "good people"; not having hard conversations.
- Running the business as the "Alex show".
- Marketing a mediocre product ("the sandwich sucks").
- Identity tied to the business (ego blinds you to obvious flaws).
- Optimizing tax on income instead of building asset value.

## Examples / case details
- Acquired company: 85% of customers churned within 2–3 months. Cut them, niched to the best 15%, raised price 10×, and sold more units → 10× company.
- Agency founder (same "go to zero" theme) earned ~nothing for 9 months while building the new model.
- Dentist buying-power association (~500 dentists, charge ~$10K against ~$30K saved). Owner wanted a $20–30M sale. Proposal: roll up ~10 practices/yr (~$500K each), keep 20%, then sell the roll-up machine (~$20–30M profit × 10) ≈ $300M vs ~$25M.
- YouTube media company with 40M+ subscribers monetizing only via AdSense: constraint = no product ("97/100 on media, 0/100 on stuff to sell").
- Gym Launch grew from ~$3M to $37M/yr once Leila built and empowered the team. It has grown ~40% since the 2021 majority sale under CEO Kale (Alex still owns 30%+) through many small improvements.
- $100M Offers sold at $0.99 with one post (~20K followers). It sells more every month; last month ~25,000 copies (~1,000/day), no marketing spend, ~14,000 five-star reviews.
- Franchise: 6 corporate + 14 franchised, ~$1M to open, ~$1.3M top line, ≤$150K profit per store. The fix was ops, not franchise marketing: cut a 500-item menu to the top 15, consolidate vendor buying, and recover 1% of profit per month for 12 months. [inference: +12 points of margin on ~$1.3M ≈ +$156K/store, roughly doubling profit; he says "tripled or whatever."]
