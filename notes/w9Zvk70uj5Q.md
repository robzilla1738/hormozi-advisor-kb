# How To Create Luck
id: w9Zvk70uj5Q / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w9Zvk70uj5Q / duration: 535s (~9 min) / tags: 12-mindset-leverage, 04-lead-gen-channel, 13-quit-or-pivot

## Core thesis
"Volume negates luck." Successful people understand probability; "luck" is a placeholder word for people who don't. Volume both overcomes bad odds and builds the skill that improves the odds, so you "get luckier." Most people quit because they're unwilling to suck. Lesson from coaching his 18-year-old neighbor doing cold calling for real estate wholesaling.

## Frameworks & models
- **Volume negates luck (and skill)**: increase n and eventually you strike gold; volume → skill → higher hit rate.
- **Bricks on the bridge**: many small skills must be laid before the first dollar crosses; the first deal/dollar is always the hardest. Avoid "half-built bridges" (learn 2–4 of ~10 skills, then jump to a new bridge).
- **Binary progress trap**: measuring only closed/not closed hides real progress (opening, holding conversation, fewer hang-ups).
- **Early-luck danger**: a first-day close (the 1-in-10,000 hit early) can mess you up; you may need to sift through extra no's to "pay back" the ones you skipped.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Illustrative: ~1,000 cold calls per deal for a beginner; 5,000 calls with no deal → "do another 5,000." (His example email math, 100,000 sent → 1,000 replies → 500 closes, he calls "not realistic math"; only to show probability.)
- Quit test: only quit if you can show the entire industry (here, wholesaling) has disappeared; otherwise the gap is skill, and skill comes from volume.
- Expectation check: top-1% income after one month of a new skill is unreasonable, so stop believing you should have it.
- After 4 years, one deal/month would put him at ~2× the average college grad's earnings, plus 4 years' experience (his claim).

## Playbook steps
1. Pick a proven model. 2. Do it the right way (his neighbor's first 3 weeks were "wasted" doing the system wrong). 3. Set a volume target over a long horizon (a year+). 4. Track sub-skill progress, not just closes. 5. Keep laying bricks until the first dollar crosses.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Expecting to be a millionaire in year one (social media expectations).
- Ego refusing to be bad: "that's what stops 99% of people."
- Hopping between opportunities.
- Over-analyzing an early lucky win.

## Examples / case details
- 18-year-old neighbor pushing off college to cold-call for wholesaling; "night and day" better after a month despite no deal.
