## Frameworks & models
- **Validity test for frameworks**: start with an anecdote of what worked, extract the principles, then try to break them (did a version without those traits also work?). "If I can make something that no one can disprove then they become canon." Most marketers' systems "you can immediately break," so they don't spread.
- **Compression editing rule**: if you read two sentences and only one is needed to keep the meaning, cut the other. Repeat. The book went from 1,000+ pages to about 270, aiming for "tweet after tweet."
- **VC vs everyday-business growth**: VCs "spray and pray" across ~50 investments because one 100× has to cover the portfolio. Fund return timelines drive the founder-pressure tactic. Non-winner-take-all businesses (mortgage, insurance, photography) can grow without that pressure.
- **Most important thing first** (macro calendar): no meetings until noon or 1pm, and the morning block goes to the current top priority. Right now that's writing.
- **Discipline reframe (Chris, citing Trevor)**: the "most disciplined" often just found an enjoyable substitute ("find your pickleball"; 90 minutes ≈ 1,000 calories).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- "I'd rather have all of our businesses triple over 5 years… and not burn 49 relationships." "I'd rather take a 10x when I can get it than force a 100x and lose a 10x."
- Buy any experience you can from anyone willing to sell it: "I'd rather get the lesson without the scar."
- Fix your weakest area first. He trains body parts in order of weakness, and a visible weakness moves up the priority list. [inference: the same constraint-first logic as his business advice; "not my deficiency, not my constraint, not my problem."]
- Recovering from setbacks: drop to the lowest load that doesn't hurt, then add 10 lb per week. Don't stop entirely.

## Playbook steps
Book-writing process: 1. Figure out what to say (the longest phase). 2. Write it (the easiest). 3. Cut half and fill in, then cut again, compressing repeatedly. 4. Pressure-test every principle against counterexamples with his co-writer (Trevor).

## Mistakes he warns against
- Program-hopping out of ignorance. In his 20s he chased each sales letter (kettlebells, bodyweight, isometrics) before learning "the few things that matter."
- Publishing frameworks that can be easily disproven.
- "A beautifully sounding theory that isn't effective in the real world is pointless." The validity of the model is how much money you make (said by Chris, and Alex agrees).
