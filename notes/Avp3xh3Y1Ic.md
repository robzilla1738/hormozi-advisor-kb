## Core thesis
Recorded after his mother died unexpectedly. Mental toughness = the chance a bad thing changes how you act against your goals. It isn't binary. It has four independent components you can rate and train, which makes it a skill rather than a trait.

## Frameworks & models
**Four components of mental toughness** (on a behavior-over-time chart: baseline → bad event → deviation → recovery → new baseline):
1. **Tolerance:** how much or how long hardship before behavior changes (fuse length).
2. **Fortitude:** how far behavior falls once triggered (a 5-minute walk vs quitting your job, divorcing, drugs).
3. **Resilience:** how long to restabilize (5 minutes vs 5 years).
4. **Adaptability:** new baseline vs old. Higher = hardship beat strength *into* you, same = medium, lower = beat it out of you. Any permanent change, positive or negative, is "trauma" by his definition (a lasting behavior change from an aversive event) and can be a force for good.
- Examples: 10/10 person ("life happens for them") vs 0/10 person (breakup → +50 lb, loses job, drugs, never recovers). Mixed profiles, e.g. high tolerance with low fortitude ("long fuse, but when they snap, they snap").

## Playbook steps (training each one)
- Rate yourself on all 4 and work on the biggest gap.
- **Low tolerance:** be more upset about letting something control you than about the thing itself. "Don't hand them the keys to your mental car."
- **Low fortitude:** notice the first deviation, don't justify it, recall where this road led last time. Stops the "bad things come in threes" snowball.
- **Low resilience:** return to normal behavior regardless of feelings. Break the link between feeling and action. (Easiest for him to improve.)
- **Low adaptability:** ask "how can this serve me?" What would the main character of a happy-ending movie do next? Guilt (breaking your own rules) and shame (breaking others') are useful only until they change behavior.
