# The Oh Sh*t Moment (ALEX HORMOZI)

## Core thesis
Early COVID (spring 2020) Gym Launch message to gym owners: don't wait for reopening. Many members won't return ("the oh sh*t moment"), because forced adoption dragged remote fitness ~10 years forward and "convenience and speed always win." Owners should think like business owners, not gym owners: learn phone sales and build a remote program now, with wider reach, cheaper leads and higher margins. The tactical numbers are time-bound, but the principles generalize.

## Frameworks & models
- **Denial vs adaptation**: people rationalize not changing, as Blockbuster did ("people value the experience"). Always bet on convenience.
- **Business owner vs gym owner**: a $1M/yr income from one facility is unrealistic unless it's huge (e.g., 6,000 members at $100/mo, which needs a ~$5M build-out). Remote removes the capacity and radius caps.
- **More at-bats beat a higher close rate**: remote gyms got ~40% of leads onto a sales call vs ~20% in person. Closing 29% by phone vs 42% in person nets ~11.6% vs ~8.4% of leads closed (math checks; he cites "a consistent 11%").
- **Radius math**: reach grows with the square of the radius. A 24-inch pizza is 4× a 12-inch, and 5→25 miles is ~25× the area (he says "10 or 20 or 50×"). Bigger targets give cheaper, effectively unlimited leads. A "semi-local" appeal adds trust for first-call sales.
- **Unit economics he reported (averages, at the time)**: about break-even on the front end, ~80% gross margin on the back end. $1,500 in ad spend gives ~$6,600 in upfront cash within 30 days. At $2,200 spend, ~50 people remain in a $200/mo continuity after losing ~44 who didn't convert, about a $10K/mo recurring business in 30 days. [inference: implied continuity conversion ≈ 50/94 ≈ 53%; he says "60."] He contrasts this with Gym Launch losing money in the first 30–60 days.
- **Use the external "why"**: COVID gives a reason to sell remotely. Keep consults by phone even after reopening ("it ended up being more convenient").

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Make decisions with math, not fear or discomfort with a new skill (phone sales).
- Post-crisis winners have lower overhead, higher margins and the ability to reach customers where they are.
- Strong operators with strong communities will recover better, but they should still diversify.

## Playbook steps
1. Learn to sell over the phone.
2. Launch a remote offer marketed to a ~25-mile radius.
3. Front-end near break-even, then a ~$200/mo continuity.
4. Keep the remote line running after reopening.

## Mistakes he warns against
- "Holding out a couple more weeks."
- Assuming customers will return to a less convenient option.
- Limiting marketing to a 5-mile radius.

## Examples / case details
- A friend in the space reported gym owners in a "denial phase."
- A troll claimed online fitness would vanish once gyms reopened.
- He says ad CPMs were back to normal within 2–3 weeks.
- He was tempted to start a remote fitness company himself, possibly by hiring ex-gym owners as salespeople.
