# What Millionaires Know That You Don't

## Core thesis
Two founders with the same simple strategy diverge 10–30× because of execution, and execution speed comes mostly from **pattern recognition in people**: knowing the "ideal scene" for each role, meaning what an excellent person in that seat looks like. That is why repeat entrepreneurs rebuild faster. Mis-hires are costly in time, so lower your tolerance for non-excellence. Learn what "right" looks like from someone who has built that department many times.

## Frameworks & models
- **Strategy is simple, execution isn't**: find something people want, charge a lot, deliver so well they tell others. Lawn care at $1B vs one barely at $5K/mo: same strategy, different execution.
- **Ideal scene**: you can't hire well for a role until you know what it looks like done right. Paying the "ignorance tax" to learn it is where speed comes from. Outside eyes who already know it let you skip steps.
- **Two ideal scenes for leadership hires**: (1) the sequence to hire department heads (marketing, sales, HR, finance, customer success, product, legal); (2) what a person exceptional at the role looks like, not just what the role does. Then find and duplicate that pattern.
- **Mis-hire cost cycle**: time to onboard + time to realize they're bad + time to offboard, while the department stalls and you can't fill the next role.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- A players are evident within the first couple of days. He has had A players decline, but never seen a non-A player become one.
- Keep lowering your tolerance for non-excellence.
- Don't promote by default: top salesperson ≠ sales director (may be a great individual contributor and a poor manager); "mom can do some math" ≠ controller.
- Don't take hiring advice from people one step / ~6 months ahead. Ask someone who has built and scaled that type of department multiple times over a long period.

## Playbook steps
1. List the leadership roles you'll need and decide the hiring sequence. 2. For each, get the ideal-scene profile from someone who has built that department repeatedly. 3. Hire to that pattern. 4. Judge fast (first days); cut non-A players quickly. 5. Once a role works, duplicate the pattern.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Thinking you'll win on strategy.
- Hiring when you don't know what good looks like, then hoping "it'll get better."
- Promoting top ICs or trusted family into roles they lack requisites for.
- Too much tolerance for mediocre people.
- Advice from people barely ahead of you.

## Examples / case details
- Went 0 for 7 on executive assistants over a decade because he didn't know the ideal scene. The current EA syncs immediately: she keeps a prioritized task stack with time estimates and slots tasks into his open windows ("I've got 13 minutes" → "do this"). He'll never be without one now.
- Portfolio (mostly e-learning, coaching, service businesses) repeatedly makes the "mom as controller" / "top rep as sales director" mistake.
- Acquisition.com cited at ~$85M/yr.
