## Core thesis
To solve a problem you don't know the answer to, use the "consultative method." Find the people in your community who already beat it, interview them all, and tabulate their tactics. Then keep only what nearly all of them share. He used this to solve churn for Gym Launch's thousands of gyms and found 5 shared retention tactics. Churn drives LTV and sellability.

## Frameworks & models
- **Churn math**: 3% monthly churn → 0.97^12 ≈ 69% kept per year (31% lost). 9% (his stated industry average) → 0.91^12 ≈ 32% kept (68% lost). He also loosely says "lose three-quarters." The math confirms ~68%. 3% is roughly top-1% gym performance.
- **LTV = price ÷ monthly churn**: $100/mo at 3% → ~$3,300 (33 months). At 9% → ~$1,100. That's 3× the revenue per customer and much more profit.
- **High churn makes a business unsellable.**
- **Consultative method / voting system**: (1) Poll the community for people who meet a hard bar: <3% monthly churn for 6 straight months. 20 of ~1,000 qualified, about the top 2%. (2) Round table: ask each one everything they do ("And then what? How? Why?"). His notes ran four pages. (3) Group into buckets. (4) Weight by frequency. A tactic only 1 of 20 uses is probably not essential. (5) Reduce to the fewest tactics everyone does.
- **The 5 retention tactics**:
  1. **Consumption accountability**: if a member hasn't shown up by ~Wednesday, escalate and keep reaching out to get them scheduled.
  2. **Exit interviews**: cancelling requires talking to someone. You get data on what's broken, and venting reveals pain you can solve, so "over half" get resold into a higher tier (DIY → coaching). A cancel becomes an ascension.
  3. **Member events**: a regular community component. Quarterly usually works.
  4. **Personalized handwritten cards** naming specific progress (Charlotte moving from the black band to the yellow band, down 10 lbs). Sometimes they include gift cards to bring friends, which creates referrals.
  5. **Personal check-ins every ~2 weeks** (every 14–21 days) unrelated to the service: "How's life?"
- **"Do not automate what you should systematize."** Once customers notice automation they stop paying attention, and it withdraws from the goodwill bank. Mostly better to say nothing than to automate.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Track churn as a metric. He claims 95% don't.
- Target <3% monthly churn for recurring service businesses.
- Personalization is required for the cards; generic thank-yous don't work.
- If you sit on top of a community, assume someone in it has already solved your problem.

## Playbook steps
1. Set a hard qualifying bar for the outcome. 2. Poll the community and invite those who qualify to a round table. 3. Interview exhaustively. 4. Bucket, then weight by frequency. 5. Ship the minimal common set. For churn: escalate non-use, require exit interviews with an ascension offer, run quarterly events, send personalized cards, check in personally every 2–3 weeks.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Not tracking churn.
- Automating customer-success touches.
- Thinking "this only works for gyms."
- Constantly feeding new acquisition tricks into a leaky bucket.

## Examples / case details
- Background: gyms founded 2013, six by 2016, funded from cash flow. Then two years of turnarounds for a percentage of revenue, then licensing the acquisition system to other gyms (Gym Launch).
- Exit-interview resell script: "No one ever contacted me." "You're in the DIY plan… you should be in coaching where we hold you accountable… that's our fault, we should have asked better questions on the front end."
- He mentions acquisition.com at about $85M/yr.
