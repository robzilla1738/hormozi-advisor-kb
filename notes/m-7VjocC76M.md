## Core thesis
Everything you sell is products, services, or access, and each can be physical or digital (6 categories). Stack categories to escape commodity status and raise enterprise value. When you need cash fast, sell a scarce, high-touch, prepaid access offer to a small slice (~20%) of demand, keeping the rest pent up.

## Frameworks & models
- **3×2 matrix**: Products (physical: supplements; digital: Netflix/media), Services (physical: massage; digital: marketing agency), Access (physical: concert tickets; digital: virtual event / Slack access to him).
- **Margins**: digital access and digital products are almost 100% margin; digital services less. Services are hard to scale (culture, training, onboarding), which is why buyers dislike them.
- **Pairing**: add a service to products, digital to physical, or access to anything, to differentiate and charge monopoly-like prices.
- **Cash-on-demand access offer**: a more individualized, higher-touch version of your solution, e.g. 5 people, 1:1 or small group, 1 year, big outcome, guarantee "or I keep working with you for free until you do," paid up front, no discount needed.
- **Controlled burn of demand**: sell about 20% of available demand and keep ~80% pent up (could sell 20 → sell 5; could sell 100 → sell 20). Grow the pool and the slots grow next time (20 → 40).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Need $5K–$50K quickly? Sell the prepaid high-touch access offer to existing clients/audience.
- Never sell out all your demand. Liquidating all goodwill means starting from scratch.
- To raise enterprise value, add low-hard-cost categories (digital/access) rather than more people-heavy service.

## Playbook steps
1. Map what you sell onto the 6 categories.
2. Add a complementary category (service to product, digital to physical, access).
3. For cash: design a limited-slot, 12-month, outcome-guaranteed access offer, prepaid in full.
4. Cap slots at ~20% of estimated demand.
5. Repeat later with more slots as the base grows.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Seeing yourself as only "a physical products business" and staying a commodity.
- Going for the biggest launch possible and burning all audience goodwill — "which is why they're always poor."

## Examples / case details
- Portfolio categories listed: publishing (digital), brick-and-mortar photography studio chain, certification business, online version for trainers, Gym Launch (licensing), Prestige Labs (supplements), ALAN (software for agencies working leads). He doesn't name minority-stake, personality-driven brands.
- Says high-ticket access buyers tend to be the least needy and produce great testimonials.
