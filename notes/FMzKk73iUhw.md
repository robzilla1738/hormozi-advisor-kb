# How To Get Customers So Fast It Feels ILLEGAL

## Core thesis
Ten-ish levers for getting customers fast: give away what others charge for, keep the founder close to marketing with fewer/better (well-paid) marketers, ask "what would it take to be #1?", sell in a vacuum, invest in pre-production over post, minimize "need-to-believes," split-test the right things, win via higher LTV ("ability to spend more"), build affiliate deals "so good it hurts," and use the truth (damaging admissions) as an ally.

## Frameworks & models
- **Free-better-than-paid lead magnet (5 steps)**: look at what others charge for → check your hard cost to deliver → give it away free → count leads → measure conversion. Ideally make it better than what others sell.
- **Fewer, better marketers**: marketing is like engineering — top talent produces 5–25× (even orders of magnitude) more output; build technical career ladders and pay up with performance incentives. The founder must stay close to marketing (MrBeast, Jobs examples).
- **"What would it take to be #1?" / "What else would have to be true?"**: assume the end goal, reverse-engineer 1–2 key things, check if you have the resources (often you do), then put everything toward them. His chain: best investor → proprietary deal flow → biggest business brand → books + more/better content than anyone. Credits Schwarzman (What It Takes): hard is a constant, so go big.
- **Selling in a vacuum**: sell *where* no one else sells (empty pond) or sell *what* no one else sells (unique tech — Elon); or create value at scale for inbound demand so the choice is "you or nothing." Removes price/terms competition.
- **Pre > post** ("an ounce of pre is worth a pound of post"): the idea/concept has the most leverage; spend time on past winning hooks, notes, props; post-production in education exists only to enhance comprehension. (Physics Wallah-style whiteboard channels as proof.)
- **Need-to-believes** (from Perry Belcher): minimize what a buyer must believe. Prestige Labs needed two (gyms should sell supplements; and ours) → hard sale. Real estate, Amazon, Uber need ~one or a "half belief." Free content/branding knocks out beliefs before purchase.
- **Split-test priority**: 1) offer, 2) packaging (headlines/subheads), 3) images, 4) third-party integrations (form/CRM/scheduler tools) — biggest winners came from #1 and #4; tool swaps "exploded" sales 4 times in his career. Wins are mostly permanent: one-time effort, ongoing gains.
- **Ability to spend more = LTV**: CAC across competitors is usually within 1–2× ; LTV is the real difference (Starbucks ~$14K LTV). Ad costs only rise, so LTV must rise too. Biggest LTV lever: raising prices.
- **Affiliate "peel-off" model**: carve a piece of your product (e.g. 3 massages) and let partner businesses sell it for whatever they want above your minimum and keep 100%. Your cost = delivery (3×$30 = $90) for a pre-paid, qualified customer; convert 1 in 3 to a $2K offer → $270 CAC. Recruit many small businesses ("nodes") not super-affiliates.
- **Damaging admission formula**: "[bad], [bad], [bad], BUT [good]" — "but" amplifies the good; claim the negative before they think it (Eminem/8 Mile). Small size → scarcity, personal touch.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- A-player marketer can produce 5×, 10×, 25× a B-player; in knowledge work the gap can be orders of magnitude (vs ~3× for manual work like lawn mowing).
- 10–30% lifts at 3–4 funnel steps compound to 2–3× results; doubling revenue at the same fixed costs can 3–4× profit.
- "Leads cost too much" is rarely the problem — usually LTV is too low. Google your industry's average CAC to benchmark.
- First campaign in a new market/offer is always the best; don't chase that CAC — build the business to tolerate worse.
- 20% referral fees don't change behavior; discounts need ~50%+ to really move demand. Affiliate incentives must change behavior or they're useless.
- Founders must personally know the core business, how to get customers, and how to deliver; most else can be outsourced.

## Playbook steps
1. Find what competitors charge for; give a better version free. 2. Keep marketing team small and skilled; founder involved. 3. Ask "what would it take to be #1" and reallocate resources. 4. Do more pre-production (hooks, notes, props). 5. Reposition offer to need ≤1 belief. 6. Split test offer → headline → image → tools. 7. Raise prices / expand LTV so you can outspend. 8. Offer 100-business peel-off affiliate deals. 9. Lead with damaging admissions.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Founder drifting away from marketing; bloated marketing teams (Gym Launch: 17 → 5 people and output tripled).
- Incremental (10%) thinking instead of order-of-magnitude.
- "We'll fix it in post."
- Cradle-to-grave selling that requires multiple beliefs in one step.
- Charging less than bigger competitors without understanding why they charge more.
- Chasing the first-campaign "high" of cheap leads.
- Lying in ads — ruins reputation.
