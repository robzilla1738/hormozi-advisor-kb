# I told myself I was wealthy until it came true.

## Core thesis
Entrepreneurship = beliefs + skills + character traits, and all three must line up at each level of growth. The stories you tell yourself about yourself shape beliefs and character. Instead of affirmations, which he considers "stupid," make *arguments*: "I am X because…" backed by past evidence. Despite the title, he rejects saying "I am wealthy" as an affirmation.

## Frameworks & models
- **Beliefs / Skills / Character ladder**: two poles and a rung. You need all three to climb. If two are already built several levels above you, fixing the lagging one gives an instant jump of several rungs, which is what an "overnight success" looks like.
- **Skills are straightforward**: course, book, content, practice, apply.
- **Arguments, not affirmations**: find past moments when you showed the trait, then tell yourself "I am this way because…" with that evidence. Use the same mechanism people use to tear themselves down. Over time it goes from active to passive, "a whisper and then a shout."
- **Leila's question**: "What's the evidence?" for negative self-beliefs.
- **Strategy = what you say no to** that still looks like it makes sense on paper. Focus is a character trait.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Diagnose which of the three is the constraint. He says marketers who can't sell or build, manage and lead a sales team are missing a skill, and fixing it takes them from $3M to $10M "very easily."
- Wisdom/discernment is the trait he works on most: "if you make the right call the whole world is your oyster."
- Integrity means sometimes giving up the quick buck. Patience: macro patience, micro speed.
- When employees "always suck," look at the owner's character before the marketing.

## Playbook steps
1. Identify the lacking belief, skill or trait.
2. For a skill, study and practice.
3. For a trait: recall past instances → state "I am ___ because ___" daily as an argument.
4. Challenge negative stories by asking for the evidence.
5. Borrow belief from someone you trust until you believe it yourself.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Blaming childhood trauma as a "convenient story" for anxious or angry behavior. "I chose to stop giving trauma power."
- Using "because" to excuse impatience, cheating or disloyalty.
- Jumping between ventures (gyms, chiros, dentists) without focus and discipline.
- Not promoting a product out of insecurity.

## Examples / case details
- A friend's fitness app (he was a highly ranked CrossFit Games competitor but not the winner): $20K/mo with zero promotion. Once he believed it was good enough to promote, it went to ~$150K/mo. Only his belief changed.
- Hormozi's gyms → licensing model, on a mentor's advice and with his wife's (Leila's) belief in him: ~$2–3M → $7M in the transition year (most growth in the last ~4 months) → $28M the next year.
- A ~$5M/yr entrepreneur blaming childhood trauma; a caller complaining "employees suck" → "Have you met you?"
