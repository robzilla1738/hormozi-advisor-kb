# You're Not Behind: My System for Outlearning Everyone

## Core thesis
Asking "how do I know it'll work?" shows it won't: "you are going to be the thing that makes it work." He says he has spent $500K+ on business education and has never had a negative ROI, even from bad teachers, because he aims to be the best student anyone has ever had. Two lenses explain unequal outcomes. Top-down is your belief level (5 levels of competence). Bottom-up is the skill stack you bring in: success comes from the bricks you already have. Replicate before you iterate. Every experience pays off, either directly or through inversion.

## Frameworks & models
- **5 levels of competence** (inclination × active/passive):
  1. **Positive active**: "no one has proven it can't be done." Will be the best student regardless of teacher (Elon's first principles; Roger Bannister's 4-minute mile).
  2. **Positive passive**: "he did it, so I can." A-minus players; replicate before iterate.
  3. **Neutral passive**: "let's see if it works for me." The majority; mediocre customers.
  4. **Negative passive**: "might work for others, probably not me." Half-asses it.
  5. **Negative active**: "I'll prove it works for everyone but me." The special snowflake; one-star customers. But they have the highest potential to jump to level 1 if they flip and channel that energy into proving it right, and they then become brand champions.
- **Domain audit**: you may sit at different levels in fitness, marriage and business. Copy the beliefs from your most successful domain into your weakest.
- **Bridge of bricks**: earlier programs gave you bricks; the last one gets the credit because it was most recent, "not because it was the most important." Meta-skills let you act on vaguer instructions ("build a nose strip company" → he can, because he has the stack). Programs fail when you're remedial on one step.
- **Inversion**: from bad teachers, bosses or parents, learn what not to do, so every experience has positive ROI.
- **Replicate before you iterate**: copy the teacher "to the tea," get the same result, then improve using your other skills. Iterating first means you never learn why it worked.
- "Stop trying to hit me and hit me" (Morpheus) / "There is no try" (Yoda): "try" allows the possibility of failure.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Don't make your success depend on teacher quality; that "puts the power in their hands."
- When something "didn't work," first check whether you actually executed it as designed.
- With level-5 customers, ask "do you want to be right or get the outcome?" Don't give up on them.
- Score your team (and yourself) 1–5 on this scale.
- If someone else in your industry did it bigger, "that's a me problem not a universe problem."

## Playbook steps
1. Place yourself on the 5-level scale for each domain. 2. Borrow the mindset from your best domain. 3. Diagnose missing skill bricks rather than blaming the program. 4. Replicate exactly, then iterate. 5. Learn from many teachers and stack them. 6. Use inversion on bad experiences.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Blaming the program or vendor when you didn't do it. The salon owner's gift card play failed because clients used the cards themselves: no referral name, no three-way intro.
- Iterating before replicating.
- Crediting only the last teacher and calling the earlier ones idiots.
- Wanting to prove the teacher bad: "like drinking poison and hoping the other person dies."
- "No one can sell like I can": there are people who sell better; you just haven't found them.
