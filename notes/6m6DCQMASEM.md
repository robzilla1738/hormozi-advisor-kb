Speakers: Alex plus ~10 business owners doing $1M–$10M/yr (hot-seat format). Captions are rough.

## Core thesis
Most "growth" problems are really something else. Fix LTV/price before switching ad platforms. A CEO of a sales-driven company has to know sales. Six simultaneous problems usually mean you're missing three good people. If a business can't afford to hire, the model is broken (10× the price or 10× the delivery efficiency), not short on people. A price increase is the fastest profit lever.

## Frameworks & models
- **Diagnostic, goal-based sale**: sell the goal, not the program. Price per week × the weeks needed to reach the goal (e.g. $129/wk, 40 lb at ~1 lb/wk = 40 weeks), add a reconditioning/maintenance period at the end, and guarantee "I'll keep working with you until you hit it, provided you do X/Y/Z."
- **Back-end affiliate attach**: supplements add ~50% more revenue with zero delivery. Sell on the 2nd touch (a nutrition consult ~48 hrs after the start, $500–2,000) with an assumed close tied to the client's daily routine ("put these next to your coffee… use the card on file?").
- **LTV-before-channel math**: adding a performance fee lifts value per customer from $3K to ~$10–12K, so 3:1 ROAS becomes ~12:1. Even if more spend drops the base 3:1 to 2:1, you stay at roughly 8–9:1 [12×⅔ = 8; he says 9]. Don't turn off a channel that works. Layer new channels on top.
- **How people learn sales**: (1) watch lots of game tape of good closers (modeling); (2) immediate corrective role play on one script section at a time until they "breathe it", so they can listen on calls instead of planning their next line.
- **Script as Bible**: step 1 in a sales turnaround is that everyone follows the script exactly. Only then do tweaks mean anything. Simplify: "most times when we look at a script we cut half of it."
- **Closer personas**: "killer" closers rarely make good managers; caring, attentive closers sometimes do (Jacob at Gym Launch moved from call floor → setting → closing → training).
- **Three legs of the stool**: acquisition, delivery, ops (HR/finance/legal: "make sure no one goes to jail").
- **Insult vs criticism**: an insult attacks character; criticism states the gap between what was said and what happened. Use it in partner conversations: "You own sales, we have no sales. Either you change what you do or I stop being here."
- **Talent spend levels**: first $250K/$500K/$1M hires are new territory, like first $5K/$10K/$100K-a-day ad spend. Buying back your time gets more expensive at each level.
- **Model vs people problem**: if the founder works all hours for ~$90K profit on $600K revenue, you can't hire. 10× the price or make delivery 10× more efficient first. "Trying to scale what you're currently doing is a bad idea."
- **Vendor-first channel playbook** (Leads book, agency chapter): hire an agency for a new channel, tell them you'll take it in-house in ~6 months, and have them train your hire.
- **Agency in-house transition offer** (flip side): offer to switch from done-for-you to consulting when the client brings work in-house, and stay until their team outperforms you. That extends LTV and makes you a teacher instead of a vendor. Consulting fee starts at ~20–30% of the DFY fee (e.g. ~3% of ad spend when DFY is 10%).
- **Media as marketing spend**: brand IP and content (characters, universe) are marketing. Fund them from the economic engine and cap spend by ROI. "Mickey Mouse has to sell shirts."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If close rate is 40%+ and ads stop being profitable above ~$1,500/day, the constraint is creative volume, not sales.
- Fix LTV before adding a new ad platform.
- Don't promote your top closer to sales manager by default.
- CEO of a sales-led business should get on the phones until they can teach the process. Otherwise the sales leader has leverage over you.
- Six problems at once means you're missing ~3 smart people (e.g. a service-department head and a lot head at a used-car dealer).
- Hire finance first: "find your salary in the next 90 days." He guesses ~$250K/yr in cost cuts and that margin could nearly double [his estimate].
- Local Meta creative fatigues in ~2–3 weeks, so make new creative every ~21 days. Keep Meta in-house (a young, tech-savvy local-ads hire). Use vendors for local PPC and SEO first.
- Want bigger clients? Go get them: outbound to high-value targets, niche content, or ads that call them out ("if you're not spending $100K/month, scroll past — don't mess up my pixel"), plus LinkedIn.
- Raise prices even without justification unless you have a demand or conversion problem.
- Partnerships: clear division of roles. If one partner is satisfied and the other isn't, have the hard conversation without pulling punches.
- Never say "creating value out of nothing."

## Playbook steps
Sales turnaround: 1) everyone runs the script; 2) owner takes calls to simplify it (cut ~half); 3) drill one section at a time through game tape + role play; 4) iterate the script once compliance is consistent.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Selling programs or time packages instead of the outcome.
- Shutting off a profitable channel to test a new one.
- Hiring salespeople and "hoping for the best" (~95% of companies can't train sales).
- Hiring when the model can't fund it.
- Key-client concentration (an agency with 2 whales = ~90% of revenue) while outbound sales has no owner.
- Keeping the same landing page for 8 years with no lead-quality feedback.

## Examples / case details
- Fitness coach: $2K/$3K/$5K for 3/6/12-month packages, LTV ~3×, LinkedIn single-image ads.
- Real estate/wholesaling sales team: deals fell from ~70 to ~40/mo after the manager left, the top closer was promoted, and the #2 closer left.
- Used-car dealer (former pipe welder): inventory went 15 → 30 → 45 → 100 cars. Vans make $6–12K margin vs ~$2K per car. ~$500–600K profit.
- Cannabis ad agency: $600K revenue, ~$90K profit, 4 months in. Verdict: model problem.
- Garage-door roll-up: $7M revenue, $1.5M cash flow, leads mostly from 8 home-warranty companies, ~1 marketing lead/month. Plan: vendor PPC, in-house Meta; acquisitions could "immediately triple."
- Anime clothing e-com brand: 14K-member Discord, mascot IP. Fund it as marketing capped by ROI; study Walt Disney and VeeFriends.
- Dentist (implants, 15 yrs, half referrals and half Google Ads): raise price, document the existing sales process by recording calls, and eventually buy other practices.
- Company Acquisition.com bought: Alex wanted to double the price, but after 9 calls the founder agreed to +50%, and close rates went *up* because the product had been mispriced.
- His father: booked out 9 years and still won't raise prices.
