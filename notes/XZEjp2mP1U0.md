## Core thesis
The "pi equation" (named after "pie," not π): **inflow ÷ outflow rate = hypothetical max**. He calls it the equation he uses most. Asking for inflow, churn, average price and current revenue tells you whether a recurring business is growing or shrinking, where it will plateau, what a customer is worth, and which of the three levers to pull.

## Frameworks & models
- **Use 1, customer cap**: new customers/month ÷ monthly churn = max customers. 5/mo ÷ 10% = 50. At 30 clients with 2/mo and 10% churn, the cap is 20, so you're shrinking from 30 to 20.
- **Use 2, LTV**: avg revenue per month ÷ monthly churn. $1,000 ÷ 20% (avg 5-month stay) = $5,000.
- **Use 3, revenue cap**: new MRR signed/month ÷ % revenue churn. $10K/mo ÷ 10% = $100K/mo cap. Currently at $200K means fix something; at $50K you'll double and then cap.
- **Combined**: new customers/mo × LTV = monthly revenue plateau. 20/mo × $5,000 = $100K/mo (vs the $20K/mo you see in month one).
- **Growth decelerates toward the cap** (an asymptote) because churn eats a bigger absolute number as the base grows. In practice businesses cap at about 80–90% of the theoretical max because of volatility.
- **Golden ratio**: monthly referral % ÷ monthly churn %. If referral > churn (e.g. 10% refer, 5% leave), the business grows without paid acquisition ("net negative churn"; he calls it "the promised land"). It doesn't last forever, because scale eventually breaks referral or churn.
- **Three levers**: price, churn (together these make LTV), and inflow.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Ask in this order: inflow? churn? average price per user? current revenue? Compare current revenue to the cap: above the cap = shrinking, below = growing.
- CAC budget from LTV: aim for about 3:1 LTV:CAC. On a $5K LTV, spend "less than $2,000" (~$1,667 at exactly 3:1 [inference]).
- Expect the plateau at about 80–90% of the computed max.
- To grow beyond the cap, change at least one of inflow, price or churn. Nothing else moves it.

## Playbook steps
1. Measure monthly new customers (or new MRR), churn % and average monthly revenue per client. 2. Compute the cap and LTV. 3. Compare them to current revenue to diagnose growth or shrinkage. 4. Set CAC from LTV (3:1). 5. Pull the weakest lever. 6. Build referrals toward referral % > churn %. 7. Keep doing it consistently: recurring revenue takes time to compound.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Judging a recurring business by current monthly revenue without projecting the cap.
- "Entrepreneurial ADD" / shiny object syndrome: quitting a working inflow before it compounds to the plateau.
- Expecting growth to continue linearly. It slows toward the cap.

## Examples / case details
- Born from his gym days (a recurring business with poor tracking). He wanted to "see into the future" and worked out the division while driving.
- Worked step example (rough, as spoken): 10 sign up, 2 leave → 8; +10 → 18; lose ~20% → ~15; +10 → 25. Net adds shrink from about 10 → 8 → 7. [Note: 20% of 18 is 3.6. He says "3" and also misspeaks 10% for 20%. The direction is correct.]
