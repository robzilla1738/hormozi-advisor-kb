## Core thesis
A four-level blueprint: (1) wealth fundamentals: own your way to wealth rather than earn it, focus instead of diversifying, play the long game; (2) tactics for the first million: hungry crowd, one avatar/product/channel, a valuable offer, the core four advertising methods, founder-led sales, paying yourself, activity-based goals; (3) stay rich and get richer: get rich once, market in four directions to build enterprise value, protect your reputation, compound; (4) enjoy wealth: define "enough" and play an infinite game.

## Frameworks & models
- **Millionaire** = net worth excluding primary residence > $1M (assets − liabilities); liquid vs illiquid net worth.
- **Earn vs own**: earn $2M pre-tax (e.g. $200K/yr × 10 yrs, saving 100%, halved by tax) vs build a business with $250K/yr automated profit and sell at ~4× = $1M. Similar income, but owning gets the multiple and time works for you.
- **Don't diversify** ("a hedge against ignorance," Buffett). Cups analogy: one cup overflows sooner than seven half-filled cups. The rich get wealthy on one stream and diversify only to preserve it. "It's not which one will work, it's which one will you work on."
- **Five stages of an opportunity**: uninformed optimism → informed pessimism → valley of despair (most quit and hop; "poo on both sides of the fence") → informed optimism → achievement → reset a 10× target.
- **Tower in 10 seconds vs 10 days**: rushing builds a structure that caps out. Sometimes the fastest way to $10M is to restart from zero and build it right.
- **Make → promote → fix the product → promote** cycle: don't crank marketing on a product people complain about; improve it until customers tell friends.
- **Market > offer > persuasion** (toilet paper during COVID). Market needs all four traits: pain, purchasing power, easy to target, growing.
- **One avatar, one product, one channel** to $1M+. Sell 3× as much of the same thing; any single channel can pass $1M.
- **Value equation**: (dream outcome × perceived likelihood) / (time delay [micro + macro] × effort & sacrifice). Hidden costs often exceed price. The offer changes marketing, sales, and delivery all at once.
- **Eight ways to advertise**: core four (warm outreach, content, paid ads, cold outreach) + lead getters (referrals, employees, agencies, affiliates).
- **Paying yourself**: risk tolerance decides. Brick-and-mortar napkin split: ~33% to you, ~30% to growth (new locations), the rest to reserves. Or a "watermark": reserve the cost of planned growth (e.g. 2 locations/hires per month), keep a fixed watermark in the account, distribute everything above.
- **Bank-account PRs**: a personal goal to keep raising your balance forces cash flow out of the business and lower personal spending; a full account means less-stressed, better decisions.
- **Activity goals**: make inputs the goal, not outputs. **Rule of 100**: 100 primary actions a day for 100 days (100 warm reach-outs, 100 cold reach-outs, 100 minutes of content, or $100/day ads) usually gets the first result. "Count in hundreds."
- **Scientific-method goals** (portfolio companies): problem (tied to a constraint) → hypothesis (if X then Y) → measure X and Y → did we do it / did it work?
- **Get rich once**: fortunes are made taking big risks with small money and preserved taking small risks with lots of money. Never bet the empire on a pot of gold. (Bezos long-tail quote.)
- **Quad marketing calendar**: market to prospects → customers, customers → repeat buyers, candidates → employees, employees → engagement and ascension. "Genius with 1,000 hands" = great at customer acquisition, bad at the people side.
- **Enterprise value**: a founder-dependent business making $2M adds ~$1M/yr after tax to net worth; the same business run by systems is a ~$10M asset. Acquisition.com takes $2–5M businesses to ~$10M at ~7× = ~$70M.
- **Reputation as a bouquet**: a brand is an association between something known and something unknown. One bad flower (scandal) taints all. Goodwill (customer surplus) compounds multiplicatively and faster than revenue (Teremana, Fenty, Proper 12). "Anything × 0 = 0."
- **Compounding**: needs a long horizon and no interruption. Location growth compounds (Panda Express added 600 in one year after 45 years to reach ~2,000). "Boring is what makes you rich." Patience = knowing what to do in the meantime (marshmallow-test kids used distraction, not willpower).
- **Two levels of wealth**: needs met, then playing the game. Lavish living ≈ $150K/month ≈ just under $2M/yr after tax (~$4M pre-tax); ~$50K/month after tax is "1% living." Price your actual dream list.
- **Infinite game**: the point is to stay in business, stay married, stay healthy. "You win as long as you never quit."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Under $1M: don't add products, avatars, or channels. If you can't pass $1M profitably on paid ads, it's a business or pricing problem, not a channel problem.
- Founders should do marketing and sales personally early on: it's cheaper, the skills are required, and it lets you tell real from fake hires and vendors.
- Build a sales script by backtracking from what made people buy; you'll need to teach it, because hires will never be as convicted as you.
- Interview ~20 people for a role, not 3.
- Decide a problem isn't a problem before solving it (e.g. no logo redesign if homepage conversion isn't the constraint).
- A 10% chance of a 100× payoff is worth taking, but never risk the farm once you've won.
- Never risk reputation for short money.
- In year 10, 10% growth on $100M beats the odds of a new venture hitting $10M in year one: don't restart out of boredom.

## Playbook steps
1. Pick one opportunity, ideally one you can own. 2. Validate the market (pain, purchasing power, targetable, growing). 3. One avatar, one product, one channel. 4. Craft the offer via the value equation; strip hidden costs. 5. Pick one of the core four; founder sells and scripts the process. 6. Pay yourself by a set split or watermark; set bank-account PRs. 7. Set input goals (rule of 100); run problem → hypothesis → measure → review. 8. Build the people side (quad marketing) to create enterprise value. 9. Protect reputation, let goodwill and equity compound. 10. Define "enough" and keep playing.

## Mistakes he warns against
- "Seven income streams" early on: spreading time across crypto, forex, flipping, Airbnb, ATMs.
- Hopping opportunities in the valley of despair.
- Outcome goals that cause easing off once hit (a friend who kept peaking at $10–30K/month and falling off).
- Reinvesting everything and taking nothing out (he lost his gym-sale money and had nothing set aside).
- Relying on a financial advisor whose incentive is fees.
- Buying status items to test them (he bought a Bentley and sold it at a loss ~6 months later).
