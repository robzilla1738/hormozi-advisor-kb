# Building a $3,500,000 Business for Two Strangers in 52 Minutes

## Core thesis
Live consult with Calvin & Air, owners of Basil & Co Thai Cuisine (Diamond Bar, CA): $3.5M/yr revenue, 19% net margin (~$670K profit; they called it "gross profit" but 19% of $3.5M ≈ $665K [inference]), ~100K guests a year, lines out the door, opening a 100-seat second location (the first has 72 seats). Their goal is 20 locations and $80M. Alex's view: the product (food) is what matters most, and marketing only speeds up the timeline. So the fastest wins are small pricing and menu changes in a high-volume, thin-margin business, plus simple trained server scripts for upsells, getting first-timers to come back, and reviews. Nail the model before scaling.

## Frameworks & models
- **Product > marketing**: "On a long enough time horizon, the product of the business is the only thing that matters. Marketing just pulls your timeline forward." Good marketing on a bad product only makes it decline faster.
- **Pennies on volume**: changing $16.00 to $16.99 is about a 6% price increase. With no drop in sales volume, margin goes from 19% to ~25%, about +30% profit (~$200K/yr; he says "~$220K"). Cross-check: 100K guests × 2 items × ~$1 ≈ $200K.
- **Credit card processing fee line** (+~3% on the menu) ≈ +15% profit at a 19% margin (3/19 ≈ 16%, so the math checks).
- **Surge / weekend menu**: a separate weekend menu with +$2 per dish when you're already turning people away, like Uber surge pricing or lunch vs dinner menus.
- **Menu layout rules (any business with a "menu")**: (1) highest prices at the top to anchor; (2) within each section, rank items by gross profit; (3) feature the items that bring people back. Add a "first time here? don't miss this" highlight box. Ideally the highest-margin dishes are also the most loved.
- **Cross-sell** (one of the "8 ways to make a customer more valuable," from his upcoming book): add "pairs well with [drink]" under each dish. Alcohol at 1% of sales → 6% adds about +25% profit (the industry norm is 5–20%).
- **Table tent with ONE item** (the rotating weekly drink special). The staff taste five cocktails each week and pick one, which gets them bought in and gives them a story to tell. Keep the look upscale.
- **Peak-end bias**: people remember the emotional peak and the ending. Give first-timers a surprise free dessert at the end, plus the manager's business card with "free dessert" written on the back as a personal comeback offer (better than a coupon). Customers can photograph the card.
- **Review asks at the point of sale (must be immediate)**: (a) "I can take care of a drink if you leave us a review," per person at the table; or (b) the manager ask: "your server Judy gets a bonus at X five-star reviews." Future-reward versions don't work. Review count compounds: a 4.7 with 13,000 reviews beats a 5.0 with 50.
- **AOV breakpoint**: a free gift threshold set just above the current average ticket ($80 weekday / $100 weekend vs a ~$60 average). Best for takeout and online orders.
- **Loyalty programs** (he's "split"; for a great restaurant, "the food is the loyalty program"; they matter more for med spas). If you run one: birthday freebie, first drink free, and **priority seating when there's a wait** (the strongest perk). Capture lead source at signup. Use **running starts** on punch cards (pre-filled punches make people 2–3× more likely to finish, per studies he cites).
- **Value equation** (offers book, p.56) applied: speed → skip the line; perceived likelihood → reviews; effort → pairings remove decision friction.
- **Training as skits / if-then behavior rules**: drill one scenario per pre-shift huddle, 5–10+ reps each (~20 reps/hour). The five scenarios: first-timer greeting (napkin/coaster marker) → drink special pitch → pairings/side upsell ("Do you want to go ahead and take one of those? A lot of people do.") → comeback offer (first-timers) or review offer (returning guests). Frame it for servers as dollars of tips per question.
- **Culture** = reward and punishment. He cites a McKinsey study: 1 in 3 strategies succeed, and culture is the difference. Put a leaderboard in the back for alcohol sales, with $50–100 or first pick of schedule as the prize.
- **Affiliates/partners for launch**: a pre-opening VIP influencer night (free or at cost, in exchange for content) with a trackable offer. Reuse their content as ads afterward. Pick guests with "the biggest megaphones."
- **$100M Scaling Roadmap**: he places them at stage 5. Weak financials are the reason he focused on pricing; they didn't know margin by dish.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- No percentage-off discounts. Either full price or free item with a clear path to becoming a full-paying customer. Prepay or close-now incentives are a different thing.
- .00 pricing suits luxury ($29–50 entrées). An upper-middle place should use .99.
- Review incentives must be immediate and at the point of sale.
- Only one item on the table tent.
- Nail it then scale it: before location #2, know the build cost, time to profitability, payback period, grand-opening plan, and the hiring/culture plan. "It might take you less time to get your second location, but it'll take you more time to get your 10th."
- Don't stack every price change at once if it's uncomfortable. The owner decides.

## Playbook steps
Final prioritized list: 1) add .99 to entrées; 2) reorder the menu by profit; 3) add alcohol pairings under entrées; 4) drink special + table tent; 5) first-timer napkin + comeback offer; 6) manager card / VIP treatment and reviews for returning guests; 7) daily training on the five scenarios. Estimated lift from Alex: .99 ≈ +30% profit; menu reorder 10–20%; alcohol 40%+ (his stated guess); first-timer program ~10% (~$300–400K revenue, more important for location #2 and weekdays); reviews ~20%. "Fast and easy" to implement.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Expanding before the core model is dominant.
- Using marketing to prop up an inferior product.
- Training by telling instead of drilling reps.
- Delayed incentives ("next time you'll get…").
- Turning people into discount seekers.
- Loyalty programs that spam texts.
