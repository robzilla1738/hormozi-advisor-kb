# I Lived on $0 Income for 3 Years Growing My Business...

Signal: medium

## Core thesis
Wealth comes from the gap between income and expenses, and peace of mind depends on that ratio, not on the absolute amount you spend. Alex walks through his living expenses season by season, from $0 to a portfolio doing $100M+/yr, to show that his lifestyle grew far slower than his income. He also took distributions to de-risk once he had lost everything more than once. His closing point is "spend on what you value, not what society told you to value," and get to "enough" fast so you can play the game for love of the game. This is a personal-finance and lifestyle story, not a tactical business video.

## Frameworks & models
- **Income-to-expense ratio = wealth and peace of mind.** There are two levers: earn more (invest spare dollars in skills to raise earning capacity) and spend less. If one month or one year of income buys several years of living, you move toward your "FU number" fastest.
- **Relative spending:** living on $1M/yr while making $15M (~7%) is "less" than living on $100K while making $400K (25%).
- **Value test before buying:** "am I really getting $500 of value from this shoe… $2,000 from this belt?" Ask whether you want it for status or because it really converts to value for you.
- **Benefit-to-cost substitution:** he dropped private flights (~$50K) for JSX semi-private (~$200–400 a ticket, about 1/100th the price) because it delivered almost all of the benefit he cared about (speed, no hassle).
- **Seasons ledger:** outcome / income / expenses for each phase of life.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If the goal is material wealth accumulation, lower personal expenses means more accumulation.
- Take distributions as you grow. Reinvesting every dollar "gave myself an excuse" not to focus on profitability. It is "sometimes a business strategy, very rarely the right one."
- Keep personal cash reserves so you can fund the company through a shock. COVID was expected to last 12 weeks and lasted two years.
- Pay cash for houses and cars, with no payments. [inference: he states this as his own practice, not as a universal rule]
- Don't copy a wealthy person's current spending (eating out, flying private) without the income ratio behind it.

## Playbook steps
1. Early stage: keep personal expenses near zero and reinvest. He lived on ~$70K of after-tax savings for 3 years.
2. Once the business works, start taking distributions to de-risk.
3. Let income grow much faster than lifestyle, and bank the difference.
4. Before each upgrade, run the value/status test. Try it, and drop it if it doesn't matter to you (the Bentley).
5. Spend spare dollars on skills to raise earning capacity.
6. Reach "enough," then play for the love of the game.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Not taking distributions. He lost everything multiple times after years of building.
- Living paycheck to paycheck on high incomes. He mentions friends in their 50s who regret it.
- Buying status items you don't value. The Bentley was sold within 6 months at a loss, spent 3 of those months in the shop, and they "were happier for it."
- Fear-driven over-work. In Austin they "over-indexed" on building and had less fun than they could have. He calls it a necessary lesson, not exactly a regret.

## Examples / case details
- **Season 1:** slept on the gym floor, car owned outright, lived on the gym's cash sales, 500+ Chipotle transactions in a year, income grew to ~$15K/month.
- **With partners (1 to 6 gyms):** no dividends; lived 3 years on ~$70K saved; shared a room in a 7-person house in Costa Mesa for $400–700/mo.
- **Condo:** rented it out at $3,500/mo.
- **Gym turnarounds with Leila:** extended stay at $1,800/mo, a Prius owned in cash, revenue ~$200–300K/mo, low margin and operationally complex.
- **Albuquerque:** rent ~$1,200/mo (captions also say "200"), date night once a week, Leila cooking. The business went from $300K/mo to $1.5M/mo, and they banked ~$1M/mo.
- **Austin:** with ~$4–6M saved after taxes, bought a $1.8M house in cash (the captions garble this as "120 million"). The car was an i8 given by ClickFunnels as an affiliate perk. They ate out 1–2×/mo. Revenue went from $1.5M to $4M+/mo, and they accumulated ~$30M after tax in cash, real estate and stocks. They sold the house for ~$4M 2.5 years later.
- **2021:** sold three businesses, the house and the Bentley. Now: condo rent ~$15K/mo, food ~$15K/mo, travel ~$15K/mo. The portfolio went from ~$7M to $13M/mo.
