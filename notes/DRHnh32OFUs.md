## Core thesis
Success and happiness reconcile once you choose what you're solving for. Business, fitness, and marriage are infinite games, where the point is to keep playing, so "winning" means doing the things that make you successful: define a perfect day and live as many in a row as you can. Hyper-success often comes from "broken" traits; if your goal is contentment you don't need them. Ignore others' work-life-balance projections if you love the game. Podcast clip (Acquisition.com channel), conversation with a host [inference: likely Chris Williamson / Modern Wisdom]. Some points (rat study, Steffi Graf, Jason Pargin quote) come from the host.

## Frameworks & models
- **Finite vs infinite games** (Simon Sinek): finite = known players, rules, end. Infinite = keep the game going. Applying finite rules to infinite games fails (Vietnam example). "The point of the game is to keep playing."
- **Three common traits of hyper-successful people** (study he cites): superiority complex (deserve more), massive insecurity (never enough), impulse control. Big "toward" desire + big "away" pain + staying on path.
- **Toward + away motivation**: starving rats pull harder toward cheese when a cat smell is behind them (host cites Jordan Peterson).
- **"Champions are broken"** (Dr. Kashey): they lack an off button.
- **"What problem are we solving?"**: his most frequent question to portfolio companies. Contentment and "richest man" require very different trade-offs.
- **Work that feels like play**: intersection of what you love, are good at, and can be paid for. Steffi Graf scored 10/10 on both desire to train and skill.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Define the metric you're optimizing for axiomatically before judging your life or others'.
- If optimizing pure outcomes, the most "broken" competitor wins; decide whether you want that.
- Don't sacrifice what you prefer to satisfy others' definition of balance.
- Probe your limits but temper them; he says you learn burnout's onset only after burning out "like 30 times."

## Playbook steps
1. Name the problem you're solving (contentment vs maximal achievement).
2. Define your perfect day; stack them.
3. Pick work you'd do longer than anyone because it feels like play.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Treating infinite games as winnable endpoints.
- Copying influencer habits (5am, cold plunges) as the "secret."
- Admiring heroes without seeing they neglected everything else.
- Letting others project their theory of mind (burnout) onto you.
