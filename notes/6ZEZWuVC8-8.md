A re-shared older video from his gym-owner days ("Gym Lords" era). The intro says the portfolio is now ~$85M/yr, and the numbers in the main video are from back then.

## Core thesis
Attention, not time, is the entrepreneur's golden resource. Time without attention is impotent. To grow you have to do *less*: delegate, say no to better and better opportunities, close the open loops draining you, and kill side businesses so all your attention goes to one thing.

## Frameworks & models
- **Attention is money**: "that's why they call it *paying* attention."
- **Bowl of marbles**: each marble is a unit of attention, spread across childhood trauma, marriage, health, spirituality, and business. Collect the small marbles (resolve them) so you have more potency for the one thing you're growing.
- **Open loops/cycles**: unresolved issues (toxic relationships with parents or friends, unhandled tasks) leak attention. Complete the cycle to get it back.
- **Attention → seeing → wisdom**: wisdom is seeing what others can't, and you see more with more attention available (the "slept on it, math problem became easy" effect).
- **0→1 traits ≠ 1→2 traits**: hustle and "I'll outwork anyone" get you started. Delegation and leadership come next, so your identity has to stay fluid.
- **Capacity inversion**: with little attention left, small problems feel like explosions. With lots of capacity, big problems seem small.
- **Protective layers**: Alex → Leila → COO → EA team (4 EAs). No one, including employees, has direct access to him. His billionaire friend's phone only rings for whitelisted contacts.
- **One-number litmus test**: define the number you need (e.g., the gym's EFT count for the lifestyle you want). For every opportunity: "Will this help me grow this number? Yes → do it; no → ignore it."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- "In order to grow you need to do less," through delegation plus choosing what not to do.
- "The better you get, the better the opportunities you have to say no to."
- Kill side hustles and side businesses: do one thing. "One fat paycheck is better than two."
- Coaching diagnostic: "Where's your attention?" Someone who seems scattered can't grow.
- If you need a drink to unwind, you have unresolved things. He quit drinking; it took ~6 months before the desire flipped.
- Leave negativity immediately, "nothing personal."

## Playbook steps
1. Audit where your attention goes (he paid a coach for 9 months, 90 min/day, just to talk through what he was thinking about). 2. List open loops: relationships, habits, unresolved issues. Close them. 3. Remove drains (alcohol, chaotic social or love life, direct-access channels). 4. Define the one number. 5. Run every opportunity through the yes/no litmus test. 6. Consolidate or kill extra businesses. 7. Build gatekeeping layers as you scale.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Believing "time is money" and filling time while absent.
- Being "everywhere and at the same time nowhere."
- Multiple locations or businesses diluting attention: a gym owner with four gyms making less than he did with one, because fixed costs eat the margin.
- Starting several businesses at once hoping one takes off ("all of them could take off").
- Identifying with outworking everyone. The Tony Stark image of the entrepreneur is "a farce."
- Thinking your market is too small: a real-estate coach doing $200K/mo said there weren't enough agents. Her market outnumbers micro-gym owners ~100:1, yet his revenue outpaced hers ~20:1.

## Examples / case details
- Typical $100K-coaching client: $1–3M/yr gross, stuck, attention spread across 4–5 businesses, taking little home.
- He told 2–3 people recently to consolidate. Within 30 days, two of them had 3×'d total revenue (his claim).
- Example of focus: a $100M roofing company that only repairs roofs, without GC/windows/gutters.
- For 3 years he recorded two videos a week in a Facebook group; this was among the most-watched and most-shared.
