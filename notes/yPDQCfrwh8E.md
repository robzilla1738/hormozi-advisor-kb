## Core thesis
This is a sequel case study on an Acquisition.com portfolio company. Doubling sales (56 → 93 units/month) broke fulfillment. That is the "problem–solution cycle": every solution creates the next problem. The fix was boring customer-success work: a choreographed onboarding, a CRM with KPI tracking, commissioning CS on activation metrics, and a paid back end ("wall") after the 16-week program. Over 60 days, time to value went 47 → 34 days, CSAT went from untracked to 98%, and ascension revenue went $0 → $140K. He credits about $6M of added enterprise value: recurring profit × a ~6× multiple.

## Frameworks & models
- **Problem–solution cycle**: solving one problem creates the next (10× sales → fulfillment breaks). Use it on purpose: solve the customer's first problem, then introduce the next one, which you know better than they do (weight loss → loose skin, wardrobe, six-pack program). Meet them where they are.
- **Time to first value / retention point / activation**: e.g., Breaking Bad viewers who reach episode 3 have ~87% likelihood of finishing. Find the moment that turns a customer into a long-term commitment and pull it as far forward as possible. Break outcomes into small chunks with leading indicators of progress.
- **Communicate more often to make value feel faster**: an agency checking in every 2 weeks gives 1 positive touch. Daily "here's what we did for you" updates give 14 with the same work.
- **Gym Launch activation**: a high-ticket sale in the first 7 days tripled LTV. Later the activation metric became how fast clients launched an internal email campaign. With LTV going ~$17K → ~$50K, there is ~$33K of near-pure profit available to spend on making that one event happen (e.g., "let's send that email together right now," with a tech person on the call).
- **Retention vs acquisition ROI**: ~$60 of staff time (2 × 30-min calls at $20/hr, as in the video) to keep a customer for a few more months, vs ~$1,000 to acquire one. He calls it ~17× more profitable. Allocate effort to the most efficient bucket until it's exhausted.
- **Proactive, milestone-aware CS calls**: "most people at this point are at A or B, where are you?" instead of "what's going on, John?"
- **Free-after-program = endless liability for one-time income**, and it reduces client success ("if I can do it whenever, I do it never"). A known paid "sting" at the end drives action.
- **First 24 hours after purchase**: open and close 4–6 small promises (split one task into six checkpoints).
- **Leading vs lagging indicators**: churn is lagging. Find leading indicators (gym no-shows, Netflix logins) and drive them.
- **Weighted CS commission**: target range per metric. 100% → 2×, 50% → 1×, 0% → 0× (e.g., reaching week 12 of 16 = 75% weighting).
- **EV logic**: recurring revenue reduces risk (higher multiple), and added profit gets multiplied. If CAC stays the same and LTV doubles, you can sometimes market 10× more, not just 2×.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you don't measure a metric, you won't improve it. Tie individual and team incentives to the metrics once they're tracked.
- "You can predict how good someone is at whatever skill based on the quality and quantity of the data they track."
- Churn won't be fixed by one thing: "golden BBs, no silver bullets."
- Treat CSAT with caution: timing bias (right after onboarding), on-call vs voluntary differences, and responders skew very happy or very sad. Survey at weeks 0/2/4/8/12 but don't over-survey.
- Roll out a new paid back end slowly so you don't shock the system.
- At $100K–$1M/yr it's normal not to have all this. Fix it when it's actually the constraint.

## Playbook steps
1. Track TTV, CSAT, and ascension. 2. Choreographed onboarding: sales→CS handoff with the next meeting booked before the call ends (no one in "no man's land"); pass client goals from sales to CS; reset expectations to match what sales said; resell the purchase (their why, plus stories of similar people); outline the steps to the target. 3. CRM tracks TTV and churn automatically; automated CSAT segmented by rep. 4. Commission CS weighted on activation and leading-indicator metrics. 5. Build a paid back end/wall at program end once clients are succeeding (10% ascended at launch → 27% over 60 days).

## Mistakes he warns against
- Arbitrary, agenda-less check-ins.
- Throwing a sold customer into a big bucket with no onboarding; first contact days later.
- Sales/CS disconnect, which causes cold feet and early refunds.
- Giving free ongoing access after the program.
- Commissioning only sales, when much more profit is driven by customer success.
- Bragging about vanity CSAT.
