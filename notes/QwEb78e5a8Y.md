## Core thesis
Hot-seat Q&A at acquisition.com HQ with Alex's between-segment commentary. Repeated lesson: the lowest-risk move is doing more of what already works — ask "why can't we do more?" and the answer is the constraint; solve it rather than switching avatar, model, or business. Also: over-compensate mistakes, fix churn via onboarding consumption, get data before ideating, train behaviors not traits, and quantify what a supply-side hire is worth.

## Frameworks & models
- **Make it more than right**: a sales rep sold six people into an event that didn't exist; instead of refunding, team spent the day with them and took them to dinner. Disney stat (as cited): 37 magical moments to overcome one tragic moment → avoid tragic moments; when they happen, over-compensate — wronged-then-super-compensated people become your biggest ambassadors (lesson credited to Leila).
- **Consumption precedes churn**: low attendance (≈7% show up) → low renewal/high churn.
- **"Book a meeting from a meeting" (BAMFAM)**: closer books the onboarding call; last 5 minutes of every session, everyone books their next one; automated reminders + manual outreach from the session lead.
- **Personal on-ramp before group**: 4–6 one-on-one sessions (start with 2) before "graduating" into group — group becomes a prize. Sell as A/B close: "group, or one-on-one with John?"; if they pick group, gift 1–2 one-on-ones.
- **Data first**: "nine times out of ten" owners have a problem but no data on what precedes it; once collected, solutions become obvious.
- **Why can't we do more?** → constraint. Only proper reason to chase a new avatar: you've run out of the existing one. If you itch for new, add a channel, not a new customer type.
- **Scaling paid ads**: when ROAS drops — better lead magnet, better creative, CRO on pages, sales efficiency, or all four; change one at a time ("why business takes time").
- **Enterprise B2B**: 10–15 touch points, fewer deals per rep, multiple stakeholders; increase conversion and stick by adding more people on both sides.
- **Smallest skill deficiency**: traits are bundles of skills; hire for the smallest/fastest-to-train gap (2 days to train sales vs 2 months to train kindness → hire kind, train sales).
- **Operationalize vague terms**: "patience = figuring out something to do in the meantime"; "lazy" → "doesn't respond to Slack quickly"; then ask what blocks them.
- **Stop / Start / Keep** feedback; **Document → Demonstrate → Duplicate** training (prefers checklists over SOPs).
- "You lost the championship in the draft" (Chick-fil-A head of people) — recruiting before process.
- **Leverage test for channels**: least time or least money for the most money.
- **Holdco paths**: concentrated (few diverse companies with a ~5-person team adding value, like traditional PE) vs vertical roll-up (Constellation-style; centralize costs, spread best practices). Fewer ways up the mountain the bigger the goal; a $50M exit is possible in almost any niche.
- **Supply- vs demand-constrained**: "you're not in the physical therapy business, you're in the recruiting physical therapists business"; use Core Four + headhunters + staff referrals to get staff.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- "The lowest risk-adjusted return move in any business is to do more of what's already working."
- Problems present on both paths (sales efficiency, fixed coaching costs) are not reasons to switch paths.
- $3–5K/day on Meta is "nothing" for a proven consumer product; 50–100K/day is plausible.
- If one speaking event a year drives significant revenue → target ~50/yr.
- Holdco with 14 dissimilar companies: decide "who do I want to be when I grow up"; 80/20 the portfolio, go passive/return equity on the rest.
- Referral bounty sized to value: PT ≈$350K gross profit/yr → $20K referral beats $500; paying $50–100K to get one is rational; use contingencies if worried.
- Raising prices is good up to a point; beyond it you become a luxury business and change the model.

## Playbook steps
1. Churn: track attendance → BAMFAM from sale → personal 1:1 onboarding → book next session at end of each → reminders.
2. Growth: list channels → find highest-leverage → ask what stops 3×/10× → fix that constraint one variable at a time.
3. Leaders: define behaviors → Stop/Start/Keep → document, demonstrate, duplicate.
4. Supply-constrained: design career path for producers; treat recruits as customers; outreach + big referral bonuses.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Refunding instead of over-compensating.
- Ideating without data.
- Going B2B/new avatar because you hit difficulty in B2C ("woman in the red dress").
- Vague feedback ("stop being lazy").
- PE no-man's-land: too many unrelated companies with no synergy.
- Changing the business model instead of solving the real constraint; starting side businesses (gym owner → real estate → Airbnb → cleaning company spiral).
