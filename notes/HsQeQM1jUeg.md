## Core thesis
Alex's origin story as an online fitness coach (Free Training Project → Skinny to Swole): get proof before anything, using free work for friends, then referrals, then paid. Includes what worked (walk the walk, live cheap, start free, templated one-to-many delivery, niche, recurring model), what didn't (no ascension, quitting a working business for "legit" brick and mortar, no offer, no sales call, caring about others' judgment, no help), the "rule of 300" warm-outreach method, and 13 lessons for beginners. Contains a Skool promo.

## Frameworks & models
- **Four stages to first customers**: (1) Homies: friends trained free for before/afters (~6 said yes; took ~6 months). (2) Referrals from them (6–10 more), but free meant they didn't do anything. (3) Free Training Project: referees donate $500–$1,000 to a charity of their choice for skin in the game. (4) Paid: Skinny to Swole, $200/month for 12 weeks; moved charity donors to paying him with little loss; 20 clients = $4K/month, ~$12K total.
- **Proof > offer**: "100 pieces of proof and a terrible offer" beats an amazing offer and no proof. Proof needs to be similar enough to the prospect (his covered several avatars by accident).
- **Why referrals first**: free; convert higher at higher prices; referred customers refer more; and if you're not getting referrals, "you're not that good," so let as few people know you suck as possible, then pour gas on the fire.
- **Rule of 100** (from $100M Leads): 100 warm reach-outs, 100+ cold, 100 minutes making and posting content, or $100/day on ads, every day.
- **Rule of 300** (Skool experiment): (1) make one post; (2) leave 100 thoughtful comments, ideally in a group/community of ideal customers (you compete only against other commenters; piggyback on others' reach, "like mini ads"); (3) apply rule of 100 to the new "lukewarm" list: people who liked your post, commented, liked your comments, or replied to your comments (warmest). Open with a question ("what drew you in? are you looking to…"), not "thanks." Takes ~2–4 hrs/day.
- **Non-monetary payment**: testimonials, feedback, and referrals can be worth more than the price, so free isn't uncompensated.
- **Full price or free** (Chick-fil-A philosophy): never discount to close; it tells everyone your prices aren't real. Strategic list-price changes are fine.
- **Templated one-to-many delivery**: ~200-hr spreadsheet (weight/goal → macros + grocery lists from 100 meals); 10 minutes per client yet felt deeply personalized. Turn repeated questions into onboarding.
- **Niching** happens naturally toward people like you; makes delivery cheaper (templated) and more valuable (specific).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Be the walking billboard of what you sell; "proof over promise" if you must pick one.
- Live cheap to buy runway: he lived on ~$1,200/month, saved ~$50K in 2 years = 2–3 years of runway = more shots on goal. "Work more": you get paid and don't have time to spend.
- When a thing works, scale it rather than switching. ($1K/week on 4 hrs/week → 40 hrs could mean ~$10K/week.)
- Add a sales call to charge more; add an ascension/continuation offer ("more of the thing they just bought").
- Inconsistent results = inconsistent or insufficient inputs (10 reach-outs/day ≈ 1 sale per 10 days; 100/day ≈ 1/day).
- If you copy someone's actions and don't get their outcome, you're missing a variable ("nine of ten bricks"), not proof it doesn't work.

## Playbook steps
1. Get results free for people you know; collect varied before/afters. 2. Ask them for referrals after delivering value. 3. Add skin in the game (charity donation) if free referrals don't engage. 4. Launch paid with the proof; announce "open for business" post (better: image/carousel of transformations + CTA, text the link to everyone you know; add offer, bonuses, risk reversal, real scarcity). 5. Run rule of 100/300 daily. 6. Template delivery. 7. Have a next offer ready at the end of the term. 8. Sell on the phone, raise price.

## Mistakes he warns against
- No ascension: sold 12 weeks then went to find 20 new customers.
- "Stopped for new": abandoned an online business (4 hrs/week) for a 5-year brick-and-mortar gym lease because online didn't feel "legit."
- No offer, no sales process (sent buy links) → couldn't charge more → quit a good business instead of raising price.
- Decisions driven by imagined judgment (classmates, dad).
- Going solo without help; asking instead of Googling first.
- Complaining ("better to be envied than pitied").
- Needing immediate reward for work.

## 13 lessons (Slack message to Skool beginners)
1 Follow instructions. 2 Google first. 3 Post what you figure out. 4 Do the rule of 100 daily. 5 Work starts when excitement ends. 6 Your work works on you more than you work on it (POW hole-digging vs gym analogy). 7 Everything is unscalable in the beginning; that's how you get mastery. 8 Pain of repetition forces improvement → skill = leverage. 9 If you complain you are dead to me. 10 Thousands have succeeded; you're not special: find the missing variable. 11 Write down every reason to stick with it (what you run from too). 12 Business is shockingly simple but surprisingly hard: consistency. 13 Just win; pain fades, achievement stays.
