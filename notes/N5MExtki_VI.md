# If I Wanted To Scale A Service Business In 2026, Here's What I'd Do

## Core thesis
Live teardown of "Couplepreneurs" (Kyle & Ariel: ~$480K/yr, $206K profit, ~43% margin, 42 clients). They were supply-constrained (unlimited Slack access ate ~40% of their time), and instead of fixing capacity they built a second low-touch $5K product, which broke their economics. Fix: trim and stack one $25K offer around what clients actually value (deep dive, 1-on-4 quarterly strategy, 2 in-person "getaways"/yr), pull cash up front, test a "4-hour date night" acquisition event instead of a 5-day challenge, turn customers into an ad machine, and do renewals in person. Usually there are only 1–2 big levers; here it's offer + creative.

## Frameworks & models
- **"What problem are we solving?"** then "Can we solve it another way without a new product/acquisition strategy/business?"
- **Trim & Stack** ($100M Offers, Grand Slam Offer pt 2): list every deliverable/asset, then cut and combine to the leanest, most valuable package.
- **4 ways to solve a supply constraint**: raise price; change delivery ratio (1:1 → 1:4 → 1:6 → 1:many); productize; hire. Each implies a different business: hire = recruiting/training/culture business; productize = must market & sell at a higher level; price = branding business (fixed supply, brand loop pushes price up); ratio = keep doing what you do, more profit. He picked ratio (price had no room for 3–4×; productized version already failed at price; they didn't want a big team).
- **Gatorade/shot-glass analogy**: hiring coaches = diluting the X-factor into big glasses; ratio = smaller but undiluted shots of the founder, which get more concentrated as founder improves. Tony Robbins: one person, 10,000 people paying ~$5K.
- **Value per bonus**: each thing added should on its own be worth the price of the whole. "Make all the details perfect, limit the number of details" (quoting Jack Dorsey). Usually one thing gets the buyer over the hump.
- **Consumption gap (Planet Fitness)**: ~80% of members used only cardio + some weights; extra amenities create a gap between paid-for and used → "I'm only using 20% of my $59" → churn. Give 100% of the one thing they want and price for it.
- **Why they buy ≠ why they stay** (Planet Fitness pizza night). Slack was loved after purchase but didn't drive buying or renewals; it trained clients to message instead of using the community.
- **Ad machine / "fusion reactor"**: founder advertises once → customer has experience → it's documented → advertised → gets next customer.
- **Video vs image**: video has higher volatility—best and worst ads are video; images beat bad videos (imagination fills in).
- **Layaway cash pull**: pay on any plan but delivery doesn't start until X paid.
- **Lifelines escalation** (Who Wants to Be a Millionaire): give clients 2–4 "911 calls" instead of open access.
- **Shame vs guilt**: shame = breaking others' rules you respect; guilt = breaking your own. Used to reframe group-format objections.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Coaching businesses rarely scale via hired coaches; he's "never seen anyone get above 30-ish million" that way—larger ones are more distributed. Defer key-man fix until ~$4M/yr.
- Small group stops being small after ~7; 1-on-4 with 90-min sessions is fine.
- "It's easier to change a cadence than a ratio"; if changing ratio from 1:1, change it once and decisively.
- Capacity math: 4 × 90-min sessions/day → 24 couples/day-week; 2 days/week → ~120 clients ≈ ~$3M; goal 150 × ~$30K ≈ $4.5M (~10×).
- Pricing: $25K paid in full / ~$30K on payment plan; "quarter up front" = $9K down then $2,500–3K/mo starting immediately (not a gap, or you get churn); layaway downsell = 3 payments before kickoff. Fast-action: only prepayers get the deep dive and next event.
- Conversion depends most on the first payment, so a $9K first payment framed as $2.5–3K/mo vs $5K old = ~same cash up front.
- Only metric that matters is CAC: $1,500 or even $2,500 CAC on a $2,500 first payment is OK. Their lead-form ads beat funnel pages in a year of split tests → don't change it (though he'd normally expect ~25% show with a page).
- Ad volume: images from 12–16 to ~100. Organic content from 2/week to at least 1/day as a free testing ground; turn winners into ads with a 5-sec CTA.
- Once copy is right, keep it; change creative. Skool spent tens of millions on ads last year without changing copy.
- Day of week for events doesn't matter in the data he's seen.
- Renewals: only ascend, never renew into lower tier; sunset the low tier; keep existing commitments ("new terms, new agreement").
- At events: "if you're going to pitch, pitch."

## Playbook steps
1. Recap of 5 changes: (1) one new offer $25K/$30K: 1-on-1 deep dive onboarding, 1-on-4 quarterly strategy, 2×/yr in-person getaways (positioned as "marriage insurance"), light curated community; kill Slack, weekly group calls, $5K tier. (2) Test "date night" angle: 4-hour single session (five-day challenge "put vertical," cut intros/transitions), pitch at end; or lowest-risk: rerun the challenge with the better offer. (3) Organic daily → best performers become ads + CTA; slice the podcast that got a client. (4) 100+ camera-roll images as static ads. (5) Renewals in person.
2. Event run sheet (2 days): strong intro; day 1 M1, M2, break, M3, M4, pitch (value → "if today was valuable, may I tell you more?" → ~15-min stack & close → buyers get dinner with hosts); day 2 intro, M1, M2, lunch, 15–30 min soft re-pitch that teaches while handling objections ("my team tells me X, Y, Z from yesterday"), M3, M4, wrap.
3. Staff rosters with colored lanyards; each staffer knows two personal facts per attendee.
4. Vary mini-session examples across avatars, especially diverse objections (business great/marriage not, vice versa, kids).
5. Renewal incentive: first-class tickets to next event (~$6K net) or knock $5K off for immediate renewal; easiest ask: "when are you coming back? Here's the next date."
6. $2,500 rebate for a video of their first date night (permission to use; talk experience, not money) → front-end ads.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Unlimited personal access (Slack) as a deliverable.
- Solving supply constraint by launching a cheaper product at same delivery cost.
- Stacking bonuses no one uses (modules, weekly calls clients rated least favorite).
- Founders hiring a buddy trained in a day to deliver their "complex" method—"laziness," and underpaying.
- Apologizing for moving 1:1 → group; position it as better.
- Too-soft events focused on production (they closed ~1 of 50, zero renewals).
- Unquestioned limiting beliefs ("couples won't share in groups").

## Examples / case details
- Funnel: ~511 registrants/challenge, ~26 attend live, 12 calls booked, 10 held, 6 closed; 6 challenges/yr, ~$19,440 ad spend each; cost per attendee $815; show rate ~5.25%; 49% of attendees book. Day 1 ~9% → day 2 4–5%. 90–95% of sales from ads. Only 4 churned in 12 months (payment issues).
- Old offers: Rise Together $5K or $500×12 (weekly group calls); Accelerator $25K or $2,500×12 (quarterly 2-on-2, weekly group Q&A, Slack, 4–6 hr custom plan).
- Their best Instagram reel (strong-wife/masculinity) recommended as ad + CTA for "world's biggest date night."
- Estimated impacts: offer ~5× price; date-night angle 1–4×; organic/creative 1.5–2× lower CAC; renewals extend LTV in years 2–3.
