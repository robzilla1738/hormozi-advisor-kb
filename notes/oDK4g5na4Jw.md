# No BS Advice to Get Rich Like the 1%

## Core thesis
Wealth comes from focus, extreme volume, urgency, investing in skills, and accepting trade-offs at their real price. Distraction keeps people poor, and the biggest distractions are "it's not fair" and "should." Mostly a mindset monologue, with a few tactical points: building a second acquisition channel, deadline compression, the education ROI frame, and who should make decisions. Includes a mid-roll ad for Skool.

## Frameworks & models
- **Rich buy time, ambitious buy skills, poor buy stuff, lazy buy distractions.** Generational wealth transfers through skills, not assets (Sanskrit proverb: with a good son you don't need to accumulate wealth; with a bad son there's no point).
- **Focus = how many things you say no to.** Seven income streams come from owning assets after focused work has built the cash.
- **100x, not 2x.** Trades are price tags: "people want your outcome their way."
- **12×30**: work 12 hrs/day for 30 days straight. Six lessons: you aren't made of glass; you could work more; you could hit goals faster; you could improve daily; you'll have that gear for "a season or a reason"; others are outworking you.
- **Work = volume × leverage**: volume builds skill, and skill increases leverage. The lesson is domain-specific.
- **Work begins when motivation stops** (from bodybuilding: working reps begin at failure). "Whip yourself back in" after a 5–15 min dip. Cravings last ~20 min, so set a timer.
- **Requisites vs distractions**: rest, food and sleep are productive if they increase output on the one thing. Everything else is distraction.
- **They can't copy your proof**: document, don't teach. Credibility before likeability. Big business brands came from big businesses. Model the rise, not the plateau.
- **Overnight success = threshold**: years of hidden inputs, then one visible viral moment.
- **Shrink thought-to-action gap**: "decide" = to cut off. End of month → end of week (~4×), end of week → end of day (~7×), end of day → end of hour. Leila's rule: a 5-minute task with 5 minutes free gets done now.
- **Hard is for now**: three outcomes — you quit, it gets easier, or you get harder. Hard times become the "good old days" in reverse. Growing pains vs stress from stagnation or decline.
- **Masters have more ways to win**: expertise = the number of progress markers you can measure. That is what makes work reinforcing.
- **Education as investment**: price the education by the gap between current and goal income, not its cost. Learning is "bricks on a bridge": a program that fails you may have just been missing a brick other buyers already had.
- **Program process**: do everything exactly as told the first time, keep what works and toss the rest, patch gaps with your other skills, study the program's success stories to find the missing activities. "Sword of Gryffindor": only take in what makes you stronger.
- **Starting point vs rate of progress**: intelligence = rate of learning. Drop "fairness" and "should."
- **Trade-off price list**: privacy is the price of fame; loneliness of ambition; pleasure of discipline; novelty of loyalty; discretion of trust. Each is a degree, not binary.
- **Pipeline of valves**: an outcome needs every condition met. Find the unmet condition instead of getting emotional.
- **Levels of trade-offs (Nike shoes)**: 0 = should get it free (theft); 1 = "I'll pay $30" for $300 shoes; 2 = keep bidding ($30, $50, $100 … $300); 3 = source, where you set a standard above the market's.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you're required inside the business, you can only do one job. Don't run multiple businesses as the rainmaker.
- When you say "end of week," ask whether it can be end of day.
- To make a business sellable, build a second acquisition channel very different from the first so there's no single point of failure. Expect the book's timeline: outbound took 12 months, not the 12 weeks he expected.
- New channels pay "slower than you expect, then faster than you can imagine." Expect months of losses (e.g. −$5K/mo) before a jump (+$25K, +$50K).
- The person with the highest standards should make the decision in that area, regardless of seniority or shares.
- "Don't listen to the people who are closest to you; listen to the people who are closest to your goals." Your real reference group is the voices you listen to before big moves. If everyone agrees with you, be suspicious.
- Underestimated iterations: "19 drafts not two," 500 minutes of editing not 50, 1,000 calls a week for two years not two months.

## Playbook steps
1. Pick one thing and say no to everything else. 2. Run a 12×30 to discover your gear. 3. Raise volume 10–100× in the domain (he makes ~450 content pieces/week). 4. Compress deadlines (month → week → day → hour). 5. Reinvest surplus into skills, education and network, priced by income delta. 6. Do programs exactly as taught first, then patch gaps. 7. Measure more progress markers. 8. Keep bidding up effort until the condition is met; hold standards above the market's.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Seven income streams before focus (he ran 9 businesses at once).
- Wanting the outcome without the trade.
- Copying what rich people do after they're rich.
- Teaching mode before you have proof.
- Quitting a new channel after a few losing months, or a role after 3 days.
- Believing "you're young, you've got time."
- Blaming coaches or vendors instead of finding the missing brick.
- Treating education as an expense.
