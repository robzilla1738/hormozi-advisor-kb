## Core thesis
A personal fitness and nutrition video, not business. Every diet (keto, low-fat, portion control, intermittent fasting) works only by cutting calories. Biohacking is "make-believe" that's good for selling. His sustainable approach for nearly 20 years: set calories by goal, eat protein first (body weight in grams), then eat whatever you want with the remaining calories. The tangential business lesson: pick a system you can sustain forever, and beware absolutes sold to entrepreneurs. [inference]

## Frameworks & models
- **Body-type matrix**: fat (high/low) × muscle (high/low). Plot where you are and where you want to be; calories and training volume move you.
- **All diets = calorie removal**: keto cuts carbs (about a third of calories), low-fat cuts fat, portion control and IF cut total intake. Keto "fails" when people add butter and ribeyes that outweigh the carbs cut.
- **Calories = body weight × multiplier** (range ~7–21). 200 lb: ×15 = 3,000 maintenance (high side); ×11 = 2,200 moderate loss; ×10 = 2,000 faster loss.
- **Protein = 1 g per lb of body weight.** 100 g protein ≈ 1 lb lean meat, so a 200 lb person eats ~2 lb lean meat/day.
- **Napkin math**: protein grams × 5 calories (to account for fat carried along) → 200 g ≈ 1,000 cal. Then 2,200 − 1,000 = 1,200 calories of "whatever you want."
- Meal timing and count don't matter.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you can't do it for the rest of your life, don't do it.
- Nothing forbidden means no cheat days and no binges. Daily treats stop being special.
- Not losing fast enough? Lower the multiplier. "No one is immune to a calorie deficit."

## Playbook steps
1. Pick a goal multiplier × body weight = calories. 2. Protein = body weight in grams, eaten first. 3. Protein calories ≈ grams × 5. 4. Fill the remainder freely. 5. Adjust the multiplier based on results.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Believing biohacking and diet absolutes while looking the same every year.
- Restriction that leads to splurging.

## Examples / case details
