## Core thesis
If you want to get rich, "money max" by being willing to "min" for a period, and do it as young as possible. Early dollars and early skills compound more, wins mean more reputationally before ~30, early successes become stepping stones, and youth gives energy, flexibility, and adaptability. Model the rule (people who got rich young), not the exceptions. (Manifest category says "pricing" but the content is mindset/wealth. [inference])

## Frameworks & models
- **Compounding math of early dollars**: his example says $1 extra at 25 might be ~$90 by 70. The same dollar at 35 is ~1/3 as much, and at 45 ~1/9. He says early money "compounds at three to four times the rate" of later money. He is not arguing for putting 100% in the S&P 500, only that dollars invested early get disproportionate returns.
- **Skill compounding**: you need a certain number of reps to get good. Early reps give a sustained advantage (Bill Gates coding early).
- **Youth as a reputation multiplier**: youth multiplies every win (press, network, mentors, capital access). The multiplier "decays around 30" ("The moment I turned 30, I was just a white dude").
- **Stepping stones / laddering**: each venture leverages the last. His path: getting in shape → personal/online training → gym → multiple gyms → Gym Launch → sale funded Acquisition.com / family office. "The steps are set. It's just how soon you want to get to this step."
- **Youth advantages, rapid fire**: more energy; fewer requirements (no kids/spouse); geographic flexibility (DC for politics, NY for finance, LA for media: "go where the reps are"); you can live for less and reinvest more aggressively.
- **Game theory: the most adaptable player wins** when entering a new system. Youth = most adaptability, neural plasticity, energy.
- **Goal treadmill (bench 315 analogy)**: by the time you hit a goal it seems obvious, because the work already made it reasonable. So you must do "unreasonable" amounts of work.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you want to max money, accept a period of minimizing everything else.
- Go where the fish are: move to where the skills and network you need are concentrated, before you have roots.
- If it's hard to leave your current path now, it will only get harder later. Stack bets where your probability of success is highest.
- Don't wait for certainty; the world will never give it.
- Ignore advice from people who don't have what you want.

## Playbook steps
1. Decide whether you actually want to money-max (no judgment if not). 2. Cut your costs and requirements; live for less. 3. Reinvest excess cash in skills and investments early. 4. Relocate to where reps and network are dense. 5. Get early wins and use the reputation boost from your age. 6. Ladder each win into the next venture. 7. Tolerate risk and pain for extended periods with an uncertain payoff.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Modeling exceptions (Colonel Sanders starting at 63) instead of the rule.
- Waiting for certainty before acting.
- Thinking goals separate winners from losers. Per a James Clear quote he cites, it's the actions, the risk tolerance, and the pain endured.
- Taking advice from people who haven't gotten what you want.
