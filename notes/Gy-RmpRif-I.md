## Core thesis
Price is the biggest profit lever. To raise prices on existing customers, send a structured letter that re-sells current value, previews improvements, ties the increase to a values-based promise, frames cost small, and ends with a perspective P.S. Commit and don't chicken out.

## Frameworks & models
- **Price-increase letter structure:**
  1. Why I'm writing (we've been growing, good things happening).
  2. What we currently do (list hidden value: talent, staff education, new software/equipment) — gets them into the owner's cost mindset.
  3. What's coming (specialty programs, higher service levels, events) and the concrete benefit every time they interact.
  4. Tie to an uncontestable value/promise: "with integrity I can no longer provide the level I promised / claim to be best unless we do these things… which requires a corresponding increase."
  5. Frame price small: $5,000/yr → ~$100/week → ~$15/day (like insurance "a dollar a day").
  6. P.S. perspective: "If this will materially affect your life — mortgage, groceries — reach out and we'll figure something out."
- Delivery: video + direct mail; turn off comments; ask concerns be raised directly.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- At 20% net margin and $100/mo: +20% price ≈ 2x profit; +50% ≈ 3.5x profit; 2x price ≈ 6x profit.
- At 25% margin, +25% price doubles profit.
- Switching is a pain for customers, so most will stay; those who leave are usually your worst customers.

## Mistakes he warns against
- "We're raising prices" email with no reasoning.
- Backing down to squeaky wheels after committing.

## Examples / case details
- His first gym: tripled price, lost ~1/3 of customers ("the culling" — problem customers), revenue doubled, profit soared, serving 1/3 fewer. Exact gym letter in Gym Launch Secrets.
