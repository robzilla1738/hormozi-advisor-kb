## Core thesis
You're working on the wrong things: chasing **missed opportunities** (fun, shiny, hypothetical) instead of solving **problems** (things that threaten the business's survival). Growing through opportunities also grows your problems. Solving problems makes you grow by getting better, and the payoff is near-certain. Strategy is "prioritizing limited resources against unlimited opportunities." Because you have limited swings, take the sure bets (problems) first. Only open the ideas list once things are humming.

## Frameworks & models
- **Problem vs missed opportunity**: problems threaten future livelihood (customer complaints, payment-processor red flags, falling sales conversions). Missed opportunities are things you "should" do (email follow-up, retargeting, new products or channels). Taken to the extreme, opportunities become "sell everything to everyone."
- **Opportunities cluster in your favorite function**: product people see product ideas, marketers see marketing ideas, and so on.
- **Now vs future is "a dichotomy to be managed," not solved**: the visionary lives 3–12+ months out but can't take his eye off today. Alex and Leila are yin and yang.
- **Data rule**: show a metric only if a change in it would change your behavior. Hypothetical test: "if we max this out would it change what we're doing?" If no, it's noise. Every meeting should end in a behavior change. A metric must tie to getting more customers or making customers worth more.
- **4-step problem solving**: (1) condition: current vs desired; (2) action + hypothesis (e.g., churn 5% → one-on-one outreach to each new customer will lower it); (3) did we do it?; (4) did the expected result happen?
- **Limited-swings correction to Bezos**: Bezos says take a 10% chance at a 100× payoff every time. But with $20 and a $10 minimum bet you get 2 swings and roughly an 80% chance of losing everything. [inference: exact ≈ 81%.] Owners don't have unlimited swings.
- **"Big money-making ideas list"**: park opportunities there. Alex gets to do maybe 1 in 10–20 of his ideas.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Separate your to-do list into problems vs opportunities. Work on problems first.
- Revisit the opportunities list only once current problems are solved. Many ideas look stupid two months later ("the ultimate sleep on it").
- Don't pitch a new idea to the team right away; you'll oversell it.
- Cut any reported metric that doesn't drive behavior or tie to customers or customer value.

## Playbook steps
1. Dump all the "should dos" and tag each one as a problem or an opportunity.
2. Move the opportunities to the ideas list.
3. For each problem, write current vs desired, the action and hypothesis, did we do it, and did it work.
4. Audit dashboards and meetings for behavior-changing metrics only.
5. When things are humming, pick the highest-likelihood, biggest-return idea and go all in.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Chasing shiny opportunities because they're fun, while avoiding hard problems you don't know how to solve.
- Scaling before you fix problems (the problems scale too).
- Over-reporting data; vanity metrics (e.g., a vendor pushing shorter content to raise view duration without tying it to business outcomes).
- Pushing every exciting idea onto the team.
