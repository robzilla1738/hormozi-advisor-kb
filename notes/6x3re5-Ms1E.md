## Core thesis
Wealth tiers come from adding leverage on top of income: (1) income only; (2) income + public equities; (3) income + owning and selling a private asset; (4) income + owning an asset + **debt against it**, which gives liquidity while you keep ownership and control. Every path rests on first being valuable enough to generate a large income. Rich kids inherit leverage in the opportunity vehicles they pick, a bigger "measuring stick," and knowing what to say no to. Credentials he states: $42M in distributions from Gym Launch, sold for $46.2M; portfolio at ~$200M/yr.

## Frameworks & models
- **Four wealth versions (billion-dollar scale)**:
  - V1, income: earn ~$1.5B pre-tax to keep ~$1B.
  - V2, income + public equities: $100M/yr, ~$67M after tax, invested at 9% (S&P) → ~$1B in 10 years.
  - V3, income + private equity: own a business making $100M/yr, sell at ~12× = $1.2B → ~$1B after tax. Then you have to redeploy the check: will you beat the asset you just sold?
  - V4, income + debt (keep control):
    - (a) **ESOP**: sell a 33–49% minority stake to employees. A bank lends them the money with the stock as collateral. He says it's tax-free to the owner in the US, at a lower multiple (banks price risk, not upside). You keep majority control, and employees "act like owners."
    - (b) **Dividend recap**: a bank lends ~4–5× EBITDA against the business (e.g. $500M on $100M/yr). The business carries the debt, not you. Like a mortgage, the business pays it off and you still own it. He's unsure if it's tax-free: he first says you pay tax, then "might be tax-free, not sure."
    - (c) **Go public**: sell only 5–10%, and the rest gets a live market value. Borrow against it piecemeal within 24 hrs, typically up to ~50% of the stock's value (Elon used this kind of loan to buy X). The loans are tax-free because they're loans.
- **Same four at $10M net-worth scale** ($10M at ~5% ≈ $500K/yr ≈ top 1% income; passive dividends are often qualified at ~20%):
  - V1: earn $15M lifetime → $10M.
  - V2: $1M/yr (~$670K after tax) at 9% for 10 years → ~$10M.
  - V3: own a business doing $10M revenue / $1.2M owner earnings, sell at 10× = $12M → ~$10M after capital gains. HVAC, dental and accounting firms sell at 10–14×.
  - V4: join a company as an employee or partner, earn ~0.2%/yr × 5 years = 1%, it IPOs at $1B → $10M of stock to borrow against.
- **10-year investing benchmark**: earning $1M/yr for 10 years, your portfolio should reach ~$10M (pre-tax income × 10). Investing well only gets you back to the untaxed figure.
- **Three fallacies of copying Warren Buffett**: he bought his first stock at 7, weeks after Pearl Harbor; he invested through the greatest growth in US history; and luck (the #1 person has all the traits *plus* luck).
- **Opportunity-vehicle leverage**: same skill, bigger price tag. A top car salesman makes $300–400K/yr; an M&A broker earns 4% on a $200M sale = $8M for one deal. Commission on a $500M building vs. a $50K house.
- **Measuring sticks**: as a teen he measured money in Chipotle burritos ($200 shift = 28 burritos). On Wall Street a "stick" = $1M. Rich people measure in bigger units over longer time horizons.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Raise income first (skills, certifications, outbound sales at ~$150K) before obsessing over passive investing. Investing $35K/yr "is never going to happen."
- Live below your means to create a surplus you can deploy aggressively.
- Before selling a business, decide what you'll buy with the proceeds that beats it.
- Debt strategies only work if the business keeps growing. If it declines "you're in deep" and can lose it all. Loans come with covenants and must be repaid.
- Take low-paid, high-learning stepping-stone jobs (PwC, EY, McKinsey, Goldman analyst) if they ladder up. Reverse-engineer the goal into the present.
- Say no to low-leverage opportunities. Be patient and selective. Surround yourself with people who have bigger measuring sticks.
- Outsized wealth comes from contrarian bets where you have an information advantage: "opportunities look like risk while they're opportunities."

## Playbook steps
1. Become valuable: learn skills that solve expensive problems. 2. Widen the gap between income and spending. 3. Invest the surplus (index funds at minimum). 4. Own or earn equity in an asset (build a sellable business or get equity in a company likely to go public). 5. Use debt against the asset for liquidity while keeping control (ESOP / recap / borrowing against public stock). 6. Pick high-leverage vehicles and the circles to go with them.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Saving pennies on low income (the McDonald's millionaire story) instead of raising earning power.
- Selling a company without a redeployment plan.
- Treating borrowing against assets as risk-free.
- Copying Buffett's path; doing what everyone else is doing (that's no longer an opportunity).
- Saying yes to opportunities you shouldn't, which loses "years and decades."
