## Core thesis
"Getting rich is an outcome, getting better is an input." Most small businesses are stuck in "the selling business," pouring leads into a leaky bucket. The compounding vehicle is product quality: it raises LTV (repeat purchases, premium price) and lowers CAC (referrals, brand), which beats rising ad costs at scale. Build back to front: first phase is "to learn, not to earn."

## Frameworks & models
- **Leaky bucket / back-to-front**: fix why people don't rebuy or refer before buying more leads.
- **"He who can make his customers the most valuable wins"** (his rephrase of Dan Kennedy's "he who can spend the most to acquire a customer wins"). LTV = gross profit × number of purchases; purchases track product quality.
- **Two forces against scale**: cost per eyeball always rises, and conversion falls as you reach colder, less problem/product-aware audiences. Counter with referrals and LTV.
- **Brand loop**: promise → keep promise → customers tell others → CAC falls. Brand = the associations people have with your product; strong brand raises perceived likelihood and lowers risk (value equation) → premium pricing.
- **Negative word-of-mouth test**: if your CAC rises faster than market CPMs, word of mouth is working against you.
- **Three lines**: value (top), price (premium), cost (bottom) — keep them as far apart as possible via "100 BBs, no silver bullets" (greet by name, water in 30s, bread in 2 min, ask the customer's preferred pace).
- **Activation points via regression**: study the cohort that doesn't cancel, find what happened to them that didn't happen to others, drive onboarding to that point, remeasure.
- **Product as leverage**: "cut once, sell 100 times"; marketing is linear.
- **Product quality → multiples**: compounding businesses sell for 20–50×; marketing-only businesses are "only as good as last month."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Sometimes cap revenue/ad spend until referrals exceed ~30%; advertise only enough to test and fix.
- If you don't lose customers, steady sales compound (10/mo → 20, 30, 40 customers).
- When someone gives a "because" statement, ask "how do you know that?" — ignore untracked gut feel.
- Product KPIs: outputs = price commanded, repeat purchases/churn; inputs = activation metrics.
- Red flag: a marketer changing offers every ~6 months can't keep promises (can't deliver/recruit/manage).

## Playbook steps
1. Find out why people don't rebuy or refer (read between the lines).
2. Run small advertising only to test; iterate the product (cookie-batch style).
3. Identify activation points from retained cohorts; aim onboarding at them.
4. Add the "100 details"; consider a community to raise LTV (he plugs Skool, which he co-owns).
5. Scale once retention/referrals are strong.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Chasing more leads when the problem is "you can't afford them because your product sucks."
- Short-lived social/ad hacks; racing to the bottom on CAC.
- Rushing to scale out of insecurity after fixing one or two things.
- Making up stories for why you win ("mythology") — e.g. gym owners winning only because of convenience until a competitor opened across the street.
- Taking credit for wins while blaming outside forces for losses.

## Examples / case details
- Books: ~$1M/month in book sales without advertising the books; first book still sells thousands/month; buyers of book one bought book two with little promotion.
- Friend's cookie business: shadowed top bakers, baked 365 numbered batches with tasters (also generating local leads), then opened to big success.
- Gym Launch "automated lead nurture" (ALN) launch: owners who missed the webinar sent card numbers anyway because "if he launched it, I know it's good."
- His pool guy: Alex kept paying but told neighbors not to use him — a paying detractor.
- Brand ROAS "30–100x" vs 2–3× direct response; Apple sells to a line of past buyers.
- Activation examples: SEO agency → first sale from SEO; weight loss → ~7 lbs in first two weeks.
- Quotes Stanford "Professor Berglan" (as captioned): better to know why you failed than succeed and not know why.
