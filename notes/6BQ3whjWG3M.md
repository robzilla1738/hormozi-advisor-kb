# How the Top 1% Actually Think About Money
id: 6BQ3whjWG3M / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6BQ3whjWG3M / duration: 1683s (~28 min) / tags: 10-money-cashflow, 12-mindset-leverage
Signal: medium

## Core thesis
Your money goals are too small because inflation shrinks them. He says a $1M goal at 67 buys only about $170K of today's stuff, so a $4M target becomes about $24M in future dollars. Compounding still works, but you have to feed it much more: (1) increase income, (2) spend less, (3) make and save it faster. (4) What he did: live as cheaply as possible and pour the surplus into learning income-earning skills, which pay a far higher return than anything else.

## Frameworks & models
- **Inflation reality**: he says purchasing power fell about 6× over 50 years. The caption reads "$1 in 1975 = $62 today," which is likely a caption error for ~$6, since he calls it a 6× difference [inference].
- **Future vs today's dollars**: at ~9% over 50 years, $1 invested today ≈ $80 nominal ≈ $13 in today's dollars. So $200 extra gig income ≈ a $2,600 investment, a $500 belt ≈ $6,500, and a $500/mo three-year lease ($18K) ≈ $234K at retirement.
- **Time is the young person's edge**: starting at 18 vs 28 is about 80× vs 33× growth. Never flex when young.
- **Three saving mechanisms**: (a) watermark: keep $X in the bank and invest everything above; (b) invest a fixed amount first and live on the rest (he says the richest people he knows think this way); (c) a fixed amount increased over time. "Just pick one."
- **Skill ROI math (his)**: $2,000 on a sales skill takes income from $30K to $90K (≈$75K after US tax). He says living on $2,500/mo leaves ≈$35K/yr investable for life. [inference: $2,500×12 = $30K, so $75K − $30K ≈ $45K; his $35K is either conservative or a slip.] About $3K/mo invested for 50 years ≈ $31M with no raises.
- **Skill bridge / missing bricks**: different outcomes from the same program come from skill gaps ("Spanish 6 without Spanish 1–5"). Weak teachers take anyone's money. See yourself as a collector of skills.
- **Learning budget** (from a $35M/yr e-commerce founder): force yourself to spend 1–10% of income each month on experiments you expect may fail.
- **Effort + feedback loop**: artificial environments with feedback allow 100 loops a day instead of one a week. He did 20+ in-person consults a day for years.
- **Learning money ladder**: free (Skool communities, forums, YouTube, ~8 hours of his free sales training) → $10–200/mo low-ticket communities → $500–3K DIY plus some feedback → $5K–35K in-depth, in-person education plus feedback and a peer room (where he spent most of his money).
- **Give first in rooms**: give 6 hours of sales reviews to get 1 hour of someone else's expertise, and ask peers which programs are actually good.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Think in future dollars when setting goals and judging purchases.
- Prioritize income growth over saving: there's little room between zero and you, and infinite room above.
- Buy a ~10-year-old used car for about $10K cash and never have a car payment.
- Vet educators: good reputation, many testimonials from people like you, no over-promising.
- Pay for speed: getting a 5-year income jump in 1 year is the value of the learning.
- One purchase won't make you rich; don't quit after an early loss.
- "The game of entrepreneurship is risk and time": get comfortable with uncertainty.

## Playbook steps
1. Restate your number in future dollars. 2. Add side income and invest it. 3. Cut spending (no leases, no flexing). 4. Pick a saving mechanism. 5. Spend surplus on income-earning skills, climbing the ladder. 6. Set a monthly experiment budget. 7. Join rooms with people ahead of you and over-give.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Guru advice like "$100/mo → $1M" that ignores inflation.
- Flexing small money when young; car payments.
- Fear of spending on skills, and the ego of "I'll figure it out myself" (you can, but slowly).
- Being "too cheap with their time even though they're poor."

## Examples / case details
- Early life: six roommates and a split bedroom, protein shakes and Chipotle, a used car paid outright.
- $750/hr × 8 hours of ads tutoring; he says it made him "hundreds of millions."
- At a $10M+ founders event, while doing $300–400K/mo almost entirely as take-home, he heard about the learning budget. He took about 10% ($30K) and raised ad spend from ~$400 to ~$1,400/day. Gym Launch then went from $300K to 480K, 780K, 1M, 1.2M, 1.5M, 1.7M and 2–2.2M/month over months.
- He paid ~$30K to join masterminds and offered 4–6 hours of free sales-script reviews to members.
- Credentials claimed: $250M+/yr portfolio; a book launch that did $106M in a weekend; a Guinness record. Plugs for free books and the 10-stage roadmap.
