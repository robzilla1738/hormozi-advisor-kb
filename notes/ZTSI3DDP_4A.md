## Core thesis
Content is digital manufacturing, so apply Elon's 5-step "Algorithm" (from a book by Tesla's former president, as Alex describes it): question requirements, delete, simplify/optimize, accelerate, and only then automate. Aim for "qualium" (quality volume). In the "trust economy" ("show-me times, not tell-me times"), educational content that demonstrates expertise across varied avatars and problems drives commerce. Proof is everything, and the whole truth sells the most.

## Frameworks & models
- **Make good stuff vs make lots of stuff**: the magic is in the middle, "qualium."
- **The Algorithm**: (1) question the requirements; (2) delete, enough that you have to add some back; (3) simplify/optimize (most for the least); (4) accelerate cycle time; (5) automate last. "The only thing that is more efficient than automation is elimination."
- **Time-on-task vs elapsed time**: if work takes 10 hours but delivery takes a week, everything beyond 10 hours is waste and "uncaptured potential value."
- **Alex's three questions** for any requirement: What does that mean? How do you know that? So what?
- **Who, what, when**: end every meeting with who does what by when. 201 level: ask how much actual time it takes, then "what are you doing in the next 4 hours that's more important?" If the answer is nothing, do it by end of day.
- **Communication cycles**: end-of-week → end-of-day updates, which he calls "7x the speed of a team." Ask vendors for updates hourly to pull work forward.
- **AI litmus test**: am I getting more views, leads or money from using AI? If token costs rise month over month and revenue doesn't, you're wasting money.
- **Education is the door**: instructions that are easy to follow (low comprehension cost) plus conviction they'll pay off (proof). Memes and entertainment make you a media business selling eyeballs.
- **"Proof is the pudding."** Imposter syndrome is "you feeling ashamed for lying." Tell the whole truth, including where you look bad, and people believe the parts where you look good.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Remove any requirement with no evidence it matters (they dropped color grading).
- Opportunity cost: if color grading were 4 of 10 hours (40% of cost) for ~5% of quality, skip it. Two videos at 95% (or 1.5) beat one at 100%.
- Never automate before eliminating and optimizing.
- Speak only where you have proof and authority (he declines to advise on happiness).
- Build your personal brand by "doing stuff" in reality (e.g. 5 years). Documenting along the way is fine if you state facts and tell the truth.

## Playbook steps
1. List every step in your content (or delivery) process. 2. Ask the three questions of each. 3. Delete aggressively. 4. Keep the highest-return steps per hour. 5. Shorten cycles with who/what/when and daily updates. 6. Automate what remains. 7. Make educational content solving specific problems for your core avatars. 8. Lead with proof and the whole truth.

## Mistakes he warns against
- "Majoring in the minors": production, lighting, makeup, backgrounds. "The content is the container"; the stuff inside matters.
- Automating things that shouldn't be done at all.
- Teaching what you have no proof in.
- Half-truth marketing.
