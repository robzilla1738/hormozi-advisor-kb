## Core thesis
Affiliates ("lead getters," meaning anyone who promotes your stuff) are free, scalable advertising if you treat them as a customer. Make them an offer that benefits you, them, and their audience. Give it a place to live (an affiliate sales page), promote it through the Core Four, then onboard and support affiliates with fast feedback, plug-and-play collateral, and a leaderboard. He walks through his $100M Money Models launch setup. The $100M Leads launch had 500K registrants, and almost a third were referred by affiliates for free.

## Frameworks & models
- **Affiliate vs referral**: affiliates own their businesses, do their own advertising, and offer your stuff to their engaged leads in exchange for money, free stuff, or both.
- **Many names, one thing**: influencer advertising (physical products), UGC (customer-driven), affiliates (direct response), referrals (brick and mortar), centers of influence / COIs (finance), JVs. Speak each type's language.
- **Three-party win**: the prize should benefit the affiliate's audience too, so promoting it doesn't use up the affiliate's goodwill and ideally adds to it. Example: affiliate audiences submit questions through the link, and the affiliate asks them in person.
- **Two-tier prize structure**: something big and unscalable for the "killers" at the top, plus "approximations" for the long tail:
  - Top 10: in-person one-on-one fireside Q&A plus dinner.
  - Top 50: 3-hour in-person Q&A at HQ.
  - 10+ opt-ins: an exclusive audiobook made for affiliates (70+ one-minute profit tactics), a raffle for 50 more in-person seats, and a recording of the event.
- **Minimum activation point scales with friction**: bringing someone in person (e.g. a gym) ≈ 2; a text introduction ≈ 4–5; an opt-in, which is low friction, = 10+.
- **Machine = Offer → Place (affiliate sales page) → Promotion → Follow-up/support.** If any piece is missing, "it's not going to work." Make it worth it, tell them about it, make it easy.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Prefer free stuff over cash when payouts are a paperwork burden (1099s for many people) or when non-monetary value is higher than a ~10% kickback, especially for low-price items like a book.
- Email your main list about the affiliate program only twice ("FYI" plus "in case you missed it"). Promote the product, not "promoting the promotion." It's OK to email past affiliates (30K+ last year) extra, since they opted in.
- Prove attribution fast (custom link plus leaderboard), or affiliates won't send traffic.
- Never expect affiliates to build a campaign. Give pre-approved emails, posts, ads, and text messages, in every format (IG/YouTube, email/Substack/X/LinkedIn, paid-ads buyers).
- Writing the collateral yourself keeps the messaging accurate and sets expectations. Compliance comes down to: don't lie, don't exaggerate.
- "As soon as there's friction, they'll stop": easy, fast, personalized.

## Playbook steps
1. Design the affiliate offer (he spent about 2 days on permutations).
2. Build an affiliate sales page with the prizes, what to do (opt in → get a custom link → rank on the leaderboard), and how you'll help.
3. Promote: 2 emails to your list, outreach to past affiliates, an affiliate link on the buyer thank-you page, free content (this video), and ads that run to the main product funnel and then route buyers to the affiliate page.
4. Onboard and activate: opt-in (collect any useful data), instant custom link, visible attribution.
5. Collateral "blackbook": starter assets, then later a "here are the winners" update with the ads, hooks, and angles that are converting.
6. Regular communication: behind-the-scenes data (email and ad performance, which also teaches affiliates the process) plus the leaderboard, sent as often as possible.
7. The team (director of marketing) personally reaches out to affiliates climbing into the top 50–100 to help them do more.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Making the prize only a kickback that forces affiliates to hard-close their audience.
- Building for only the big players or only the long tail. You want both.
- Asking affiliates to create their own campaigns.
- Weak or unproven attribution tracking.
- Skipping any step (offer, place, promotion, follow-up).

## Examples / case details
- Some of last year's top-50 affiliates had no audience. They were just good at paid ads and ran his pre-approved ads.
- Proof of the channel: Skool gets the majority of its traffic from word of mouth and referrals; Allen (the software company he sold) was almost entirely affiliates; Prestige Labs is almost 100% affiliate sales via brick-and-mortar distributors.
- A gym owner with 100 members won't hit the top 50 but can get the 10+ tier.
