# How to Win (At ANYTHING)
id: MNll1BaskLA / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MNll1BaskLA / duration: 2845s (~47 min) / tags: 12-mindset-leverage, 07-hiring-management, 13-quit-or-pivot, 05-sales-process
Signal: medium

## Core thesis
A keynote talk to a real-estate audience. Winning means impressing future you. It follows five parts: **what** (invert: list how you'd guarantee losing, then flip it), **how** (break each trait down into observable actions), **why** (stop opportunity-hopping; build motivation through who you compare yourself to), **who** (identity is just repeated doing: "Be-Do-Have is BS", it's just Do) and **when** (now, especially when you're busy). "If you do not change, you did not learn."

## Frameworks & models
- **Inversion**: imagine the least successful version of you and list what it does to guarantee losing (impatient, late, unprepared, transactional, doesn't follow up, doesn't advertise, doesn't ask for referrals). Flip each one. "The smaller the list... the more potent." A single guaranteed-loss item becomes your north star.
- **Command granularity**: "The more skilled you are, the more general a command can be." Less-skilled people need instructions broken down to "turn on your computer." One broken link in the instructions stalls the whole task.
- **Traits = buckets of skills**: charisma = smile when people walk in, vary emphasis and tonality, remember names, ask about them, nod and then respond, keep eye contact, address everyone when you enter and exit. "If you can learn it, it is a skill, and if it's a skill you can teach it."
- **Operationalize**: describe actions "you can see with your eyes." Leave feelings, intentions and psychology out of instructions.
- **Patience** = "figuring out what to do in the meantime."
- **Binaries vs continuums**: the question isn't whether you are patient but how patient, so every trait can be improved.
- **Definitions**: learning = same condition, new behavior. Intelligence = rate of learning. Confidence = domain-specific probability, built from proof (reps in the exact situation). "Confidence comes from the past, not the present."
- **Five stages of opportunity hopping**: uninformed optimism → informed pessimism → valley of despair (most people jump to a new thing here and loop) → informed optimism (a broken-down process plus a feedback cycle) → win.
- **Motivation = deprivation**, and for money the deprivation is psychological: the gap between where you are and where you want to be. $5K→$10K/mo is half the goal; $1M→$10M/mo is one tenth, so the bigger earner is more deprived. Environment sets standards: change who you compare yourself to, ideally the person you want to become.
- **Be-Do-Have → Do**: identity is a description of behavior ("we are what we repeatedly do"). "The work works on you more than you work on it."
- **Smart / busy / broke**: you can be only two of the three.
- **When-then fallacy**: "I'll do it when I have more time" gets the order backwards. You do in order to get.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Start a new habit when you're busiest. If it's contingent on not being busy, it stops the moment you get busy again.
- If you're working a lot without progress, you're doing the wrong things. Course-correct by **elimination before addition**.
- "If your plan requires luck, it's a bad plan." Prepare in advance ("dig the well before you're thirsty").
- Rehearse until bored, in the exact domain you'll perform in.
- Measure yourself on whether you completed the actions (yes/no), not on the outcome. Pick actions that make failure unreasonable.
- When stuck, break the task down further. Chunk back up once the sub-skills exist (100 DMs → "advertise").

## Playbook steps
1. Write the list of what would guarantee you lose. 2. Flip each item. 3. Operationalize each flipped trait into observable actions. 4. Practice until second nature, then chunk up. 5. Measure completion of the actions daily. 6. Compare yourself to the future you and change your environment. 7. Start now; go back to the same conditions and behave differently.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Opportunity hopping at the valley of despair.
- Instructions that are too general for the person's skill level, then blaming them as incompetent.
- Believing traits are innate.
- Waiting until you're less busy or have more.
- Comparing yourself to people you already beat (complacency).
- Attending events without changing behavior.

## Examples / case details
- A sales rep doing 2× the previous top performer simply asked every buyer whether they wanted to bring someone else. It was already in the script but wasn't enforced. He told the director to enforce it.
- He says the ZIP code you're born in is the number one predictor of wealth, ahead of IQ, gender or race (his claim; no source given).
- Leila checked his phone screen time and found 4 hours a day of non-work use.
- Fraternity pledge classes revolted every year between day 10 and day 16. As president he told them: "this is what hard feels like."
- In down markets, agents who have been through three recessions win trust; "3× the work for the same outcome, but 10× the eyes on you."
