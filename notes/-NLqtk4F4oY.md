## Core thesis
Tribute to Munger with the lessons Alex actually uses: inversion, simplicity ("too hard"), the "unique, expensive, sticky, air, managed by an owner" business, leaving meat on the bone, brand as moat, living below your means, learning from others' mistakes, and knowing when enough is enough.

## Frameworks & models
1. **Inversion:** "How would I destroy my business in as few moves as possible?" List it, then invert. Humans spot threats far more easily than positives (10 gratitudes is hard, 100 problems is easy). E.g. destroy = nobody knows about us → invert = let as many people as possible know. Destroy = terrible product and bad reviews → invert = exceptional product people rave about.
2. **Three L's that ruin people:** liquor, leverage, ladies. Never take on debt beyond what you can very reasonably cover.
3. **Simplicity / "too hard" pile:** simple at scale becomes complex, and complex at scale becomes impossible. Choose the easy way to make money when both pay.
4. **Ideal business = unique, expensive, sticky, "air", managed by an owner:** unique = moat (brand), hard to copy. Expensive/"air" = buy for a penny, sell for a dollar (high margin) and low capex. Sticky = recurring (insurance) or reoccurring (Coca-Cola: consumers rebuy and retailers reorder steadily, more reliable further up the distribution chain). Managed by someone who acts like 100% of their net worth is in it and can never sell.
5. **Leave meat on the bone:** deals should be obviously good without an Excel sheet. Don't chisel the last points, and you become everyone's first call.
6. **Brand as moat:** Berkshire put about half its portfolio into Apple for its ability to "charge above-average prices for average products". Also See's Candies, Dairy Queen, GEICO, Ben Bridge.
7. **Rule zero of money:** spend less than you earn. Fundamentals everywhere, all the time, are already as complicated as it gets.
8. **Learn from others' mistakes:** "the lesson without the scar".
9. **Know when enough is enough:** Munger wanted independence and "overshot".

## Decision rules
- Strategic choice between a simple and a complex option → favor simple.
- Acquisition.com portfolio added ~$100M revenue organically over 3 years "doing nothing complicated".
