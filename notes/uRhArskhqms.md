## Core thesis
"The most valuable thing that we have as entrepreneurs is not our time, but our attention." Unmade decisions and unconfronted conversations each take up a slice of your thinking power until you become purely reactive. After a DUI head-on collision, Alex made a rapid series of decisions in ~48 hours: he exited three partnerships and gave away or fire-sold most of ~9–11 businesses to go all in on Gym Launch. That focus created the momentum that led to ~$3M profit within ~12 months.

## Frameworks & models
- **Unmade-decision tax**: each pending decision you already know the answer to holds a chunk of brain power; accumulate enough and simple decisions take inordinate effort. (Analogy: a homework problem that's obvious after a night's sleep.)
- **Aggregate your attention**: consolidate into one thing (advice from his "attention coach").
- **"I'm one decision away from changing my life"**, and often one conversation away from getting your attention back.
- **Regret minimization**: people regret not doing things more than doing them; "I'd rather do things than fail."
- **Sequence businesses**: run them in the right order, not all at once for status.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If simple decisions feel hard, assume you're carrying too many unmade decisions or unconfronted conversations (with a spouse, parents, siblings, partners, employees).
- "You won't die when you make those calls."
- Having many businesses while barely making money is a status play; cut to the one that's working and give it full attention.
- Accept the pain of consequences (fire-sale prices, upset partners) in exchange for regaining bandwidth.

## Playbook steps
1. List every unmade decision and unconfronted conversation. 2. Make the calls fast (he did his in 1–2 days). 3. Exit side partnerships and projects, selling what you can and giving away the rest. 4. Go all in on the vehicle that's working. 5. Use each hard call to build confidence for the next.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Spreading across many partnerships and businesses (gyms with multiple partners, dental and chiro agencies, in-person gym launches).
- Avoiding hard conversations for fear of letting people down.
- Coping with stress through drinking (he drank about half a bottle nightly).
- Managing time when the real constraint is attention.

## Examples / case details
- Before: 6 gyms (various partner splits), a 50/50 dental agency and a chiropractic agency with one partner, plus flying out for in-person gym turnarounds. He worked every waking hour with little cash, tied up in gyms and payroll.
- He crashed at 60 mph into a dark parked car while being pulled over and walked away uninjured. Leila (then his girlfriend) picked him up from jail. The coach told him, "Your lack of making decisions is going to kill you." Leila implied she'd leave if nothing changed.
- He walked away from the agencies ("you can have everything"), sold 2 gyms at a better number and gave away the rest, and exited the newer gym partnership.
- Gym Launch started in 2016; 2016 to H1 2017 was "the worst 18 months" of his life. He calls this his second-hardest decision (after quitting his job).
- Acquisition.com portfolio at the time of recording: ~$85M/yr.
