## Core thesis
Day-in-the-life vlog. Real entrepreneurship is mostly communication (meetings, calls, Zooms), not jets and watches. Growth looks jagged up close, overnight success is 1% improvements seen in retrospect, and difficulty is the barrier that makes goals worthwhile. Captions are fragmented and include other voices (team members, guests, a psychiatrist podcast host).

## Frameworks & models
- **Outputs vs the work**: the luxury signals are outputs. The daily job is talking to leaders, employees, vendors and customers.
- **Growth is jagged**: "the closer you zoom into it the more jagged it looks." One portfolio business saw bookings down about 60% after taking its "eye off the ball."
- **At high levels, money stops buying expertise**: influence, connections, introductions and deals become the currency. Paid communities use price as a qualifier (e.g. a $100K/yr golf club).
- **Five to seven meals**: people already eat the same 5–7 meals, so swap them to get 80–90% of the way on diet.
- **Friends by design, not convenience**: the "unicorn" friend roots for you, is competent where you're not, and has aligned interests and values.
- **Use the fuel you have**: purpose may just be "pay rent and not be broke." List the 10 things you hate about your life and use them as your reasons.
- **Recurring revenue requires recurring work**, often recurring work with zero revenue for a long time.
- **Difficulty is a moat**: the harder something is to get into, the fewer entrants. "Stomaching the harder thing" makes you exceptional.
- **Growth vs comfort**: "one is the price of the other." Comfort signals a plateau.
- **Alex/Leila split**: Leila is CEO and operator of Acquisition.com (hiring, training, scaling, infrastructure). Alex works on big meaty projects over extended periods.
