## Core thesis
Every entrepreneur picks one of three paths — Artist, Entrepreneur, or Hybrid — and can switch with the season. Whatever the path, the highest-leverage skill order is product > marketing > sales, because an exceptional product earns word of mouth that compounds.

## Frameworks & models
- **Three paths:**
  - *Artist* — loves the craft (plumbing, carpentry, coding, wallets). Scales by price not units: commodity → premium (word of mouth, better/faster) → luxury.
  - *Hybrid* — keeps doing the craft (or one function: product, marketing, finance, fundraising) and hires a CEO/exec team around them (Tobi Lütke, Shopify — stayed on product). Requires judgment to pick leaders → use outside advisors.
  - *Entrepreneur* — the business is the art; find market needs, build org, install operator, let it cash flow.
- **Advice hierarchy (best → worst):** done it themselves AND taken many like you there → taken many there but not done it → done it only themselves → watched others/several go there → watched one person → "your mama, uncle and neighbor."
- **Skill value order: product → marketing → sales.** Sales is easiest (learnable in a couple of weeks); marketing = translating sales persuasion to media at scale; product drives customer promotion. Marketing scales "to a point" then marginal CAC rises; without word of mouth the company plateaus.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Love the doing → start artist; if you're truly good, demand will exceed your time, then decide hybrid vs entrepreneur.
- Love business regardless of product → entrepreneur path.
- Ask: "Do I love the thing or the business of doing the thing?"

## Examples / case details
- Alex obsessed with fitness (macros, programming); coworkers offered to pay for a gym to make him stop talking; found he loved business more.
- His sequence: nonprofit online training → for-profit online → gym → chain → gym turnarounds → car/tractor(?) agency, dental agency → licensing → e-com physical products → B2B SaaS → private equity, by age 33.
- $100M Offers: $0.99, ~25k copies/month, no paid ads, recommended by strangers to his old classmates.
