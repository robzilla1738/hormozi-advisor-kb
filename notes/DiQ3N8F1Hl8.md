# Brutally Honest Advice to my Poorer Younger Self

## Core thesis
Wealth comes from actions, not rituals, labels or origin stories. Optimize your 20s for learning (paying down "ignorance debt"), not earning. The two lessons he says his younger self missed: **product** and **brand**. Both come down to **compounding**. A great product and a protected reputation make customers stay, refer and buy the next thing, so you stop having to replace customers every month with sales and marketing.

## Frameworks & models
- **Copy the climb, not the summit**: copy what successful people did on the way up, not what they do at the top (3-hour morning routines, Buffett drinking Coke). Watch for false correlations ("tall people play basketball").
- **Routine test**: keep a ritual only if it measurably raises output (5 min to feel 20% better and output rises 20% = fine). Otherwise it's a distraction.
- **Ignorance debt**: everyone owes it. Start paying it down as early as possible, through actions, not words. "The most expensive thing you ever pay for… is the information that you don't know but should."
- **Value of education = income gap**: making $50K means not knowing how to make $1M costs you $950K/yr, so that knowledge is worth the difference.
- **Seasons of learning vs. earning**: choosing to earn "right-hooks" your growth. Pick roles on big-league teams where you learn 10× the skills, even if they pay less.
- **Fast-feedback trap**: sales and marketing skills pay back fast, which hid that he was "playing the wrong game." The Matrix line: "when you're ready, you won't have to [dodge bullets]." A good enough product and brand make copy, landing pages, CRO and media buying nearly irrelevant.
- **Effort curve of quality**: 8→9 is 10× the work, 9→9.5 another 10×, 9.5→9.75 another 10×. Count hours in hundreds and thousands. About 1,000–2,000 tracked hours, looking at the work in different moods, is where the "crispy details" come from.
- **Winner-take-all**: in commoditized markets, being #1 gets outsized returns. Social media makes word of mouth easier than ever.
- **Brand = curated garden of associations**: anything that isn't the rose you're growing is a weed, even a fine flower. Trust is leverage for deals and sales (Buffett's one-page billion-dollar deals).
- **Make sales to get customers, not customers to make sales**: keep the relationship, and year 2 = year-1 customers + new ones. That is the compounding vehicle.
- **Friend filter**: keep people who talk about the future; cut those who only talk about the past.
- **Opinion filter**: the only person whose interests fully match yours is future you. Others want "the version of you who best serves them." Parents play not to lose.
- **Facts-only / "admissible in court"**: deal in observable actions and words, not guessed intentions or "why."
- **Labels limit action**: replace binary labels ("I'm bad at math") with ideals you move toward. If you want to change your behavior, change your environment.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Cut anything that isn't an action toward what you want, unless it measurably boosts output.
- Early career: choose the role with the most learning and brand pedigree over the higher salary (his 18-year-old editor asking $100K example).
- "Figured out" must be a measurable action, e.g. read 2 hrs/night on one subject, then decide within 30 days.
- Spend much longer building the product before marketing it. He used to spend 2 months building and 2 years marketing. Now: 2 years on product, then little marketing needed.
- Say no to any association that dilutes the brand.
- Age is no reason to quit. The second-best time to start is now.

## Playbook steps
1. Swap rituals for work hours. 2. Define what "figured out" means as measurable actions. 3. Take learning roles in your 20s. 4. Pour 1,000+ focused hours into one product until it's great. 5. Deliver on the first promise so every next offer sells to existing customers. 6. Guard brand associations. 7. Cut past-focused friends and drop limiting labels. Change your environment if needed.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Morning-routine obsession; copying habits rich people picked up after getting rich.
- "30 is the new 20" and assuming you have time; dead-end jobs with no growth.
- Optimizing for income too early by comparing yourself to the 0.1% on social media.
- Over-investing in sales and marketing skills while neglecting product and brand.
- Chasing new customers every month instead of compounding the ones you have.
- Letting family or others' opinions and labels steer your life; analyzing "why" and intentions.

## Examples / case details
- A DM'd founder whose coach had him on a 3-hour routine (grounding walk, gratitude journal, ice plunge). He replaced it with 3 hours of work and said it "changed my whole life."
- Alex left a defense consulting job (top-secret clearance, Ivy MBA path) to work for $13/hr at a gym.
- *$100M Offers*: began as an internal document. One post, zero paid marketing, sold out in minutes, #1 in its category for 2 years, thousands of copies/day. The cover "makes no sense," but referrals carried it.
- First book did ~2–4K copies in month 1. The second book did 250K in month 1 (vs. a typical NYT bestseller's 10–100K in 12 months). He credits first-book buyers coming back and referring.
- Gym Launch clients sent screenshots of their credit cards to support for his new software launch: "if Alex made it I'll buy it."
- He said he was bad at math until 25, dropped the calculator, and scored as well on GMAT math as on verbal.
- People back home call him a "hothead," which reinforces old labels.
