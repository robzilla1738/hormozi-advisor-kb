# how to force yourself to be consistent and do hard things

## Core thesis
Growth means the stretch between current and required, so wanting fast growth means wanting a lot of pain. Business is an infinite game with swapped-out players. Compare to learn, not to label yourself. The only guarantees: the more you roll, the better you get, and if you quit, you lose.

## Frameworks & models
- **Growth = deficiency between current and required → growing pains.** Plateaus of 1–3 years mean you haven't changed behavior. Fixing them often means two steps back (wrong leader, wrong department culture).
- **Compare without labeling:** "He's better at ads, what's he doing?" is useful. "...so I suck" is not.
- **Infinite game:** merchants existed 5,000 years ago and nobody knows who was #1. Generational pattern: build → maintain → third generation destroys.
- **Parable of the many-sided die** (from his *$100M Leads* book): you and a friend get dice with 20 vs 200 sides, one side green. Rolling green turns another side green. You can't see your side count. Keep rolling and every die eventually hits a green streak. Quitting is the only loss. "Learning how to advertise is a lot like the game of the many-sided die."
- **Mental toughness = frustration tolerance, fortitude, resiliency, adaptability.**

## Decision rules
- Expect ~5 years all-in before you "start figuring out what's going on" (side-project years don't count).
- Don't lose so badly that you "die". As long as you survive, keep playing.
