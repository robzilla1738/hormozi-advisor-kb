# More Followers Won't Make You Rich, But This Will

## Core thesis
Content should attract the right buyers, not views. A 90-day test of broad content (relationships, fitness, food, advice to his younger self) raised views but cut quality applications and book sales, so he's going back to almost all business content. Organic content is for "free facial recognition" and giving value; ads do the asking. Build credibility by stating facts and showing proof, not by "how-to" opinions. A group Q&A with Skool community creators; several tactics below come from guests, not Alex (marked).

## Frameworks & models
- **Shorts and longs are separate tracks**: in his data, shorts viewers don't become long-form viewers. People have viewing habits by platform.
- **Give:ask ratio across everything people see**: once you run ads, ads take up the "ask," so organic content should be almost all gives. CTAs pointing to free stuff count as gives. His top YouTube video has a 14-minute CTA at the end. Shorts get zero CTAs. Stories can take 1–2 CTAs a week without hurting reach.
- **Circles of awareness**: they recognize your face → they had one good experience with your content → they know your name → regular viewer. Each level raises CTR, conversion and price.
- **Put the CTA outside short videos**: description, first comment, profile. Only about 5% watch to the end, so cutting the closing seconds can bring about 10× the views.
- **Instagram pictures and carousels = email**: guaranteed reach to people who already follow you, but no one new. Reels reach new people. Use images to nurture and for CTAs.
- **Marketing law: "State the facts and tell the truth"**, and under it, "say what no one else can say, show what no one else can show."
- **Proof ladder (for edification)**: best, "I helped people like you get X"; next, "I did the thing you want, and I'm like you"; next, "I did work you won't do; here's the result" (e.g., "I made 35,000 posts, here's what I learned"); with no proof, study and break down a big, verifiable success, or quote a credible figure (a guest's Marcus Aurelius example).
- **Education = ROI on time**: show how much time and money the lesson cost you (a guest named Evelyn spent X on ads and shared the findings).
- **Treat every viewer like a stranger**: he grew most when he treated everyone like they didn't know him ("like the guy who dates his wife like it's the first date"). He's dropping "branded" content; say what you've done, not who you are.
- **Twitter as the home base for content**: tweet 5–7 times a day. Winning tweets become reels, then he reads them to camera as clips, then strings about 20 into a YouTube video. Captions are other high-performing tweets. Remix the same idea four ways: other platforms, other formats, re-shooting, reposting.
- **Platform memory for reposts**: Twitter forgets fastest and forgives volume; TikTok is in the middle; Instagram has the longest memory and caps posts at about 2–3 a day; YouTube allows no reposts (Content ID).
- **"Too good to fail"** (a roommate's strategy): make content so good that even one viewer shares it. Give away the secrets; people pay for implementation.
- **Agencies first, then bring in-house**: tell agencies on day one that you'll move the work in-house, and learn from them (Leads book chapter).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Make business content and business-adjacent content (personal development, productivity, time management). Relationship content is "too many steps" from business-owner buyers.
- Use book sales and applications as quality measures, not views.
- Instagram: about 2–3 reels a day before you hit a cap. His output: 5–7 YouTube Shorts/day, 5–7 tweets, 2–3 reels, about 4 TikToks, plus a few YouTube long videos a week (doubled with Leila's account).
- Profile check: your bio and pinned tweet must prove who you are. "I help people grow Instagram" with 79 followers fails the first proof check.
- No planned content buckets. Talk about whatever is on your mind; variety follows.
- Fame is irreversible ("like a tattoo, like having a kid"). If you don't want it, get customers the ways you know and skip personal-brand content.

## Playbook steps
1. Choose the audience you want to buy; make business and business-adjacent content only. 2. Tweet many times daily; promote the winners to reels, clips and long videos. 3. Keep CTAs out of shorts; put them in the profile, first comment and description. 4. Profile/pinned post = one snippet of who you are plus proof. 5. Title with proof or a story ("6 ways I helped X") rather than generic how-tos. 6. Run ads to do the asking; keep organic as gives. 7. Repost your top ~10% "greatest hits" on platforms with short memory.
- Guest tactics (not Alex): ManyChat comment/DM keyword → automated DM with a lead magnet or funnel (collect name, email, phone) → paid Skool on the thank-you page; an automated reply comment ("check your DM") plus a 5-minute follow-up; lives 2–3×/day with profile reviews and a comment CTA; weekly free webinar → join Skool and get a free ticket to a monthly 3–5 day virtual bootcamp (normally a few hundred dollars) that upsells high-ticket mentorship. Alex: "one of my favorite pitch strategies." Raw, unedited shorts posted everywhere.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Chasing broad views and fame for its own sake.
- Generic "6 neat tricks" posts with no proof (a one-member group posting "how to be successful").
- Getting "fluffy" once you have an audience.
- Believing outside breakdowns of his strategy ("four pillars"): "I don't do any of that."
- Revenue shares for equity/advice: "it does not work unless you're doing minuscule amounts."

## Examples / case details
- Ran a 90-day test of wide content, then switched to almost all business content.
- 35,000 pieces of content; about 8M followers across platforms over 3 years.
- Writes his own books, with no ghostwriter, so they sell through word of mouth for years rather than one-day spikes.
- Best part of fame: he can recruit anyone. Cost: no privacy, so he "can't have bad days."
- He values his social presence at about $1B, based on media volume and its conversion value.
