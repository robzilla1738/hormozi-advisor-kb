# Building a $1,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 26 Minutes

## Core thesis
Live "Cash Cows" teardown of Luis Lora's Optimum Works, an online railing store: $2.5M revenue, $384K profit, 15% net margin, 81% of customers from Google Ads, CAC doubled, about 10% repeat buyers. Alex deliberately gives only 2–3 high-impact moves so they actually get done. (1) Fix attribution first, because the numbers aren't usable. (2) Reposition around the least price-sensitive segment he already has the most of (DIY plus custom), raise prices, and send custom inquiries through a form → calendar → VSL → SMS BANT → close-on-call funnel. (3) Run a long-term email nurture to bring back repeat buyers. One-year follow-up: $3.6M revenue (+44%) and $540K profit.

## Frameworks & models
- **Sanity-check the unit economics**: the reported LTV:CAC of 0.41:1 (1:1 self-reported) "makes zero sense" for a profitable business where ~80% of sales come from Google. Either attribution is broken, or the agency is wasting spend while referral/past-buyer traffic carries sales. "We have numbers, but they're not useful," which allows "zero optimization."
- **Pick the segment**: DIY buyers are less price sensitive, the largest share (70%), and most obsessed with the product. Their AOV is close to contractors' and designers', so nothing is gained by chasing price-shopping pros, who turn you into a commodity. Raise DIY price (e.g. $873 → ~$1,000) for the same order count at much higher gross margin.
- **Custom funnel**: one-step form plus calendar → post-opt-in, pre-call VSL (gives price ranges) → SMS nurture asking BANT (budget, authority, need, timing) → call → take the card.
- **VSL structure** (same as his YouTube videos): hook → proof, promise, plan, picture/roadmap → open loop on pricing and delivery expectations → the meat (how to choose a railing) → price ranges and expectations plus BANT framing → "that's why we have the call." About 5–7 minutes.
- **Anchor**: railings add more home value than they cost, so "railings are basically free."
- **Close on the call**: A/B close ("credit card or finance? Same to us"). Tell them before the call there is a $200 discount for moving forward on the call because it saves admin. Frame the call as a buying call, not a long sales process.
- **Let them dream**: show the 3–4 looks people love most (modern, etc., plus "wild ideas").
- **Hypothetical math (his)**: double the close rate × more than double the gross margin per customer ≈ 4×. He also says doing just these steps could mean "a three or four X."
- **Long-term nurture email**: email the whole list 2×/week, rotating before/after, a cool transformation, and FAQ (the ~20 questions non-buyers ask: install, shipping...). Structure: subject → immediate reward (image, "check this out") → CTA/FAQ link → PS (discount or joke).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Give a few high-impact actions rather than many, to raise the odds of execution.
- Don't make spend decisions without attribution. The "ballsy" test is to shut off ads and watch sales (Luis declined).
- From $3M to $10M and up, "you have to know these data like the back of your hand." Colder audiences demand more targeted spend.
- Don't force continuity where it doesn't fit; use consistent nurture so you're first in mind for the next project.
- A small list relative to revenue (10K list at $2.5M) is probably a very good list.
- Put the custom-order option on every product, not just best sellers, and add a sticky "custom designs available" banner.
- Sequence: build the system (funnel/page) first, then drive traffic, then add the VSL and script, then long-term nurture. Outsource the nurture in parallel ("write a check").

## Playbook steps
1. Fix attribution and reallocate spend from losers to winners. 2. Reposition the site as "the home for DIY railings" showing custom possibilities. 3. Custom CTA on all products plus a sticky banner. 4. Form plus calendar → VSL with price ranges → SMS BANT confirmations. 5. Call: let them dream, A/B close, financing option, on-call discount. 6. Raise prices. 7. Email the list 2×/week with a rotating before/after, transformation, and FAQ.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Trusting dashboards whose numbers contradict reality (an agency reporting impossible LTV:CAC).
- A blanket "more awareness = more sales" strategy at scale.
- Selling to price-sensitive pros who shop you against competitors and commoditize you.
- Multiple sales calls ("a waste of your time").
- Over-complicating: "I could give you 20 more things."

## Examples / case details
- Spend: ~$21.6K/mo Google, $4.5K Meta (scaled to $1–2K); $300K+ marketing last year. Spend went from ~$21K/mo to $40K/mo and revenue doubled, but it was untracked. A post-purchase survey says 90%+ found them on Google.
- Mix: 70% DIY; 70% off-the-rack / 30% custom; 10–20% of sales by phone. 5-year-old business, full-time for 2 years.
- He projected a 50–60% close rate and $1,300–1,500 DIY tickets.
- **Follow-up (March 2026 vs April 2025)**: $3.6M revenue (+44%), $540K profit (up from $384K, ≈+41% [inference: computed]). Custom grew to 50% of the business. Fixing attribution exposed breakeven campaigns, and money moved to winners. After raising prices, the close rate is 20% (vs his projected 50–60%) with triple the custom leads. Luis says the biggest lever was optimizing for custom orders, including the page and sales process.
