## Core thesis
This is a hot seat at a seven-figure mastermind. A gym owner with 3 locations, planning a 4th and thinking about franchising or licensing, makes about 100K+ profit across all three; when one gym had 40K/month profit. Alex's diagnosis: the unit economics are too weak to scale, license or franchise, and the owner is in a rush. Fix the model first: consolidate, make one location wildly profitable without you, and only then multiply it. Ask yourself whether the goal is status or money.

## Frameworks & models
- **Exit routes for a gym**: sell to a customer or an employee. Use SBA loans (very cheap at the time) plus some seller financing to reach a number you're comfortable with without much cash drag.
- **Unit economics before multiplication**: franchising and licensing only work when each corporate unit has a high return on capital. Buyers look at that unit.
- **Small-market trade-off**: lower costs and less competition → should run *higher* margins. Leads are great early then dry up, so retention and word of mouth matter more. Expand to other small towns where the model is proven rather than a competitive city (Fresno), where rivals pull 250–400K/yr per location.
- **Consolidation play**: merge one location's EFT (members' recurring billing) into another. Lose maybe half but put it all on the same fixed overhead, and exit leases as they end.
- **Asset on the balance sheet**: a unit that grows without you, run by an incentivized team, can then be sold, licensed, franchised, or owned outright at scale.
- **Status vs money** self-check.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Don't open another location while existing ones profit ~30K/yr each. "This shouldn't even be discussion."
- Even adding back the 50K one-time expense, ~50K net per facility: "no one's interested in that."
- Corporate locations should run 30%+ margins before anyone considers buying in.
- Get back to ~40K/month profit from one location *without you there*, even if it takes 1–2 years. Then discuss expansion.
- He bets that cutting to one focused location would beat the profit of all three.

## Playbook steps
1. Hard look at the weaker two locations. 2. Consolidate one (move members onto shared overhead). 3. Exit leases at term. 4. Make one gym as profitable as possible (he cites 30%+ margins; ~40K/month). 5. Build an incentivized team so it grows without you. 6. Only then choose: sell, license, franchise, or own them all.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Scaling poor unit economics: "where everyone fails is they're just not profitable enough."
- Franchising for ego: there are countless 3–5 location mom-and-pop franchises, and most never get above 30 locations. Founders impress friends and family but are broke.
- Rushing ("it feels like you're in a rush"), and chasing the "easy" new market while existing units go flat. You end up with four locations and no profit.
- Making the expansion the goal rather than the work. Build the best gym out of pride, not "so that" you can do X.
