## Core thesis
There are only two ways to grow: get more customers, or make each one worth more. Alex runs the same 4-part checklist on every company, as a loop: **Math** (acquire customers at a profit, paid for by the customer), **Offer** (so good people feel stupid saying no), **Traffic** (six pools so you "never go hungry again"), and **Money** (raise lifetime gross profit until you can outbid everyone on any channel). The back end informs the front end. He credits this system with turning $1,036 into $112,496,422 in revenue over 44 months, on $3.1M of ad spend (36:1 lifetime ROAS, which checks out). It's a live keynote with audience asides (Brad / LightSpeed).

## Frameworks & models
- **15-minute lead gen**: Google how to run a Facebook lead ad, connect it to Google Sheets with Zapier, target locally or with a lookalike built from your customer list, and give away something free for a phone number. He says lead-gen firms charge about 5× for this and pay their button-pushers under $15/hr.
- **Definitions**: CAC (all acquisition costs: ads, payroll, media buyer, creative, software), COGS, gross profit, payback period, cash conversion cycle, **30-day cash** (gross profit collected from a new customer in the first 30 days).
- **Client-financed acquisition**: if 30-day gross profit from one customer > 2 × (CAC + COGS), you can grow without limit on a credit card's 30-day float. Example: borrow $100, get 2 customers at $50 each, each spends $300 ($600), fulfillment costs $100, repay $100 → $400 cash plus 2 customers. Repeat and double. "You shouldn't have a marketing budget." The constraint becomes operations, not acquisition.
- **The offer's multiplicative effect**: a better offer lifts CTR × opt-in × close rate × price at the same time, and lets you "sell in a vacuum" with no price comparison.
- **7 offer levers**:
  1. Promotion: free / discount / premium. Free converts 5–10× other offers. Order is create flow → monetize flow → add friction. A discount can be shown 4 ways: % off, absolute $ off, relative equivalent ("a month's mortgage," "a steak dinner a week"), now-vs-was. Premium: charge far more than you think. Free front end plus premium upsell produces 30–150:1 ROAS.
  2. Core offer (Value Equation): dream outcome × perceived likelihood ÷ (time delay × effort & sacrifice). He now focuses on the bottom half, like Amazon and Netflix. Give an early first win, and halve what competitors take: "it's the easiest thing in the world to sell speed."
  3. Bonuses: make the ask, then stack bonuses when they hesitate. The core product anchors and the stack makes it a steal. Bonuses replace discounts. Keep a "vault" of bonuses with a listed street value.
  4. Scarcity (units): real limited supply, or a limited number of rotating bonuses.
  5. Urgency (time): cohort-based ("new group starts Monday"), name-based (reskin the offer monthly), exploding opportunity (arbitrage).
  6. Stacked guarantees: an unconditional one on one timeline plus a conditional one tied to activation points (e.g. "make $10K in 60 days or I pay you $10K, if you attend every call and pay on time"). Jason Flatland's close: "I'm not asking you to decide, I'm asking you to make an informed decision, which you can only do inside."
  7. MAGIC naming: a magnetic reason why (season, anniversary, birthday: "think like a fraternity party planner"), announce the avatar, plus a timeframe and a container word (blueprint, kickstart, sprint, accelerator). Same program reskinned as Fall 6-Week Challenge, Spring 28-Day Blueprint, Halloween 42-Day Detox.
- **Free cash offers**: *limited free* (a bare-minimum version, then upsell the guaranteed premium one); *free with deposit* (pay up front, get it refunded or credited when they hit the result or habits; "made more money than any offer on this board"); *giveaway* (1st prize is the core package; everyone else "wins" 2nd prize, a fixed-dollar discount, e.g. $600 off $4,000); *free with prepay* (buy 3 get 3; he says prepaid churn is about 1/5 of month-to-month); *split-off free* (one session of a multi-session service like tanning or laser hair removal); *invisible free* (attend free with a card on file, billed unless you say it wasn't worth it; also fixes no-shows).
- **Advanced funnel**: magnet → up-front cash offer that covers CAC → upsell / downsell → continuity.
- **Never Go Hungry Again (6 traffic pools)**: paid, earned, owned, outbound, affiliates/partners, referrals. He says he's done $20M+ from each except earned media.
- **Branded search**: bid on your own brand terms. He puts ROAS at 20–40:1 with about 10 minutes of setup.
- **Quarterly push**: regular value content, then once a quarter a 4-day blitz on your list with a real-scarcity offer.
- **Dean Jackson's 9-word email**: "Do you still need help getting [problem] solved?"
- **Partner integration**: get your offer into a partner's onboarding to solve their activation problem (e.g. personal-trainer software needs its trainers' first 5 clients). Or be the affiliate yourself (40–50% of top line, no fulfillment), and sell their thing first so they reciprocate.
- **Golden ratio**: % of customers who refer > % who churn means growth with nothing else. About 30% of their customers still come from referrals.
- **Ask at the activation point**: the person receiving a favor is most grateful right after it, while the giver overrates it later. Ask for testimonials and referrals at the first "magical moment." Find that moment by studying what your longest-tenured customers did early on.
- **Business Genie**: 30 new/mo, 100 clients, $100/mo, 20% margin = $10K revenue / $2K profit. Doubling new clients or purchases doubles profit to $4K. Doubling purchases is better because LTV doubles, so you can spend more to acquire. **Doubling price gives 6× profit** ($200 − $80 = $120 vs $20).
- **LTGP (lifetime gross profit)**: one-time-purchase businesses: total revenue ever × gross margin ÷ total customers ever. Recurring: divide by churned customers instead. Underestimate on purpose.
- **6 ways to raise LTGP**: (1) raise price; (2) cut fulfillment cost (offshore admin at $4–12/hr, tools like Subtitle and Design Pickle, tech that makes chat ~100× more efficient, buy wholesale); (3) more purchases (less churn, better activation and onboarding, follow-up for repeat buys, better client outcomes under rev share); (4) upsells: *more* (units or time, e.g. 12 or 24 months of support) or *better* (quality, speed, access/convenience). "Fast beats free." (5) downsells: turn a no into a yes, or keep someone who's leaving by removing a feature they don't use; usually near-100% margin; (6) cross-sells: the next natural need before and after the purchase. Refer it out for commission, or bring it in-house once the partner earns more on your customers than you do.
- **Upsell price rule**: price the upsell at about 5× the core. If 1 in 5 buy (80/20), revenue doubles.
- **Loss-leader model** (credited to DigitalMarketer): $1.91 to get a car through the drive-through, $0.18 gross profit on the burger, $1.14 on fries and Coke (about 6×).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Grow on 30-day cash: 30-day gross profit > 2 × (CAC + COGS).
- Start new businesses with a free offer.
- "Eyeballs are never going to get cheaper." Your only lasting edge is making each customer worth more to you than to anyone else. "My marketing doesn't work… it's probably because your business doesn't work."
- Paid starter: a $100/day "slush fund" you've accepted losing.
- Outbound starter: 100 reach-outs a day for 6 months; use multiple channels. They put 10–20% of ad spend toward outbound, expect no ROI for about 2 months, broke even in about a quarter, and now see about 20:1.
- Partners: name the 10 biggest and spend real money getting their attention.
- Refer adjacent needs out until the partner earns more on your customers than you do, then build it yourself.
- Price is "the number one lever": on gyms (avg $129/mo, 12.5% net), +$38/mo roughly triples profit. [inference: on those figures it's about 3.4×, and he says 3.1×]
- Downsells must cost you almost no time or money.

## Playbook steps
1. Work out 30-day cash, CAC, COGS and LTGP. 2. Rebuild the offer: free/discount front end → Value Equation core → bonus stack → scarcity/urgency → stacked guarantees → MAGIC name. 3. Pick 1–2 neglected traffic pools (start with branded search, calling your own leads, the 9-word email, a quarterly push). 4. Pull every LTGP lever (price first). 5. Update the math and loop again.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Having a fixed "marketing budget" instead of client-financed acquisition.
- Commoditized offers that get price-shopped. Discounting instead of adding bonuses.
- Only one guarantee, or a lame one.
- Running the same promo until it's tired (reskin it instead).
- Not calling your own leads. Not owning your branded terms.
- Shallow partner promos (one webinar) instead of full integration.
- Asking for referrals with no set step in the process.
- Ignoring price.
- Lumping upsells and cross-sells together.

## Examples / case details
- Started in 2013 with Facebook ads the week before his gym opened. Grew to 6 locations in under 3 years off cash flow, sold them and lost everything when his business partner was indicted for fraud.
- Gym Launch $0 → $2.4M/mo in 18 months at 74% net margins. Prestige Labs $0 → $1.7M in 6 months. ALAN (agency software) $0 → $1.4M in 6 months. A recent acquisition (a brick-and-mortar chain of childhood experiences) went $40K → $700K/mo in 10 months. He cites 1,000+ success stories and 71 millionaires.
- Gym: a $99/mo bootcamp he couldn't sell became a 6-week challenge. $600 down, refunded if they lost 20 lb. $300 of supplements 48 hours later. On day 21 they're offered the $600 as credit toward a 12-month membership, and 70–80% took it. CAC fell to about a tenth and he got about 30:1 up front. That let him fill gyms "on someone else's money" before members' recurring billing (EFT) kicked in.
- B2B lead-gen agency: from $1,000 down + $1,500/mo to $4,000 once, then pay only per show. On the same $10K spend, response rate rose 2.5×, close rate 2.3×, and up-front cash went from $5K to $112K (11.2:1).
- Partner/affiliate scale: Prestige Labs has about 4,000 brick-and-mortar affiliates and sells through gym onboarding (a nutrition consult 48 hours after sign-up). ALAN has about 400 agencies white-labeling it. He's an affiliate for about 20 things. A physical-therapy client was told to teach brace and orthotics vendors' reps to sell, in exchange for referrals.
- Downsell motivator: whoever sells the low-ticket downsell gets lunch bought in their honor. He says it "worked better than commissions."
- He says he spent $50K on outbound courses and used none of it. "Just start."
- He sells consulting days at $100K each.
- **Math notes [inference]**: in the Genie example, 30 new clients a month staying 3 months gives an equilibrium of about 90 clients, not 100. His point still holds. The "Facebook makes $40/year per user" claim is about $3.33/mo, not the $2.50 he says. The agency price "quadrupled" compares $4,000 to the old $1,000 down payment.
