## Core thesis
Platforms differ enormously in money-making potential per unit of effort, so rank them with data, not follower counts. His five criteria: **who** (share of $100K+ households on the platform, per Pew), **how many** (US market share), **growth** (discoverability), **depth** (time per piece of content), and **conversion**. Then he gives his own results (share of acquisition.com site traffic, session length). Meta lesson: "look at your data." This video changed his own allocation (Facebook up, TikTok down). Stats are his account's as of recording.

## Frameworks & models
- **Platform scorecard (his numbers)**:
  - TikTok: 27% of $100K+ households / 33% US share; growth high but getting harder; depth 5–20s; conversion low (Gen Z prefers IG DMs; services are hard). ~900K followers (~1/10 of total) → 2.5% of traffic (~1/4 effectiveness per follower); 8s session. **F tier**.
  - YouTube: 89% / 83%; growth high (~80% of his views from suggested/browse); depth 8.5 min average view duration (Shorts 33s); conversion low–medium (must take people off-platform; community tab helps, 2.5M impressions vs 18M for shorts). 28.6% of traffic, converting 3–4× other platforms, so ~60% of contacts originate there; 91s session. **S tier**.
  - X: 29% / 22%; very viral (share button), word skill needed; ~34 min/day per user. Conversion high (DMs, bio link; a CRO agency with 8K followers did $5M/yr from split-test threads and 8–12 calls a day). 6.12% traffic ≈ 1:1 with its follower share; 30+s session. **A tier**. It's his content hub: all his tweets are his own, repurposed into IG captions and LinkedIn long posts.
  - LinkedIn: 53% / 30%; ~7 min/day; growth medium–high (sharing culture, connection-driven); conversion extremely high ("a hose with a laser pointer": Sales Navigator; ~40% of B2B marketers say it's their primary channel). 9%+ of traffic ≈ 2× its follower share; 40+s session. Hidden ROI: recruiting talent via DMs. **B tier** (near A).
  - Instagram: 54% / 47%; growth high (Reels; head of IG Mosseri says shares, especially off-platform, are the holy grail); depth 12–20s (skewed by his 3–5s tweet reels); conversion second only to LinkedIn (stories, swipe-ups, ManyChat keyword DMs, IG shop). 48% of traffic (more absolute traffic than YouTube) but 28s session, conversion lower than YouTube and about average. **S tier**.
  - Facebook: 68% / 68%; growth high again since Reels; conversion medium-high; 53s session (2nd highest). 150K followers → 3.8% of traffic, ≈1.5× TikTok on ~1/6 the followers ≈ 9× potency per follower (caption says "1/16"; the 9× figure only works with 1/6 [inference]). Moved from below F to **A tier**.
  - Podcast: 54% of $75K+ / 64% of Americans; very hard to grow alone (big podcasters grow via YouTube); deepest depth (20 min average listen); people only follow 3–5 podcasts; high trust but hard to act on mid-listen. **B tier**.
  - Email: 96% of $100K+ / 92%; can't grow itself; owned media; 40%+ opens; ~36:1 average return; 133s session. **A tier** ("LinkedIn Sales Navigator on steroids").
- **Honorable mentions**: Twitch (all-day streams), Snapchat (high revenue per viewer), Pinterest (visual/female-skewed niches like art, home décor, wine; an art dealer got 90% of business there), and **Amazon** as a sleeper traffic source. His book page converts ~30% vs ~2% Shopify average (15×). ~1,000 books/day = 1,000 customers/day who discover him through CTAs in the books. Amazon affiliate (~10% commission) can beat 20–30% commissions at 2% conversion.
- **Give in public, sell in private** (DM keyword funnels).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- On a budget, pick the 1–2 platforms with the highest return for your avatar; don't spread across 10. A Skool Games winner had 800K TikTok and 200K IG, with 90% of revenue from IG.
- Content must be platform-specific now; what works on one platform's shorts may not work on another's.
- Instagram penalizes over-posting: his sweet spot is 2 per day (morning and evening). TikTok and X don't penalize volume.
- If you're not making ≥30% of revenue from email, you're missing out.
- YouTube: obsess over the thumbnail (he split-tested 9) and the first ~8 seconds (8 intro takes cut from 30s to 8s). Put links above the fold in the description plus a pinned comment; keep in-video CTAs short.
- Start YouTube simply: he did 3 videos/week on a webcam; each week review the last 3 and improve one thing to build a checklist.
- Chase the right "who," not views: a dietitian with 5,800 IG followers (top post 19 likes) did ~$100K/month, about 10 sales a day of a $39–99 product plus manually emailing buyers to upsell. Educators, not entertainers: make content about what you sell and your avatar's problems.
- X/LinkedIn DMs: open with a specific compliment, never a cold ask. LinkedIn growth hack: write about bigger accounts so they comment (a comment acts like a collab; one post he commented on got ~50× its usual engagement).
- Get your first ~500 LinkedIn connections from your school and work networks.

## Playbook steps
1. Pull your traffic, contacts and revenue by source and session length from your CRM. 2. Score platforms on who, how many, growth, depth and conversion for your avatar. 3. Concentrate on the top ones, with a native format for each. 4. Use one hub (X for him) and repurpose. 5. Funnel everyone into owned media (email, podcast) for depth and conversion. 6. Consider Amazon/books as discovery plus conversion.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Buying fake followers. He bought ~70K Facebook followers from Thailand ads years ago, which poisoned distribution, and had to restart the account at zero.
- Chasing virality irrelevant to what you sell.
- Long intros (losing most viewers in the first 30s).
- Ignoring email or sending it sporadically (he admits he does this himself).
