## Core thesis
Choosing a spouse is the highest-leverage personal decision. You spend more time with them than anyone, and he cites a college economics graph of a ~0.71 correlation between subjective well-being and relationship strength. Yet people put less effort into it than into picking a car. His approach mirrors his business thinking: run a big pipeline (you only need one), find the "big domino" (become someone worth being with instead of using dating hacks), set clear priorities, match on shared goals and metrics, and measure the relationship by how much greater the sum is than the parts. A relationship and personal-philosophy video. Business relevance comes through the analogies: partner selection, priorities and trade-offs, judging people [inference].

## Frameworks & models
- **Inverse of sales**: dating is a pipeline, but you only need to convert one, so high standards ("unicorn") are fine. "Only have to find one."
- **Three camps**: (1) anyone with a pulse; (2) "I suck but they should see the real me deep down"; (3) relationship gurus' deception hacks. His alternative: the big domino. Make yourself worthy instead of 100 tweaks.
- **Exceptionally high standards, second only to the standards you hold yourself to.**
- **"You won't marry your type"** (his dad): if your type worked, you'd have a wife, not a type.
- **Judging people is a skill**: assess accurately (not good or bad). It takes pattern recognition, exposure and getting burned. You discover traits you didn't know mattered.
- **Same metrics**: pick someone who judges you on the metrics you judge yourself on. Mismatched metrics mean you can never win. Ambition should read to them as a give, not a take.
- **No compromise zone**: find someone who loves what you already do, rather than both compromising into a middle path neither enjoys.
- **Advertise the negatives** (via Rory Sutherland [caption: "Roy southernland"] on realtors): state your downsides; someone who is fine with them gets only upside. That is the "arbitrage," a steal on a partner.
- **Priorities, not a flat list**: "everything can't be a priority… this is literally strategy and business." If they trade #3 to keep #1, you chose that.
- **Price-tag acceptance**: state what you want and its cost; ask if they'll pay it too. Don't buy the $500 Nikes and complain about the price.
- **Trades of commitment**: novelty → loyalty (loyalty = ability to predict behavior); exploration → trust; the chase → a journey together.
- **Growth vs change**: your spouse marries who you are and who you'll become. Growth = more of what you are; change = becoming someone different. Making a partner "a project" is a bad sign.
- **Spouse as asset vs liability**: assets appreciate. Choose someone who keeps growing.
- **Life partner = business partner**: same goal and values, complementary skills. Success metric = how much better the sum is than the parts.
- **Cheerleader/quarterback** (via Gary Vee): a good spouse never asks you to leave the game when it's on the line or changes the rules at the 10-yard line.
- **Big goals + big work = ambitious; big goals + lazy = entitled.**

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Go on far more than 10 dates. He suggests ~100 dates gives a much better map of traits.
- Spend about 5 minutes on profile photos (it's an ad); the real work is being worth being with.
- The moment you know you won't marry someone, stop seeing them. "State the facts and tell the truth." Don't string people along on marriage or kids.
- Breaking up over different life goals is legitimate. Know your own goals first, or you're both "rolled dice."
- If someone takes you away from more than one of your priority goals, leave rather than try to change them.
- If you're deficient everywhere, start with the most important area.

## Playbook steps
1. Clarify your goals and ranked priorities. 2. Raise your own bar first; it may take "three or four iterations" of yourself. 3. Run volume (many dates) and break your type. 4. Lead with your negatives; look for people who find them neutral or positive. 5. Confirm they judge you by your metrics and will pay the price of your goals. 6. Commit and trade novelty for loyalty.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Camp 2 entitlement: expecting an amazing partner while offering nothing.
- Deceptive posturing; the truth comes out over a long enough time.
- Compromise relationships where neither gets what they want.
- Treating a partner as a project.
- Changing the rules mid-game.
- Wealthy men choosing "genetic lottery" partners who stagnate [his opinion].

## Examples / case details
- What he learned matters to him: a partner who respects him, follows traditional gender roles, and sees his work as a give.
- Leila is better at leading, managing and selling ideas to the team; he is better at selling ideas to the public (complementary skills).
- He forgot her birthday the first year (and his own); ambition was her #1, so the trade was understood.
- He amicably split from a long relationship over different life goals.
- Divorce stats as he states them: first marriages ~30% divorce; second ~70%, third ~80% (his recollection, not sourced).
- Since meeting Leila he says he is wealthier, fitter, less angry, more patient and kinder.
- Leila on being asked how to get a girl like her: the honest answer was "I would never be with someone like you."
