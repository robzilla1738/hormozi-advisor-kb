## Core thesis
A "Scale or Fail" consult with Mim Jenkinson (sticker-making education; ~A$1.1M / ~US$800K last 12 months, 30–35% profit, goal US$3M/yr, 2 staff). Delivery doesn't break if customers double, so it is a pure demand problem. Alex's fix: sell only the annual plan with a physical-kit premium, make far more creative (think in hundreds), add pain-aware hooks and narrow avatar call-outs, follow a back-loaded launch spend cadence, and stop capping ad spend: be willing to pay up to a $100 CPA when the back end supports it. Optional bolder test: position the paid bootcamp as a 7-day trial of the membership. Speakers: Alex plus guest Mim; a narrator explains the awareness framework.

## Frameworks & models
- **Business model**: $10 paid live bootcamp (3×/yr, no free entry) → upsells → Secret Sticker Society membership ($27/mo or $217/yr). CPA ~$37, AOV ~$36 (near break-even on front end), 22% of ticket buyers join the membership, LTV ~$300, ~5% churn.
- **Launch spend cadence** (4-week launch): ~5–10% of spend in week 1 purely to test creative; then scale through the next 2 weeks; ~60–70% of spend in the final week, with ~50% possibly in the last 3 days.
- **Creative volume**: "think in hundreds" (big advertisers make thousands). Sources: screenshots from the community group with highlights (static ads); AI statics; sticker-image ads; clipped daily lives (organic + paid); founder ads = top 20 historical winners × 4 settings (different background/shirt) = 80 ads.
- **UGC via activation**: a "super secret sticker set" unlocked when a member films themselves making a sticker. Make it the first-7-days activation task, so it's a quick win for onboarding AND every customer makes an ad (200 customers → 200 ads).
- **Levels of awareness** (Eugene Schwartz, explained by narrator): to scale, move hooks from product/offer-aware toward pain/problem-aware ("Are you bored?"). If CPA triples as you scale, it's usually because ads only target a narrow product-aware audience.
- **"100 ponds, no oceans"**: scale by going narrower, using many sub-specific avatar call-outs (beyond mothers vs grandmothers), not one message for everyone.
- **Physical product premium for annual**: the checkout bump offered the $47 course (~30% take). Instead bundle the physical kit/sticker paper they need anyway (value it ~$149; costs ~$10 + ~$10 shipping) and give it free only with the annual, only during the event ("costs a lot and I have to ship it").
- **Annual-only test (Test A)**: don't mention monthly at the launch; after cart close, run everyone else to the monthly (no bonuses).
- **Trial-as-front-end (Test B)**: position the bootcamp as a 7-day ($1 or $10) trial of the membership, so 100% of buyers enter continuity; the challenge doubles as onboarding; push annual; upsell tools.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If very profitable with a proven back end, don't cap spend arbitrarily: "Why don't you spend up to $100 CPA?" You get it back in the first month.
- Make annual the only offer at launch. Pitching annual moved annual take from ~5–15% (around 10%) to 30% and roughly doubled AOV (the "$91" figure).
- Only lower the annual price for more takers if LTV is low; with LTV ~$300 and a required kit, add a premium instead.
- If AOV rises to ~$300 and conversion halves, you still have the confidence (cash in first 30 days) to spend more.
- Test A before Test B. Run the test as a split with the same $10 price point so ads stay identical (one variant includes the trial).
- Optimize campaigns for purchases; track EPC (earnings per click).

## Playbook steps
1. Confirm delivery can absorb 2× customers (pure demand issue?).
2. Sell annual only, with the physical bundle as an annual-only, event-only bonus.
3. Build hundreds of creatives (UGC activation, group screenshots, clipped lives, 20×4 founder ads, AI statics).
4. Add pain-aware hooks and many narrow avatar call-outs.
5. Use the back-loaded spend cadence; raise account spend capacity; accept CPA up to ~$100.
6. Track EPC; optimize for purchases.
7. Then split-test trial-as-front-end. End goal: "make 800 ads and spend $100,000 on my next launch."

## Mistakes he warns against
- Capping ad spend "for no good reason" while profitable (last launch spent only ~$16K; Alex spent "500,000 a day at the end of my launch").
- Running a handful of creatives (2 images + 2 videos) and trying to convert everyone with the same message.
- Offering the cheaper option: people take the monthly "because you're offering the cheaper one."
- Testing two big changes at once (he's "not always the biggest fan").
- Breaking a model that already works: layer changes on top.
