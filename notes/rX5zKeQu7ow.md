# We fired everyone who was late...this is why..
id: rX5zKeQu7ow / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rX5zKeQu7ow / duration: 568s (~9 min) / tags: 07-hiring-management, 08-ops-scaling, 12-mindset-leverage
Signal: medium

## Core thesis
Business has "smarts" (strategy) and "hearts" (culture and execution), and most owners, Alex included, overrate the smarts. Strategy is simple, and competitors can see yours. The difference is whether people actually execute the basics, which depends on the leader setting the bar and being intolerant of anything less than excellence. Watch for "Vinces," managers who quietly tell staff the standards don't apply.

## Frameworks & models
- **Smarts vs hearts**: "Find something that's good that people want and you sell it for a lot more than it costs you ... do as much of that as you possibly can." The rest is execution.
- **The "Vince"**: a manager who undoes the owner's training ("we don't do any of that stuff"), creating a new cultural norm that secretly destroys the business.
- **Set the tone, then carry the bar.**
- **CEO as championship coach (John Wooden)**: with the same rules and talent pool, the winner is the aligned team that actually does the fundamentals.
- **"Advanced people never don't do the basics."**

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you've tinkered with offer, pages, pricing or terms for months with no change, the problem is likely people and culture, not strategy.
- As long as the strategy is relatively sound (not selling at a loss), execution decides the winner, even against a better-strategy competitor.
- Hire leaders for character traits, self-awareness and interpersonal skill as much as or more than functional skill.
- "Employees can't do what I can do" is a limiting belief if you want a big business.

## Playbook steps
1. Define the basics (speed-to-lead follow-up within 5 minutes, sales call framework, onboarding). 2. Set an explicit bar and enforce it visibly. 3. Coach leaders on behavior and on why the work matters. 4. Fix interpersonal friction before inventing new strategy. 5. Audit for Vinces, including yourself.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Endless strategy tinkering.
- Tolerating a manager who undermines the training.
- Owners who complain that no one follows standards while a middle manager tells staff not to.

## Examples / case details
- In a gym partnership (a partner operating a $4.2M/yr gym), the partner called a 4:00 meeting, locked the door at 4:00, and had the latecomers outside told they were fired before the meeting began. He calls this resetting the bar. Alex notes the partnership itself was "horrible" and says he's not judging which management style is right, but the second (Vince) style is "definitely wrong."
- At Smoothie King, owner Dave trained him to clean and restock between customers. The manager ("Vince," a pseudonym) told him "we don't do any of that stuff."
