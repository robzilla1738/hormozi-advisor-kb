## Core thesis
"No one cares, just win." Nobody asks whose fault it was when something works, and nobody remembers who you asked for help, only whether you won. Refusing help so a failure is "on me" is ego. Root your identity in your behaviors (doing = being), accept that risk is guaranteed, and pay whatever it takes in time, money or favors to get help. He says 2025 was his hardest year in about 8.

## Frameworks & models
- **Fault is only asked in the negative.** Nobody asks whose fault a win was.
- **Humility in the "mental soup"**: "You can learn anything you want as long as you're willing to look dumb in front of other people for an extended period of time." He calls being willing to look stupid "the most underrated money-making habit." People who identify as smart often get stuck on pride.
- **Risk is guaranteed / double-edged swords**: joy and suffering come together, like sunny and rainy weather. "If you risk nothing you become nothing." Early on, what you have to lose is mostly in your head: social standing, looking smart.
- **Anti-fragile identity**: identity comes from what you do, not what others think you do, so it stays under your control.
- **"What does this change?" test**: losing $1K of $10K savings usually changes nothing about your food, car or home, so the suffering is voluntary.
- **Memory compression**: you remember moments, not durations. Mark good moments to remember and consciously compress bad ones.
- **Sword of Gryffindor**: "only takes in that which makes it stronger." Ask what version of events makes you better.
- **Ask down in status**: the ultra-wealthy ask lower-status people about what they know best. Whoever asks the questions leaves with more.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Do exactly what a mentor or vendor says, "to a T," the first time. Keep what works and toss the rest. Aim to be every mentor's biggest success story.
- Never expect someone to invest in you more than you invest in yourself.
- "Progress is greater than pride."
- Determination beats intelligence. Determination "looks like a long string of failures until it works."

## Playbook steps
1. When stuck, ask for help immediately and pay in time, money or favors. 2. Join the room of people ahead of you before you start. 3. Execute their advice fully, then filter. 4. Run "what does this actually change?" on setbacks. 5. Anchor your identity in controllable behaviors.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Failing alone to protect your ego, then blaming others' systems or partners.
- Successful people who stop asking for help because their status has puffed them up.
- Avoiding risk and looking dumb.

- Alex joined a gym-owner mastermind before he owned a gym, to start "10 years ahead."
