# They lied to you: Why Punishment DOESN'T WORK..
id: WRW7eY6K4Jo / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WRW7eY6K4Jo / duration: 505s (~8 min) / tags: 07-hiring-management, 12-mindset-leverage
Signal: medium

## Core thesis
Power = the ability to influence events or people; leadership is getting people to want to do what you want. Beyond persuasion (the "soft side"), behavior is directed by incentives, and positive reinforcement directs behavior toward what you want while punishment only pushes people away from the punishment, in any direction.

## Frameworks & models
- **Four-box incentive grid** (from a conversation with Dr. Trevor Cashey): add a positive (cookie for doing the chore), subtract a positive (no dessert/video games), add a negative (spanking), subtract a negative (no dishes / I'll drive you instead of the bus). The two reward-side boxes direct behavior toward a single outcome; the punishment-side boxes don't direct it as well.
- **Punishment → avoidance, not compliance:** punish a kid for sneaking out and the incentive becomes "don't get caught". Activity "sprays in any direction" away from the punishment.
- **Behavioral (not psychological) management:** measure circumstance → incentive inserted → outcome (yes/no). Skip guessing what's in people's heads.
- **Break the activity into many incremental steps and reinforce each one.** Rewards usually needn't be monetary: status and feedback ("great job, that thing you did yesterday was great").

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you want someone to *do* something → reward the behavior; don't rely on punishing its absence.
- Cites Jim Collins research as he understands it: constant negative/critical feedback degrades performance over time; mostly positive feedback improves it.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Only talking to people when they do something wrong.
- Nagging (a spouse, an employee): they just learn to avoid the nagging.

## Examples / case details
- Kid cleaning room (all four boxes). Jail/punitive systems as an example of avoidance behavior.

## Notable lines
