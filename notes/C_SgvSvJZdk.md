## Core thesis
An older video (from the Prestige Labs / Gym Launch era, "$120M+ in four years"). Alex lays out Munger's inversion "prescriptions for misery": invert the problem and avoid what guarantees failure. Munger's list: use mood-altering chemicals, be envious, resent people, be unreliable, overspend your income, learn only from your own mistakes, quit after your 3rd–4th big failure. Alex adds his own: crack under pressure. Most pressure is self-created expectations; he counters it with negative visualization, expecting the worst, and the "fine mist" perspective.

## Frameworks & models
- **Invert** (via Johnny Carson): start from what you don't want and solve backward.
- **Measuring-stick money**: your base unit for wealth rises over time (Chipotle burritos → $1M), so someone always seems richer. Envy is "an imaginary way to make yourself suffer."
- **Resentment drawer**: a king writes resented names on slips, puts them in a drawer, and later sees how life dealt with them.
- **Wealth = ratio of income to expenses**, not a number. Live on ~10%. He lived on $30K/yr with $1M in the bank and >$1M income (≈3%).
- **Learning ladder by stage**: trade time/free labor to someone ahead of you (e.g., a $100–200K/yr business) → coaches → masterminds (peers) → at 8 figures, networking and advisors (give an advisor a minority stake so they're incentivized to teach) → heroes you learn from via their content.
- **Pressure tools**: (1) Negative visualization (Epictetus, via Trevor): imagine the bad event 1,000 times and feel the 1,000th reaction now. (2) Expect the worst when starting a hard behavior (assume you'll always be tired and unmotivated, so anything better is a bonus). (3) "Fine mist" (Elon Musk): everything eventually disappears, so you choose what to give meaning to. The brain is a "meaning making machine."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Before deciding, ask "what would guarantee failure here?" and avoid it.
- Spend less than you earn and avoid bad debt. Cutting spending is the lever you can change immediately.
- Offering "I'll give you half" or "I'll work free if you teach me" to an expert is a bad trade for them. Offer your time to someone only slightly ahead who needs hands.
- Don't count your failures; don't quit early.

## Playbook steps
1. Invert any goal into a list of what would cause failure.
2. Set your living expenses at ≤10% of income and invest the excess in growing the top line.
3. Pick the right learning channel for your stage.
4. Under stress, use the 1,000-times visualization to reset your expectations.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Drugs and alcohol to cope; envy; resentment; flakiness; overspending and bad debt; learning only from your own mistakes; quitting after a few failures; cracking under self-imposed pressure.

## Examples / case details
