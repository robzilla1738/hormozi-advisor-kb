## Core thesis
Mostly a casual leg workout and lifting chat with Chris Bumstead (CBum) at Alex's Vegas gym. The business value is in a few mindset asides: define your own goals and "champion" standard instead of borrowing someone else's; measure wealth against what you believe you can create, not against other people; live as many of your "best days" in a row as you can; and treat anyone who makes more money than you as knowing something about business you don't. Large stretches are training and diet talk. The speakers alternate and the captions don't label them, so who said what is partly [inference].

## Frameworks & models
- **Wealth as a measure of personal growth**: he sees entrepreneurship as "the most quantitative path" of personal growth. The goal is "the richest man that [I can] become," not the richest man in the world. Comparing your wealth to other people's is "not the right game"; comparing it to what you believe you can create is.
- **Best-days strategy**: a mentor (his first boss) said the secret of life is living as many of your best days in a row as you can. For him, a best day = hard work on things worth doing, mostly creating (books, videos, podcasts about business), plus working out with people he enjoys.
- **Risk-adjusted return (investor lens on competition)**: a reigning champion should minimize risk and maintain; someone in 5th place should take every risk, because failing costs nothing and winning changes everything.
- **Criticism vs judgment/insult**: criticism points out a discrepancy ("she's bigger than I am in this way — why?"), meaning "I could do better." Judgment says "because of that you are bad." Use criticism, not judgment.
- **Choose which games to play**: a champion makes his own rules. If the rules change so that winning no longer fits your vision, don't play. Don't judge other people's goals; their objectives differ.
- **Match work type to energy**: he treats weekdays and weekends the same. On a good-energy day he does deep creative work (book chapters, scripting presentations) and moves calls. On a low day he takes meetings and calls instead of taking the day off.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If someone makes more money than you, assume they know something about business you don't, and study it.
- If you're behind, take asymmetric risks. If you're on top, protect the downside.
- On a bad day, change the nature of the work rather than stopping. "Take the day off" can turn into a lost year.
- Don't wait until you retire (or until your sport career ends) to start a business. By then "no one cares anymore" (said in the context of athletes).

## Playbook steps
1. Identify the days you do your best work and what they contain. 2. Arrange your life to repeat those days as often as possible. 3. Benchmark against your own potential. 4. When a peer outperforms you, turn it into a specific criticism ("what do they know?"), not self-judgment.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Measuring yourself against other people's scoreboards. He had a "7-day depression" after seeing Kylie Jenner on the Forbes cover.
- Copying someone else's definition of success (e.g., admiring Michael Jordan's "miserable" drive when you want peace of mind).
- Following a plan rigidly and missing the high-value experience in front of you (an example from lifting: a kid stuck to his program instead of training with the pros in town). [inference: applies to business plans too]

## Examples / case details
- Content cadence: his team does about 450 posts/week. Filming went from one full day every 90 days (~100 clips) to every other week, then Fridays, and now "lightning strikes" sessions when he feels fresh. A stage presentation to an empty room didn't work as well as a live crowd. Instagram Lives ("2,000 people in the room") worked better.
- After selling Gym Launch, his monthly owner cash flow went to zero. Despite cash in the bank he "felt poorer," which led him to redefine what money means to him.
- At about 26–27 he took home $17M in income that year, then felt like a failure next to Kylie Jenner. The caption reads "Kendall," but the self-made-billionaire story was Kylie [inference]. This was "career defining" because it got him into brand: she made more because she had a brand.
- He spends only 1–2 days a week doing business "hardcore." The rest is making things that help other people do business.
- Vegas as home base: tax-free, central for travel, and everyone visits once a year.
- A gym partner with a manufacturing background (the caption is garbled) was among the final bids on supplement manufacturing for Prestige Labs. Alex says "probably should have gone with him."
