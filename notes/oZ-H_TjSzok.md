# No BS Advice to Get Rich in the Next 10 Years

## Core thesis
Money rules he says separate losing everything twice from reaching a ~$100M net worth by 31. Everyone trades time for money, so judge activities by return per hour and pour money back into your active skills rather than chasing "passive." Commit to a 10-year horizon. Pay down ignorance as your top priority. Save through automation and spending friction. Buy time, not status. Your stack of skills is the moat.

## Frameworks & models
- **Passive income is a lie**: it's a spectrum of return on time. Everyone has a dollars-per-hour figure. Annual income ÷ 2,000 = your 40-hour work-week rate; ÷ ~8,000 = per hour alive. If an activity beats that number, it's probably worth your time. Invest money into scaling your active income that already works.
- **"If you can't buy it twice (or 3–5×), don't buy it once."** Price purchases in post-tax hours of work. At Smoothie King he made $6.75/hr (~$6 after tax), so a $13 lunch was 2 hours of a 6-hour shift and a $30 Gap shirt was a whole shift.
- **Saving → longer horizon → bigger bets**: saving doesn't make money, but having money lets you look further out and take bigger risks.
- **10-year contract**: would you make no money for 10 years in exchange for adding 1–2 zeros to your net worth? Then act like it, making bets so that failing to 10× in 10 years is "unreasonable." Potential earning vs actualized earning: an internship that 10×s your potential beats $100K now. "Instead of the S&P 500, put it in the 'me 500.'"
- **Only get rich once**: don't bet what you have and need for what you don't have and don't need.
- **Partner test**: he had 9 failed partnerships before Leila. A partner must bring money, experience, or time/energy you lack, and the contribution must be clearly defined.
- **Money = encapsulated time**: $100 may be 4 hours to one person and 4 minutes to another. Constantly re-appraise your calendar.
- **Measure to manage**: check your bank account before social media each morning. He did this until ~$20M, then moved to monthly or quarterly checks.
- **Learn to make money before making money make money**: $1,000 into skills beats chasing 10% → 20% returns (Dogecoin risk). Everyone has 5–9 a.m. and 5–9 p.m. Work earns twice: you make money and you aren't spending it. "Patience is figuring out what to do in the meantime."
- **Downside analysis**: list the ways you could lose, estimate likelihood, then multiply by 3 to correct for optimism. "If you don't know how you can lose money in something, you're going to lose it."
- **Cost of ignorance**: the gap between your goal and where you are ($1M goal vs $50K = $950K a year). Pay it down with relational capital, favors, or money (least efficient).
- **Declarative vs procedural knowledge**: masterminds and networks teach that things exist (unknown unknowns, breakthroughs). How-to teaching is more predictable.
- **Document → Demonstrate → Duplicate**: he paid an ads expert $750/hr for 8 sessions and learned the skill.
- **Status**: "To impress poor people you outspend them; to impress rich people you outwork them." "Buy time like a rich person, buy stuff like a poor person."
- **Buy time back**: the average American spends ~96 hrs/month on chores that cost ~$1,500/month to outsource, so it's logical if you earn more than ~$15/hr.
- **Reference group**: who you compare yourself to matters more than who you spend time with. Only take life advice from people whose dreams for you are bigger than yours.
- **Invest first, spend the rest**: the Switzerland example. Automate investing and add friction to spending. Advanced: keep spending fixed as income rises. Automate deposits and manualize withdrawals (pay bills by hand, delete shopping apps, don't save cards).
- **$1M is easier than $100K**: a bigger goal forces a different solution set (a car instead of running).
- **Earnings formula**: total value created × ability to negotiate your share × scarcity (how few others can create it). Uniqueness comes from a skill stack.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Do the highest $/hr activity until all your hours are spent on it, then find a higher one.
- Buy only if you could buy it multiple times. Ask "Would I work X hours for this?"; if not, don't buy.
- Partner only for clearly contributed money, experience or time you lack.
- Outsource chores if you earn more than ~$15/hr.
- Multiply your estimated downside by 3.
- Before paying for learning, know whether you're buying declarative or procedural knowledge.
- If you pay for help with a favor, "pay in full": do the whole job as if they were a paying customer, which earns real reciprocity.

## Playbook steps
1. Compute your hourly rate.
2. Check your bank account daily.
3. Automate investing and add spending friction.
4. Spend 5–9 a.m. and 5–9 p.m. on building skills.
5. Use savings to buy into rooms and education (he saved $50K over 2 years and paid $10K (caption says $110,000; likely garbled) [inference], then $25K–35K, for rooms).
6. In those rooms, give full favors (he did free sales training for members) and barter later.
7. Learn new skills with document/demonstrate/duplicate.
8. Buy back chore time.
9. Stack skills into a unique set.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Chasing passive income and under-investing in what is already working.
- Partnering out of insecurity (one of his two big losses).
- Status spending: watch → car → condo → yacht → jet.
- "There will always be time to make money later": "spend now, work later is the fundamental crux of poverty."
- Optimism blindness on new opportunities.
- Waiting too long to pay for learning.
- Half-done favors.
