# How I Would Build a $10M Service Business (If I Had to Start Over)

## Core thesis
This is a Scale or Fail case with Caleb, owner of Keys Roofing (St. Louis, a premium roofing/gutter contractor, 2 months old, ~$41K revenue at 59% profit, goal $7–10M in 3 years). Hormozi's advice for a resource-constrained small business: put every effort into the highest-return activity. Go all in on the channel that already works (door-to-door). Re-center the business on the high-volume repairs competitors ignore, and raise the repair ticket. Add a menu upsell, two VSLs, a membership credited toward repairs, and a speed premium. 90 days later: ~$32K/mo → ~$247K/mo, 85 services/mo, 62 five-star reviews (from under 20).

## Frameworks & models
- **Exploit what's been cast aside**: repairs (~$1,500 ticket, 64% GP ≈ $1,100) were ignored by other roofers. If GP per repair rises to ~$2K and you can sell 3× as many repairs as replacements (~$7K GP each), a systemized repair business is very attractive. Bundle all the non-replacement work to reach $2.5–5K tickets. Small jobs "chunk up" to future jobs.
- **High-volume > lumpy big tickets**: more stability, more reps for salespeople (shorter cycle), less insurance/payment admin, basically no cash-flow constraints. "With volume comes reliability," and reliability lets you forecast and cut cost basis by half or two-thirds. Early on you want "a heartbeat in the business."
- **Knowing how to go get money = extra lives**: being able to knock 20 doors and get a deal on demand is rare and valuable ("cats have nine lives").
- **Menu upsell (his favorite chapter of $100M Money Models)**: (1) unsell what they don't need (builds trust); (2) prescribe what they do need; (3) A/B fake choice (Monday or Tuesday?); (4) card on file. "Getting the first sale is the hard part. Getting the approximate sale is significantly easier." Works best for transactional sales done in one conversation.
- **VSL structure**: intro → promise (what they'll get and the pain they avoid) → proof (similar homeowners nearby, "houses as big or bigger than yours") → the plan → old way vs new way (your named process) → objections 1..n, each a belief-breaker plus proof. ~7 minutes. Two VSLs: a front-end one about the company before the first sale, and a back-end one before the onboarding/upsell call about what people don't know about their roofs. Reframe: a roof plan raises resale value; "you're fixing your house for the next guy."
- **Membership credited to repairs**: VIP maintenance at $15–20/mo paid annually ($180–240), with $2 in repair credit per $1 paid, plus an annual inspection. Variant: $500/yr, cut to $200 if they leave a review. The goal isn't the money but a guaranteed appointment to sell more. Turns one-time into recurring revenue → higher LTV and value.
- **Speed premium / priority boost**: when supply-constrained, let customers pay more (e.g., +20%, refunded if you miss the deadline) to move to the front of the line. Same work, more money. "Speed's all margin."
- **Commission framed per door**: 10% on a $4K repair = $400 at ~50 doors per sale → "want to make $10? Knock that door."
- **Scaling roadmap stage**: at 4–5 people (the 1–4 → 5–9 transition), install sales tracking. Treat salespeople as your new customer/avatar and market to recruit them.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Small business: focus all limited time and money on the single highest-return activity. "Don't be cute": scale the channel you're already good at.
- At zero the steps are identical (start with an LLC). At scale, advice has to be tailored.
- If you're supply-constrained and unsure how to add margin, add speed incentives.
- He prefers high-volume, shorter-cycle offers for cash-flow reliability early on.

## Playbook steps
1. Go all in on door-to-door as the primary growth engine and recruit a door-to-door "army" with clear per-door economics.
2. Push repairs: bundle to raise the average ticket to $2–4K.
3. Front-end VSL before the close. Back-end VSL before the onboarding call.
4. Onboarding rep runs the menu close: unsell → prescribe → A/B → card on file ("the hood's already popped").
5. Add the speed/priority premium at the end of the repair.
6. Add the review ask plus a maintenance membership credited toward repairs for recurring revenue.
7. Hire repair techs (3–5 jobs/day each). Add sales tracking as the team grows past 4–5.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Chasing only big replacement tickets while ignoring easy high-volume work no one competes for.
- Spreading thin across too many initiatives: "we need to knock more doors and sell more repairs."
- [inference] Membership offers fail on timing and scripting. Caleb hadn't found the residential pitch yet, and Hormozi said that tweaking placement/wording can get "80% buying."

## Examples / case details
- Start: ~$41K revenue in 2 months, $24,248 profit (59%). Replacements $18–25K average ticket. Close about 1 in 20 doors.
- After: 15–20 services/mo → 60–80 (then 85). Repair tech doing 3–5 jobs/day. Bought a custom ERP/CRM. Revenue ~$32K → ~$247K in 90 days. Reviews <20 → 62.
- Speed test: a commercial client's emergency job, "tomorrow for $300 more." They took it and bought the next job.
- Caleb on the menu upsell: taking options off the table "automatically builds that trust... people are just taking every recommendation."
