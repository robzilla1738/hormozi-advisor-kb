## Core thesis
"No half measures": either pitch hard or don't pitch at all. Alex bombed a mastermind talk by rushing a half-hearted pitch with no pre-frame. The better default is to keep giving and hold back the ask. Goodwill makes people hand you things, and when you do withdraw from it, ask hard and mean it.

## Frameworks & models
- **No half measures**: ask hard or don't ask. Skipping scarcity, guarantees and bonuses because the audience "already knows" them removes the things that persuade. "People still need it to make decisions."
- **Give…get model**: his extension of jab-jab-jab-right-hook is give, give, give → get, get, get (unasked) → give again. Reciprocity means people want to repay.
- **Goodwill = relational capital**: an ask is a withdrawal. Don't nickel-and-dime with small asks; make one clear, deliberate one.
- **Hyper-learning in emotional moments**: when humiliated or angry, deliberately pick the lesson to ingrain (via Dr. Cashy, as he refers to her: strong emotion → heightened learning).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you pitch, run the full persuasion structure (pre-frame, story, offer elements), even to sophisticated audiences.
- Usually you're better served holding back the ask.
- Being annoyed with objections during Q&A ("if you need a payment plan, don't buy it") kills the room.

## Playbook steps
1. Default to giving value. 2. Let goodwill accumulate. 3. When you ask, signal clearly that you're asking and use full offer mechanics. 4. After a failure, consciously extract the lesson while emotions are high.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Speed-running a 90-minute pitch deck after 2.5 hours of value with no pre-frame.
- Dismissive responses to objections (payment plan, vegan supplements).
- An exec at his gym business half-pitching ("you don't need the scarcity thing...") and bombing.

## Examples / case details
- Fitness mastermind: a 3-hour slot, about a third of the promised audience (~30 people), and a $2,000 product. It was the first time he'd sold anything under $16K, a pared-down version of a licensing model used by "4,000 locations" at 1/8 the price. The company was doing ~$4M/month then.
- Scam disclaimer: he doesn't DM or WhatsApp people asking for money. Impersonators thrive because he doesn't ask Mozi Nation for anything.
