## Core thesis
Three numbers matter in any business he invests in or partners with: **CAC** (fully loaded), **LTV measured as gross profit, not revenue**, and **30-day cash** (gross profit collected in the first 30 days). He wants LTV:CAC above 3:1 and 30-day cash:CAC at or above 1:1. The second lets you finance acquisition with other people's money, such as interest-free credit for 30 days, which is how his companies grew fast.

## Frameworks & models
- **CAC = all acquisition costs**: sales commission, ad spend, marketing team, related software.
- **LTV = lifetime gross profit**, not lifetime revenue.
- **30-day cash** (his version of payback period): what a customer pays you in the first 30 days. If it is at least CAC, credit card float (~30 days interest-free) funds acquisition.
- **Customer cash curve**: −$200 CAC at day 0 → +$50 first purchase → upsells reach $200 by day 30, so you're break-even → recurring revenue at days 60/90/120 is "for free."
- **Levers to raise 30-day cash**: upsells, a liquidation offer, special offers in the first week or two, ancillary products, affiliate or referral-out revenue. A higher 30-day cash lets you spend more on acquisition and "open up further my acquisition net."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- LTV(gross profit):CAC > 3:1 → sufficient profit to grow.
- 30-day cash:CAC ≥ 1:1 → break even within the credit window and use OPM.
- If below these metrics, fix it with offers or upsells before scaling.
- Base all decisions on gross margin.

## Playbook steps
1. Compute fully loaded CAC. 2. Compute gross margin → lifetime gross profit. 3. Measure gross profit collected by day 30. 4. Check the ratios (3:1 and 1:1). 5. If short, add upsells, first-week offers or ancillaries to lift 30-day cash. 6. Scale spend on credit once 30-day cash covers CAC.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Using revenue LTV. In the food example $700 of lifetime orders at a 10% margin = $70 gross profit, so spending $200 to acquire is "horrible."
- Getting lost in vanity KPIs (CPL, CPC, CTR) before the three big buckets.

## Examples / case details
- 80% gross margin service with a $1,000 lifetime spend → $800 GP → CAC must be under ~$266 for 3:1. He says "265.66"; $800/3 = $266.67 [inference: minor arithmetic slip in the video]. If 30-day cash exceeds ~$260, acquisition is paid back in 30 days and the remaining ~$500+ is profit.

- "I don't actually need to use my own money to acquire customers."
- "If something's below these metrics then you just know that you have to fix it."
