## Core thesis
Price follows value, and value has four parts: (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice). Push the top up and the bottom down. Since it's a fraction, driving the bottom toward zero makes even a small promise "infinitely valuable." Beginners only inflate the promise. The biggest companies (Amazon, Netflix, Uber) win by shrinking delay and effort, and he sees that as the strongest moat.

## Frameworks & models
- **Value equation**: Dream outcome (top, increase), Perceived likelihood of achievement (top, increase), Time delay (bottom, decrease), Effort & sacrifice (bottom, decrease).
- **Dream outcome defines the category.** Example: for men, making money raises status more than losing weight, so money products cost more. The other three variables decide price within a category, e.g. a $5 weight-loss ebook vs $50,000 liposuction.
- **Perceived likelihood**: a surgeon on his 10,000th surgery vs his first. You pay more for the experienced one even though he takes less time, so fulfillment time should never enter your value math.
- **Effort vs sacrifice**: effort = what they must do and don't want to; sacrifice = what they must give up that they enjoy. They are two sides of one coin.
- **Side-by-side scoring**: personal training scores 1/4 (dream yes; likelihood, speed and effort all zeros), liposuction scores 4/4. That gap is the difference between "arm wrestling" someone for an hour over a $1,000 package and selling $40,000 packages over the phone in under 15 minutes.
- **Dad's questions = the components**: "If I made you $239,000, would you pay $42,000?" Then: how long (11 months), am I sure (likely), what do I have to do (~15 hrs/week), and pay as you earn. Answering those made it a yes.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- When competing against free, compete on speed: "Fast beats free."
- To enter a market, do what the best player does in half the time.
- Increase likelihood with testimonials (1,000 beats 1), because certainty reduces risk.
- Frame the dream outcome as the real job to be done, then describe it vividly in the prospect's own terms.

## Playbook steps
1. Define the dream outcome correctly (job to be done) and describe it viscerally. 2. Increase perceived likelihood (proof, testimonials, track record). 3. Shorten time to result. 4. Remove effort and sacrifice. 5. Score your offer against alternatives on all four.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Only promising bigger outcomes. He calls it the most amateur move and says it's where he started.
- Using "value" loosely without defining it.
- Pricing by how long fulfillment takes.

## Examples / case details
- 600–700 clients at his highest-level program (~$42K/yr) at an in-person event when he was 28; 135 employees flown to Austin.
- A friend runs DMV expediting in New Mexico: 14 locations, $50 vs the free government service, and "they murder it."
- Precision Nutrition (Dr. John Berardi; caption reads "Bardi"; nine-figure exit): certifications were really bought for community and belonging with "people just like you," not continuing education.
- Lyft priority pickup for an extra ~$5; Amazon two-hour delivery; Netflix removes the effort of watching.
- Portfolio at ~$85M/yr at the time; book promoted at 99 cents.
