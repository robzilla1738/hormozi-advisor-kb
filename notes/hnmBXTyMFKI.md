## Core thesis
"My market is saturated / too small / red ocean" is almost always false: unless you sell to ~140 people in a rural town, your market is 100–1,000× bigger than you think and you've tapped <1% of it. The real constraint is skill (ads, awareness levels, channels), not market. Red oceans have the most fish. ## Frameworks & models
- **Pie illusion**: competitors entering feels like splitting the pie into quarters; actually your slice is one medium on one platform out of all content platforms (IG, FB, TikTok, YouTube, X, radio, TV, Taboola, Google, direct mail, email, Skool...), all paid ad platforms, and all outreach methods (mail, DMs on each platform, calls) — plus every format within a platform (stories, carousels, shorts, long-form, community posts).
- **Awareness levels**: if you only advertise to people already aware of your solution/product, you hit a ceiling; larger markets sit at lower awareness.
- **Puddle → pond → lake → ocean**: start niched (biggest fish in a puddle, fewer competitors, niche pricing power, capped but high upside), expand as skill grows (his path: trainer → nutrition → gyms → business).
- **5 directions to move a market**: up-market (gyms → franchisors/multi-location), down-market (trainers), adjacent (chiropractors), broader (health & wellness: chiros, med spas, weight-loss clinics), narrower (spin or dance studios).
- **Local business expansion**: (1) expand the location's capacity (knock walls, more seats, shorter classes, fewer breaks, more hours), (2) add channels, (3) open another location.
- **Cost of switching**: in year 3 of A, a new B starts at its year 1 while A is at year 4; growth is easier when bigger, so you stay behind.
- **Business model shapes**: info/coaching = fast start, hard to scale (no revenue retention); SaaS = slow start, burns money/time, then scales fast; e-com = scales fast but cash flow/inventory/supply chain/3PL pain; service = slowest but steady, people-heavy.
- **Supply vs demand constraint by industry**: fitness coaching = easy talent (people would do it free), hard retention; accounting = sticky customers, hard talent; cleaning = hard talent. If you're the inverse of your industry, you're doing something very right or very wrong (or just starting).
- **Restate excuses as skill gaps**: "no good salespeople in my market" → "I don't have the skill to attract, hire, manage a good salesperson"; you become source.
- **Cooler-story razor**: at 85, pick the cooler story; the hard thing is usually right — "if the easy thing were the right thing, you wouldn't have had to make this decision."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Local: if you need ~200 customers and there are 400K–1M people in your city, market size isn't your problem.
- One avatar, one product, one channel until ~$1M/yr. Exception: local businesses in C/D markets (he guesses <50K people in a 10-mile radius) likely need multiple channels.
- $10M/yr is reachable in pretty much any business (except brick-and-mortar with ~100 people in the market) — by duplicating the model into new markets.
- Test: "If I only did one thing for 40 years, would I be successful?" Probably → stick with it.
- Can't scale ads past ~$1K/day? Your ads/awareness skill is the problem, not Facebook saturation.

## Playbook steps
1. Define your market position (most small owners take anyone with a credit card).
2. Make content in more places, more of it, better.
3. Add ads, then pair outreach → omnipresence, consistently for a long time.
4. When the niche is outgrown, move one of the 5 directions.
5. Local: maximize the four walls → add channels → new locations.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Blaming the market to protect your ego.
- Confusing "not enough leads" with "market too small."
- Quitting at the hard part of your model's curve instead of recognizing it's a feature.
- Wandering eye toward new opportunities; never going all-in.
- Playing for the "pity award."

## Examples / case details
- A gym-software sales rep candidate's company (unheard of to him) did ~$10M/month on outbound — ~5× Gym Launch then — which broke his belief and led him to outbound.
- First gym: cut classes 45 → 30 min to fit ~1.5× more classes, cut breaks, extended hours.
- acquisition.com portfolio did >$250M aggregate revenue last year (his claim); local businesses ~35% of his audience (his estimate).
