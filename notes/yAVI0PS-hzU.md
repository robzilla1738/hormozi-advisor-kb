# Unlock High Income Skills Without a Degree
id: yAVI0PS-hzU / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yAVI0PS-hzU / duration: 582s (~10 min) / tags: 12-mindset-leverage, 05-sales-process, 07-hiring-management
Signal: medium

## Core thesis
"I'm bad at X" usually just means "I haven't put in the reps." Ask: **would it be reasonable for me to be good at this given the investment I've made?** Expect to be reasonably bad until you've done so much that it becomes unreasonable to still be bad. High-income skills like sales, marketing and business math are learnable by anyone who puts in the hours.

## Frameworks & models
- **Success stack**: skills → character traits (to keep doing the skills) → correct beliefs, which run through the whole process.
- **Reasonableness test**: zero hours of sales training means it's unreasonable to be good at sales. The same goes for marketing courses and study hours.
- **"If someone can, therefore I can"**: a cornerstone belief. "No one's done it and I'll be first" is a next-level belief.
- **Actions are agnostic** to deservingness and skill. An hour a day of sales practice for a year likely puts you in the top 10%/5%/2%, because most people do nothing.
- **Business math is basic arithmetic**: you only need multiplication, division, addition and subtraction. Being "bad at math" is usually not understanding the concept behind the term (e.g., cost per acquisition).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If you want to earn far more without a valuable skill, learn to sell. If you know where to look, you can "pretty much write your own paycheck."
- Beliefs like "I'm bad at math" or "I hate employees" never serve a business owner. Drop them.

## Playbook steps
1. Name the skill you avoid. 2. Count the hours you've actually invested. 3. Commit to daily practice (e.g., 1 hr/day). 4. Expect to suck for a long time, and keep going until it's unreasonable to be bad. 5. Remove crutches (he stopped using a calculator).

## Mistakes he warns against
- Identifying with being poor at a required skill, which shuts down learning and keeps people in the beginner stage.
- Excusing yourself with emotions or procrastination instead of admitting the effort gap.

## Examples / case details
- A friend wanted more income but "isn't a sales person," with zero hours of training.
- Mastermind attendees froze at basic CAC/LTV math.
- An 18-year-old neighbor he semi-mentors learned wholesaling from free YouTube videos and a cold-calling book Alex recommended, cold-called a free list, and closed his first deal.
- Alex thought he was horrible at math until 23 (cheated through high school math). He stopped using a calculator and studied GMAT math 4 hrs/day for 4 months ("16 phone books of problems," as captioned), reaching the 99th percentile.
