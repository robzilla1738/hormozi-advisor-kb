## Core thesis
Work isn't hours or effort. Work = output = volume × leverage, and work rate = output ÷ time. Making money means getting more output for your time, so out-leverage rather than outwork. New things (shiny objects, many businesses) kill leverage. Focus multiplies it, and "better is better than new": doing the boring things you already know you should be doing in one business is the biggest lever. This is a live stage talk; he says he rewrote it the night before.

## Frameworks & models
- **Work = outputs = volume × leverage**. Work rate = output ÷ time ("output per minute"). In business, output = enterprise value. Volume is capped by time and physical ability; leverage is uncapped.
- **Leverage ladder (cold-calling example)**: same calls → better skill → auto-dialer (talk time) → better list → right season/timing → better offer → record the pitch and send it to 10M people → a team of 15 (or 1,500) callers while you make zero calls.
- **No passive income, only "less active"**: everyone is paid dollars per hour (money ÷ time). Count all analysis, sourcing and execution time.
- **Stages of change** (uninformed optimism → informed pessimism → crisis of meaning / valley of despair → informed optimism → success). Most people loop back to uninformed optimism at the valley. "In the game of entrepreneurship you restart years later." It's like re-fighting the same boss.
- **Learning = same condition, different behavior.**
- **How to stay poor**: do lots of things, for short periods, without help, make every mistake yourself, and repeat them. Flip each one to make money.
- **Magic wand question**: "If I ended all your projects except the one with the most opportunity, how easy would it be to grow?" The answer is usually "really easy." What blocks it is hard conversations (firing, shutting down, layoffs).
- **Woman in the red dress**: distractions scale with your level ($1K/mo → $10K → $100K/mo opportunities). You must "flex the no muscle" at every level.
- **Traits are behaviors, not feelings**: patience means not acting despite feeling impatient (from Leila). Act like the person, and through repeated exposure the feelings fade.
- **"If I were coaching me"**: you have the most information and no agenda, so take your own advice.
- **Negative word-of-mouth test**: if your ad costs rose faster than CPMs, you have negative word of mouth (the "invisible hand").
- **Quality leverage**: a 9.5/10 book takes about 20× the work of a 7/10 but gets about 1,000× the sales, so about 50× output per unit of input.
- **Who / What / How**: acquisition.com recruits the who, gives the what, shows the how, and the business then scales by default.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If your vehicle doesn't compound with time, change vehicles. Otherwise pick one and stay: "ordinary business done for extraordinary time creates extraordinary results."
- It doesn't matter much which one you pick; the picking (saying no) is what grows it.
- Call leads within 5 minutes; he claims this triples sales.
- Spend about 12 months getting the product right (even at break-even) rather than marketing a bad product.
- Interview 10 more people for a role. A bad hire costs 6 months.
- Judge yourself by commitment to the activity, not by the outcome ("your work works on you").

## Playbook steps
1. List everything you know you should be doing in your existing business but aren't.
2. Order it by the input required versus the output expected, and work down the list.
3. Ignore new exciting things. Use the magic wand to cut side projects and have the hard conversations.
4. Install "boring but better" cadences: split-test landing pages weekly, split-test emails, make creative before you need it, run sales role-play daily, run customer success role-play daily (refunds, complaints), and take extra interviews.
5. Ask "if I were coaching me, what would I say?" and do it.
6. Commit to the daily activity (e.g. prospect every day) regardless of results.

## Mistakes he warns against
- "No one can outwork me" (hours and effort aren't work).
- Believing in passive income.
- Jumping vehicles at the valley of despair, and having multiple businesses ("I had nine businesses and I was broke").
- "Don't leave money on the table" thinking: the real cost is lost focus.
- Waiting to see which of many businesses takes off.
- Shortcutting the product and over-marketing it (a bologna sandwich restaurant).
- "Special snowflake" reasons reality doesn't apply to you.
- The quitting reflex learned from leaving your job: it only works once.
