## Core thesis
There are four ways to get money (steal, inherit, marry, trade), and realistically only trading is left. Trades can be structured six ways, from least to most leverage. You are paid in proportion to the risk you take on, especially *perceived* risk: the best deals look risky to others and aren't risky to you (mispriced bets). Move up the pyramid by shifting where the risk sits.

## Frameworks & models
- **The 6 trade structures** (worst → best):
  1. **I work, then you pay**: W-2 employment. Trades upside for reliability; lowest risk.
  2. **You pay as we go**: contractors/vendors (half now / half later, milestones). Some money comes up front, but vendors get fired far faster.
  3. **You pay, then I work**: paid in full up front (surgeons; attorney retainers drawn down and re-upped). Requires owning a business/leverage. Variants: **"business owner layaway"** and third-party financing.
  4. **When X happens, you pay me**: rev share, profit share, outcome bonuses, accelerators, equity, % of ad spend. Pay is divorced from time and tied to skill at producing outcomes.
  5. **Buy and sell risk itself**: insurance. "When nothing happens, you still get paid"; a "reverse lottery."
  6. **No matter what, you pay me**: taxes (government holds a monopoly on violence and carries physical risk). For businesses: control the money flow.
- **Moving up as an owner/solopreneur**:
  - Level 4 → **royalties/licensing**: get paid off the top. Prefer rev share over profit share, because profit can be manipulated and "top line's top line."
  - Level 5 → **sell guarantees/warranties** without being an insurer (AppleCare). In services, charge a premium to take on the risk of delay, difficulty, or failure. (Follow local law.)
  - Level 6 → **control the money flow**: franchisors that collect and then remit; payment processors.
- **Mispriced risk**: "Humans overestimate the downside and underestimate the upside" (Bezos). People count the number of failures, not absolute return. A stock can only go to zero but can go up 100× (Lynch).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- "You will be compensated in proportion to the risk you're willing to take," meaning perceived risk.
- Take on unaccounted-for risk you're comfortable with; it's usually mispriced in your favor.
- A 10% chance of a 100× payoff: take it every time (book quote; long-tail business outcomes vs baseball's cap of 4 runs).
- Layaway: let them set the payment plan, but work starts when it's paid off. People pay for speed. If they stop paying, you lost nothing.
- Even W-2s can shift risk: e.g., offer to work for $60K base with $180K upside on a $100K job (only when you're good).
- Business-building razor: study companies that are 100+ years old (insurers) to see how risk gets compensated durably.

## Playbook steps
1. Identify where you sit on the pyramid. 2. Move from paid-after to paid-before (full up front, retainers, layaway). 3. Add outcome-based pay (paid when you hit top 3 in Google Maps, etc.). 4. Prefer revenue-based royalties over profit share. 5. Bolt on guarantees/warranties/risk premiums. 6. Where possible, sit in the money flow (collect, then remit).

## Mistakes he warns against
- Believing guru talk that employment is riskier than ownership. He says almost half of owners don't make money, and the median is about minimum wage in California.
- Thinking contracting is safer: vendor engagements last 3–12 months (temp/gig 1–3) vs 3.9-year average employee tenure (BLS), which he calls ~5× the turnover.
- Profit share when you could get rev share.
- Ignoring the risk of not achieving what you want in life.
