## Core thesis
"Sales is a transference of conviction": a believer, a non-believer, and trust as the bridge between them. Scripts and training make up for a lack of conviction, but the biggest systematic lever on close rate is making the sales team believe deeply in the product. Conviction is measured by how deeply they believe, not whether they believe. Closing is driven "not based on skill but based on heart."

## Frameworks & models
- **Conviction transfer**: the prospect has to believe your conviction more than their own doubt. Skills only communicate clearly and build trust so conviction can cross over.
- **Mojo cup**: prospects pour doubt into reps, so the sales director has to refill their conviction continually.
- **When a rep who started hot declines**, it's not skill, because they could already close. Usually they heard something negative from a customer. Training won't fix it; conviction will.
- **Sales/CS bridge**: a weekly long-form meeting where customer success shares client wins. It builds cross-department knowledge: sales learns what is being misunderstood, and CS gives sales ammunition.
- **Organic buying analogy**: people buy on a believed recommendation ("have you gone to that sandwich shop?"). Conviction makes your pitch feel like a referral.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If a rep doesn't believe in the product, no script will work. Fix belief (and the product) first.
- Rep slump after strong start → diagnose conviction, not skill.
- Great salespeople often come from former clients who used the product.

## Playbook steps
1. Run a weekly CS × sales meeting to share client wins. 2. Build a testimonial and review collection pipeline. 3. Get clients on calls with the sales team to tell their stories. 4. Have the sales director read testimonials to the team every morning, which "reminds us why we do what we do." 5. Keep the drills and scripts as tools on top of conviction.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Obsessing over drills and scripts while ignoring belief.
- Reps focused on their commission instead of the prospect missing out.
- Treating a fallen-off top performer with more training.

## Examples / case details
- Mortgage-lead sales consulting gig: he asked "John," the 9-person team's problem rep, "How good are the leads?" John hesitated, which showed no conviction. Alex modeled a convicted answer: his cousin the realtor uses them, and he's studying for a realtor exam to buy the leads himself.
- Gyms: Saturdays closed about 100% of walk-ins. Those were weigh-out ("graduation") days with clients crying and the team present, and consults took half as long.
- Religious evangelists and pastors with deep faith convert without sales training. The converter can never believe less than the convert.
