## Core thesis
Small businesses usually try to grow by adding more: traffic, channels, people and money. That tends to bring lower conversion, higher churn, chaos and overwhelm. The step before adding is getting more out of what you already have, using five "boring" principles of management: expectations, accountability, communication, meeting cadence and recognition. She says these built Gym Launch, Prestige Labs and Acquisition.com. The leader is the coach, and she cites ~30% variation in team success from the coach and says the CEO's influence is about the same.

## Frameworks & models
- **Five principles of management:** rate yourself 1–10 on each, then fix the weakest one.
- **Expectations** are "the verbalization of how someone should act or something should be done." Be explicit: "make your bed like a five-star hotel would." State the obvious, because a new hire's last company played a different game ("Monopoly vs checkers"). She says there are four levels of expectations; the highest is **core values**, the root beliefs of the organization.
  - **Values over rules:** values can get people to follow "99%" of what you need without a 40-page SOP. "Speed is king" replaces rules like clocking in by 8am and replying to leads within 60s.
  - Values should fit the industry: Gym Launch uses "do the boring work"; a tech friend uses "always innovate."
  - The eggs at 11:15 test: McDonald's says no, a mom-and-pop says yes. Values tell staff which behavior you want.
  - Values are "the internal brand." Acquisition.com's "unimpeachable character" rules out OnlyFans or porn deals that otherwise fit the criteria.
- **Accountability = expectation × measurement, amplified by feedback.**
  - Expectations: job description, core values, stated expectations.
  - Measurement: scorecards and KPIs for the role, e.g. sales count and close rate.
  - Feedback: meetings, 1:1s, end-of-week reports, surveys, recognition, quarterly reviews. More feedback means more accountability.
  - "You don't delegate accountability, you disseminate it." The CEO is the "chief accountability officer." Hold your direct reports most accountable and they copy it downward.
- **Self-assessment reports:** make people fill in daily or weekly scorecards rating themselves. "You are showing them themselves," like a client stepping on the scale.
- **"You are the scale":** she cites a holiday study where people who weighed daily lost 1.9 lb and those who didn't gained 4.9 lb. No report replaces you telling people how they're doing. Show them the low-performance and ideal-performance ends of the spectrum.
- **Signs of low accountability:**
  - Hard signs: low revenue per headcount, hidden expenses, weak sales and upsells, high staff churn.
  - Behavioral signs: complaining, the boss resenting the team behind their backs, unfinished projects, high activity with low output.
- **Communication:** "trading your assumptions for curiosity and actively listening." If behavior didn't change, you didn't communicate.
  - She cites an MIT badge study of ~30 businesses that found four keys: Energy (number of exchanges, verbal and non-verbal), Engagement (how evenly energy is spread across the team), Emotional control (staying calm when receiving input) and Exploration (talking outside your core team).
  - Her formula is energy × engagement + emotional control × exploration.
  - In that study, communication predicted success better than individual skill or intelligence.
- **Meeting cadence, the 3 Ps:**
  - Pulse = activity × size. Too few meetings leads to chaos, silos and no follow-through. Too many leads to low speed and low morale.
  - Purpose: tactical meetings cover the how and the short term; strategic meetings cover the why and the long term.
  - Preparation: the right information sent ahead, the right people (contributors plus decision makers) and an agenda.
- **Recognition** is acknowledging behavior, effort or results that support goals and values. "Recognition reinforces behavior… be careful what you recognize, you will get more of it."
  - When: outcome or goal, effort (micro-recognition along long goals, to avoid an "extinction curve"), milestone, or a core value shown.
  - Who: one-to-one < in front of the team (social) < peer-to-peer.
  - What: formal (MVP, employee of the month, birthdays, Gym Launch "Jedi Award") vs. spontaneous (Slack shout-outs, handwritten cards, wow gifts, core-value t-shirts).
  - How: written, verbal or reward.
  - "Recognition scrambler": combine the options above.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Fix management before adding channels, people or spend.
- Expect people to need to hear something about 7 times before they believe it, so repeat the values and tie every decision to them, including hiring, investments and markets.
- Even experienced hires need accountability. She "was wrong" about this for 3 years.
- Short-term fear in a hard conversation beats the long-term dysfunction of firing someone you never told.
- When angry at a teammate, ask what you fear it says about you as their boss. "You hired them."
- Recency beats intensity: an immediate high five beats praise 10 minutes later and drives behavior more than delayed comp or bonuses. Smarter people tolerate more delay, but recency still wins.
- Send information before the meeting. Tactical items can often be Slack or a call instead.
- She says "liked and trusted" beats "respected, not liked," because people who like you work more.
- Pick ONE principle to improve. Trying all five at once is too much.

## Playbook steps
1. Rate yourself 1–10 on all five principles.
2. Write core values tied to the behaviors you need and repeat them constantly.
3. For each role, set expectations (job description and values), a KPI scorecard, and feedback channels such as 1:1s and self-rated weekly reports.
4. Pour accountability into your direct reports first.
5. Raise communication energy and spread it evenly. Assign cross-team projects to force exploration.
6. Set meeting pulse by activity × size, define each meeting's purpose, and prepare information, people and agenda.
7. Build formal and spontaneous recognition, including peer-to-peer and core-value shout-outs. Test it on staff, then have staff use it on clients.

## Mistakes he warns against
(The speaker is Leila.)
- Adding traffic, channels or headcount to fix a business that is badly managed.
- Long rule lists or SOPs in place of values.
- Assuming the obvious is obvious to new hires.
- Delegating accountability to managers.
- Being "too nice" and talking about people behind their backs.
- Thinking "I shouldn't have to babysit experienced people."
- Blaming the team for being lazy or not fitting a "high performance culture."
- Running meetings without studying how. She spent ~90% of her time in meetings and had never read a book on them.
- Having too many or too few meetings.
- Recognizing nothing, or recognizing the wrong things.
- Relying only on comp plans to drive behavior.
- Leaders with harsh written communication. She mentions a bright hire everyone disliked because of hers.

## Examples / case details
- At 24 Hour Fitness, manager "Mike" was clear, predictable, encouraging and candid, and the gym was top on the West Coast for PT sales. His replacement was unclear, unpredictable and fear-based, threatening to run her over with his truck. Within ~3 months the gym fell below average and the top trainers left.
- Gym Launch in 2018 had 120 FTEs and a ~$4.5M month, yet the team felt ineffective. She realized it was her fault. After she started holding people accountable, headcount went from 120 to 75 with the same revenue. She called it "the lowest of my career" and "the best thing we ever did."
- She cites McKinsey: two of three strategies fail, and companies focused on the "soft stuff" (culture, accountability, talent) reach a ~78% success rate.
- She cites recognition stats: −18% turnover, +66% positive culture, +23% profitability, +14% engagement and +56% recruiting ability. No sources are given [inference: treat these as unverified].
- After the first team meeting, the ops manager thanked her in tears.
- She sends recognition via handwritten.com cards, gifts on Administrative Professionals Day, and spa days coordinated with a spouse.
- Gym Launch staff got a core-value t-shirt whenever someone exemplified that value.
