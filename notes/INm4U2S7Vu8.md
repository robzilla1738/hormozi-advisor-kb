## Core thesis
Investing rules written as if-then statements, so good principles repeat into good outcomes over a long horizon. The theme is controlling downside: diversify, get second opinions, know how you lose, refuse pressure, demand diligence, models, track records, contracts and good partners. Say no far more than yes. (Plug: Acquisition.com invests in $3M–$100M companies.)

## Frameworks & models
The rules as stated (24 per title):
1. If you can't buy it twice (really 3–6×), don't buy it once. His biggest allocation might be ~10%.
2. If you're worried about something, buy insurance (e.g., annuities for fear of running out of money).
3. If a deal feels like a grand slam, get a second opinion.
4. If you don't know how you can lose money, don't do it until you do; find *all* the ways.
5. If it feels rushed, don't do it; pause under pressure.
6. If you only have one deal on the table, don't do it; always have backups (the "only one person on the planet" dating analogy).
7. If you buy with the intention of selling, you're trading, not investing. Business and investing are different skills.
8. If we don't agree, we don't do it, even when he has decision authority. He uses the same rule in his marriage.
9. If it's complicated, pass. If it feels above your head, it probably is.
10. FOMO means slow down.
11. No diligence, no deal.
12. No model, no deal. Show the math, projections and assumptions: "what has to be true?"
13. Non-responsive means no deal. Judge people on meticulous, prompt replies and on setting and meeting expectations.
14. If it costs peace of mind, don't buy it.
15. If it doesn't build your reputation, don't do it; "no neutral moves."
16. No track record, no deal; performance over charisma (Napoleon: "I'd rather have lucky generals").
17. If it's the same thing you've always done well with, do more of it (a 4th hard-money loan, not crypto day trading).
18. Always under-borrow: any number × 0 is still 0.
19. Ask stupid questions, define terms, draw pictures, see examples. "Pretend I'm a golden retriever that doesn't speak English." Always play the fool.
20. If we're not in the GP, we're not in the deal (once you have ~$10–30M investable).
21. If it's a bad person, it's a bad deal. Leila's people-picking has "never been wrong"; bad partners make the paper irrelevant.
22. No contract, no deal. Walk through every failure scenario up front. Anyone who says "handshake deals only" is shady; run.
23. Contract defensively, deliver offensively. Once signed, act as if the contract doesn't exist and treat a year-long client as week-to-week.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Max ~10% in any single allocation.
- Get the GP stake once you have $10–30M+ to invest.
- Entrepreneurs over-see upside, so you need a partner who actually pushes back; a yes-man adds no value.

## Playbook steps
1. Before a deal, list every way to lose. 2. Line up alternatives. 3. Get a second opinion on "grand slams." 4. Require diligence, a model and a track record. 5. Check counterparty responsiveness and character. 6. Under-borrow. 7. Contract every failure mode. 8. Then over-deliver.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Overconcentration, hype-driven deals, rushing, FOMO.
- Mixing trading or business logic into investing.
- Chasing variety over repeating what works.
- Leverage that risks zero.
- Pretending to understand.
