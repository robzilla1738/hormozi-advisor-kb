## Core thesis
A three-level, end-to-end blueprint to a first $1M: Level 1 is fundamentals (who you are, who to sell to, what to sell); Level 2 is getting them to buy, buy more, and building a team; Level 3 is keeping your advantage (leverage), sticking with it (don't interrupt compounding), and getting better (common-factors learning loop). It works as a compact tour of most of his core frameworks (Core Four, CLOSER, 8 ways to raise LTV, management diamond, three business pillars). Contains a Skool Games promo segment (a Cybertruck/$100K giveaway framed as "2024") and an Acquisition.com workshop ad.

## Frameworks & models
- **Who you are = knowledge, skills, motivation, environment.** Knowledge points you to the high-return paths (you can't pursue private equity if you don't know it exists). "Traits" like confidence/charisma are bundles of sub-skills. Skill stack: sales → marketing ("sales done one-to-many": media, headlines, web pages, conversion) → acquisition; each new skill makes earlier ones retroactively more valuable. Learning sales takes about as long as learning to pack warehouse boxes but pays far more.
- **Motivation comes from deprivation**; raise it with a reference group that earns far more. **Environment is friction or lubricant**: "How do I make it as easy as possible to work as hard as I can?" Filter: does this increase or decrease the likelihood I hit my goals?
- **Season of no**: say no to social gatherings, entertainment, and distractions while between peer groups.
- **Million-dollar math**: 10,000 × $100 = 100 × $10,000 = $1M; finding 100 is far easier. $10K split over 4 years = $2,500/yr ≈ $200/mo; 100 customers ≈ 2 per week; at 100 outreaches/day that's 2 of ~700 per week. (Elsewhere: 25 customers × $10K/yr × 4 yrs = $1M, pre-tax.)
- **Who to sell to, the 3 P's**: Pain (help someone like you overcome what you overcame), Passion, Profession (a skill from your job). Pain and passion are often "two sides of the same thing."
- **Niche filters**: they have the problem, urgency to solve it now, money to spend, and authority to spend it. If they have the problem but no money, "you got a nonprofit."
- **Tesla Roadster path**: start with fewer people paying more (premium, specific, those you can help most), then move down-market as resources grow.
- **What to sell: Unique, Expensive, Sticky, Air** (low cost to deliver / high gross margin). Coca-Cola is his example of all four. A one-person service business meets all four; its problem is scale.
- **Scalability is a degree, not yes/no**; it's measured by the incremental cost of adding a customer (cell carrier, Facebook, Skool: near zero). Very scalable businesses tend to be demand-constrained; expertise businesses (law, accounting) are supply-constrained and often use partnership structures.
- **Core Four + lead getters**: warm outreach, cold outreach, content, paid ads; plus customers, employees, affiliates (e.g., gym ↔ chiropractor referral swap), and agencies who do the core four on your behalf.
- **CLOSER**: Clarify why they're there → Label the problem (current, desired, gap, obstacle: "the monster"; you're the sword) → Overview past pain (pain cycle: "What have you done before? How did that work?"; link what they liked to your solution, what failed to why yours differs) → Sell the vacation (three-pillar pitch, each pillar 1–2 statements plus an analogy; examples for easy ideas, metaphors for hard ones; talk about Maui, not TSA) → Explain away concerns → Reinforce the decision.
- **Six concern categories**, grouped as blaming circumstances, other people, or avoiding: time, money, decision-maker/authority, fit/preference, others' past experiences with other products, and stall ("give me time," distinct from "I don't have time").
- **Reinforce/onboarding**: in the first 24–48h post-purchase, make as many promises as possible and keep them; BAMFAM (book a meeting from a meeting); baton-pass sales → onboarding → delivery; pass notes so the customer never repeats themselves.
- **8 ways to make a customer worth more**: raise price, cut cost, more times (subscription), something else (cross-sell: gum + Nicorette patches), more quantity (bulk), higher quality (4mg vs 2mg; priority support, senior reps), lower quantity (downsell turns a no into a small yes; raises money per person talked to, not per customer), lower quality (economy version: junior reps, chat only). Applies to anything (whiteboard walkthrough).
- **5-5-5 pricing model** (he says "five to five to five"): every 5 sales, raise price 20% ($5 → $6 → $7.20…) until people stop buying; then pick the price that maximizes gross profit × conversion rate.
- **Leaky bucket**: fix retention or new customers never stack up.
- **Company size is capped by cumulative brains/knowledge in it**; hire people with 20 years in finance/HR to add their lives to yours (Jobs built the team that built the iPhone).
- **Management diamond**: they didn't know what (communicate clearly), how (Document → Demonstrate → Duplicate), when (give a deadline and ask how long they expect it to take), or why (explain how their role feeds the machine), plus bonus #5: something blocking them ("best chef, no eggs").
- **Three pillars/tripod**: acquisition (marketing + sales), delivery (product, fulfillment, customer success), operations (IT, legal, finance, payroll, HR, recruiting). Operations is the one people forget.
- **Partner rule**: a partner must bring a skill (a different pillar), time, or capital you don't have. Buddy partnerships that share one pillar make the tripod top-heavy.
- **Leverage = how much you get for what you put in.** Forms: brand (higher CTR, conversion, and price; a branded tee at $30 can outsell a blank at $5), people, code, books, skill. **Volume × leverage = output**; early on, compensate for low leverage with volume (1,000 calls/emails/door knocks/content), because volume builds skill, which is leverage. "You have to work hard in order to work smart."
- **Five stages**: uninformed optimism → informed pessimism → valley of despair → informed optimism → success. Most restart at a new "uninformed optimism" instead of pushing through.
- **Woman in the red dress**: shiny opportunities are "agents in disguise" that kill compounding. Focus = quality and quantity of what you say no to (Jobs: "What have you said no to lately?").
- **Compare year 2 of the new thing to year 5 of the old thing**, not to year 2.
- **Common factors analysis**: produce volume, compare top 10% vs bottom 10% (content, ads, sales calls, customers/avatar), and do more of what the top has.
- **Relationship with goals**: love = what you're willing to give up to keep something. People filter: "Is my goal achievement more or less likely with this person in my life?" Neutral is a no.
- **Infinite games**: the point of business is to stay in business.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Pick the niche where you can create the most value (help the person you can lift $100K over the one you can lift $10).
- Choose a market that is flat or growing, never shrinking (retirement homes yes, print newspapers no).
- Investors (he cites A16Z, YC) prefer founders who lived the problem 5+ years. Selling to people like you is a shortcut to knowing their language and pains.
- Until $1M you can use just one channel. If two, use warm outreach + content (cheapest, easiest). Start cold only after warm. Paid ads are the most expensive but scale fastest.
- Never discount to make a sale. If you test a price, hold it for the committed number of sales.
- Beginner-easiest LTV levers: raise price, get them to buy more times, sell more quantity. Cost cutting is valuable but slower. New products (cross-sell, quality tiers) are more advanced.
- If every diamond box is checked, nothing blocks them, and they still won't do it (e.g., a new rep who "really hates" talking to people), it's not a fit.
- After $1M, set the goal at 10, then 100.

## Playbook steps
1. Build knowledge and a high-return skill (sales → marketing); fix your environment; enter a season of no.
2. Pick the who via the 3 P's and the filters (problem, urgency, money, authority; growing market); start narrow and premium.
3. Design a unique, expensive, sticky, low-COGS offer (~$10K, possibly split over time).
4. Warm outreach offer: "I'll help you free for 3–5 hours; at the end I'd love to make you an offer." Pair with content showing results.
5. Close with CLOSER; onboard with kept promises, BAMFAM, and passed notes.
6. Raise LTV: 5-5-5 price increases, repeat purchases, quantity.
7. Hire for missing knowledge; manage with the diamond (what/how/when/why/blocker); balance the three pillars; partner only for a missing skill, time, or capital.
8. Get volume → skill → leverage; refuse red-dress opportunities; run top-vs-bottom-10% analysis continuously.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Selling cheap to many instead of premium to few when you have no resources.
- Long product monologues in sales ("this is when salesmen blab"); talking about the product instead of the destination.
- Unclear expectations after purchase (promised 30 minutes, took 2 days → cancellation).
- Blaming employees before checking the diamond.
- Buddy partnerships over-indexed on one pillar; neglecting operations.
- "Work smart, not hard" as an excuse to skip volume.
- Chasing the next opportunity (dropshipping → crypto → wholesaling); the "millionaires have seven income streams" myth confuses sequence ("tall people play basketball"). Successful people concentrate, then diversify, and returns go down after diversifying.
- "No one can sell like me" really means "I don't know how to teach selling."
