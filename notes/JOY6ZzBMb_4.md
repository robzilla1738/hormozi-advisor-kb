# Simple Operational Framework (ALEX HORMOZI)
id: JOY6ZzBMb_4 / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JOY6ZzBMb_4 / duration: 847s (~14 min) / tags: 08-ops-scaling, 07-hiring-management
Signal: medium

## Core thesis
Operations is the under-studied "unspoken secret" of why bigger businesses win. Structure ops with three variables: the activities each outcome needs (click → opt-in → schedule → show → buy → buy again), the time each activity takes per customer, and expected inflow. Put those numbers on each role's calendar. That shows how busy each person is, where the waste is, and when you need a new role. Audience: owners doing several hundred thousand to several million a year (recorded during the COVID quarantine).

## Frameworks & models
- **Business as pipeline: click → close → resell** (resell = retention or ascension).
- **Activities × time per unit × inflow = role design.** List outcomes, then activities, then minutes per customer. Multiply by inflow and block the time on a named person's daily calendar.
- **Activity ratios**: "for every 10 customers we need to do four of these, two of these…", used to size capacity.
- **Utilization**: compare the hours you've allocated with paid hours (e.g. a coach "at 50% utilization"). Spare capacity becomes a strategic choice (content, outreach, more clients).
- **Breaking points**: when a role's blocked activities exceed one person, peel a whole block of activity off to a new role.
- **Time study**: Excel sheet, every 15 minutes logged for a week; "you are never more efficient than when you do a time study."

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Online fitness example: front-end sale 30 min + nutrition orientation 30 min + ascension 30 min = 1.5 hrs per new client. At 10 new clients/wk that's ~10–15 hrs/wk.
- Fulfillment at ~2 min/client/day × 100 clients = 200 min (~3h20m) a day, blocked as 90 min AM + 90 min PM.
- Built-up coach role: fulfillment + onboarding + orientation + halfway/transformation meetings ≈ 6 of 8 hrs a day, assuming they're efficient. "You get what you tolerate."
- Stagger onboarding slots (MWF one time, T/Th another) so customers can make one.
- If someone at 40% utilization says they're too busy or asks for a hire, ask what they're doing outside the parameters.
- When a department's numbers "feel weird/heavier/lighter," run a week-long time study (e.g. "why do you have 3 hours of emails?").

## Playbook steps
1. List desired outcomes along click→close→resell. 2. List activities for each. 3. Time each per customer/unit. 4. Multiply by inflow, customer base and attrition. 5. Block the activities on each role's calendar. 6. Check utilization; decide what to do with spare capacity. 7. Identify the breaking point where a new role is needed. 8. Time-study employees to find unaccounted tasks and cut or fix them with a process.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Founders with promotional traits obsessing over marketing/sales and never studying ops.
- Paying full-time pay for half-time partial roles.
- Assuming non-core busywork is malicious. Usually people don't understand the bigger picture, though sometimes they're padding time to keep their job.

## Examples / case details
- Gym Launch copycats with access to the same materials are "one-tenth, one-twentieth" the size, and he says ops execution is the gap.
- He and Leila run time studies on any department whose numbers look off.
