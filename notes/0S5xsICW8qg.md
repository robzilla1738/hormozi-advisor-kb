## Core thesis
A tiny, highly niche audience can produce big profit if the content speaks directly to the buyer of what you sell. His example is a registered dietitian (kept anonymous) who takes home just under $1M a year. She teaches other RDs to bill insurance correctly. She has 5,800 Instagram followers, about 2,800 accounts reached per month, ~19 likes per post, and all of her customers come from organic Instagram. "Business metrics don't follow vanity metrics." Followers make you famous. Understanding business makes you rich.

## Frameworks & models
- **Niche vs broad content trade-off**: broad content gets more views but fewer buyers. Niche content gets fewer views, but "the relevancy is through the roof." A dentist-only podcast has few listeners, and they're the right ones.
- **Algorithmic niche serving**: algorithms show niche content to people interested in it. His own feed is philosophy, comedy and gym equipment, and he's shown accounts with 500–10K followers. He says Instagram's head talked about favoring smaller creators.
- **Every view is a person**: 10 views is 10 people, and 25 people is a workshop or a lunch-and-learn.
- **Content engine = answer customer questions**: one post plus one story a day, every post answering an RD question, every other post adding "link in my bio has more stuff you might like."
- **Value ladder**: a low-ticket cheat sheet of codes (10+ sales a day; her first sale was $39) plus a ~$4–5K higher-ticket offer (~3 sales a week).
- **1,000 true fans** (from a book he couldn't name): 1,000 die-hard fans at $10/month. He says that's $100K/year; the math actually gives $120K/year. [inference: arithmetic check]
- **Rich vs famous**: people who like you a lot buy. Many people who like you a little don't.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- If your goal is money, judge content by business metrics, not views or subscribers. His business metrics were CPMs on YouTube videos, opt-ins on acquisition.com, and book sales.
- If you have deep niche expertise, make the content that gets 40 views and 100 likes and surely helps one specific person.
- Avoid topics where everyone has an opinion and there's no objective proof, like relationship content. Stay where you have credibility.
- Show up the same whether 2 people or a full auditorium are watching (his Jordan story from "The Last Dance").

## Playbook steps
1. Pick a narrow problem for a specific buyer you're expert in. 2. Post daily (1 post + 1 story) answering their actual questions. 3. Add a soft CTA (link in bio) on alternating posts. 4. Build an email list from the start; she made her first sale with 8 people on the list, and it's ~2,900 now. 5. Offer a low-ticket product plus a high-ticket product. 6. Track sales, opt-ins and CPMs, not views.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Chasing views and followers in the belief they'll make you rich.
- Not starting because your numbers would be low ("don't be afraid of the empty room").
- Going broad. He did it twice. (1) For ~18 months before selling Gym Launch he talked general business instead of gyms, and organic sales went to "basically zero"; it took ~6 months to work out why. (2) 3–6 months before this video, a wider-content experiment (marriage, relationships, fitness) raised views while CPMs, opt-ins and book sales fell. He has since gone back to "only business," and business metrics "skyrocketed."
- Not paying the $4.99/month to verify your account when it's your main income source.

## Examples / case details
- RD: ~$100K added income for the average dietitian who uses her coding system. He thinks she could make $3–5M/yr profit with a few changes he suggested (not detailed). His own reach is ~20M/month, for comparison. She has 20+ years in the industry. She started while working 100-hour hospital weeks, one day a week on the business.
- Acquisition.com portfolio insurance brokerage: the head had ~5,000 YouTube subscribers when they invested, and videos get 100–500 views on complex specialty insurance. The business does "many millions" a year.
- Her motivation is missionary: "I just want to be useful."
