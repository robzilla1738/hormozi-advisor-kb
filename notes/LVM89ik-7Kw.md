## Core thesis
There are only 7 categories of things you can sell, each in physical and digital form (14 total). Pick based on resources and the skills/knowledge you already have (often absorbed from family or odd jobs); with ~$100 you will usually sell your time as a service. Then use the "Delivery Cube" to make that offer more valuable or more scalable/profitable.

## Frameworks & models
- **7 sellable categories (x physical/digital = 14):** 1) Products (water bottle / PDF), 2) Services (massage, lawn, car repair / making ads, media buying), 3) Access (leasing a building / Netflix library), 4) Attention (billboard / Facebook & Google eyeballs), 5) Risk (insuring a building / cyber insurance; paid guarantees like AppleCare), 6) Money (cash/banking interest / digital money), 7) Endorsement/brand (logo licensing for % of sales / Instagram check mark). Offers can combine categories (rock concert = access + drinks + t-shirts + recorded digital version).
- **Choosing what to sell:** (a) resources you have, (b) skills/experience — including osmosis knowledge from parents, carers, people around you, odd jobs. Then: which problem is biggest that I can solve with my resources? Sweet spot = "the person I provide the most value to for the least amount of money to me."
- **Delivery Cube (6 frames):**
  1. Group size: 1:1, small group (e.g., semi-private of 5), 1:many (webinar to 1,000).
  2. DIY / done-with-you / done-for-you: DIY sells unlimited but lower value; DFY most expensive, sometimes easiest to sell, hardest to deliver.
  3. Support medium: chat, email, phone, Zoom. Email = more scalable, less valuable than real-time Zoom.
  4. Consumption: visual, audio, text, live vs recorded. Live in-person = most valuable, least scalable; recorded digital = most scalable, least valuable.
  5. Speed & convenience: hours (24/7 vs 9–5), days per week, response time (e.g., 24h guarantee vs 3-minute response). Tradeoff: overstaff so customers don't wait, or customers wait while staff run 100% utilized — successful companies do both.
  6. **10x / 1/10th test:** What would I change to justify 10x price (e.g., $10k → $100k)? What would I do to deliver the same value at 1/10th price while staying profitable?

## Decision rules & thresholds
- With ~$100 starting capital, physical products and software are mostly precluded → sell time (services).
- More live/real-time/in-person/1:1 = more value, less scale; recorded/async/DIY = more scale, less value.

## Playbook steps
1. List the 14 options; mark the ones you have any exposure to (family, jobs).
2. Pick the biggest problem you can solve with current resources.
3. Package for the person you can give the most value at least cost to you.
4. Run the six cube frames to tune value vs. scalability; run 10x and 1/10th tests; consider internal staffing ratio.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Believing you have no experience — you absorbed knowledge from people around you.
