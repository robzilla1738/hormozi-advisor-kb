## Core thesis
There are only two ways to grow: get more customers, or make each customer worth more. Measure worth as **lifetime gross profit (LTGP)**, not vague "LTV." There are six levers to raise it; price is the most powerful and least used. (Portfolio described as Gym Launch, Prestige Labs, ALAN and others at ~$110M in sales.)

## Frameworks & models
- **LTGP vs LTV**: a $10 meal that costs $9 to deliver and $5 to acquire looks like 2:1 on revenue, but it's $5 spent for $1 of gross profit. Gross profit is before rent, payroll and other overhead (that's net).
- **Six LTGP levers**:
  1. **Raise price.** Price itself creates allure (a $20 Chanel purse wouldn't be cool) and can put you in another category.
  2. **Cut cost to fulfill** with tech, process or outsourcing (the $9 meal made by a robot for $2).
  3. **Raise purchase frequency.** Get every client to buy one more time.
  4. **Upsell**: a better or bigger version of the same thing (Honda→Lexus, 20oz→32oz soda).
  5. **Cross-sell**: the next adjacent need (burger or fries with the soda). Often where most profit is; enables **loss leaders** if you know your numbers.
  6. **Downsell**: monetize people who say no ("sawdust" / excess capacity). Either refer them to partners for affiliate or referral commissions, or offer a cheaper-to-fulfill version for the same need (done-for-you → done-with-you/DIY; personal training → group or at-home).
- **Price leverage example**: cost $10, price $20 → $10 GP. Raise to $30 → $20 GP, which doubles LTGP (math checks out).

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Don't set price by averaging the market. "Everyone else is broke," and small businesses stay small by thinking small.
- Model pricing on **mature** big businesses (past rapid growth, sustained). Ignore funded market-penetration pricing.
- Make your terms precise (upsell vs cross-sell vs downsell): "if you can be more nuanced in how you think, you will think better."
- Only refer downsells to good partners.

## Playbook steps
1. Calculate LTGP per customer.
2. Test a price increase first.
3. Find fulfillment cost cuts.
4. Add reasons to buy again.
5. Build structured upsells; map "what's the next thing they'll need?" for cross-sells.
6. Build a downsell product or referral partnerships for people who say no.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Judging the business on revenue LTV and ignoring delivery cost.
- Having no downsells, which wastes paid traffic and sales calls.
- Copying the pricing of small or struggling competitors ("the dentist with bad teeth").
- Limiting beliefs around raising price.
