## Core thesis
"I have nothing going for me" really means "I have nothing to lose". Removing the downside should *lower* your threshold for action. Most of the perceived downside is imagined judgment, usually from one or two specific people; naming them breaks the fear.

## Frameworks & models
- **Every position has advantages:** small players are nimble and personal ("with me you'll get my attention, you're just a number to Alex"); big players have proof ("thousands of success stories"). Use the advantage of your position.
- **Eliminate downside → lower action threshold.** Quoted from Jack Butcher: get rich by taking lots of risk with small amounts of money, stay rich by taking small risks with lots of money.
- **Name the voice:** ask "who specifically?" until you hit one person (often ~6 questions deep, e.g. "my uncle", "my mom at Thanksgiving"). Then: "Will your belief that Uncle Harry sees you as a failure be the sole reason you live below your potential?" Shame only exists in the shadows.
- **Content frames (guest):** treat early content as practice/preseason, not the game. Track many stats so something always goes up. Treat impressions as a room of people ("100 impressions = a room of 100, I'd be stoked").

## Decision rules
- When you start, few people are watching by design, so failure is barely seen.

## Examples
- Gym Launch: told smaller competitors exactly how to attack him on sales calls.
- Guest's nightclub flops (150 people), with tricks like smoke and padding the back of the room. Guest's podcast took 3–3.5 years at 3 episodes/week to get appreciable listens.
