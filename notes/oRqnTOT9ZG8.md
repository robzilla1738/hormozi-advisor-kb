## Core thesis
There are four ways to monetize an audience, ordered along a continuum of difficulty, risk and exit value: Affiliates → Sponsorships → Partnerships (equity) → Your own brand. Promotion work is similar for the first three; only the compensation differs. Build deals by balancing four variables: exit value, cash in/out, work required, and risk.

## Frameworks & models
- **Affiliates**: paid after sales. Fastest, standard terms, low risk for both sides, uncapped. Exit value is low: worth a multiple of the cash flow, discounted by how durable the underlying business is (US military recruiting vs a disappearing info-arbitrage offer).
- **Sponsorships/endorsements**: paid first. You act as a traffic source priced on CPMs. Two forms: posting to your audience, or the more scalable **whitelisting** (the brand runs your "I just partnered with X" ad well beyond your audience). It's win-win: the brand grows you, and your face and warm recognition convert better. Terms are à la carte and set by relative leverage (huge influencer + tiny company = great deal; small influencer + Nike = maybe none). Little exit value unless you're a true media company bundling many influencers into umbrella deals (similar to a talent agency).
- **Partnerships**: minority or majority equity (0.1%–99%) set by relative size. Structure: **upfront + performance + time vesting**, e.g. 25% of the equity upfront, 25% on performance (1% per 1,000 customers, capped at 10%), 50% vesting over 4 years.
- **Your own brand**: white label (faster, cheaper) vs custom (costs more, gives pricing power and no identical competitor; he prefers custom). You start at 100% equity and pay others down the line.
- **Not mutually exclusive**: a partner can also earn affiliate or sponsorship pay.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- As an influencer taking equity, get some equity upfront no matter what. The public association is permanent: "you transfer all of that goodwill."
- The more money you already have, the more you should lean toward equity over sponsorship cash, if you believe in the product and a future exit. More risk demands a disproportionate reward.
- Define deliverables up front with who/what/where/when/why/how. Example: link in bio one week a month, 1 long-form mention, 5 short-form posts/stories, a monthly whitelisting ad recording. Never just "I'll promote it."
- Integrate promotion tastefully. Don't become a shill.

## Playbook steps
1. Start with affiliates to prove audience-product fit. 2. Move to sponsorships with whitelisting. 3. Negotiate equity partnerships (upfront + performance + vesting). 4. Launch an owned brand, custom if you can afford it. 5. Tune every deal on exit value, cash, work and risk.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Sitting on an audience for years without a monetization construct.
- Vague promotion commitments.
- Generic white-labeled products that customers discover are generic: "not a good look."

## Examples / case details
- Prestige Labs: 5,000+ affiliates, $20M+/yr, still running.
- ALAN (software): 1,000+ agency affiliates white-labeling the software.
- $100M Leads book launch: 30,000 affiliates.
