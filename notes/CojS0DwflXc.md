# 3 Simple Steps To Close Any Sale

## Core thesis
When selling, only three kinds of statements should come out of your mouth: **questions**, **restatements**, and (only once they are sold) **short stories/analogies** that explain how it works. Don't teach. Prospects won't act on tactics. They act only when their beliefs are broken and they trust you can help. Coach them to the conclusion instead of "technobabbling" about your program. Never pitch until you believe they'll say yes. This came out of a game-tape review with the Gym Launch sales team.

## Frameworks & models
- **Teaching vs coaching**: teaching = declarative facts and tactics (ignored until they buy). Coaching = leading questions that break beliefs.
- **Three statement types**: (1) leading questions (most frequent; question-based frameworks for complex sales); (2) restatement ("So what I'm hearing is X is the main issue, you've tried Y, and it hasn't worked because Z. Does that sound about right?"); (3) pre-packaged mini stories/analogies for the "pitch".
- **Breaking beliefs > tactics** (from a friend he calls "talking more" in the captions): everyone benefits from breaking beliefs; only ~10% benefit from tactics, and those people usually aren't buying from you.
- **Question sequence**: why are you here / what are you struggling with / where are you now / where are you trying to go / what have you tried / how's that working / why does it matter in the bigger context → restate the diagnosis → "do you want to hear how our program works?"
- **Proposal rule**: lawyers don't ask questions they don't know the answer to, and most people don't propose without knowing the answer is yes. Don't pitch unless you expect a yes. Stay in the question/restate loop until you find "the thread to pull".
- **Closers ask hard questions**: challenge the prospect's paradigm with a negative restatement ("sounds like there's no way for you to succeed?") so they argue the contrarian side and sell themselves.
- **Rep expertise scales with deal complexity**: B2C transactional (e.g. supplements by phone) = heavily scripted, low variability. Complex B2B = reps must be real "business consultants".

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Pitch only when you believe they'll say yes. Ideally they ask "how do I work with you?", or you ask permission: "would you like to know how the program works? I think it might be a good fit."
- If they demand the pitch early, retake the frame: "I don't think we're ready for that; I need more context about your business."
- Within the first 5–10 minutes (often 5), experienced reps can tell whether they'll close. If not, shift state with challenging questions.
- If your script has paragraphs of explanation, it's wrong. It should be questions plus restatements.
- Watch talk time and engagement in call recordings (Gong). Engagement drops when reps monologue the pitch for ~5 minutes.

## Playbook steps
1. Ask context questions (current state, goal, attempts, why it matters). 2. Restate and get agreement on why they haven't succeeded. 3. Ask permission to explain. 4. Use short stories and analogies to get agreement on *what* must happen, not *how* (no programming or software details). 5. Keep several analogies ready in case one doesn't land. 6. Close when they ask how to move forward.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Teaching and giving tactical advice on the call. Early Gym Launch: "your pricing is all wrong, do this..."; owners took notes, didn't buy, and ignored him.
- Technobabble (credited to Russell Brunson): periodization, software, systems.
- Tying your ego to close percentages, or ego-babbling to prove expertise.
- Pitching someone you don't believe will buy.
- Letting the prospect command the frame.

## Examples / case details
- Gym Launch belief story: you'll never be free until you learn to market. Can you outsource acquisition, lead nurture and sales and still have a reliable business if that vendor dies? "Give a fish vs teach to fish."
- Weight-loss analogies: the goal isn't to make you work out but to make you like working out more than watching TV. Can you give up cookies, cake and ice cream for life? No, so we'll find foods you like more and you'll stick with it, because willpower isn't sustainable.
