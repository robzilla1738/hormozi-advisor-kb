# If You Have Less Than $10,000 Saved. Please Watch This Video...
id: ymFWgFiKUvM / url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ymFWgFiKUvM / duration: 1625s (~27 min) / tags: 10-money-cashflow, 12-mindset-leverage
Signal: medium

## Core thesis
Staying broke is a decision. The median American earns ~$3M over a lifetime (even minimum wage earns >$1M), but much of it goes to lenders and status spending. Wealth is the ratio of what you earn to what you need. Push personal risk and fixed costs to near zero so you can take business risk ("shots on goal"), then invest. Above all, invest in your own earning ability, which beats the S&P. Personal finance advice; he says the steps are modeled on Dave Ramsey's baby steps. Captions are rough in places.

## Frameworks & models
- **Six steps** (Ramsey-like): 1) Starter emergency fund of $1K-5K, saved before attacking debt, to learn the habit. 2) Pay off consumer debt with the psychological "snowball": fastest-to-pay-off first, not highest rate, to get early wins. 3) Expand to 3-6 months of expenses in an interest-bearing account, never invested ("your brick of safety"). 4) Eliminate risky spending: turn in leases, buy a $5-10K used car in cash, get down to one car, downgrade rent; on a mortgage, refinance if rates are lower or move to a 15-year fixed and pay it down. 5) Invest 15% of pre-tax income, automated; optionally another 15% in education; check accounts daily. 6) Pay off the mortgage so fixed costs are covered and everything else compounds.
- **Personal risk vs business risk**: keep personal risk as low as possible so you can go on offense in business. Low burn let him survive losing everything twice more and take second and third shots. "Saving money won't make you money but it will allow you to think further out and make bigger bets."
- **Translate prices into future money**: $200 Jordans at 9% for 40 years ≈ $6,000+ (checks: 200×1.09^40 ≈ $6,280). "If you wouldn't buy those shoes for $6,000, maybe don't buy them today for $200."
- **$250/month ≈ $1M in 40 years at 9%** (checks out at ~$1.1-1.2M with monthly compounding). He uses it as a game: $1,000/mo ≈ $4M, $2,000/mo ≈ $8M. $500/mo → he says $2.1M, and at a 4% withdrawal that's ~$80K/yr.
- **Translate prices into workdays**: at ~$100/day post-tax, a $600/mo car lease costs 6 of your 22 workdays; add $1,200 rent and you're at 18 of 22.
- **Extra-shift math**: an extra $2,500/mo invested from age 20 for 55 years ≈ $52M (caption shows "99.7%", likely ~9.7%; at 9% it's ~$46M [inference]).
- **Invest in yourself > S&P**: $10K in the S&P at 10% earns $1K. A snowblower that takes you from 3 driveways a day to 20 earns far more.
- **Who gets the compounding**: ~35% of the US paycheck goes to lenders (his claim). Someone compounds that money either way; the question is whether it's the bank or you.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- Number one rule of money: spend less than you make. Keep expenses very conservative relative to income, even when income jumps.
- Pay off guaranteed 24% credit card rates before chasing 9-10% market returns.
- Save first, then pay off debt.
- Cars: buy used in cash (his first cars had 100K+ miles); check Carfax, insurance, and repair costs.
- Ask "what are you willing to give up to be a millionaire" rather than what you're willing to do. Early on it's elimination, not addition.
- "Do you want to look rich or be rich?" Many high earners are paycheck to paycheck; his wealthiest employees were not the highest earners.
- Charlie Munger: the first $100K is a b***h; do whatever it takes (Alex says that's ~$150-200K today).
- He stopped checking accounts daily once past ~$20M.
- The day you stop investing in your education is the day you decide you don't want to earn more.
- Pair it with the **30×12 challenge**: 30 straight days, 12 hours a day, no days off. Cutting spending and adding earning time is a double effect.

## Playbook steps
1. Buy-nothing challenge: disconnect cards from Amazon, leave your wallet at home, pay only for essentials (rent, insurance, gas, discount groceries, packed lunch). 2. Build a $1-5K fund. 3. Snowball consumer debt. 4. Build 3-6 months of reserves. 5. Cut fixed costs (car, rent, second car, vices). 6. Automate 15% pre-tax investing, plus education. 7. Earn more with extra shifts or by taking on more work at your job. 8. Pay off the house.

## Mistakes he warns against
- Not counting your mortgage, car payment, and credit cards as debt.
- Raising your lifestyle as soon as income rises.
- Trying to keep up with the Joneses or look rich.
- Leasing cars and paying for convenience ($500/mo ≈ $2M of convenience).
- Investing money from the emergency reserve.

## Examples / case details
- At $2K/mo pay he lived with 6 roommates and shared a bedroom. While making $1M/month, he lived on under $15K/mo with $1,200/mo rent, and a $100 Bonefish Grill dinner felt like balling out.
- Earned ~$55-80K/yr in consulting and saved ~$50K after tax, which funded his first gym. He rented a bedroom for $400, then lived inside his gym (said it wasn't legal).
- Domino chain: gym → gym turnarounds → Gym Launch → Prestige Labs → ALAN → Acquisition.com. Claims 9 failed businesses and 7 failed partnerships.
- Showed up at an early Gym Launch event (Albuquerque) in Leila's old car with a cracked windshield. Someone said, "I thought you were supposed to be rich."
- His doorman went from a $600/mo lease to a cash car and found watching the debt shrink reinforcing.
