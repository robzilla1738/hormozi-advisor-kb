# Should I quit, pivot, push through, or start something new?

**Bottom line:** Almost always push. Fix the one constraint in the business you have instead of starting a new one, because businesses grow to the level of the entrepreneur's incompetence and the next venture caps at the same level. Pivot only when data disproves a foundational assumption ("need-to-believe"), when the market is shrinking, or when years of real effort in the vehicle haven't compounded. When you do switch, go all in: sell or shut the old thing, with no straddling. Quit a *money-losing unit* fast, and quit *entrepreneurship* never.

Sources key: `(id)` = https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=<id>. Speaker flags are noted where someone other than Alex is speaking.

## Diagnose first

**Questions to answer before deciding:**
1. **What's stopping this business from 5×?** If you can't answer clearly, nothing else matters yet [Entrepreneurs, stop starting new businesses](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s59jbVBprn0) (s59jbVBprn0).
2. **Is a need-to-believe disproven?** List the first-principles assumptions the business needs (buyers exist on the channel, can be reached, can be profitable, and the market isn't dying). Pivot only if one is proven false with data (q9OmYf_IlQ0, rj7rzOdj84A).
3. **Is it you or the idea?** Do others succeed in your industry? If yes, the gap is skill: stay and get better (Tw6kJkVcTL0, w9Zvk70uj5Q).
4. **The 3× test:** "If we tripled customers in your current business, would you still want the new thing?" If no, fix the current business (s59jbVBprn0). Variant: if 10× more money would keep you in, fix the business instead of switching (YONIUpk-kAs).
5. **Supply- or demand-constrained?** It's never both at once, and the constraint moves like an accordion (sGv2BTUCcCM).
6. **Are you in pain, bored, or seeing a "woman in the red dress"?** Emotion-driven switches are the most common error (ug5N9qmFVKQ, 07jC6ooRIHw, 52tcB5FopAg).
7. **Unit numbers:** profit per unit or location, gross margin, LTGP:CAC, churn and M12 retention, runway in months (_8olk4-VFP8, uYds0zcAFWM, FiL0r5_dUvM).
8. **Time in:** how many years of *all-in* effort (side-project years don't count) (WOZGZK7K-Jo, Xb9ZOA6zypU).

**Branch by situation:**

| Situation | Default | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-revenue, < 1 year in | Push. Volume and skill are the gap; expect ~5 years | VgEvIIpDYG8, WOZGZK7K-Jo, w9Zvk70uj5Q |
| Several businesses, under 7 figures | Pick one and cut or fire-sell the rest now | qxQIcDrre1E, uRhArskhqms, hfwZwPGsbIo |

## Core principles

1. **Most strategies work; switching is what fails.** "We need perfect conviction about our plan more than we need a perfect plan." Each switch sends you back down the mountain [How To Progress Way Faster](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-UzJOk85OZI) (-UzJOk85OZI).
2. **The next business caps at the same level.** Businesses grow to the level of your incompetence. Plateaus usually mean you don't know how yet, not that the vehicle is wrong (s59jbVBprn0, q9OmYf_IlQ0).
3. **The wall is the real business.** After the basics are solved, a second- or third-order constraint shows up, and that's exactly when the "woman in the red dress" appears. Price what breaking through is worth, and "marry" the business (07jC6ooRIHw, rj7rzOdj84A).
4. **Every change costs ~20% up front.** Take only bets with big upside, and skip the 5% tweaks (uYds0zcAFWM, hfwZwPGsbIo, XwzU4RikbGs, 0lMn_-EXyhQ).
5. **More before better before new.** "The lowest risk-adjusted return move in any business is to do more of what's already working" (QwEb78e5a8Y, kloJJeiysxg, yflKMUffctE, --9kOXNCUdE).
6. **Pivot on data, not feelings.** Pivot only when a foundational assumption is proven untrue; otherwise push and iterate (rj7rzOdj84A). In traditional businesses (plumbing, HVAC, accounting) a need-to-believe almost never fails, so not scaling is "a you problem" (q9OmYf_IlQ0).
7. **Quit the vehicle, never the game.** Business is an infinite game: "if you quit, you lose" (WOZGZK7K-Jo, Nh8Oc7ERdIU). "Success and failure are on the same path, failure is just an earlier exit" (_KlZoPxbStk).
8. **Volume negates luck.** Quit only if the whole industry has disappeared; otherwise the gap is skill (w9Zvk70uj5Q).
9. **Change your timelines, not your dreams.** Founders break what works while waiting for a slow fix to kick in (kQFSiEDvXws). If you're 2 years in and not making money, "multiply that by three" (Xb9ZOA6zypU).
10. **Cut fast and absorb the pain.** After his DUI crash he exited three partnerships and fire-sold most of ~9–11 businesses in ~48 hours. That led to ~$3M profit within ~12 months (uRhArskhqms). "Some of the best money you ever spend is deciding to stop spending bad money" (8C_6qojTA78).
11. **When you switch, burn the boats.** "Neither of them will work" if you bridge both (GDqmlwY_uj4, Rm4zRdLAyjw, FiL0r5_dUvM, Tu6YDG0AZ5k).
12. **Decide big, irreversible calls in a controlled state.** If your answer flips day to day on unchanged data, don't decide yet. "Mistakes love a rushed decision" (7YMjZgr7sHM).

## Decision rules & thresholds

### Push vs pivot
- **Pivot only when a need-to-believe is disproven** (e.g. TikTok bans accountants, newspapers dying) (q9OmYf_IlQ0, rj7rzOdj84A).
- **Paid-channel test before abandoning a channel:** (1) are buyers on the platform? (2) can I reach them? (3) can it be profitable? If all three are yes, keep iterating and "just not run out of money while we try." His example: lose $100K over 6 months to unlock a channel that adds ~$15M EV to a $10M business (q9OmYf_IlQ0).
- **New channels pay "slower than you expect, then faster than you can imagine."** Outbound took 12 months, not the 12 weeks he expected. Expect months at −$5K/mo before the jump (oDK4g5na4Jw, qqjGxVW-Ae0).
- **Don't judge on tiny tests:** flyers need 5,000 minimum, then daily volume (ULGT0Qpglek). Don't judge a volume test after 1–2 days (0lMn_-EXyhQ).
- **Stuck at $1M/$3M/$10M for years** usually means changing too much and working on the wrong thing (XwzU4RikbGs). A plateau over a year means "you're not good enough": increase reps (d0dBZbd13jM).
- **Vehicle switch:** if 1, 2 or 5 years in with no significant progress, ask whether this is the best vehicle for your skills, traits and beliefs. Weigh that against compounding, because the second 5 years usually returns far more than the first (GDqmlwY_uj4).
- **"If your vehicle doesn't compound with time, change vehicles."** Otherwise pick one and stay: "ordinary business done for extraordinary time creates extraordinary results" (RdAKXJlMIZM).
- **Test:** "If I only did one thing for 40 years, would I be successful?" Probably → stick with it (hnmBXTyMFKI).
- **Experience-based no-pivot rule:** after ~8 years in a business, don't pivot to a new one; expand or copy the leader instead (bgBIO6nZawg). In year 10, 10% growth on $100M beats the odds of a new venture hitting $10M in year one (VBoRLJimVzc).

### Whether to change something
- Don't trade a guaranteed ~20% loss for a hoped 5% gain. Require **~40% upside at ~50% odds** before changing anything. Let an idea "season" 2–4 weeks (hfwZwPGsbIo).
- Only take bets with **≥20% upside**. Prefer medium-impact/high-confidence over big-impact/low-confidence (uYds0zcAFWM).
- Small businesses (≤10–20 employees): about **one big change per year**. If one move could double the business, don't do three (0lMn_-EXyhQ).
- A follow-up sequence test vs a proven control with maybe +10% upside and −50% downside: no. Assume the downside will happen (5Kt-EYieNko).
- Once you've diagnosed a constraint and started fixing it, **don't change the model, price or deliverable while you wait**. Put the urge into speeding up the chosen fix (kQFSiEDvXws).
- When you take on the true constraint (culture, brand), expect months to years, and expect it to feel worse at month 6. "Give time time" and don't leave half-built bridges (YONIUpk-kAs).

### Killing units, locations, side projects
- If a unit loses money and there's "not a specific reason we can't shut it down," shut it down. Treat unprofitable "impact" products as charity or lead nurture (6Fg4VXjRphQ).
- Close any break-even or losing location that isn't the future of the business (BMF2fWHyDrg).
- Don't open another location while existing ones profit ~$30K/yr each. Get one back to ~$40K/month profit *without you* first. Corporate locations should run 30%+ margins (_8olk4-VFP8).
- Don't open location #2 until #1 has held or grown for 6 straight months without you; the minimum is a 1-month-away test (cnbHgYYHzyE, unshZobTt6Q).
- At capacity and unprofitable: **raise prices** before adding locations or product lines (F84olnKkseM, cnbHgYYHzyE). When supply-constrained, raise 50–100% (sGv2BTUCcCM).
- Litmus for which business to keep: the one you put the least time into that still makes money (rhVxX5_8xUw). Under seven figures with multiple businesses, ask: if one vanished, how easy would it be to make the other win? (qxQIcDrre1E).
- If unlimited losses are possible, stop immediately, however much is sunk (Dave Ramsey, guest, -HJg4TYBgtI).

### Starting a second business or line
- Don't start a second business until you pass the owner test (MLkFOaxIdxM). You can own several businesses but can't be CEO of two (F84olnKkseM, s59jbVBprn0, sKCrYIWPHLc).
- If you're not doing the basics (every platform, regular email, SEO, sales training), don't start a new business. Finish your top-25 obvious list first (yflKMUffctE).
- Don't add product lines at ~$3M just because one ad channel stopped scaling. Pull the 4 lead levers first (9gVdCR7W8o8).
- **Bored at $1–5M?** Don't enter an arena where incumbents have a 10-year head start. Channel novelty into new front ends or content (ug5N9qmFVKQ, hfwZwPGsbIo).
- Info → SaaS: he has "yet to see" one succeed at a true software exit. If you jump, sell or shut the old business: "No half measures" (FiL0r5_dUvM).
- A side business taking 10% of income takes 50–100% of bandwidth in a crisis (Rm4zRdLAyjw).
- When an opportunity is orders of magnitude better, sell or exit the old one even cheaply. He calls this "unreasonable" advice, to be decided on opportunity cost (0EqJD2o-Mnk).

### Quitting a job or entrepreneurship
- Side income must match the job and hold for 6 months (Nh8Oc7ERdIU), or exceed it or be very reasonably about to (A9qHKjFPJ-E).
- Quitting a job you want to leave with nothing to lose is a free lottery ticket (U_s0ekwPK5g).
- Don't use "not my passion" as a reason to quit; check whether it's really intolerance of boredom (UDBkiBnMrHs).
- Give a new direction ~36 months before judging it (aFoMYz_jWcs, YFA8AS5Cu2w), and at least ~100 days / 6 months before chasing the next shiny thing (XGm2ERU9qtA).
- Expect ~5 years all-in before you "start figuring out what's going on" (WOZGZK7K-Jo, VgEvIIpDYG8).

### Selling / exiting
- Wanting to sell most when in pain is a signal to fix the pain (hire), not to sell. Exits are messier and smaller than the headline (earnouts, rolled equity, ~20% tax) (52tcB5FopAg).
- Before selling, decide what you'll buy with the proceeds that beats it (6x3re5-Ms1E). Don't sell early if one more year can fix things and grow profit (fD-sxKiB30M).
- Bad reasons to sell, from his own self-rebuttals: being "legit," a rite of passage, proving net worth, an "F you" to doubters (_gcqwupsza8).
- When exiting a business or partnership, "do right by everyone": refunds, buyouts, leases, vendors (Xb9ZOA6zypU, oK2_u-uS_Bo).

## Playbooks

### A. "My business has plateaued and I want to start something new"
1. Run the **3× test** and the **10× money test**. If more of this would satisfy you, the problem is the business, not the vehicle (s59jbVBprn0, YONIUpk-kAs).
2. Name the constraint as a solvable question ("what's stopping us from 5x?"). Triage supply vs demand (s59jbVBprn0, sGv2BTUCcCM).
3. Ask people in your market what the hardest part of the business is. That's the big hairy problem (q9OmYf_IlQ0, rj7rzOdj84A).
4. **Price the wall:** what is breaking through worth in enterprise value? Frame it as "if in 5 years this is a $100M enterprise, is it worth it?" Expect 2–5 years (07jC6ooRIHw).
5. Make the list of ~25 obvious things you aren't doing yet. Do "more" first (1 → 5 content pieces, salespeople, editors) (yflKMUffctE).
6. Say no to side partnerships, networking lunches and side businesses (07jC6ooRIHw, rhVxX5_8xUw).

### B. Losing money / runway is short
1. List each unit's revenue and profit. Kill the unit that loses money; treat education or "impact" as a free lead magnet (6Fg4VXjRphQ).
2. Point all traffic at the one thing that makes money. Reprice to an outcome ($250/hr → a $5,800 package + 5% of deals) and keep ≥80% gross margin (6Fg4VXjRphQ, 3yAiVjcImQ4).
3. If at capacity: raise price before anything else (sGv2BTUCcCM, cnbHgYYHzyE).
4. Cut costs and break leases. In a crisis, use the "permission" to move to a better model (3ktgTBNlg7M).
5. Expect the fix to break delivery. Use the new cash to hire supply (6Fg4VXjRphQ).

### C. Deciding whether to pivot the vehicle
1. Write the need-to-believes. Gather data on each (q9OmYf_IlQ0).
2. Check market direction: is the market shrinking? Is the customer moving (e.g. COVID moving fitness remote)? "Gym Launch is loyal to gym owners, not to gyms" (_tF3ODk56hg, JSsuK_0kdzA).
3. Check whether others win in your space. If they do, model the leader instead of pivoting (Tw6kJkVcTL0, bgBIO6nZawg).
4. If a pivot is justified: set the date, sell or shut the old business, and put the same skills in a higher-margin vehicle (service → done-for-you → licensing) (Xb9ZOA6zypU, GDqmlwY_uj4).
5. First crossroads in business? Take the lower-risk path. If you take the big swing, burn the boats (Tu6YDG0AZ5k).

### D. Too many businesses
1. Rank them by profit per unit of your attention (rhVxX5_8xUw).
2. Pick one. "Either of the choices will work if you stick with it and neither of them will work if you don't pick" (rhVxX5_8xUw).
3. Within ~48 hours, exit, give away or fire-sell the rest and accept the losses (uRhArskhqms).
4. Make the unmade decisions and have the unconfronted conversations; they drain attention (uRhArskhqms, 6ZEZWuVC8-8).

### E. Feeling burnt out, want to quit
1. Separate real burnout (a measurable drop in output per hour → rest, then resume) from an emotional reaction (mNcDKnq6-q8).
2. If the plan is solid, keep commitment to it: "when you feel stress you do not need to behave stressfully" (vthPawWn6ws).
3. Decide big irreversible calls rested, away from your usual space, and only when the answer is stable over days (7YMjZgr7sHM).

## Anti-patterns

- **Chasing the "woman in the red dress"** when the business hits its real wall (07jC6ooRIHw, rj7rzOdj84A, 5Kt-EYieNko).
- **Breaking a working business to cure boredom or status needs.** He made this "business solves my personal problems" mistake for ~4 years (ug5N9qmFVKQ).
- **Straddling / bridging** two vehicles during a switch (GDqmlwY_uj4, Rm4zRdLAyjw).
- **Bolting on software to escape a service or info business** (pw1PcHfYP2w, FiL0r5_dUvM).
- **Changing model, price or deliverable while a fix is still maturing** (kQFSiEDvXws).
- **Expanding a weak unit** (franchising or licensing ~$30K/yr locations) (_8olk4-VFP8, F84olnKkseM).
- **Selling out of pain** (52tcB5FopAg).
- **Reacting to competitors.** His biggest single loss, about $25M, came from reacting to a competitor (ln24y0FPJHo, tdLLGKoBojo). "Most times no reaction is the best decision" (FTgJ0mQi0uU).
- **Learning the wrong lesson** from a failure ("no one can sell like me") or from a fast early success (SasEJE4FI-I, FTgJ0mQi0uU).
- **Calling it "not scalable"** when it's just hard (kQFSiEDvXws).
- **FOMO and Hail-Mary optimism when cash-constrained.** "I'd rather have a very sure 20% than a really risky 40" (tdLLGKoBojo, 5Kt-EYieNko).

## Worked examples

- **Latin-music lawyer** (6Fg4VXjRphQ, 3yAiVjcImQ4): $300K revenue at −$100K profit, ~6 months of cash. Software burned ~$150K/yr, about the whole loss. Fix: kill the software, make education free nurture, and reprice services from $250/hr to $5,800 + 5% of deals. "You literally have everything in place. They were just organized wrong."
- **3-location gym owner** (_8olk4-VFP8): ~$30K/yr profit per location and planning #4 or a franchise. Alex said consolidate and get one location back to ~$40K/month profit without the owner first. He bets one focused location beats all three.
- **Gym Launch rock bottom → licensing** (Xb9ZOA6zypU): payment processor holds plus 35% refunds under done-for-you. The same skills repackaged as licensing went from ~$100K/mo to $2M+/mo, with $17M profit the next year.
- **The 48-hour cut** (uRhArskhqms): after the DUI crash, exited 3 partnerships and fire-sold ~9–11 businesses. Went all in on Gym Launch → ~$3M profit in ~12 months.
- **COVID pivot** (_tF3ODk56hg, 3ktgTBNlg7M): with 25–30% of independent gyms closed, Gym Launch moved to remote coaching. Advice: break leases, keep the best 1–2 trainers, 100 reach-outs a day.
- **Selling at 31** (_gcqwupsza8): sold 2/3 stakes in Gym Launch, Prestige Labs and ALAN (>$50M total; two at a $46.2M valuation) after 18 months to 2.5 years of deliberation. Money was a wash; he sold to focus on Acquisition.com.
- **Trophy Outlet** (BMF2fWHyDrg): $1.72M revenue, 6.4% net margin. Alex made the owner pick one model (gag-gift media/e-commerce vs B2B league repeat) instead of both. He picked gag gifts.

## Metrics to watch

- **7 days:** daily input volume vs plan (reach-outs, content, calls). Is the decision stable day to day on unchanged data? (7YMjZgr7sHM, w9Zvk70uj5Q)
- **30 days:** profit by unit or location; cash runway; the constraint metric (lead flow if demand-constrained, capacity or hiring if supply-constrained); close rate after any price raise (sGv2BTUCcCM, 6Fg4VXjRphQ).
- **90 days:** LTGP:CAC by unit (3:1 for leveraged models; ≥15–30:1 for manual ones), revenue retention or M12, and whether need-to-believes still hold (uYds0zcAFWM, FiL0r5_dUvM, q9OmYf_IlQ0).
- **Annual:** one big change made? Growth vs the ~10%/yr floor ($1–10M businesses should grow much faster) (cnbHgYYHzyE, 0lMn_-EXyhQ).
