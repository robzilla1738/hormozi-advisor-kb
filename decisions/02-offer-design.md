# How do I design an offer so good people feel stupid saying no?

**Bottom line:** The offer is the fastest, highest-leverage fix in most businesses. One better offer raises response rate, close rate *and* price at the same time (4GjwtnA76ig, neTSqOAMgao, F3NyhOtRpOE). Build it with the value equation: raise the dream outcome and perceived likelihood, and cut time delay and effort. Sell one core offer to one narrow, valuable avatar, and back it with a guarantee you can actually afford (5MHQr-Z17Hc, _qspvJAq34M, 3yAiVjcImQ4). Fix the offer before channels, ads or the money model (neTSqOAMgao).

## Diagnose first

**Signs the offer is the problem:**
- Ads get clicks or opt-ins but don't convert, or new reps ramp slowly. "the offer is usually the core problem" (F3NyhOtRpOE).
- Deals are lost "on price." Hourly or commodity framing leaves buyers only price to compare (zzleYxkf39k).
- Fewer than ~20% upgrade from core to upsell, which points to an offer issue on the back end and/or front end (n6SHLmKcY0E).
- You can't explain it in 1–2 sentences (pxVeOkOVr2w). You can't "make the pitch by just stating the offer and shutting up and pointing to how to pay" (YONIUpk-kAs).
- You can't back it with a strong guarantee. "If you can't have the strong offer and the strong guarantee behind it then the product sucks." (neTSqOAMgao)
- An affiliate or partner offer doesn't change their behavior. "the offer wasn't compelling enough or you made it too hard" (--9kOXNCUdE).

**Check these in order: market → offer → persuasion** (4XWlHPyAico). A great market can rescue a weak offer, while a negative market sinks everything. If the market is neutral or positive, fix the offer next, because it's the easiest and fastest lever.

**How the answer branches by stage:**

| Stage | Offer posture |
|---|---|
| **Pre-revenue** | Free or risk-free offer to get proof. The first ~5 customers get a free case study in exchange for usage, feedback, a review and referrals (w7g08dVTwaE, HsQeQM1jUeg, HxEQCHpZzHk). Make it risk-free while unproven, so "everyone always got way more value than they paid me" (Xb9ZOA6zypU). |
| **< $1M** | "One product, one avatar, one channel until you're at a million bucks a year." (3yAiVjcImQ4) This largely holds up to ~$3M (neTSqOAMgao, VBoRLJimVzc, TIH1w-KuATk). |
| **$1–10M** | Two offers: front end + back end (EZLQzR-_83A). Raise LTGP via product, churn, price or one high-value back-end tier (neTSqOAMgao). |
| **Cash crisis** | Run the "baddest offer" to your existing list first (xRxVpjeHmeA). A scarce, high-touch, prepaid access offer to ~20% of demand raises $5–50K fast (m-7VjocC76M). |
| **Service** | Repackage existing capacity using the Delivery Cube: group vs 1:1, speed tiers, access, done-for-you vs DIY (t9Vv7rK-4Jc). Deliver the result in half the time (t9Vv7rK-4Jc). |
| **Physical / e-com** | The same value equation and enhancers apply. Amazon is the model for hitting every variable (NA61omfYgvI). |
| **Info / community** | Stack what people will actually pay for (exclusive access, content, events, newsletter) and make every bonus worth the price (5MjjpB8SPMo). |

## Core principles

1. **"Make offers so good they'd feel stupid saying no."** (XwZH-lOKG9c) "If you have a good enough offer, you don't have to sell hard." (pxVeOkOVr2w)
2. **The value equation:** (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice). Beginners only inflate the promise, while durable moats come from shrinking the denominator (5MHQr-Z17Hc, _qspvJAq34M).
3. **"Fast beats free."** Against free or cheap competitors, win on speed and risk (_qspvJAq34M, s7QA1TJKlbQ, XwZH-lOKG9c, t9Vv7rK-4Jc).
4. **Win one vector clearly:** speed, risk, price or ease. They are the value equation under other names (s7QA1TJKlbQ).
5. **De-commoditize into a category of one** so the decision is value-based, not price-based (F3NyhOtRpOE, ZuJryiwxjDw, -Pz0NpcILOY).
6. **Narrow the avatar.** "Narrower niche = higher price and fewer competitors" (nIk3DedjxJM). More than half of Acquisition.com's portfolio narrowed rather than broadened (reisEL_D7xc). The avatar needs 3+ descriptors (nIk3DedjxJM).
7. **Pick the avatar you can create the most value for** (AN2KpRBsmRY). Be "able and willing to turn away worse customers" (5MjjpB8SPMo). "When you have the wrong avatar, the volatility of your business is based on the volatility of your customers." (CoPs-Bk8M9Y)
8. **One core offer, with payment terms instead of a menu** (EZLQzR-_83A).
9. **"Your free stuff should be better than their paid stuff."** (7NMH1oAkgLY, WrCt0R3FBFs, hHkdbr6_JJs) "Look at what everyone else is buying, make it better, make it free, monetize another way." (ovL6Z5z0jxQ)
10. **Proof beats promise.** "Competitors can copy your offer, but they cannot copy your proof." (JDR-R--4HhM) 1,000 testimonials beat 1 (_qspvJAq34M).
11. **Timing matters.** "The right offer at the wrong time is still the wrong offer." (n6SHLmKcY0E) "Offer timing matters just as much if not more than the offer itself." (FiL0r5_dUvM)
12. **Clear beats clever.** "The offer is still king." (YONIUpk-kAs)

## Decision rules & thresholds

### Guarantees
- **Four types:** (1) unconditional (strongest), (2) conditional ("if you do X and don't get Y, we…"), (3) anti-guarantee (no refunds, framed as the work being on them or a proprietary secret), (4) implied/performance (rev share, profit share, kickers) (5MHQr-Z17Hc).
- **Formula:** "If you don't achieve X by Y, I will Z." "It's the or what that gives the guarantee power." (unshZobTt6Q)
- **It's math.** +20% sales against refunds rising from 1% to 5% still nets ~+15%. "Having the guarantee is just a math equation." (5MHQr-Z17Hc) "The point of a guarantee is to maximize net conversion." (--9kOXNCUdE)
- **Size it to conversion:** if 25% back converts the same as 100% back, offer 25%. Even ~10% back can change behavior (--9kOXNCUdE).
- **Conditional stipulations** should be the 2–3 actions your best customers took. Use the fewest possible, because each one weakens the guarantee (5MHQr-Z17Hc).
- **Stack them:** e.g. 30-day unconditional + 90-day conditional, which turns the sale into a trial (5MHQr-Z17Hc).
- Spell out the outcome, deadline and remedy. Never say only "results guaranteed" (5MHQr-Z17Hc).
- "Guarantees only work if you have stakes." (s7QA1TJKlbQ)
- Real-world refund rates: 2 refund requests over years on an unconditional gym guarantee (unshZobTt6Q). ButcherBox refunds were "so small I don't even know the number" (guest Mike Salguero, orMbq2LtzKE). "If you fear refunds, fix the product."
- **Pull-back rule:** design a near-irresistible version (e.g. free unless it works, or 2× back), then pull back only until about 90% would still say yes (neTSqOAMgao).

### Free, trials and front ends
- Free front ends usually get leads at about half the cost (nSQdjim8CsE). "The two most powerful words in marketing are free and new." (ovL6Z5z0jxQ) Going from $20 to $0 "gets 10 times the interest" (Lc8DNduiwKA).
- Front-end offer should break even or close to it on the first transaction, with profit by day 30 via continuity (_tF3ODk56hg).
- **Massage math:** $100 offer to 100 people → ~1 yes. A $10 trial → ~20 yes, and ~1/3 buy the $100 package → ~6 customers for ~$200 lost (7NMH1oAkgLY).
- **Free-service math:** $25 hard cost (worth $250+) with 1 in 4 buying → $100 CAC (Mst4hreQYl0).
- Free work that costs you money is valued more than a free PDF (7NMH1oAkgLY).
- Go order-of-magnitude, not incremental. Competitors offered 14–21 day trials, so he gave a full 6-week program (ovL6Z5z0jxQ).
- Limit early free customers to ~5 (w7g08dVTwaE). Give free services only to leads with budget, authority, need and urgency (Mst4hreQYl0).
- "If you can't close free trials, you have other problems." (pxVeOkOVr2w)
- "Learning how to monetize free is one of the biggest tools in your toolbox." (pxVeOkOVr2w)

### Lead magnets (offer before the offer)
- A lead magnet is a mini-offer that completely solves a narrow problem and reveals the next problem your core offer solves (Mst4hreQYl0, 7NMH1oAkgLY).
- Test packaging and headline more than contents. The name can change uptake 2–10× (Mst4hreQYl0).

### Enhancers: scarcity, urgency, bonuses, naming
- "Scarcity is limiting quantity, urgency is limiting time." (HwPXctiw_rY)
- Don't sell after a stated deadline or cap, to protect future scarcity (JDR-R--4HhM). Cap capacity, then reopen when demand builds (QQGHCG8d1So).
- Promotion limits: gift cards 2 per person, high-ticket to ~5% of customers, prepay deals capped at a fixed number (mHrAjWni65E).
- **MAGIC naming:** Magnetic reason why, Avatar callout, Goal, Interval, Container word (e.g. "Busy Mom's 30-Day Relaxation Getaway") (mHrAjWni65E). "We have to always answer the reason why we're making an offer." (xRxVpjeHmeA)
- Any reason works as "wrapping paper": holiday, anniversary, birthday (YUXLJauT4eY). Birthday + anniversary offers give ~1/6 of your list a personal reason each month (HwPXctiw_rY). "Once you nail the offer, you just go endless variations and wrapping paper on it." (HwPXctiw_rY)
- **Bonuses:** byproduct ("sawdust") bonuses work as bait for upsells (vZfatNSouDQ). Drop bonuses only beginners love (5MjjpB8SPMo). Every feature must justify 3–4× the price or be cut (FiL0r5_dUvM). Place recurring bonuses just past average churn (eX3Ch_HuR70).
- Risk reversal on pages: show how to cancel, activation speed and the signup steps (HwPXctiw_rY). Put trust next to the action and answer the #1 fear directly (xRxVpjeHmeA).

### Structure and money model
- A **money model** is "a series of offers deliberately structured to generate the most upfront cash, get the most leads together, and get the highest lifetime value" (nSQdjim8CsE).
- Upsell ≈ 5× core. Fewer than ~20% taking it signals an offer issue (JE2_7elAcxM, n6SHLmKcY0E). "Offering people to buy more of what they just bought almost always" raises LTV (mRlSb0O5QNU).
- Annual-only at launch moved annual take from ~10% to 30% and roughly doubled AOV (j2TZMFkj71Q).
- Package monthly as a fixed term ($12K = 6 × $2K) for 2–3× LTGP (jOM0m34dxz4).
- **Downsell only disqualified leads.** Track core close rate first. The downsell must add sales without cannibalizing (MwhsM7qijy8).
- Avoid lifetime deals, which bring one-time income with forever delivery (TooAB8Ow6cQ).
- Prefer high-volume, shorter-cycle offers early for cash-flow reliability (o64cI6tebnU).
- $30K offers need qualifier or set calls, not a straight payment page (U_s0ekwPK5g, n6SHLmKcY0E).

### Niche and avatar
- Choose the avatar with the best LTV:CAC relative to market size (QTZsh3BgOwY).
- Don't switch or broaden the niche until you've hit its market cap. "Why don't you just double your business?" (neTSqOAMgao)
- If churn comes from customers failing, change who you serve. Don't keep rebuilding offers (thDTmy7VGIw).
- Under seven figures with several businesses, pick one (the "niche-hopping fallacy") (qxQIcDrre1E).

## Playbooks

### A. Build a grand-slam offer from scratch (5MHQr-Z17Hc, _qspvJAq34M, 4GjwtnA76ig)
1. Pick a starving crowd: a valuable, growing market you can reach (4GjwtnA76ig, 4XWlHPyAico).
2. Define the dream outcome as the real job to be done, in the prospect's own words (_qspvJAq34M).
3. Map every micro-step of the customer journey and list every problem at each step (5MHQr-Z17Hc).
4. Write a solution for each problem. Rate value and incremental cost (L/M/H) and keep the high-value, low-cost items.
5. Rewrite claims using the four variables: bigger outcome, more proof, faster, easier.
6. Add a guarantee (stack unconditional + conditional) and enhancers (scarcity, urgency, bonuses).
7. Name it with MAGIC (mHrAjWni65E).
8. Price high and justify it with value (see [03 Pricing](03-pricing.md)).
9. Test: can you state it in 1–2 sentences and point to how to pay? (pxVeOkOVr2w, YONIUpk-kAs)

### B. Offer isn't converting
1. Confirm it's the offer: ads click but don't convert, reps ramp slowly, upsell take is under 20% (F3NyhOtRpOE, n6SHLmKcY0E).
2. Check the avatar. Narrow it to the top 20% and their shared traits (5MjjpB8SPMo).
3. Strengthen the denominator: speed and ease. Fast beats free.
4. Strengthen the guarantee until you're near ~90% would-say-yes (neTSqOAMgao).
5. Change one big thing at a time (--9kOXNCUdE). "When you change anything, you guarantee you incur the cost of change, but you do not guarantee that you get the benefit of change." (HxEQCHpZzHk)

### C. Launch a free / lead-magnet front end (7NMH1oAkgLY, Mst4hreQYl0, ovL6Z5z0jxQ)
1. Look at what competitors sell as their core offer, and make it better and free.
2. Solve one narrow problem completely, and reveal the next problem your paid offer solves.
3. Qualify with a dropdown or BANT, and route unqualified leads elsewhere.
4. Do the math: hard cost per trial × trials ÷ buyers = CAC, then compare it with LTGP.
5. Test names and headlines (2–10× swings).

### D. Repackage a service without new cost (t9Vv7rK-4Jc, m-7VjocC76M)
1. Place your current offer on the Delivery Cube: 1:1 vs group, speed, access, DIY/DWY/DFY, physical vs digital.
2. Create a faster tier (half the time) and a VIP-expectation tier (e.g. 60-minute vs 24-hour support).
3. Add digital or access categories rather than more people-heavy service to raise enterprise value (m-7VjocC76M).

### E. First 5 customers (w7g08dVTwaE)
1. Personal messages to every contact (100/day), asking for referrals, not a sale.
2. A free case study to the first 5 in exchange for usage, feedback and a review.
3. Start charging once people refer, then raise the price as results come in.

## Anti-patterns
- **Only inflating the promise** while ignoring time and effort (5MHQr-Z17Hc, _qspvJAq34M).
- **Multiple avatars, products or channels under $1M** (3yAiVjcImQ4, VBoRLJimVzc, neTSqOAMgao, QTZsh3BgOwY). "neither of them will work if you try to do both."
- **Building a second low-touch product instead of fixing capacity.** In the Couplepreneurs teardown, a $5K product broke the economics (N5MExtki_VI).
- **A sprawling menu** for every budget (EZLQzR-_83A).
- **Hourly pricing** that makes you a commodity (zzleYxkf39k).
- **Changing offers every ~6 months.** It's a red flag for a marketer who can't keep promises (Uki3IUkUu7Q).
- **Selling harder instead of fixing the product** (neTSqOAMgao, orMbq2LtzKE).
- **Lifetime deals** (TooAB8Ow6cQ). Showing the downsell to qualified buyers (MwhsM7qijy8).
- **Breaking scarcity** by selling after the deadline (JDR-R--4HhM).
- **Taking money from people likely to be unhappy** in early betas. Keep early negative word of mouth "concentrated and quiet" (HxEQCHpZzHk).
- **Pitching a $30K offer to cold strangers** on a straight call funnel (n6SHLmKcY0E).

## Worked examples
- **First gyms:** a pre-sold, risk-reversed 6-week challenge ($600) with ~$5 leads and 1 in 4 closing → ~$20–25 CAC. Then upsell supplements and an annual membership with the challenge fee credited, so gyms opened at full capacity on customer money (lXRqOG2yn6E, GKvAQIe5vpo).
- **Sticker-education business:** annual-only with a physical-kit premium; annual take ~10% → 30%, AOV roughly doubled (j2TZMFkj71Q).
- **Theme park in a cash crisis:** run the baddest offer to the existing list, and add a day-one money-back guarantee and safety proof on the page (xRxVpjeHmeA).
- **$100M Offers book:** 200K+ copies in a year through word of mouth, built as "a complete solution to a narrowly defined problem" (-TOYJHax5x8).
- **Email challenge:** a 7-day challenge to ~15% of an old list produced $2.4M in 5 days for a portfolio company (OpeN4O5myIg).
- **ButcherBox referrals (guest Mike Salguero):** a "nearly free" $20 trial box for the friend beat cash rewards (orMbq2LtzKE).

## Metrics to watch
- **7 days:** opt-in and click → lead conversion. Close rate off shows *and* off offers (JE2_7elAcxM). Free-trial → paid conversion. Name and headline test results.
- **30 days:** refund rate vs conversion lift (net conversion) (5MHQr-Z17Hc). Upsell take rate (≥20%). Annual or prepay take rate. 30-day cash vs CAC.
- **90 days:** LTGP:CAC by avatar (QTZsh3BgOwY). Churn of new-offer cohorts. Don't overhaul on month-1 churn for offers under ~90 days old (-j8_YCWZ05Q). Testimonials and case studies gathered (proof inventory).
