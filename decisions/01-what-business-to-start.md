# What business should I start (or which vehicle should I be in)?

**Bottom line:** Pick a boring business with *no idea risk*: an established need people already pay for, in a flat or growing market, where you have (or will go get) real experience. Your only variable is then execution. Start with what your resources allow (with ~$100 that's a service, meaning your time sold at a high ticket). Prefer models that are sticky, high gross margin and low complexity. Then do one avatar, one product, one channel until ~$1M, because the vehicle you pick sets the ceiling ("it's not how hard you row but what boat you are in").

Sources key: `(id)` = https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=<id>. Speaker flags are noted where a guest is speaking.

## Diagnose first

**Collect before choosing:**
1. **Goal:** income ($100K / $1–3M per year) or a sellable asset ($10M+ exit)? This decides info vs traditional business [Rm4zRdLAyjw](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rm4zRdLAyjw).
2. **Resources:** cash on hand, monthly burn, runway in months, skills you already have (often absorbed from family or odd jobs) [LVM89ik-7Kw](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LVM89ik-7Kw).
3. **Experience:** have you *done* the thing you'd sell? If not, go do it first [VgEvIIpDYG8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VgEvIIpDYG8).
4. **Market direction:** flat, growing or shrinking? Is demand growing faster than the supply of competitors? (GDqmlwY_uj4)
5. **Unit economics you can reach:** price, gross margin (service target 80%+), likely LTV, CAC, cash payback (aOq1Hdz0w3k, i7bLRKwKSms).
6. **Advantage score:** Sticky / Expensive (margin) / Expansion (growing market) / Air (low complexity, low capex) / Unique (moat) (3fsJFUvA6Ts).
7. **Your life situation:** income need, dependents, risk tolerance, current job (A9qHKjFPJ-E, oLsWhSRypzM).

**Branch by stage:**

| Situation | Default move | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Broke, ~$100, no skills | Sell your time as a service: pick a skill people already pay for, learn it, give the first 5 away for reviews and referrals | LVM89ik-7Kw, jfW6gL6hKhk, w7g08dVTwaE |
| Employed, wants out | Side hustle until it matches your income *and holds for 6 months*, or try switching role or company first | Nh8Oc7ERdIU, A9qHKjFPJ-E, oLsWhSRypzM |
| Student / under ~25 | Take learning-rich roles (sales first). College only if aid ≥50%, top-20, or a degree-gated career | -wnnwCqGeNc, 5RiR6cBLkFg, nxL4ZfVqKLI |
| Has savings and wants an asset | Consider buying a small cash-flowing business with seller financing ("agree on price, then agree on terms") | KNl83INePXI |
| Already running a business < ~$10M | **Don't start a new one.** Fix the constraint in this one (see 13-quit-or-pivot) | s59jbVBprn0, MLkFOaxIdxM |
| Has capital and success | Prefer capital-intensive vehicles (fewer competitors) or paths 3–4 (invest in or run others' businesses) | 3fsJFUvA6Ts, Lc8DNduiwKA, sL16tsGafcQ |
| Has an audience | Monetize in order: affiliates → sponsorships → equity partnerships → own brand | oRqnTOT9ZG8, 4pZwlE86A5Y |

**Branch by model ("shape"), each with its built-in "big hairy problem"** (qsXxckCbci0, Rm4zRdLAyjw):
- **Service:** no capital needed, high margin possible. Hard part: talent and delivery at scale. Scales by chain, franchise, licensing or tech-enabled service (O1As2zxy0es).
- **Info / education / community:** fastest and easiest to $1–3M/yr with a team of ~10. Hard to scale to $100M or sell: knowledge isn't sticky, and there's key-man risk (Rm4zRdLAyjw, VxKwz6hBVZU).
- **E-commerce / CPG:** constrained by free cash flow, inventory and marketing cost. The #1 metric is repurchase rate after first try (qsXxckCbci0, rj7rzOdj84A).
- **Software:** only for founders with an in-house technical co-founder. Expect "making no money for basically seven years" self-funded (8C_6qojTA78, pw1PcHfYP2w).

## Core principles

1. **Choose ideas with no idea risk; take only execution risk.** Established needs (customers, money, jobs, skills, weight loss, entertainment) don't change. Failure can then only be your execution, which teaches you business [2 Types of Business Risk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A9qHKjFPJ-E) (A9qHKjFPJ-E).
2. **Market > offer > persuasion.** "Great entrepreneur, poor market, market wins. Poor entrepreneur, great market, market wins" [Watch these 55 minutes…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qxQIcDrre1E) (qxQIcDrre1E). As long as the market isn't negative, move on to fixing the offer (4XWlHPyAico).
3. **The perfect business is "Unique, Expensive, Sticky Air"**, run by a risk-averse, long-term operator of unimpeachable character (VaE4pcf9PI8). If you get only one advantage, pick stickiness (3fsJFUvA6Ts). "The way to create a fortune in business is to only sell things that people do not stop buying" (3P1XjUvo1b4).
4. **The opportunity vehicle is the biggest lever.** Each step up in vehicle leverage added "about a zero per month" to his income [How I find MILLION DOLLAR opportunities](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GDqmlwY_uj4) (GDqmlwY_uj4). A vehicle is judged by units you could sell × profit per unit × competitive and supply/demand dynamics (aOq1Hdz0w3k).
5. **Do an old thing better, in a niche you know.** "Don't sell how to do something you've never done" (VgEvIIpDYG8). Pick a boring existing business you've had exposure to and do it faster and easier (u4kgZ3pf8cU). Selling to people like you is a shortcut to their language and pains (AN2KpRBsmRY).
6. **Resources pick the category.** There are 7 things you can sell (×2 for physical/digital). With ~$100, physical products and software are mostly ruled out, so sell time (LVM89ik-7Kw).
7. **The model is the moat.** LTGP:CAC plus payback speed decides who wins the attention auction. "Methods expire; models endure" (i7bLRKwKSms, jzKpAtzKQ54, QGcjweehrvU).
8. **Sell very expensive to few or very cheap to everyone. "The middle is where people die"** (uWdIgftpvBI). Wealth follows a power law, so price for the top (ZuJryiwxjDw).
9. **Focus: one avatar, one product, one channel until ~$1M.** Pursuing several "virtually guarantee[s]" you succeed at none (0_Gf5v8DEMY, VBoRLJimVzc, hnmBXTyMFKI).
10. **Pick what the world pays for, not your passion.** "Passion happens as a consequence, not a requirement" (6uhd-FiCggg). "Do I love the thing or the business of doing the thing?" decides the Artist / Entrepreneur / Hybrid path (qLM5G7N3l3I).
11. **Bootstrap the first business** to pay down "ignorance debt" with your own money, not friends' and family's. No debt on a first business (BO_59sGxztY, sL16tsGafcQ, u4kgZ3pf8cU).
12. **Pick a dominant game whose prize you actually want.** "Play dumb games, win dumb prizes" (P14HA83uNJE). Ask whether time helps or hurts you in this opportunity (E732S9fteRc).

## Decision rules & thresholds

### Market selection
- Market must be **flat or growing, never shrinking** (retirement homes yes, print newspapers no) (AN2KpRBsmRY). He cites a newspaper ad platform shrinking ~25%/yr as the thing to avoid (qxQIcDrre1E).
- Avoid markets that **can't pay** (a résumé service for the unemployed tanked), **can't be targeted**, or are **shrinking** (F3NyhOtRpOE).
- Avoid huge markets with entrenched supply (competing with Verizon). Look for a niche sliver with less competition, or demand growing faster than entrants (e.g. demand +20%/yr vs competitors +10%/yr) (GDqmlwY_uj4).
- **Red oceans have the most fish.** Unless you sell to ~140 people in a rural town, your market is 100–1,000× bigger than you think. Local: needing ~200 customers in a city of 400K–1M means market size isn't your problem (hnmBXTyMFKI).
- More competitors means more buyers, as long as you aim to be #1 (bgBIO6nZawg).
- Pick the niche where you create the most value: help the person you can lift $100K over the one you can lift $10 (AN2KpRBsmRY).
- The avatar needs 3+ descriptors (nIk3DedjxJM). Use 2–5 common traits, never more than 5 (M2VP64Tc60w).
- If you're new, **niche down**. Narrow is easier to attract and deliver to. "Oftentimes people go too broad too soon" (M2VP64Tc60w). Narrower means higher price and fewer competitors (nIk3DedjxJM).
- Boring markets (dry cleaning, lawn care) aren't contested by the smartest people (yflKMUffctE).

### Economics of the model
- **Service gross margin ≥ 80%.** At $1,000/mo, delivery must cost under $200 (aOq1Hdz0w3k, unshZobTt6Q, CGSd00h-6zI). Buffett looks for ~99%; most gyms Alex turned around ran ~66% ("horrible") (VaE4pcf9PI8).
- **LTGP:CAC > 3:1, 5:1 great.** If it's bad he tosses the business out without looking further (QGcjweehrvU).
- **Recover CAC within 30 days** (rule of thumb) (i7bLRKwKSms). Front-end offer should break even and turn profitable by day 30 via continuity (_tF3ODk56hg).
- **LTV floor:** making real money below ~$1K LTV is "super super rare." Starting out, aim for the $5K–$15K sweet spot and over-deliver. A $1K deal isn't worth hand-to-hand sales; it needs to be closer to ~$10K (BSwJQsogah0).
- Low-ticket ($10–$100/mo) needs heavy ops and support skill for volume, which is a different skill from acquisition (BSwJQsogah0).
- High ROIC makes you "a magnet for money." A $100K franchise paying back in 3 years is mediocre; $300K back in year one is enticing (3fsJFUvA6Ts). Active investments of your time should target 300%+/yr, not ~10% (QGcjweehrvU).
- "Get in the right boat": sell things people structurally don't cancel (alarm systems, insurance, payment processing, cell service), not gym memberships (41EvCgwPrDc).
- Raising capital to grab share only makes sense with real network effects (3fsJFUvA6Ts). Raise only when the thing can't be profitable without OPM, i.e. years of planned losses (sL16tsGafcQ).
- Franchising is the "most expensive form of equity" usually chosen because founders are in a rush (BO_59sGxztY).

### Scaling a service: pick one vehicle (O1As2zxy0es)
- **Private chain** if a new location costs < ~6 months of a location's profit (e.g. <$40–50K build-out vs ~$400K/yr profit) and the service needs little specialized skill.
- **Franchise** if build-out is very expensive. Needs 100+ locations to make real money; 90–95% of franchises never pass 100.
- **Licensing** if you're cash-poor, need cash flow now, and the IP is easy to copy anyway. Accept a smaller exit; you need roughly 80% yearly licensee retention for a ~6× multiple. (The "plus" after 80 is garbled in the captions.)
- **Software / tech-enabled** only if you hit ≥80% yearly retention and have an in-house technical co-founder. Never outsource the build.

### Info vs traditional (Rm4zRdLAyjw)
- Goal is $1–3M/yr of income and you're willing to keep working → info/education is the fastest path.
- Goal is a $10M+ exit or an asset that runs without you → traditional business. Accept slower growth and reinvesting cash.
- Already at a $3–4M/yr traditional business → stay; the best returns come at the end.
- Whichever way you switch, cut or sell the old one completely. A side business taking 10% of income takes 50–100% of mental bandwidth in a crisis.

### Bolting on software
- Info or service businesses adding software for stickiness or a tech multiple are making a "terrible idea." Above ~10% *annual* churn, "you're just a service business." If users cancel the software when they cancel your service, it's just service (pw1PcHfYP2w). Caption garbled at the threshold; ~10% is [inference] from context.
- Consumer software should keep >60% of logos by year-end, ideally >100% revenue retention (qsXxckCbci0).

### When to quit the job
- Don't quit until side income exceeds the job, or is very reasonably about to (A9qHKjFPJ-E). A stricter version: it must **match your income and hold for 6 months** to rule out seasonality. If 40 hrs/wk on it doesn't out-earn your job, you're not doing it right (Nh8Oc7ERdIU).
- Go only with reasonable savings, a tested vehicle, and known people succeeding at that specific thing. Expect 3–5 years of lower income. SBA medians he cites: $49K/yr owner income (oLsWhSRypzM).
- Pursue an opportunity only if you still win when none of the expected good things happen (A9qHKjFPJ-E).
- Under 30 with no responsibilities: "no reason not to go 100% all in" (ub1D6RQocRU). If underwater on bills, get your "oxygen mask" on first (XGm2ERU9qtA).

### Partners
- Start with no partner. Take one only for money, skills or time you lack. Two promoters who like the same things means one is unnecessary. Every business needs acquisition, product and operations (yJfn_pWzFqg, oZ-H_TjSzok).
- Software needs a technical co-founder. Make the key expert a long-term incentivized partner, not a consultant (U_s0ekwPK5g).

## Playbooks

### A. Broke, starting from zero (≈ first $100K)
1. **Cut costs to the floor.** Everything beyond food and shelter is profit to bank (jfW6gL6hKhk). He lived on ~$1,200/mo and saved ~$50K in 2 years = 2–3 years of runway (HsQeQM1jUeg).
2. **Pick what to sell in one sentence** (nIk3DedjxJM): What (3 Ps) → Who (avatar with 3+ descriptors) → How it delivers the good stuff → how it removes the bad stuff → combine. Pick your "best bad idea" fast; picking starts the feedback loop. Skip the unique mechanism until you have reps.
3. **Start = bank account + LLC + decide what to sell.** Promotion and selling come first (VgEvIIpDYG8).
4. **Warm outreach for first 5 customers:** 100 personal messages/day or ~4 hrs/day. Ask for referrals rather than a sale. Give at most 5 a free case study in exchange for usage, feedback and a review. Start charging once referrals arrive. At capacity, free clients pay full rate or leave a review (w7g08dVTwaE, nIk3DedjxJM, CaiLcj6tzBQ).
5. **Validation = a stranger handing you money.** Expect ~1% conversion on any cold activity; a beginner may need 3× as many reach-outs (u4kgZ3pf8cU).
6. **Do the math backward:** annual goal → weekly revenue → ÷ LTV = units → close rate → show rate → daily outreach. "Fuzzy targets don't get hit" (BSwJQsogah0).
7. **When something works, scale it, don't switch.** $1K/week on 4 hrs/week → 40 hrs could mean ~$10K/week (HsQeQM1jUeg).

### B. Choosing between several ideas
1. Strike any idea with **idea risk** (new need, unproven demand) (A9qHKjFPJ-E).
2. Strike any in a **shrinking or can't-pay market** (F3NyhOtRpOE, AN2KpRBsmRY).
3. Score each idea on the five advantages (Sticky, Expensive, Expansion, Air, Unique) and on LTGP:CAC potential (3fsJFUvA6Ts, QGcjweehrvU).
4. Prefer the one where you have **experience** and where you see people making money. It should be something you're "at least not repelled from" (VgEvIIpDYG8).
5. If even, **follow the money** (6uhd-FiCggg) and **the simpler option** (Munger "too hard" pile, -NLqtk4F4oY; "beginners: simplest model, fewest moving parts," IFpHasZ7jN4).
6. Commit. "Either of the choices will work if you stick with it and neither of them will work if you don't pick" (rhVxX5_8xUw). "Commitment is the elimination of alternatives" (6uhd-FiCggg).

### C. Starting a service you'll later scale
1. Sell your own capped 1-on-1 time first: 100% margin, fast learning, and cash flow (uWdIgftpvBI). Price it so it's worth your time.
2. Package top-down: done-for-you → done-with-you → do-it-yourself (H_ZLs1-jOKQ). Use the Delivery Cube (live/1:1 = more value, less scale; recorded/DIY = more scale) (LVM89ik-7Kw).
3. Go semi-private (small group) to multiply hourly yield. Tutoring math: 1:1 at $60/hr vs 1:8 at $35/student = $280/hr, ~4.5× (CGSd00h-6zI).
4. Hold 80%+ gross margin. Replace yourself with staff and keep the spread (CGSd00h-6zI).
5. Once proven, pick one scaling vehicle: chain, franchise, licensing or tech (O1As2zxy0es).

### D. Opening a brick-and-mortar location
1. Pre-sell for ~3 months. Spend disproportionately on pre-launch (e.g. $30–40K instead of $10K) to open at full capacity and be in the black month one (aBPWCdaJJqA, QGcjweehrvU, lXRqOG2yn6E).
2. Use a cash-up-front, risk-reversed offer plus upsells, so customer cash funds the buildout (lXRqOG2yn6E, unshZobTt6Q).
3. Buy equipment from foreclosures and liquidations at 5–10¢ on the dollar (xZ8d9g6BcKM).
4. Don't open location #2 until #1 has grown without you for 6 straight months. Test it with a 1-month vacation (unshZobTt6Q).

### E. Employee or student weighing the jump
1. Try the lower-risk options first: switch company, switch role, ask how to add more value (oLsWhSRypzM).
2. Don't jump industries. Half-pivot within your industry, or switch function (Nh8Oc7ERdIU).
3. Learn sales first ("if no one buys it, it's zero") (Nh8Oc7ERdIU). Prefer the job with the best mentors, even at lower pay (-wnnwCqGeNc).
4. Build the side hustle to the quit threshold above, then go.

## Anti-patterns

- **Chasing passion.** "Follow your passion" gets cause and effect backwards (6uhd-FiCggg, _KlZoPxbStk, UDBkiBnMrHs).
- **Teaching or serving a niche you've never operated in** (agency or coaching for X without having done X). The result is poor client results, churn and no testimonials (VgEvIIpDYG8).
- **Big-or-go-home idea risk** when you don't have VC-style unlimited spins. His software friend "has gone home more than he's gone big" (A9qHKjFPJ-E).
- **Self-funded software as a non-technical founder.** It's the worst way to "make money while I sleep" (8C_6qojTA78, pw1PcHfYP2w, O1As2zxy0es).
- **Competing on price without a day-one cost strategy.** "There's no advantage to being the second cheapest player" (Lc8DNduiwKA, s7QA1TJKlbQ, 4GjwtnA76ig).
- **Running several businesses at once / niche-hopping** under seven figures. All could work; none will if you pursue them all (qxQIcDrre1E, 0_Gf5v8DEMY, GDqmlwY_uj4).
- **Straddling** an old and a new business during a switch. "I've yet to see anyone succeed big" (Rm4zRdLAyjw).
- **Debt or OPM on a first business.** Quoting Buffett: "if you're smart you don't need it, if you're dumb you shouldn't use it" (u4kgZ3pf8cU).
- **Partners with the same skills**, or equal splits that don't reflect risk. He had 9 failed partnerships (yJfn_pWzFqg, unshZobTt6Q).
- **One channel controlled by another business** (Groupon) (unshZobTt6Q).
- **Selling small customers** whose churn is structural (they go out of business), unless your cost to serve is near zero (thDTmy7VGIw).
- **Doing the done-for-you model without controlling fulfillment.** This caused his 35% refund rate (Xb9ZOA6zypU).
- **Expecting results on a 90-day horizon.** About a third of the questions he gets come from too short a horizon; expect ~5 years (VgEvIIpDYG8).

## Worked examples

- **Alex's first gym:** ~$5 leads, 1 in 4 closed on a $600 risk-reversed 6-week challenge, so ~$20–25 CAC against $600 up front plus supplements. Ads ran at roughly 20–30:1 in launches (his ballpark). Customer cash funded each buildout, so he "could outspend everybody in my market" (lXRqOG2yn6E).
- **Semi-private tutoring:** ~50 students at 3×/week at $35/session. 4 teachers × $280/hr × 4 hrs ≈ $4,400/day minus ~$800 teacher pay ≈ $3,600/day, vs ~$3,300/day needed for $1M/yr. Building to ~$1,000–1,100/day could take ~6 months (CGSd00h-6zI).
- **Niche RD (registered dietitian):** 5,800 Instagram followers, ~19 likes/post, just under $1M/yr take-home teaching other RDs insurance billing. "Business metrics don't follow vanity metrics" (0S5xsICW8qg).
- **Gym Launch's vehicle switch:** the same skills repackaged from done-for-you into licensing grew from ~$100K/mo to $2M+/mo, with $17M profit the next year (Xb9ZOA6zypU).
- **Unspeakable** (guest YouTuber, ~200M monthly viewers): Alex advised against launching cash-eating CPG and a parts marketplace. Instead: double down on the media cash cow, build a co-host bench, and take product deals for equity (4pZwlE86A5Y).
- **Fro-yo teardown:** pay-by-weight, bigger default cups, high-margin items first, and failed competitors' equipment for ≤$20K. At low ticket, word of mouth or affiliates are the only profitable acquisition. "To beat everybody you've got to be better than the bottom 25%" (xZ8d9g6BcKM).

## Metrics to watch

- **7 days:** reach-outs per day (target 100), conversations, free case-study takers (≤5), hours on promotion (target ~4/day) (w7g08dVTwaE, 0_Gf5v8DEMY).
- **30 days:** first paid customers. Gross margin per customer (≥80% for service). CAC vs first-30-day gross profit (payback ≤30 days). Referral count (start charging once referrals arrive) (aOq1Hdz0w3k, i7bLRKwKSms, w7g08dVTwaE).
- **90 days:** LTGP:CAC (≥3:1), monthly churn, testimonials and reviews collected, side income vs job income (quit only once it matches and holds 6 months), runway months left (QGcjweehrvU, Nh8Oc7ERdIU).
- Average churn and LTV over a trailing 90 days / 6 / 12 months; daily data is too noisy for small businesses (QGcjweehrvU).
