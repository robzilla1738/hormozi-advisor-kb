# What should I charge, and when should I raise (or never lower) my price?

**Bottom line:** Price is the biggest profit lever: a raise drops almost entirely to profit, and on thin margins a small raise can multiply profit (Gy-RmpRif-I, XwZH-lOKG9c, 41EvCgwPrDc). Set price from the value the market will pay, never from your costs or your own wallet. Raise it step by step until **conversion × price** (total profit) starts falling, then step back (reisEL_D7xc, uaLNfijnp-8, 41EvCgwPrDc). Almost never cut price to win deals. Change terms, features or value instead (StVqS0jD7Ls, BZQtuK-ucDM).

## Diagnose first

**Collect these numbers before you change a price:**
1. Current price, **gross margin** (delivery cost ÷ price) and net margin.
2. **Close rate** on qualified calls or checkout conversion, with a baseline sample size (oRMG_HpOAN4).
3. Capacity: are you **supply-constrained** (full, waitlist, founder-bottlenecked) or **demand-constrained** (empty calendar)? (sGv2BTUCcCM, jqo0lVveh98)
4. LTGP per customer, CAC, and 30-day cash per customer (Cr0KdqZ954c, QGcjweehrvU).
5. Who complains about price: your **ideal** customers or non-ideal ones? (-j8_YCWZ05Q)
6. When you last raised prices, and whether you have kept up with inflation (BMF2fWHyDrg, uaLNfijnp-8).
7. Churn: do customers stay through their *worst* month at this price? (41EvCgwPrDc, U_s0ekwPK5g)

**How the answer branches:**

| Situation | What the videos point to |
|---|---|
| **Pre-revenue / first customers** | Free case studies for the first ~5–20 (w7g08dVTwaE). Start charging once people refer. Then raise about 20% every ~5 sales until conversion × price falls (reisEL_D7xc). Sell premium first, because low LTV makes the math brutal (BSwJQsogah0, KhFlD54nQrY). |
| **< $1M, service** | Usually underpriced. Aim for 80%+ gross margin and price at 5–10× delivery cost (mRlSb0O5QNU, 6Fg4VXjRphQ). Sell one core offer and fit budgets with payment terms, not tiers (EZLQzR-_83A). Your own capped 1-on-1 at 10× price is an easy premium (uWdIgftpvBI). |
| **< $3–5M (any model)** | "Charging more is often among the biggest levers" (OQf2Ba-Lp_4). Fix LTV/price before adding ad platforms (6m6DCQMASEM). |
| **$1–10M** | Add one high-value back end or tier at 5–10× (ZuJryiwxjDw, neTSqOAMgao). Raise 3–6% a year at minimum (oRMG_HpOAN4) and review price quarterly (TooAB8Ow6cQ). |
| **$10M+ / mass market** | Margins compress as you scale (40%→30%→10%) (EUW3rMp-Uvg). Pricing power then comes from brand and word of mouth (VPre_XMgKjs, 9unucIBuNio). Only compete on price with a real day-one cost advantage (4GjwtnA76ig, Lc8DNduiwKA). |
| **Info / education** | Payment plans are not continuity (FiL0r5_dUvM). Price the one-time thing to match its value, and make every feature justify 3–4× the price or cut it (FiL0r5_dUvM). |
| **Subscription / recurring** | Price for what they'll keep paying in their worst month, not the most they'll say yes to today (41EvCgwPrDc, U_s0ekwPK5g). Add annual or prepay options (41EvCgwPrDc, jOM0m34dxz4). |
| **Physical product / e-com** | Price power comes from out-checking competitors on every variable and from brand (NA61omfYgvI, VPre_XMgKjs). Prefer custom over white label to protect pricing (Jmkq5RLjm0U). |

## Core principles

1. **Price is the #1 profit lever.** "Pricing's everything. It's the number one lever on how much money you make." (XwZH-lOKG9c) He calls pricing "one of the most underutilized levers in the entirety of business" (Q2VUuUGfpNY) and "the single largest lever on profit" (k-3PoOT4vOM).
2. **Price from market value, never cost.** "The price that you charge should never be in any way related to what it costs you and only related to what the market will allow." (y4VoOKa1ywQ) Also see VaE4pcf9PI8, 9xAMe0QBFhU.
3. **Don't price from your own wallet.** What's easy or cheap for you isn't for others (-Pz0NpcILOY, F84olnKkseM, ZuJryiwxjDw).
4. **Price is a math problem, not an opinion.** "This is a math problem, not an 'Alex, what should I price it at' problem." (41EvCgwPrDc) Also see rc7cxL7ql7Y.
5. **Raise value, not just price, and keep value far above price.** "I'm all about raising prices but I'm all about being 10 times more valuable than my price." (PArqypiHcyQ) Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice) (_qspvJAq34M). Create customer surplus by raising value, not by cutting price (3P1XjUvo1b4).
6. **Never be the second-cheapest.** "There's no advantage to being the second cheapest player in the marketplace" (Dan Kennedy, quoted in Lc8DNduiwKA). Alex adds that there *is* an advantage to being the most expensive (ONV__y1z7MI). He cites McKinsey: 85% compete on price and 1% win (k-3PoOT4vOM).
7. **Sell very expensive to a few or very cheap to everyone.** "The middle is where people die." (uWdIgftpvBI) Human-delivered services should go upmarket (spXH1kJ6q-Q).
8. **Price for the top 20%,** the customers who get the most value and have the money (CoPs-Bk8M9Y, ZuJryiwxjDw, cnbHgYYHzyE). "When you solve rich people problems you get to charge rich people prices." (ONV__y1z7MI)
9. **Category of one beats comparison.** Price so differently that buyers think "something entirely different must be going on" (F3NyhOtRpOE, ZuJryiwxjDw). "I'd rather just be in the value surpassing game." (zzleYxkf39k)
10. **Premium price improves the customer.** It buys commitment and results: "If you want to help someone, you have to charge them enough that it would hurt." (4GjwtnA76ig) Also see ONV__y1z7MI.
11. **Terms over price.** "A lot of people are always way too fixated on the price and not enough on the terms." (Jc2UW3nlNBA) Fit budgets with payment options, not cheaper products (EZLQzR-_83A).
12. **Brand = pricing power.** "You can measure the strength of a brand based on how much of a premium you can charge on a commoditized product." (VPre_XMgKjs) Buffett test: if a 10% raise needs a prayer, it's a bad business (4Yz8ggEv0NU, VQM3DrnVTcs).

## Decision rules & thresholds

### Close rate tells you if you're underpriced
- **~80%+ close** usually means underpriced. "If you're closing at 80% or more… you're typically underpriced by 3 to 4x." (A_tx40lNpf8) Also: ~90% → raise aggressively (eq1bnI0JoXU); 85–90% → raise (uaLNfijnp-8); >~80% → room on price (OQf2Ba-Lp_4); "very high close rate" = underpriced (3yAiVjcImQ4).
- **~71%** suggests the market will pay more (sBJppqCeFGI).
- **Healthy band ≈ 30–40%** with a proper process (sBJppqCeFGI). His personal-sales rule of thumb is 35%: above it raise price, below it fix the process first (A_tx40lNpf8).
- Benchmarks vary by model. About 40% for a two-call mid-priced consumer service, 80%+ for in-person low ticket, about 5% for investments sold over the phone (JE2_7elAcxM).
- **Gasp test:** "If you didn't get a gasp from a price tag, you didn't go high enough." (NcD2t9qt-fM)

### When to stop raising
- Stop when **conversion × price** goes down (uaLNfijnp-8, reisEL_D7xc, 41EvCgwPrDc). "I just keep edging up the price until people stop saying yes." (nrounb8NlFQ) Then step back one notch or add a bonus (QTZsh3BgOwY).
- Doubling price while close rate falls from 30% to 20% is "a great deal" (QTZsh3BgOwY, oRMG_HpOAN4). [inference: revenue per call goes from 0.30P to 0.40P, +33%.]
- Get a statistically meaningful sample first. At a 40% baseline close, 2 early no's are expected (oRMG_HpOAN4). Hold a test price for the committed number of sales (AN2KpRBsmRY).
- **Raise cadence:** after your first 10–20 case-study customers, raise about 20% every 5 sales ($0 → $1,000 → $1,200 → $1,500) (reisEL_D7xc). Raise at least 3–6% a year (oRMG_HpOAN4). Firms that review price quarterly are more profitable than those that review annually or never (ProfitWell, cited in TooAB8Ow6cQ).
- Older businesses that haven't raised 50%+ through recent inflation are leaving money on the table (BMF2fWHyDrg). A first raise of 15–20% after 3–4 flat years is only "right-sizing" (uaLNfijnp-8).

### Margin and markup floors
- **Service gross margin ≥ 80%.** He won't invest below that (A_tx40lNpf8, 9xAMe0QBFhU, aOq1Hdz0w3k, 6Fg4VXjRphQ, CGSd00h-6zI, mOKl6dodMTg). Buffett looks for about 99% (VaE4pcf9PI8).
- **Price ≥ 5× delivery cost; 5–10× is the target** (mRlSb0O5QNU, 6Fg4VXjRphQ). For example, $100/hr lawyer cost on a $5,800 sale means delivery must take under ~11 hours (6Fg4VXjRphQ).
- Markup ladder on $100 cost: 70% GM = $333, 80% = $500, 90% = $1,000 (A_tx40lNpf8). A $60 service with $20 cost is 66% GM; fix it by cutting cost to $12 or raising price to $100 (9xAMe0QBFhU).
- $1,000/mo service → delivery < $200. At a 50% net target, the other 30% funds marketing, admin and the rest (aOq1Hdz0w3k).
- "Your net margins cannot exceed your gross margins." (A_tx40lNpf8)

### Profit math of a raise
- At 20% net margin and $100/mo: +20% price → 2× profit; +50% → 3.5×; 2× price → 6× profit. At 25% margin, +25% doubles profit (Gy-RmpRif-I).
- Gyms (avg $129/mo, 12.5% net): +$38/mo ≈ triples profit (XwZH-lOKG9c; the note's [inference] check gives ~3.4×).
- +10% basically doubles profit (4Yz8ggEv0NU). [inference: that holds only near a ~10% net margin.]
- Teardown: +10% at an 82% close rate pays as long as close stays above ~65%, for about +25% net profit (OQf2Ba-Lp_4).
- **Before any cut,** work out the extra volume needed to replace the lost margin per unit (often 2×) (BZQtuK-ucDM).
- Expect some customers to leave. He lost a third on a raise, and that was fine because the rest paid more (BZQtuK-ucDM). Double price and lose 30% of clients and you make more money serving fewer people (sGv2BTUCcCM).

### Constraint-based rules
- **Supply-constrained / full and unprofitable → raise price** before adding locations or products (F84olnKkseM, h6y0nYVZgwE, cnbHgYYHzyE). Raise "by a lot": 50–100% (sGv2BTUCcCM). Use the extra margin to fund 2–3 backups per role (0EqJD2o-Mnk). "Selling 3 at 2× beats 5 at half." (cnbHgYYHzyE)
- **Demand-constrained** can also be fixed by price when you can't afford to acquire customers (sGv2BTUCcCM). If you can't afford ~$100–200 to get a service customer, that's a monetization problem, not a marketing one (-UvmyixFszw).
- **Cut supply → raise price** to the new point on the demand curve (iFhiK5zORGk). Scarcity drives price (QQGHCG8d1So).
- Raise even without a justification unless you have a demand or conversion problem (6m6DCQMASEM).

### Discounts, prepay, and terms
- **Never change price to close a deal.** Change terms (payment plan) or features (downsell) (StVqS0jD7Ls, AN2KpRBsmRY). "It's either full price or it's free." (h9UyQiLjSHg, HsQeQM1jUeg)
- **Never lower price without getting something in exchange** (gza5RtQCVsA).
- No percentage-off discounts. The exceptions are prepay and close-now incentives (h9UyQiLjSHg). Discounted deals must be paid in full up front (mHrAjWni65E).
- **Prepay discount:** at least 10% (A_tx40lNpf8); 10–20%, offering the highest discount first and stepping down (1UhvBSQFy6A); about 5% baked into quarterly prepay (k-3PoOT4vOM). Call it a "prepayment discount," not "paid in full" (1UhvBSQFy6A).
- Prepay uptake: buy-10-get-2 → ~15–20% take it; moderate discount plus a bonus → ~30–40% (A_tx40lNpf8). Monthly plus annual at ~16% off is a typical setup; annual LTV can be ~5× (41EvCgwPrDc).
- **Financing:** third-party financing raised overall sales ~35% (A_tx40lNpf8). Push financing/upfront payment from 30–50% of patients to 80–90% (uaLNfijnp-8). "Financing will not save your business." (A_tx40lNpf8)
- More payment options → higher conversion, but reps may default to the lower-cash options (EZLQzR-_83A).
- Layaway: let them set the plan, but work starts once it's paid off (rMJIOK_FgJk).

### Tiers, upsells and anchors
- **Upsell ≈ 5× core price.** If ~20% take it, the average ticket doubles (JE2_7elAcxM, 1UhvBSQFy6A). Each new tier at 5–10×, expecting ≤20% to take it (ZuJryiwxjDw). Customers are "fractal 80/20" (nSQdjim8CsE).
- List a **10× (or 100×) tier** everywhere you sell (uWdIgftpvBI). An anchor at ~10× core ($500 → $5,000/mo) lifts takes on the anchor *and* the old top package (ovL6Z5z0jxQ).
- Down-sells at 1/10 price taken by 10% move revenue only ~1%. Give that product away free to attract better customers instead (reisEL_D7xc).
- **Tier on evidence:** if *ideal* customers say "too expensive", add a lower tier. If non-ideal customers say it, ignore it, because the price is filtering (-j8_YCWZ05Q). Skool added a $9 tier next to $99.
- Ethical price discrimination: add a small perk so higher payers really get something different (Lc8DNduiwKA). Same resources can carry different expectations, e.g. VIP support in 60 minutes vs 24 hours (t9Vv7rK-4Jc).
- Surcharges: same-day at +10% → 20% and keep raising, with VIP pushed until people say no (zzleYxkf39k). Speed incentives add margin: "Speed's all margin." (o64cI6tebnU)

### Recurring-specific
- Price for the client's **worst month**, not their best. SMB churn follows their cash flow (U_s0ekwPK5g, 41EvCgwPrDc). Alex notes he no longer preaches charge as much as possible for recurring offers (41EvCgwPrDc).
- He'd take $5K/yr upfront over $1K/mo at the same close rate. Monthly payers cancel around months 4–5 (~$4–5K), while $5K/yr at 2% monthly churn ≈ $20.8K LTV (41EvCgwPrDc).
- Package monthly as a fixed term ($12K as 6 × $2K): +1–2 months of retention, 2–3× LTGP (jOM0m34dxz4).

### Price reveal
- Reveal price early. It costs more per call but improves ROAS, because the people it shocks wouldn't close anyway (4GjwtnA76ig).
- **Disagreement:** a luxury styling teardown says *don't* show price on the page; qualification plus a description "give us 80%" (nrounb8NlFQ). [inference: reconcile it by revealing price before the call (VSL or setter), not necessarily on the landing page.] Handle objections *before* presenting price (1UhvBSQFy6A).

## Playbooks

### A. Raise prices on existing customers (Gy-RmpRif-I)
1. Do the profit math at your current margin (see table above) and commit. Don't chicken out.
2. Write a letter that: re-sells the value they already get → previews coming improvements → ties the increase to a values-based promise → frames the cost as small → ends with a perspective P.S.
3. Expect complaints. Any price change triggers them, even a cut (tdLLGKoBojo). Watch behaviour, not words (0EqJD2o-Mnk).
4. Optional: let the team accept a smaller raise if needed. You must be sold on it, and he accepted 10% instead of 20% (-Koq14DPXC4). Don't stack every change at once if it's uncomfortable (h9UyQiLjSHg).
5. At capacity with free or legacy clients: they must match the full-price rate to continue, or leave a review (w7g08dVTwaE).

### B. Find the right price for a new offer (reisEL_D7xc, QTZsh3BgOwY, 41EvCgwPrDc)
1. Start at free or cheap for case studies (first 5–20). Start charging once people refer (w7g08dVTwaE).
2. Raise ~20% every 5 sales. Track **revenue per sales call**, not close rate alone.
3. Test from low to high. Raising is easy to explain; cutting means downgrading existing customers (41EvCgwPrDc).
4. Stop when conversion × price falls. Step back one notch, and refund the difference or bonus anyone who paid more (41EvCgwPrDc).
5. Go higher than feels comfortable. If there's no gasp, go higher and walk down using the anchor (NcD2t9qt-fM).
6. Raise again as proof grows (his price at 100 testimonials was lower than at 2,000) (F3NyhOtRpOE).

### C. Prospect says it's too expensive (StVqS0jD7Ls, gza5RtQCVsA, EZLQzR-_83A)
1. Don't touch the price. "Nothing is ever too expensive, it's just not worth it." (StVqS0jD7Ls)
2. Offer terms: payment plan, financing, layaway (EZLQzR-_83A, rMJIOK_FgJk).
3. Then a prepared down-sell ladder where each lower price asks for more from them (less support, longer commitment, a testimonial) (gza5RtQCVsA).
4. If you lose deals to price a lot, fix the offer and sales process, not leads. "Customers will always go to price if you don't give them any other variables." (zzleYxkf39k)

### D. Fix a broken margin (9xAMe0QBFhU, mRlSb0O5QNU, mOKl6dodMTg)
1. Compute gross margin per offer. Kill offers that are net-negative or trivially profitable (BYpTRiRqS1Y).
2. Below 80%: raise price to 5–10× delivery cost, or cut delivery cost.
3. Mispriced add-ons: price at ~5× or kill them (a $150/mo 1:1 add-on → think $8–10K) (8VqSFBMMS4M).
4. Match price to the service actually delivered. For example, gym 1-on-8 → $300–400/mo, not $100 unlimited (mOKl6dodMTg).

### E. Add a premium tier (uWdIgftpvBI, ZuJryiwxjDw, ovL6Z5z0jxQ)
1. Create a capped 10× option, e.g. your own 1-on-1 time at 5–10% of your hours.
2. Show it everywhere and say it out loud ("It's 10 grand an hour" (uWdIgftpvBI)), then shut up. If they balk, offer the scalable version with "90% of the elements." (uWdIgftpvBI)
3. Make it something you'd happily deliver at that price: "either do less or charge more." (ZuJryiwxjDw)

## Anti-patterns
- **Lowering price** to fix churn or volume. A 25% cut to reduce churn left churn unchanged and cost ~$5M/yr (BZQtuK-ucDM). "Most times when you lower your prices, you make a lot less money."
- **Cheapness as a reaction.** If cheap is your strategy, it must be built in from day one (s7QA1TJKlbQ, 4GjwtnA76ig).
- **Pricing at industry standard or the market average** when "everyone else is broke" (Q2VUuUGfpNY, aUnorufFIq8). Copying funded penetration pricing (Q2VUuUGfpNY).
- **"A little more for a little less"** than competitors (F3NyhOtRpOE).
- **Negotiating on price** mid-call (StVqS0jD7Ls, HsQeQM1jUeg). Discounting without an exchange (gza5RtQCVsA).
- **Hourly pricing** that commoditizes you (zzleYxkf39k, 3yAiVjcImQ4). Per-session pricing ties price to time; prefer prepay (CGSd00h-6zI).
- **A sprawling menu** to fit every budget (EZLQzR-_83A). "If your employees can't explain all your products and prices, your customers can't either"
- **A premium price on an undialed product.** Brand earns the first sale and the product earns the repeat (VQM3DrnVTcs, Jmkq5RLjm0U).
- **Charging for a first "consultation" call** (3yAiVjcImQ4).
- **Selling mid-priced, high-touch service to tiny businesses**, which brings structural churn and price pressure (CoPs-Bk8M9Y).
- **Feeling bad about low COGS** (VaE4pcf9PI8, 9xAMe0QBFhU). "You got to get over this mental barrier around what you're able to charge for things that don't cost you a lot of money."
- **Consumer contracts as a retention tool.** They mostly cut sales without cutting cancellations (41EvCgwPrDc).

## Worked examples
- **Gym triple:** tripling gym prices with more value doubled revenue and more than doubled profit (BZQtuK-ucDM).
- **HVAC duct cleaning ($1.25M, 38% net, 82% close):** +10% to ~$1,650/unit ≈ +$100K/yr profit if close stays >65% (OQf2Ba-Lp_4).
- **Dental practice:** 85–90% close → raise $28K → $35K, and push financing to 80–90% of patients (uaLNfijnp-8).
- **Med spa:** Botox GP $58 + 40% upsell × $1,300 GP ≈ $578 per customer vs $202 CAC. The lever is upsell size and leading with the high-ticket item, not cheaper Botox (rc7cxL7ql7Y).
- **Legal service:** $5,800 sale → delivery ≤ ~$1,100–1,200. Kill the money-losing software unit (6Fg4VXjRphQ).
- **Moving company:** drop hourly framing, add a same-day surcharge (+10→20%), VIP $165 → $225/hr (zzleYxkf39k).
- **Portfolio high-ticket test:** $50K/35K/25K/15K brought the same revenue. Alex preferred the lower price for more customers, word of mouth and value gap, *as long as margins hold* (TooAB8Ow6cQ).

## Metrics to watch
- **7 days:** close rate per price point and revenue per sales call (reisEL_D7xc). Gasp or no-gasp reactions. Price objections tagged ideal vs non-ideal (-j8_YCWZ05Q).
- **30 days:** gross margin per offer (≥80% for services). 30-day cash vs CAC (≥1:1; growth target 30-day GP ≥ 2×(CAC+COGS)) (Cr0KdqZ954c, XwZH-lOKG9c). Prepay/annual take rate. Upsell take rate (~20% target).
- **90 days:** LTGP:CAC (≥3:1) (QGcjweehrvU). Churn of customers on the new price vs the old cohort (41EvCgwPrDc). Net profit change vs the pre-raise forecast. Next scheduled price review (quarterly) (TooAB8Ow6cQ).
