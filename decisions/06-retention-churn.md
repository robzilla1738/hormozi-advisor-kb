# How do I keep customers longer, and when is churn my real problem?

**Bottom line:** Fortunes come from selling things people don't stop buying. Retention is the strongest advantage a business can have and the "gold standard" metric (3P1XjUvo1b4, 3fsJFUvA6Ts, uYds0zcAFWM). Use the hypothetical-max math (inflow ÷ churn) to see whether churn or acquisition is your cap. If churn is high, fix onboarding, expectations, avatar and product *before* pouring in more leads. Most churn is structural (wrong customer) or self-inflicted (overwhelm, bad onboarding), not flaky customers (XZEjp2mP1U0, afbP6sB_Atc, thDTmy7VGIw).

## Diagnose first

**Ask these four, in this order:** "What's your inflow? What's your churn? What's your average price per user? It's the first thing I'm asking." (XZEjp2mP1U0)
- **Hypothetical max customers = new customers per month ÷ monthly churn %** (XZEjp2mP1U0, f_ceDZqhKbw, QGcjweehrvU). [inference] 20 new/month at 10% churn caps you at 200 customers; at 5% churn, 400.
- If current revenue is above the cap, you're shrinking. Below it, you're growing. Only inflow, price or churn moves the cap (XZEjp2mP1U0).
- Average churn over trailing 90 days, 6 months and 12 months. Daily numbers are too noisy (QGcjweehrvU).
- Measure retention on the **original cohort**: 100 customers on Jan 1 → how many of *those* remain a year later (A_tx40lNpf8). The metric he cares about most is **M12**, the share who reach month 12 (FiL0r5_dUvM). The #1 investor metric is how many of last year's customers still pay you (TooAB8Ow6cQ).
- Then sort where churn comes from:
  1. **Structural.** Customers go out of business or have volatile income (tiny businesses, beginners). No product fix solves it, so change who you serve or the price (thDTmy7VGIw, CoPs-Bk8M9Y).
  2. **Onboarding / expectations.** Churn spikes in the first 30–90 days (afbP6sB_Atc, 3fsJFUvA6Ts).
  3. **Overwhelm / too much stuff.** "Overwhelm is the main cause of churn." (kloJJeiysxg, TooAB8Ow6cQ)
  4. **Product isn't good enough.** No referrals, no testimonials, poor results (TNQyFxVbkBs, VgEvIIpDYG8, HsQeQM1jUeg).
  5. **Billing frequency.** "Churn is directly correlated with frequency of billing." (41EvCgwPrDc)
- **CAC litmus:** CAC should rise only with CPMs (~15%/yr). If it doubles or triples fast, suspect anti-referrals from unhappy customers (afbP6sB_Atc).

**How the answer branches:**
- **Cash crisis (payroll within ~30 days):** don't lead with retention systems, which take ~12 weeks to pay off. Do the cash plays first, then use the cash to fix retention (SvIcS-Q1Hl4).
- **< $1M:** sell to everyone early if you must, but the sticking point usually arrives at ~$1M (sometimes $3M), when you have to start saying no to non-ideal customers (F84olnKkseM).
- **$1–5M:** pause scaling and build customer success, with a CS lead tracking time-to-value, NPS and health scores, all aimed at referrals (EUW3rMp-Uvg).
- **$3–10M:** cutting churn is one of the main LTGP levers (neTSqOAMgao).
- **Want an exit?** Build revenue retention. If you only want cash, run the proven playbook more times (--9kOXNCUdE, BMF2fWHyDrg).

## Core principles

1. **"The way to create a fortune in business is to only sell things that people do not stop buying."** (3P1XjUvo1b4) "If I could do it all over again, I would only focus exclusively on things that people already don't cancel." (41EvCgwPrDc)
2. **Stickiness is the #1 advantage.** If you could have only one advantage, pick it (3fsJFUvA6Ts). "The gold standard should absolutely be revenue retention, bar none." (uYds0zcAFWM)
3. **Retention comes from customer surplus,** meaning value far beyond the price. Raise value rather than cutting price (3P1XjUvo1b4, PArqypiHcyQ). A 25% price cut to reduce churn left churn unchanged and cost ~$5M/yr (BZQtuK-ucDM).
4. **Churn is a signal, not bad luck.** Churn means the market is telling you you're no longer in the customer's best interest. Treat every relationship as week-to-week (PArqypiHcyQ). "Churn is a result." (yPDQCfrwh8E)
5. **Don't pour leads into a leaky bucket.** "If you can't fix churn, there's no point in doing any of this… why would we pour more into a leaky bucket?" (n6SHLmKcY0E) Also see LGq0uVspNms, f_ceDZqhKbw, K8MFC9t7snY.
6. **Low consumption is your fault, not the customer's.** If onboarding is too hard, fix consumption and onboarding rather than marketing harder (K8MFC9t7snY).
7. **Subtract, don't add.** "Doing less can often retain more." (-j8_YCWZ05Q) "You can never promote too much; you can put too much inside the community." (kloJJeiysxg, TooAB8Ow6cQ)
8. **Wrong avatar = structural churn.** "When you have the wrong avatar, the volatility of your business is based on the volatility of your customers." (CoPs-Bk8M9Y) "Your CAC will go up, but then your stick problem will get fixed." (BYpTRiRqS1Y)
9. **No silver bullet.** "With retention it's not the bullet, it's all of these things put together." (sPkMHh8zTMI) Also "golden BBs, no silver bullets" (yPDQCfrwh8E).
10. **Retention compounds into acquisition.** A higher LTV at equal CAC wins every ad auction (K8MFC9t7snY). "LTV is the arms race of advertising." (FMzKk73iUhw) Doubling LTV through retention more than doubles profit (sPkMHh8zTMI).
11. **Product quality is the retention engine.** If customers aren't referring, fix the product before scaling promotion (TNQyFxVbkBs, Uki3IUkUu7Q). "Software is not sticky. Really good software is sticky." (pw1PcHfYP2w)
12. **Payment plans are not continuity.** "People in the education space mistake a payment plan for continuity." (FiL0r5_dUvM) "If you want something that's sticky, then make the business about the sticky thing." (FiL0r5_dUvM)

## Decision rules & thresholds

### When to fix churn vs scale acquisition
- If fewer than 7 in 10 stay month to month, retention is your highest-ROI lever (-j8_YCWZ05Q).
- At 15–20% monthly churn, fix churn before acquiring anyone. Stay quiet until the metrics are right, then "blow the doors off" (U_s0ekwPK5g).
- Don't spend on acquisition until retention holds. You want a business that would still be fine for 3–4 months with zero new sales (I64gYLBrics).
- Scale marketing spend only after customers stick and refer (K8MFC9t7snY). Sometimes cap ad spend until referrals exceed ~30% (Uki3IUkUu7Q).
- Low churn + good LTV:CAC → just spend more on ads and make them better (TooAB8Ow6cQ, kloJJeiysxg).
- **Counterpoint:** in high-churn local services ("easy in, easy out," e.g. a 300–500 member gym), the goal is usually capped by **inflow**. Don't wait for conversion or retention improvements before scaling inflow (XC_lklN9KmE). [inference: reconcile it this way. If churn is near your category benchmark, scale inflow. If it's far worse (e.g. 15–20%+ monthly, or new cohorts churning faster than old ones), fix churn first.]
- Churn changes are huge. 5% → 10% halves lifetime revenue, and 10% → 5% doubles it (QGcjweehrvU).
- LTV math: $100/yr at 50% retention ≈ $200 LTV; at 80% ≈ $500, or 2.5× (A_tx40lNpf8). $200/mo at 10% monthly churn ≈ $2,000 (-UvmyixFszw). [inference: LTV ≈ monthly gross profit ÷ monthly churn.]

### Avatar and pricing levers
- If churn comes from customers failing, change who you serve. Don't keep rebuilding offers (thDTmy7VGIw). For low churn, "go upmarket to people who don't churn, or create customers who don't churn" (bgBIO6nZawg).
- Serving small customers requires a near-zero cost to serve, or pricing for their *worst* month. One agency charged $299/mo against a ~$1,500 standard and clients never cancelled (thDTmy7VGIw). The best SMB model he saw was automated SEO/reviews at $300/mo (U_s0ekwPK5g). For SMB agencies: ≤ ~$400/mo with near-zero delivery, or go upmarket. Avoid $1.5–3K/mo SMB retainers (jqo0lVveh98).
- 33%/month churn in very small businesses is 11× worse than 3%; he bets it can get closer to 10% (rc7cxL7ql7Y).
- Concentration: no customer above 20% of revenue, ideally ≤ 5% (HKbFUWJwEG0).
- "Get in the right boat": sell things people structurally don't cancel, like alarms, insurance, payment processing, banking and cell service (41EvCgwPrDc).
- Enterprise isn't inherently more valuable. "It's just stickier." (--9kOXNCUdE)

### Billing structure
- **Add an annual plan today** at ~16–17% off ("buy 10 get 2"). Annual churns less, with one decision instead of 12 (-j8_YCWZ05Q, 41EvCgwPrDc). Annual LTV can be ~5× (41EvCgwPrDc). Sweeten it with VIP perks (41EvCgwPrDc). Quarterly is a middle ground.
- Package monthly as a fixed term ($12K = 6 × $2K, then month-to-month) for +1–2 months of retention and 2–3× LTGP (jOM0m34dxz4).
- Renewal as opt-out: restructure a 12-month program into $2,500 every 6 months, with a "legal right" (billing) plus a "psychological right" (reminder push) (8VqSFBMMS4M).
- Start the monthly payments immediately after the down payment. A gap invites churn (N5MExtki_VI).
- Renewals only ascend. Never renew into a lower tier, sunset the low tier, and keep existing commitments (N5MExtki_VI).
- Don't stretch a $25K course into $2K/mo forever. After the value is delivered they're paying community prices for something worth less (qsXxckCbci0).
- Add a CPI clause to continuity contracts and use it every 2–3 years (41EvCgwPrDc).
- Consumer contracts mostly cut sales without cutting cancellations. They matter for enterprise (41EvCgwPrDc). A "can't come back for a year" cancel penalty had no effect he could see; use a friendly goodbye instead (TooAB8Ow6cQ).
- Proactively downgrade underusing customers. At one portfolio company they became the second-highest-LTV avatar (afbP6sB_Atc).
- Avoid lifetime deals: one-time income, forever delivery (TooAB8Ow6cQ).

### Onboarding and the early window
- If you have no onboarding, add it first (afbP6sB_Atc). If onboarding is rushed, lengthen it, because it multiplies LTV (hnfh2jqNVEQ).
- Focus on the first 30 days, then getting past month 3, then month 6 (3fsJFUvA6Ts). Churn falls sharply with tenure, so design to get people past month 1, day 90 and month 6 (-j8_YCWZ05Q).
- Over-communicate in the first 24–72 hours: where, when, who welcomes them, reconfirm (h6y0nYVZgwE). Onboard the next day to cut cold feet and refunds (8VqSFBMMS4M).
- Automate or group onboarding for low-ticket, high-volume offers; weekly group onboarding works even at ~$100/mo (sPkMHh8zTMI, kloJJeiysxg).
- Offers or communities under 90 days (or 6 months) old show inflated churn. Don't overhaul because of month-1 churn unless nearly everyone leaves (-j8_YCWZ05Q).
- Fix the expectations set in sales. Mismatched promises drive early churn (afbP6sB_Atc).

### Engagement and save tactics
- Place unlocks and recurring bonuses just past average churn. Average stay of 4 months → unlock at month 5 (sPkMHh8zTMI, eX3Ch_HuR70).
- Always announce the next event at or before the current one. Otherwise churn rises after events (sPkMHh8zTMI).
- Track attendance. Dropping from 3×/wk to 2 to 1 means intervene ("I missed you"). Touch base every ~2 weeks (h6y0nYVZgwE).
- Exit interviews can save ~half by moving people to a higher-support tier (h6y0nYVZgwE). Tell customers at signup that cancelling requires an exit interview "so we can make the product better" (sPkMHh8zTMI).
- Ask cancellers why with a DM script, tag the reasons (price, overwhelm, not using, missing feature, not what I expected), and fix the top 1–2 every ~20 cancels (-j8_YCWZ05Q).
- Ask the longest-tenured customers why they stay. That answer is your retention engine (-j8_YCWZ05Q).
- Show value: log in and present their numbers monthly (~2 hrs/account is worth it) (n6SHLmKcY0E). Report value cumulatively ("hours saved"), which lifts renewals by maybe a few points (41EvCgwPrDc).
- Small things drive cancellations: rude staff, lateness, a dirty bathroom. The stated reason is usually a cover (PArqypiHcyQ).
- Rotating, constrained variety extends LTV for high-frequency consumables (rOTT4vZgB5Y). Keep classroom content under ~2 hrs (Skool data) (TooAB8Ow6cQ).
- CSAT caution: timing bias and extreme responders. Survey at weeks 0/2/4/8/12, but don't over-survey (yPDQCfrwh8E).
- Only change the product for macro threats when the metrics show it, e.g. churn rising ~10%/month (jqo0lVveh98).

### Referrals (the payoff of retention)
- Systematic referral asks at specific moments bring in ~30% more customers at any scale (qpQvdBFW_yI). Referral discount ceiling ≈ your normal CAC.
- Referrals are worth 2–3× cold customers, so be willing to pay the most for them (sPkMHh8zTMI). About 1 in 10 people refers about 10, so over-deliver for everyone (4Yz8ggEv0NU).
- Ask right at the moment of the result (film the testimonial as they step off the scale) (7wN8n3qrqnQ). "Now we're not asking for referrals. We're just saying who do you want to bring?" (B-ogfFiQpXg)
- 20% referral fees don't change behavior. Incentives must change behavior or they're useless (FMzKk73iUhw).
- "If you're not getting any [referrals] it means you're not that good" (HsQeQM1jUeg).

## Playbooks

### A. Churn diagnosis sprint (2 weeks)
1. Compute inflow, monthly churn (trailing 90 days), ARPU and the hypothetical max (XZEjp2mP1U0).
2. Build cohort curves: % of each start month still active at M1, M3, M6 and M12 (A_tx40lNpf8, FiL0r5_dUvM).
3. DM every recent canceller, tag the reasons, and rank them (-j8_YCWZ05Q).
4. Interview the longest-tenured customers about why they stay (-j8_YCWZ05Q). Interview peers who already solved churn and keep only the tactics nearly all of them share (the "consultative method") (y3T4GqoH0Us).
5. Classify the churn as structural (avatar), early (onboarding/expectations), overwhelm, or product. Fix the top 1–2 causes only.

### B. Fix early churn: onboarding and expectations (afbP6sB_Atc, h6y0nYVZgwE, 8VqSFBMMS4M)
1. Audit what sales promises against what delivery provides, and align the scripts.
2. Choreograph the first 72 hours: welcome, where/when/who, a first quick win.
3. Onboard the next day, or run group onboarding weekly.
4. Realign CS incentives to retention, not ticket closes.
5. Result in the SaaS case: cancellations cut 61%, ~$2M in profit saved in 6 months (afbP6sB_Atc).

### C. Subtract to retain (communities, programs) (-j8_YCWZ05Q, kloJJeiysxg, TooAB8Ow6cQ)
1. List everything inside the product. Find the one thing long-tenured members cite.
2. Cut or move the rest outside as promotion. Keep courses under ~2 hrs.
3. Show up daily: clear notifications, moderate, read complaints every morning, and delete bad actors (-j8_YCWZ05Q).
4. Post when a problem cluster appears, not on a fixed schedule (kloJJeiysxg).

### D. Restructure billing (41EvCgwPrDc, jOM0m34dxz4, -j8_YCWZ05Q)
1. Add annual at ~16% off plus VIP perks, and quarterly as the middle option.
2. Convert month-to-month into a fixed-term program that then rolls monthly.
3. Make renewals opt-out, with a reminder push.
4. Place a bonus or unlock just past the average cancel month.

### E. Fix structural churn (thDTmy7VGIw, CoPs-Bk8M9Y, BYpTRiRqS1Y)
1. Segment churn by customer size and type. Find the "vein" of highest-LTV customers (hnfh2jqNVEQ).
2. Qualify the front end to that vein. At Gym Launch, qualifying the front end halved sales, but escalations went from 22 to 0 and new clients paid more (hnfh2jqNVEQ).
3. For customers you keep at the low end, either drive cost-to-serve to near zero or price for their worst month.

## Anti-patterns
- **Marketing harder at a plateau.** More people just learn your product sucks (K8MFC9t7snY). "No one who has bad churn stops selling." (jqo0lVveh98)
- **Launching another product to fix churn** (K8MFC9t7snY). Bolting on software for stickiness (pw1PcHfYP2w).
- **Cutting price to reduce churn** ($5M/yr lesson) (BZQtuK-ucDM).
- **Adding more stuff.** Overwhelm causes churn (kloJJeiysxg).
- **Overhauling on young-cohort data** (-j8_YCWZ05Q). Knee-jerking on a single cancel reason (-j8_YCWZ05Q).
- **Mid-priced, high-touch service to tiny businesses** (CoPs-Bk8M9Y, jqo0lVveh98).
- **Treating a payment plan as continuity** (FiL0r5_dUvM, qsXxckCbci0).
- **Not tracking churn at all.** He claims 95% don't (y3T4GqoH0Us).
- **Selling more than you can deliver well.** If new clients churn while early ones stick, sell fewer and deliver the original level of value (U_s0ekwPK5g, yPDQCfrwh8E).
- **Leading with retention projects in a 30-day cash crisis** (SvIcS-Q1Hl4).

## Worked examples
- **SaaS portfolio company (55+ avatar):** churn spiked 90 days after a new product launch. Fixing onboarding, sales expectations and CS incentives cut cancellations 61% and saved ~$2M in 6 months (afbP6sB_Atc).
- **Doubling sales broke fulfillment:** 56 → 93 units/month. The fix was choreographed onboarding, a CRM with KPI tracking, and CS cadence (yPDQCfrwh8E).
- **Annual vs monthly:** $1K/mo payers cancel around months 4–5 (~$4–5K LTV), while $5K/yr upfront at 2% monthly churn ≈ $20.8K LTV (41EvCgwPrDc).
- **Skool:** added a $9 tier next to $99 for ideal customers who found it too expensive, and hid the classroom for new communities because building a course was a churn reason (-j8_YCWZ05Q, TooAB8Ow6cQ; Skool/Sam data).
- **CPA firm at 70% retention (target 80%+):** fix onboarding and communication first, then hire the expert (bgBIO6nZawg).
- **Concierge medicine (referral engine):** a 300-person member event → 100 scans → 25 sales ≈ $2.5M LTV (B-ogfFiQpXg).

## Metrics to watch
- **7 days:** new-customer activation and first-win rate. Onboarding completion within 72 hours. Attendance or usage drops flagged (h6y0nYVZgwE). Cancel reasons tagged.
- **30 days:** monthly churn vs your benchmark row above. M1 cohort survival. Save rate from exit interviews (~50% is possible) (h6y0nYVZgwE). Annual-plan take rate. Referral asks made vs referrals received.
- **90 days:** trailing-90-day churn and hypothetical max (QGcjweehrvU). M3 cohort survival, then M6/M12 over time. Revenue retention (net of upgrades). CAC trend (rising faster than CPMs suggests anti-referrals) (afbP6sB_Atc). Referral share of new customers (target ~30%+) (Uki3IUkUu7Q, qpQvdBFW_yI).
